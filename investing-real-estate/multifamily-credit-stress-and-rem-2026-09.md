# Multifamily Credit Stress and REM (September 2026)

Assessment of a claim circulating on social media: multifamily landlords who bought at sub-5% cap rates on 5-year loans are underwater, banks are extending and pretending instead of marking losses, and rising vacancy, repair, heating and interest costs will force keys back to lenders. Question: does this mean REM can fall substantially further?

## The claim, point by point

| Claim | Status (Sept 2026) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Most multifamily have 5-year loans" | **Wrong as stated.** | About half of the ~$2.2T of multifamily debt is Fannie/Freddie ($1.11T, 50%), mostly 7-10 year fixed. Banks hold ~$650B, typically 3-5 years. The real short-fuse debt is 2021-22 floating-rate bridge loans (2-3 years plus extensions) held by mortgage REITs, debt funds and CRE CLOs. |
| Sub-5% cap rate buyers are underwater | **Largely right for leveraged 2021-22 buyers.** | Illustrative: a 4.5% cap bought in 2021, revalued at 5.75% with flat NOI, is down ~22%. At 70-80% LTV that wipes most of the equity. With the 10Y at 5.26% (highest since 2007), a 5.75% cap rate is only ~50bp over Treasuries vs a historical spread of 200bp+, so cap rates have room to rise further. |
| Refinancing would kill the margin | **Right.** | 70% LTV at ~7% costs 4.9% of value a year against 4.5% NOI: negative leverage, coverage below 1.0x. Floating-rate borrowers are hit directly by Fed hikes (25bp to 3.75-4.00% on Sept 16; October hike odds ~70%). |
| Banks are extending and pretending | **Partly right, and visible.** | Bank multifamily delinquency is 1.41% (Q2 2026) vs 7.69% in CMBS (Aug 2026, up from 6.86% a year earlier). Part of the gap is loan quality, but CRED iQ calls it "a workout-driven cycle rather than a resolving one", with 90+ day delinquencies and charge-offs rising. Arbor modified ~$1.9B of multifamily bridge loans with maturity extensions. |
| Vacancy rising | **Out of date.** | Vacancy peaked earlier in 2026 after record deliveries (696k units in 2024, 531k in 2025). Deliveries fall to ~380-420k in 2026; one source has national vacancy down to ~7% in September. Rents are still flat to slightly down (-0.4% y/y). |
| Repair costs rising | Plausible, not checked. Insurance and property taxes (not mentioned) are the bigger operating-cost problem. | |
| Heating costs rising | **Mixed.** | Diesel/heating oil is at records ($6.31-6.52), which hurts oil-heated Northeast buildings. Natural gas is cheap: Henry Hub ~$2.9-3.0, and the 2026/27 winter strip is at its lowest of the year. Many newer buildings bill tenants for heat. |

**Net:** the mechanism is real, and the current macro (Fed hiking, 10Y at a 19-year high) makes it worse. But the stress is concentrated in the 2021-22 floating-rate bridge vintage, not "most multifamily", and it is already visible and partly priced.

So the claim bears directly on about a quarter of REM, and multifamily is only part of that quarter.

| March 2020 | -56% | -53% | Repo margin calls, forced MBS selling |
| 2021-23 | -47% | -39% | Fed hiking cycle, rate volatility |

REM's price also drifts down over time because many holdings pay out more than they earn (NAV erosion), so judge it on total return.

## Can REM fall substantially further?

Yes, but the multifamily story is not the main reason. The larger risk is the agency half and the rate backdrop:

- **Rates.** The 10Y is at 5.26%, the Fed is hiking, and Howell argues the 10Y "should test 6%" as yields catch up with 7-8% nominal GDP ([[market-newsletter-digest-2026-09-23]]).
- **Suppressed volatility.** The MOVE index is ~79-81, vs 135 in October 2023 at lower yields ([[market-newsletter-digest-2026-09-21]]). Agency mREITs lose book value when rate volatility and MBS spreads jump (2013, 2020, 2022). A catch-up in rate volatility is the scenario that produced REM's large drawdowns outside 2008.
- **Leverage.** Agency REITs run ~7-8x leverage on repo. Funding stress, as in March 2020, turns a spread move into forced selling.

**Rough scenario math (illustrative, my assumptions):**

| Scenario | Commercial (24%) | Resi credit (20%) | Agency (53%) | REM price impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Credit-only (the claim plays out, rates calm) | -35% | -10% | 0% | ~-10% |
| Credit + rate shock (10Y to ~6%, MOVE to 120+) | -40% | -20% | -20% | ~-24% |
| Funding crisis (2020-style) | -55% | -45% | -45% | ~-45 to -50% |

The claim alone points to a roughly 10% further decline. A "substantial" (25%+) fall needs a rate-volatility or funding shock on top. Given where rates and the MOVE index sit, that shock is the variable to watch.

## Watch list

- MOVE index vs 10Y level (the gap is the vulnerability).
- Agency MBS current-coupon spread; NLY/AGNC quarterly book value (late October).
- Trepp multifamily CMBS delinquency (7.69% in Aug) and special servicing.
- Bank multifamily 90+ day delinquencies and charge-offs (FDIC Q3 data).
- ABR, BXMT, STWD dividend decisions.
- October FOMC decision.
