# Branded Footwear and Apparel: $DECK vs. $BIRK vs. $AS vs. $ONON

**Prices as of October 15, 2026:** DECK $86.35, BIRK $35.33, AS $29.94, ONON $35.58. FX: EUR/USD 1.1208, CHF/USD 1.2056.
**Latest reported quarter:** the June 2026 quarter for all four (DECK fiscal Q1 FY27, BIRK fiscal Q3 FY26, AS Q2 2026, ON Q2 2026).
**Next reports:** DECK October 22 (after close), ON November 11, AS November 17. BIRK's fiscal year ended September 30; its Q4 report has no confirmed date.

## Bottom line

All four trade 29-30% below their 52-week highs, but for different reasons, and the valuations differ more than the businesses do.

* **Deckers is priced for no growth.** At 11.6x guided FY27 EPS and about 8x EV/EBIT, with $1.6B of cash and no borrowings, its forward earnings yield on EV is close to 10%. At a 9% cost of equity that implies about zero long-run growth. The debate is whether HOKA (69% of Q1 sales) is maturing: it grew 7.7% in Q1, and operating income fell 6% as SG&A rose 12.7%. EPS was flat only because the share count fell 7.4%.
* **Birkenstock is the only one with real debt, by choice.** It borrowed to buy back stock: gross borrowings are €1.69B and net leverage is 1.4x adjusted EBITDA excluding leases (1.7-1.8x including them). Coverage is comfortable (about 10x underlying EBIT to net finance cost in Q3). Constant-currency growth is 15%, but tariffs, FX and a higher tax rate mean reported 9-month EPS is *down* (€1.32 vs. €1.36) while adjusted EPS is up. At 13.6x forward earnings it is cheap if pricing power holds.
* **Amer Sports is the growth stock and is priced like one.** Revenue grew 32% in Q2 and guidance is about 24% for 2026, but the stock trades at 23x guided 2026 EPS. Two things flatter the current numbers: Q2 gross margin included a 390bp one-time tariff refund, and Greater China is 34% of revenue. It repaid its term loan this year and now has net cash excluding leases, but carries $954M of leases.
* **On is the cleanest balance sheet with the most visible deceleration.** It has CHF 1.15B of net cash (excluding leases), and financial income exceeds financial expense. Constant-currency growth is 21.6%, but a strong franc cut reported growth to 13.5%. Wholesale grew only 4.8% reported, and guidance was trimmed to "low-20%" constant currency. At 19.5x forward earnings, it needs that growth to hold.

On a simple "what growth is priced in" test (below), the market asks the least of Deckers and the most of Amer Sports.

How the derived rows were built (this page's arithmetic, not company figures):
* EV uses stockanalysis.com share counts (DECK 136.18M; BIRK 177.89M; AS 581.82M; ONON 334.21M economic shares, with Class B counted at its 1/10 economic weight) and balance sheets at June 30, 2026. EUR and CHF amounts are converted at October 15 spot rates.
* "Implied growth" treats forward earnings as a proxy for steady-state free cash flow and solves *cost of equity minus earnings yield on EV*. It is crude: it ignores reinvestment needs (AS guides $400M of 2026 capex) and margin changes. Its job is to rank how much each price assumes, not to value the stocks. The 9% cost of equity reflects a 10-year Treasury near 5.2% and a 10-year TIPS yield of 2.83% ([[market-newsletter-digest-2026-09-26]]). Higher real yields hurt the long-duration names (AS, ONON) more than DECK, which earns its value sooner.
* Deckers' EV/EBIT uses guided FY27 sales of $5.885B (midpoint) at an operating margin "slightly better than 21.5%."
* ON's PEG looks flattered: 2025 net income was depressed by a CHF 154M FX loss in H1 2025, which inflates the EPS growth base.

**What each price assumes:**
* **DECK:** HOKA flattens and UGG stays mid-single-digit; buybacks of about 80% of free cash flow do the compounding. Any sign HOKA stabilizes at high-single-digit growth looks undervalued at this multiple. Piper Sandler's bear case (January 2026) is that investors are overestimating HOKA's addressable market.
* **BIRK:** Mid-teens constant-currency growth continues and the premium price point survives. William Blair downgraded on the pricing-power question in August, and L Catterton sold about $1B of stock at $39.35. The sponsor overhang is the near-term technical issue.
* **AS:** 2026's 24% growth decays slowly, Arc'teryx keeps expanding in China and the West, and margins expand from 14.2-14.5% toward the segment margins of Arc'teryx (~22.5%) and Salomon (16-16.5%).
* **ONON:** Low-20s constant-currency growth persists after the deliberate wholesale slowdown, and the 65%+ gross margin holds. Currency is the swing factor: every point of franc strength comes straight off reported USD growth.

* **Birkenstock is the only name where leverage matters, and it is a choice.** In June it issued €900M of 4.50% notes, repaid €428.5M of 5.25% notes, and kept the rest for buybacks of up to $500M. Q3 finance cost of €43.0M included a €10.6M ASR fair-value loss and an €11.7M loss from derecognizing the old notes. Excluding those, coverage is close to 10x. The risk is that leverage rises if EBITDA disappoints while buybacks continue, and the guided year-end ratio has already moved up from 1.3-1.4x to 1.6-1.7x.
* **Amer Sports deleveraged quickly.** Non-current borrowings went from $792M at December 31 to zero, at the cost of a $50.5M debt-extinguishment loss in H1. What remains is mostly lease interest from a growing store fleet. Leases ($954M) are the largest of the four, which matters if the retail rollout (Arc'teryx is adding up to 12 China stores) meets a slowdown.
* **Deckers and On carry no financial risk.** Their exposure is operating: inventory and wholesale order books, plus leases ($472M and CHF 562M).

Inventory and operating margins for DECK and AS are computed from the releases (operating margin = operating income ÷ net sales).

**Deckers.** HOKA $703.5M (+7.7%), UGG $278.0M (+4.9%); other brands -18.1% as Koolaburra is phased out. International grew 8.4%, domestic 3.2%. Gross margin improved, but SG&A grew more than twice as fast as sales, so operating income fell. Inventory falling while sales grow means no channel-stuffing risk. Full-year guidance assumes HOKA low-double-digit and UGG mid-single-digit growth, so HOKA has to accelerate from Q1's 7.7%. Deckers reports on October 22, the nearest test of any of the four.

**Birkenstock.** Constant-currency growth was even across channels and regions (APAC +23% cc, Americas +14% cc), and DTC outgrew B2B for the first time in two years. The pressure is below revenue: 9-month reported gross margin fell 290bp (tariffs and FX), and the guided tax rate rose to 30-31% from 26-28%. Adjusted EPS (€0.74, +19% in Q3) is the best case for the business; reported net profit fell 15% in Q3, partly from refinancing one-offs.

**Amer Sports.** All three segments grew 20%+: Arc'teryx +32.5% (omni-comp +17%), Salomon +37.4% (DTC +52% on sneaker franchises), Wilson +24.3%. Asia Pacific grew 60% and Greater China 35.5%. Strip out the 390bp tariff refund and Q2 gross margin was about 61.7%, still up about 320bp. Q3 guidance slows growth to 18-20% with operating margin of 13.5-14.0%. Corporate expense guidance rose to $240M from $220M. China is a concentration risk (34% of H1 revenue).

**On.** The quarter missed on revenue (CHF 850.3M vs. about CHF 878M expected), and the stock fell up to 22% on the day. Management called the wholesale slowdown deliberate, to protect pricing ahead of 2027 launches, and said Q3 growth will be below Q4. Americas grew only 4.5% reported (13.0% cc). Apparel (+47.7%) and accessories (+88.3%) are growing fast from a small base (8% of sales). Gross margin of 65.4% and the raised full-year floor of 65% show pricing power intact. The issue is volume, not price.

## 4. How they compare

* **Cheapest on current earnings:** DECK, by a wide margin. It is also the only one whose operating income is falling.
* **Best growth at a reasonable price:** BIRK on headline multiples (13.6x forward, 0.85 PEG), with the caveats of leverage, a sponsor seller and margin pressure.
* **Highest quality growth, highest bar:** AS. It is the only one raising revenue guidance, but adjusted numbers include one-time tariff refunds and China concentration.
* **Strongest balance sheet with an execution question:** ONON. Pricing power is intact and volume growth is slowing. Currency explains much of the reported weakness.

**Shared risks:** US consumer sentiment (University of Michigan at 48.1 in September), tariff policy (AS and ON guidance exclude or one-off the refunds; Deckers assumes no refund collection), and FX. BIRK and ONON report in EUR and CHF, so their USD-converted multiples move with exchange rates, and the strong franc cut ON's Q2 reported growth by 8 points. DECK and AS report in USD.

**Data caveats:** Share counts, consensus estimates and short interest come from stockanalysis.com (October 15, 2026). Company figures come from the June-quarter releases. BIRK's adjusted EBITDA and ON's EBITDA guidance are company non-GAAP/non-IFRS measures. A September 2026 cause for Deckers' fall from about $100 to $78 was not found in search; the price path is recorded, not explained.
