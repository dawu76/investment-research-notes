## Executive Summary

CrowdStrike is the world's leading AI-native, cloud-delivered cybersecurity company, operating the Falcon platform — a single-agent, unified security architecture that spans endpoint protection, cloud security, identity security, AI-driven security operations, and threat intelligence. Revenue is ~95% subscription-based, generating $4.812B in FY2026 (+22% YoY) with ~78% GAAP subscription gross margins, ~22% non-GAAP operating margins, and $1.235B in annual free cash flow (26% FCF margin).

The company navigated the largest IT outage in recorded history on July 19, 2024 (a faulty Falcon sensor update crashing ~8.5 million Windows devices globally) and emerged with 97% gross revenue retention and re-accelerating growth. FY2026 became the first year CrowdStrike exceeded $1 billion in net new ARR ($1.01B for the full year). Q4 FY2026 (January 31, 2026) delivered ending ARR of $5.25B (+24% YoY) and record quarterly net new ARR of $331M (+47% YoY) and record quarterly FCF of $376M. FY2027 guidance calls for revenue of $5.87–5.93B (+22–23%) and ARR of $6.47–6.52B (+23–24%).

The Falcon Flex subscription model — allowing customers to consume any Falcon module via a pool of tokens — is now generating $1.69B in ARR (+120%+ YoY) with 1,600+ Flex customers. **In plain English: CrowdStrike sells a cloud-delivered security operating system that protects any device, workload, or identity — and once enterprises standardize on Falcon, switching costs are prohibitively high.**

## What They Sell and Who Buys

### Products — The Falcon Platform

CrowdStrike's Falcon platform operates as a unified "security operating system" with a **single lightweight agent** deployed on endpoints, workloads, and identity infrastructure. All telemetry flows into the **Security Cloud** — a proprietary data fabric that trains AI models and enables cross-module correlation that point-product vendors cannot replicate.

**Falcon Platform Modules (~30 total):**

| Domain | Key Products | Use Case |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Endpoint (XDR)** | Falcon Prevent (AV), Falcon Insight XDR, Falcon Fusion | Next-gen antivirus, endpoint detection & response, SOAR |
| **Cloud Security** | Falcon Cloud Security (CNAPP, CSPM, CWPP) | Cloud workload and container protection |
| **Identity Protection** | Falcon Identity Protection, Falcon Adversary Intelligence | Identity threat detection; stops lateral movement |
| **Next-Gen SIEM** | Falcon Next-Gen SIEM, LogScale | AI-driven security operations; log management |
| **Threat Intelligence** | Falcon Intelligence, Adversary Intelligence | Threat attribution; CTI; counter-adversary ops |
| **Managed Services** | Falcon Complete (MDR), Falcon OverWatch (24x7 threat hunting) | Fully managed security; human + AI SOC |
| **Data Protection** | Falcon Data Protection | Data loss prevention on endpoints |
| **Exposure Management** | Falcon Exposure Management | Attack surface management |

**Falcon Flex:** A consumption-based subscription model where customers purchase a pool of "Falcon Flex tokens" that can be applied to any module. Flex ARR reached $1.69B as of Q4 FY2026 (>120% YoY growth), with 1,600+ Flex customers. Average deal size >$1M ARR; after a "reflex" (Flex expansion), customers see an average 26% ARR lift.

### Customers

- **Scale:** Not disclosed precisely, but CrowdStrike reports customer metrics by ARR cohort
- **Module adoption (Q3 FY2026, latest disclosed):** 49% of customers use 6+ modules; 34% use 7+; 24% use 8+
- **Next-Gen SIEM + Cloud Security + Identity combined ARR: >$1.9B** (Q4 FY2026; fast-growing non-endpoint pillars)
- **Segments:** Large enterprises, government (FedRAMP authorized), financial services, healthcare, critical infrastructure
- **Geography:** Americas-dominant (~65%+); expanding internationally

### Buying Motivation

Security teams are under pressure to: (1) detect and stop sophisticated adversaries faster, (2) consolidate from 50+ point products to fewer platforms, and (3) reduce dwell time (time attackers remain undetected). CrowdStrike's single-agent approach eliminates the integration complexity of multi-vendor security stacks and provides a unified data model for AI-driven detection. The Falcon Flex model lowers procurement friction and enables gradual expansion across modules.

## Revenue Quality

### Predictability and Recurrence

- **~95% subscription-based** with ratable revenue recognition over multi-year contracts — extremely high visibility
- **Gross Retention Rate (GRR): 97%** — best-in-class for enterprise SaaS; maintained through the July 2024 outage and into FY2026
- **Ending ARR:** $5.25B (Q4 FY2026, +24% YoY) — the primary forward revenue indicator
- **Record net new ARR:** $331M in Q4 FY2026 (+47% YoY); $1.01B for all of FY2026 — first year exceeding $1B in net new ARR
- **Falcon Flex ARR:** $1.69B (Q4 FY2026, >120% YoY); 1,600+ Flex customers with an average 26% ARR uplift post-reflex

### Module Cohort Analysis

Customer module adoption depth is a key revenue quality indicator (Q3 FY2026, latest disclosed):
- Customers using 6+ modules: 49% (up from 48% in Q1 FY2026) — deeply embedded, high-switching-cost relationships
- Customers using 7+ modules: 34% (up from 32%)
- Customers using 8+ modules: 24% (up from 21%) — fully "platformized" on CrowdStrike
- As module count rises per customer, ARR per customer increases and churn probability falls toward near-zero

### The July 2024 Outage — Post-Mortem

The July 19, 2024 sensor content update failure crashed 8.5 million Windows endpoints globally — the largest IT outage in history. Despite the severity and widespread brand damage, CrowdStrike:
1. Maintained 97% GRR in subsequent quarters
2. Offered "customer care packages" (discounts/credits) that were absorbed into Falcon Flex deals
3. Re-accelerated net new ARR by Q3 FY2026 to +73% YoY
4. Signed a landmark Microsoft Marketplace partnership (February 2026)
5. IPO'd a "post-outage" narrative where customers demonstrated loyalty over alternatives

The outage paradoxically validated CrowdStrike's centrality to enterprise security: even after a near-catastrophic failure, enterprises chose to stay rather than face a multi-year, high-risk migration to alternatives.

### Concentration Risk

- No single customer >10% of revenue (disclosed in 10-K)
- Some government concentration but mitigated by broad enterprise diversification
- Americas-heavy geography — international expansion is a growth lever

## Capital Intensity

### Asset Model

CrowdStrike is **low capital intensity** — no hardware manufacturing, no owned physical infrastructure:

- **Security Cloud:** Runs on distributed, cloud-hosted infrastructure (not owned data centers)
- **Capex:** Primarily internal use software capitalization and modest IT infrastructure; low as % of revenue
- **R&D:** Expense-based (not capex); the key investment is human capital (AI researchers, threat intelligence analysts)
- **Working capital:** Annual subscription pre-payments generate deferred revenue — a favorable cash conversion dynamic

### Cash Conversion

FCF reached $1.235B in FY2026 on $4.812B revenue = ~26% FCF margin. Q4 FY2026 alone generated a record $376M in FCF (29% margin). The FCF margin trajectory is improving: FY2025 was 27%, FY2026 ~26% (absorbed outage headwinds), and management guided FY2027 FCF margins >30%. The strong FCF generation has enabled CrowdStrike to pursue bolt-on acquisitions without dilutive equity issuance. Charlotte AI usage grew 6x YoY in FY2026, with AI-driven detection rate (AI-DR) adoption up ~5x — indicating that the platform's AI investments are translating into measurable customer workflow adoption.

## Growth Drivers

### 1. Platform Consolidation — Module Expansion Within Installed Base

49% of customers use 6+ modules (Q3 FY2026), and 24% use 8+ modules. The path from 6 modules to 10 modules is the single biggest revenue lever within the existing base. Each additional module adds ARR with near-zero incremental cost to serve. Falcon Flex removes the procurement friction — customers can "flex" into new capabilities without negotiating new contracts. Flex ARR of $1.69B (+120%+ YoY) with 1,600+ customers and a 26% average ARR uplift post-reflex signals this flywheel is accelerating. FY2026 was the first year CrowdStrike exceeded $1B in net new ARR ($1.01B).

### 2. Non-Endpoint Pillars — Cloud, Identity, Next-Gen SIEM

CrowdStrike began as an endpoint company but has built credible #2 or #3 positions in cloud security (CNAPP), identity protection, and next-gen SIEM. The combined ARR of these three pillars exceeded $1.9B as of Q4 FY2026 (up from >$1.3B in FY2025) and is growing significantly faster than endpoint. As enterprises consolidate security vendors, CrowdStrike competes directly with Palo Alto Networks (PANW) for cloud security and identity workloads, with Zscaler for network security, and with Microsoft Sentinel/Defender for SIEM and endpoint.

### 3. AI-Native Security — Autonomous SOC and Charlotte AI

CrowdStrike's "Charlotte AI" — a generative AI security analyst embedded in the Falcon console — saw usage grow 6x YoY in FY2026, with AI-DR (AI-driven detection rate) adoption up ~5x. The Security Cloud processes trillions of events daily — providing a training dataset that is arguably the largest and most labeled in cybersecurity. This enables AI-driven threat hunting, automated triage, and autonomous SOC operations. CrowdStrike's AI-native strategy is now customer-validated: Charlotte AI and AI-DR represent both a product differentiation lever and an ARPU expansion opportunity within existing Flex deals. The AI security analytics market is a $20B+ opportunity by 2030.

### 4. Government and International Expansion

CrowdStrike holds FedRAMP High certification and is deeply embedded in U.S. government security programs. International markets represent a significant underpenetrated opportunity, with EMEA and APAC growing faster than the Americas. The Microsoft Marketplace partnership (February 2026) dramatically simplifies procurement for Microsoft-centric government and enterprise customers globally.

## Competitive Edge

### Moat Analysis

| Moat Type | Strength | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Switching Costs** | Very High | Single agent on every endpoint; removing CRWD requires re-architecting endpoint security, re-deploying a new agent, migrating 18+ months of telemetry/context; 97% GRR even post-catastrophic outage |
| **Data / AI Network Effect** | Very High | Trillions of events/day from enterprise endpoints globally; the Security Cloud trains AI models unavailable to competitors; more sensors = better detection = more sensors |
| **Brand and Adversary Intelligence** | High | CrowdStrike's Adversary Intelligence team (ex-government operators) is the most respected in the industry; "naming" nation-state adversaries (Fancy Bear, Cozy Bear) = unmatched credibility |
| **Platform Architecture** | High | Single lightweight agent (no conflicts, no compatibility issues); single console; single data fabric — competitors with multi-agent approaches have inherent complexity disadvantages |
| **Scale** | High | Dominant market share in enterprise endpoint; largest labeled security telemetry dataset globally |

### Durability Assessment

The moat is **exceptional and demonstrated under pressure**. The July 2024 outage was the ultimate stress test — an existential brand event where every competitor accelerated sales motions against CrowdStrike accounts. The result: 97% GRR, no material customer losses, and FY2026 delivering record net new ARR of $1.01B — the first year exceeding $1B. Q4 FY2026 net new ARR of $331M (+47% YoY) validated that the post-outage recovery is fully complete and demand is robust. The AI/data network effect is self-reinforcing: as the largest endpoint telemetry collector globally, CrowdStrike's detection models improve faster than competitors', widening the performance gap over time. Charlotte AI usage up 6x YoY and AI-DR adoption up ~5x YoY are concrete evidence this flywheel is accelerating.

The Microsoft Marketplace partnership (February 2026) is strategic: rather than competing as adversaries, CRWD and MSFT now collaborate, allowing customers to deploy Falcon via Microsoft credits — dramatically lowering adoption friction for the Microsoft-centric enterprise market.
