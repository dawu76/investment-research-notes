# Market and Investment Newsletter Digest — July 16, 2026

## 1. TSMC Q2 2026: Massive CapEx and Arizona Upgrades Meet a Muted Market

TSMC reported Q2 2026 results that beat estimates, accompanied by aggressive increases in long-term capital plans.

*   **Financial Performance:** Q2 revenue reached **US$40.2bn**, and gross margin rose 150 bps QoQ to **67.7%**. operating profit was **NT$767bn**.
*   **Guidance Upgrades:** 
    *   **Revenue Growth:** Raised full-year revenue growth guidance from >30% to **slightly above 40% YoY** in USD terms.
    *   **Annual CapEx:** Raised full-year capex guidance from $52–$56bn to **$60–$64bn**.
    *   **Arizona Investment:** Added **$100bn** to its Arizona fab expansion, taking total planned investment to **$265bn** in a new **"6 + 2 + 4" layout** (six chip-making fabs, two advanced packaging fabs, and four additional fabs), which will include 2nm production.
*   **Gross Margin Dilution:** Guided Q3 gross margins to **65%–67%** (below expectations of 69.9%). The faster-than-expected ramp-up of the **2nm process** is projected to dilute gross margin by 3%–4% in H2 2026. The 2026–2028 capacity CAGR for 2nm is now projected to exceed 70%.
*   **Pricing Power:** Average Selling Prices (ASPs) rose **15%**, indicating that node-wide price hikes from last month are starting to impact financials.
*   **Demand Dynamics:** CEO CC Wei noted that while clients may be double-counting expectations, TSMC verifies real demand using its own data center construction and equipment shipment surveys, confirming clients are not warehousing chips.

> [!TIP]
> TSMC's massive upgrades carry far more structural weight than public statements from chip designers or model builders, representing a verified capital commitment to the physical capacity of AI.

## 2. ASML Q2 2026: Installed Base Management & Pricing Power Shift

ASML delivered strong Q2 earnings, highlighting underappreciated drivers of semiconductor tool demand.

*   **Financial Health:** First-half revenue reached **€11.8bn**, with Q3 guided at **€11–12bn**. Full-year revenue is tracking toward **€43–45bn**, placing the company at the low end of its 2030 target range (€44–60bn) four years early. A new Capital Markets Day was set for June 2027 to revise long-term models.
*   **Install Base Management (IBM):** The Q2 gross margin beat was driven by the IBM business (upgrades and services on already-installed fleets), which brought in **€2.8bn** (€300m above guidance). ASML is capturing high-margin, software-led upgrades (e.g., NXE:3600 to NXE:3800) as foundries scramble to enhance existing tool productivity instead of waiting for new tool shipments.
*   **Litho Intensity & Memory:** Memory revenue is guided to grow **75% YoY in 2026** (outpacing Logic at 25%), driven by high lithography layer counts (litho intensity) on advanced DDR and HBM nodes. Low-NA EUV shipments are on track for **65 units** in 2026.
*   **High-NA EUV Commercialization:** Intel Foundry officially qualified High-NA EUV (NXE:5000/5200 class, ~$350m per unit) for high-volume production on its **18A process**, demonstrating the tool's commercial maturity.
*   **Pricing Flex:** Reports indicate ASML is planning price hikes, including a **10% increase on DUV systems** for Chinese chipmakers and higher EUV pricing for TSMC. China continues to represent **20% of sales** (€8.6–9.0bn).

## 3. The AI Capex Debt Supercycle & GPU/Power Bottleneck Shift

As chip supply catches up, the structural constraints of the AI trade are shifting from silicon to utility infrastructure.

*   **Hyperfunder Capex:** The combined 2026 capex plans of the four major hyperscalers (Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft) have reached **$638bn**. Barclays projects this will rise to **$1.0tn in 2027** and **$1.2tn in 2028** (a 43% CAGR). Over the last 12 months, hyperscaler capex forecasts rose **93%**, while revenue expectations rose only **12%**.
*   **Bottleneck Shift:** Ben Pouladian argues that Nvidia’s ~$110bn prepayment and supply chain buildout have successfully ended the GPU/chip shortage. The primary bottleneck has shifted downstream to **energized real estate, permitting, water access, and skilled tradesmen**.
*   **Power Plays:** **Bloom Energy** is flagged as a high-conviction utility play due to its ability to deploy fuel cells on-site in **under 80 days** (bypassing transformer constraints), compared to GE Vernova's turbine backlog which stretches to 2028–2029.
*   **Inference Economics:** Pouladian estimates that Anthropic’s inference gross margins run at **70%–80%+** (comparable to Salesforce's 72%–77%), indicating that frontier labs can fund training compute out of operational cash flows rather than pure venture capital cash burn.
*   **The China Defection Threat:** A five-person company doing agentic coding work worth $1m might pay a Western lab $400k–$500k, versus **$6,000** using Chinese open-source models (DeepSeek, Qwen). This creates a massive geopolitical and economic pricing threat ("influence through inference") as China exports cheap model API access.

## 6. Howard Marks on AI Autonomy and the 2008 Melt Down Playbook

Famed distressed-debt investor Howard Marks discussed AI and detailed historical precedents of market crisis management.

*   **Marks' AI Upgrade:** Marks upgraded his AI view, citing **autonomy** (handing AI a job without specifying the method) and **unpredictability** as unprecedented features. He notes that **second-level thinking** (going against consensus and being correct) remains a human-only edge.
*   **2008 Melt Down Playbook:** In 2007–08, Oaktree Capital raised an **$11bn fund** and deployed an average of **$450m per week** for 15 weeks ($7bn in a single quarter) immediately after Lehman's collapse, despite having zero data and zero confidence. 
*   **Buffett Proxy:** Oaktree and Berkshire both held Enron's Osprey debt. Buffett ceded his proxy to Oaktree (Bruce Karsh's team) to run the restructuring, suggesting a precedent where Berkshire's record cash pile is deployed by backing trusted third-party managers rather than solo stock-picking during a systemic crisis.

## 7. Macro Economy: June PPI Softens & BoJ Reverse Carry Trade lessons

Inflation prints fell while analysts highlighted the systemic risks of historical carry trades.

*   **June PPI:** June PPI fell **0.3% MoM** (driven by gasoline price drops), pushing July rate hike expectations to 11% (first hike delayed to December). Core PCE is projected to print at **+0.17% MoM**.
*   **The BoJ Reverse Carry Trade:** Michael Gayed analyzed the Bank of Japan's 2006 rate hike exit. The yield gap between the 0.25% BoJ rate and the 5.25% Fed rate funded a **$1.0tn carry trade**. The subsequent unwind caused USD/JPY to appreciate 30% (from 124 to 87), triggering a global deleveraging where US stocks fell 57%, EM fell 67%, and Japanese exports collapsed 45%.
*   **Bank of Korea:** Hiked **25 bps** (first hike in 3.5 years) citing AI growth, while the KOSPI plummeted 6% on leveraged ETF speculation.
