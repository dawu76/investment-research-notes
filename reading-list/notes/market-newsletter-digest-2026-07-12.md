# Market and Investment Newsletter Digest — July 12, 2026

## 1. Macro Policy & Liquidity: The Carry Unwind Setup, Repo Mania, and Tom Lee's 8,000 Target

### Themes & Key Observations
- **Setup for a Carry Recompression**: The Invesco CurrencyShares Japanese Yen Trust (FXY) closed at 56.95, just 55 cents above its 52-week low of 56.40, while the S&P 500 VIX collapsed to 16.15 (a 27% decline from its June peak). Simultaneously, high-yield credit spreads (HYG/LQD ratio) remain historically tight. Michael Gayed notes that this three-way configuration—yen at multi-decade lows, equity vol at multi-month lows, and credit complacency—creates a textbook setup for a reverse-carry episode, which will rotate capital defensive.
- **The Equity Repo Mania**: Overnight dollar funding markets have seen volatile spikes in the "equity repo" market (leveraged stock finance). Soaring stock prices, leveraged ETFs, and a booming IPO market have overloaded dealer balance sheets under Basel III. Primary dealers have been charging as high as +150 bps over SOFR to finance baskets. Traders can monitor this leverage thirst via the "equity futures basis" (spread between money market borrowing rates like SOFR and index total return rates).
- **The S&P 8,000 Call and Four Tests**: Tom Lee (Fundstrat) projects the S&P 500 will reach 8,000 by year-end (up from 7,700), representing $400 EPS for 2027 at a 20x multiple. However, he warns of an H2 correction triggered by four tests: (1) Kevin Warsh’s hawkish monetary policy, (2) SpaceX lockup expiries draining market liquidity, (3) corporate margin compression, and (4) the AI equity bubble. He notes Bitcoin is "not broken" at $58K (based on production cost support) unless it falls below $29K.
- **Corporate Free Cash Flow Mismatch**: The free cash flow of the five largest hyperscalers is rolling over hard (undergoing a historic margin transfer to Nvidia and silicon makers), transferring huge structural capital risks downstream. S&P 500 Q2 earnings estimates rose to 23.6% growth, but 60% of this growth is driven by AI hardware, whereas the median index stock is growing at just 9%.

## 2. AI Hardware & Infrastructure: TSMC's Grating Coupler Failure, Data Center Backlash, and Claude Code Banned

### Themes & Key Observations
- **TSMC CPO Plan-A Delayed**: TSMC has botched its high-density 2-D grating couplers and delayed SiN PDK development. This has forced Nvidia to temporarily move its optical interconnect program to Tower Semiconductor. While Nvidia’s Plan A was slow-and-wide (50-64G NRZ) 8-wavelength DWDM on TSMC's COUPE Co-packaged Optics (CPO), Plan B is now 200/400G PAM4 on 16-wavelength DWDM on Tower’s Silicon Photonics (SiPho) Near-packaged Optics (NPO) (detailed in [[cpo-and-npo-optics]]). Tower's solution carries a penalty of lower channel density and worse power efficiency but is immediately viable.
- **SaaS and Developer Tool Stack Split**: China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) classified Anthropic’s Claude Code developer tool as carrying a security backdoor risk, leading Alibaba to order its engineers to immediately uninstall the tool. This follows Washington restricting Anthropic's frontier models. 
- **Populist Data Center Backlash**: Bruce Mehlman reports a surging local backlash against data center construction. Active data center opposition groups doubled in Q1 2026. 14 states have considered bans or restrictions (New York passed one, pending Gubernatorial veto), and over 100 local moratoriums have passed. The issue is populist (Outside vs Inside) over electricity/water utility usage rather than partisan (Red vs Blue).
- **Token Supplier Balance Sheet Stress**: SiliconFlow processes 578.5 billion tokens a day but reports a negative 119% gross margin in its Hong Kong IPO, exposing the brutal cash burn and pricing wars among pure-play token suppliers.
- **Sovereign AI Adoption**: UBTECH's full-body companion robot (costing up to Rmb 990,000) saw 13,000 deposits placed before pricing was disclosed, reflecting massive domestic retail AI demand in China.

## 3. Corporate Finance & Deal Structure: SpaceX exit liquidity, Jersey Mike's Franchise Model, and Fannie/Freddie GSE Reform

### Themes & Key Observations
- **SpaceX Exit Liquidity Warning**: George Noble warns that SpaceX's IPO (priced at 90x–140x revenue with $5B in losses last year) is a manufactured [[funding-short-squeeze]]. Less than 5% of shares were float, forcing index-tracking Russell/Nasdaq ETFs to buy $7.3 billion of mandatory shares. However, lockups will release 20% in August and 28% after Q3 earnings (1.3B shares), expanding float by 900% and providing exit liquidity for early insiders.
- **Jersey Mike's S1 Franchise Model**: Jersey Mike's filed for a public offering at a [[valuations]] range of $12 billion following its $8 billion sale to Blackstone. CJ Gustafson shows it is an asset-light royalty clipping business. Jersey Mike's owns almost zero of its 3,000+ stores, but clips ~11.5% off the top of all registers in America run by 630+ franchise owners.Peter Cancro (founder) walked away with billions, distributing $50M to his stepson and $40M to his CFO.
- **Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (GSE) Reform**: Share prices of FNMA and FMCC are highly volatile ahead of a critical housing finance efficiency report under Executive Order 14393 (due July 11th weekend). If the Trump Administration reduces the Enterprise Regulatory Capital Framework (ERCF) requirements or deems the Senior Preferred Stock (SPS) Liquidation Preference (LP) paid off, common shareholders stand to inherit all retained earnings, driving stocks up 3–4x initially (and 6–7x long-term).
