## Bull Case

### Section 1: Secular TAM & Structural Market Position

- The Zero Trust market is estimated at $41.7B in 2025 and projected to reach $88.8B by 2030 — a 2x expansion in 5 years — while Zscaler's own TAM estimate sits at ~$96B, meaning the company currently captures roughly 3.7% of its addressable opportunity at $3.5B ARR. The runway dwarfs the current revenue base.
- Global 2000 penetration stands at ~40% and Fortune 500 penetration at >45%, which sounds mature until you invert it: more than half the Fortune 500 has not yet standardized on ZS, and the Global 2000 still has 600+ enterprises that represent the highest-density, highest-ACV logos available. The installed base is large; the greenfield is larger.
- Zscaler holds ~34% SSE market share per IDC — a dominant plurality in a market that is still in early consolidation. Competing architectures (VPN, legacy MPLS, on-prem firewall stacks) are not yet fully replaced in most enterprise networks. The structural migration from hardware-defined perimeters to cloud-native Zero Trust is a decade-long replacement cycle, not a two-year sprint.
- Zero Trust Everywhere — Zscaler's most comprehensive, highest-ACV platform bundle — grew from 130 customers to 550 in a single year, a 4x increase. This is not incremental upsell; it is evidence that customers are expanding to the full platform stack, compounding revenue per logo at the exact moment the bear thesis suggests the platform is stalling.
- The 31.3% single-day crash reprices the stock against a temporary sales leadership disruption, not against the multi-decade secular shift in enterprise network architecture. At ~$127, the market is capitalizing a transient event as if it were a permanent structural impairment of a platform with 34% market share, $96B TAM, and sub-4% penetration of the total opportunity.

### Section 2: Revenue Quality — Installed Base Is Fortress-Grade

- Net Revenue Retention of 115% means Zscaler's existing customer base expands revenue by 15% annually with zero new logo contribution. On a $3.5B ARR base, that is approximately $525M of incremental ARR generated purely from the installed base — before a single new logo is signed. Sales leadership disruption affects new logo pipeline; it does not unwind expansion economics already embedded in multi-year contracts.
- RPO of $6.5B grew +30% YoY, representing approximately 1.9x trailing ARR. This forward revenue visibility — contracted, not speculative — means more than 18 months of current ARR is already on the books. A pipeline disruption in Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 does not threaten revenue that is already contractually committed.
- Non-GAAP gross margin holds at ~80%, stable and structurally anchored by the platform's cloud-native, multi-tenant architecture. As ARR scales, incremental revenue flows through at high incremental margins — a dynamic that produced a record non-GAAP operating margin of 23.0% in Q3 FY2026, up 140bps YoY, despite CapEx headwinds from AI infrastructure pull-forward. The leverage is real and compounding.
- FCF of $136M in Q3, even with CapEx pressure, represents a ~16% FCF margin for the quarter. The FY2026 FCF margin guide of 22.8–23.3% — even after a 370–380bps cut — implies $760M–$775M of annual FCF at the guided revenue midpoint. This is not a profitability story in jeopardy; it is a story of deliberate investment in AI infrastructure that temporarily compresses near-term free cash flow while building competitive durability.
- $1M+ ARR customers grew +10% sequentially in Q2 FY2026 to 728. This cohort represents the stickiest, most strategically embedded customers on the platform. Churning a $1M+ ARR relationship requires ripping out core network infrastructure and retraining thousands of users. These accounts do not leave because two sales leaders departed.

### Section 3: Platform Expansion Signals — Q3 Beat + Symmetry Plugs the Gap

- Q3 FY2026 revenue of $850.5M beat the Street estimate of $835M and grew +25% YoY. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.08 beat $1.01. A record non-GAAP operating margin of 23.0% was delivered in the same quarter the company absorbed CapEx pressure and integration costs from Red Canary. The core platform execution is not impaired — the quarter itself is evidence of that.
- ZFlex TCV surpassed $290M in Q2 FY2026, growing +65% sequentially. ZFlex is Zscaler's consumption-based licensing model designed for AI and cloud-native workloads — its explosive uptake signals that enterprises are adopting ZS as the security layer for non-human identities and agentic workloads, not just employee endpoints. This is the next growth vector, and it is already inflecting.
- Zero Trust Everywhere customer count reached 550 in Q2 FY2026, up from 130 a year prior — a 323% increase in 12 months. Each Zero Trust Everywhere customer represents a significantly higher ACV than a single-module deployment. The platform upsell motion is working at scale despite all the headline noise.
- The Symmetry Systems acquisition ($175M, announced May 19, 2026) directly addresses the specific gap Bloomberg Intelligence flagged: ZS "lacks identity security for AI agents which may limit larger order closings." Management identified the gap, moved within weeks, and closed the acquisition. This is proactive product roadmap execution.
- The appointment of Dr. Swamy Kocherlakota as EVP Agentic AI Security Engineering — paired with Symmetry's technology — signals that Zscaler is building a dedicated organizational capability around AI-native security. Red Canary's ARR guide was raised from $95M to $130M for FY2026, demonstrating the MDR acquisition is outperforming initial expectations.

### Section 4: Why the Most-Feared Bear Scenario (Sales Leadership Disruption) Actually Strengthens the Bull Case

- The FY2027 guide of 16–17% ARR and revenue growth was the single catalyst for the 31% crash — but the analyst community's near-unanimous characterization of it as a "kitchen-sink" guide is the most important data point the market is ignoring. Management explicitly cited two undisclosed senior sales leadership departures as the rationale for a conservative guide. If those departures are temporary personnel events — not product failures or competitive displacement — then the guide floor reflects contingency planning, not structural ceiling.
- At NRR of 115% and RPO of $6.5B (+30% YoY), expansion ARR from the installed base alone can drive meaningful growth independent of new logo momentum. Modeling conservatively: if NRR holds at 115% on a $3.5B ARR base, the expansion contribution alone is ~$525M — equivalent to approximately 15% ARR growth before a single new logo closes. The bear's feared scenario of "multi-quarter new logo pipeline disruption" is partially pre-hedged by the mathematical reality of the expansion engine.
- New leadership is already being built into the organization. The CMO hire (Sunil Frida) and EVP Agentic AI hire (Dr. Swamy Kocherlakota) reflect active C-suite reconstruction, not an organization in paralysis. Sales leadership departures are painful; they are also among the most recoverable enterprise disruptions, typically measured in two to four quarters before new pipeline normalizes.
- At ~$127 per share against FY2026 guided revenue of $3.33B, the stock trades at roughly 10.7x forward revenue — a valuation last seen when ZS was growing meaningfully slower and carried a fraction of today's RPO and margin profile. If growth re-accelerates to even 20% in FY2028 — requiring nothing more than stable leadership and continued Zero Trust adoption — the current entry price will look like a generational mispricing of one of enterprise security's two or three structural winners.

**Bull Summary:** The core bull thesis is simple: Zscaler is the dominant platform in a $96B TAM that is still in the early-to-middle innings of a once-in-a-generation enterprise network architecture replacement, and the 31% crash repriced a temporary personnel disruption as if it were a permanent structural failure of that platform. The evidence against permanent impairment is overwhelming — 115% NRR, $6.5B RPO growing at 30%, a record 23% operating margin, and 550 Zero Trust Everywhere customers growing 4x in a single year are not the metrics of a platform losing competitive relevance. At ~$127, investors are offered the rare opportunity to buy a company with 34% SSE market share, a $290M ZFlex TCV inflecting toward AI-native workloads, and a direct answer to its most cited product gap (Symmetry, $175M) at a multiple that assumes the conservative kitchen-sink guide is the permanent growth ceiling rather than the clearing of the bar. The crash is the opportunity, not the confirmation.

## Bear Case

### Section 1: The #1 Structural Risk — Sales Leadership Disruption Is Deeper Than "Prudent Caution"

- Two senior sales leaders departed and neither has been publicly named nor replaced as of May 28, 2026. This is not a standard personnel transition — the opacity itself signals the departures were involuntary or acrimonious, which matters for team morale and pipeline continuity across a 2,000+ person go-to-market organization.
- Enterprise sales cycles at ZS's scale average 9–18 months for $1M+ deals. The lost institutional knowledge and relationships carried by two senior sales leaders cannot be replaced within one fiscal year. Historical precedent across enterprise software (Salesforce 2019, CrowdStrike 2022) shows meaningful pipeline disruption lasting 4–6 quarters minimum post-departure.
- The math on Q4 FY2026 and into FY2027 is brutal: Q4 FY2025 net new ARR was approximately $174M (backing into FY2025 reported ARR). If Q4 FY2026 comes in at $140M — a reasonable haircut given the disruption — then annualized ARR additions fall to roughly $530–560M vs. FY2026's run-rate of $640M+. That alone pulls FY2027 exit ARR toward $4.0–4.1B vs. the Street's prior $4.4B+ model.
- Bloomberg Intelligence stated explicitly: "muted new customer additions are likely to weigh on the top line." New logo ARR has been the weakest component of ZS's growth for two consecutive quarters, and the $1M+ customer count of 728 (last reported Q2 FY2026) has been growing at a decelerating rate.
- Management's FY2027 initial guide of 16–17% growth, given from a position of visibility into Q4 pipeline, is itself the confession. CEOs do not issue 16% growth guides for a $3.5B ARR business with "temporary" disruption — they issue them when they genuinely do not see the pipeline to guide higher.

### Section 2: Competitive Bundling — PANW Is Accelerating While ZS Is Decelerating

- PANW SASE ARR reached $1.3B+ growing at 34% YoY, against ZS's FY2027 guided growth of 16–17%. In absolute dollar terms: if PANW SASE adds ~$440M in net new SASE ARR over the next four quarters (34% of $1.3B) while ZS adds ~$560–580M (16–17% of $3.5B), the gap closes from 2.7x ZS's size to 2.3x within one year — a measurable share shift.
- PANW now counts 6,800 SASE customers including one-third of the Fortune 500. ZS's $1M+ ARR customer count of 728 is growing slowly. PANW's bundled Strata/Prisma/Cortex platform creates procurement leverage that a pure-play ZIA/ZPA vendor cannot match when CISOs are rationalizing vendor counts.
- Bloomberg Intelligence flagged ZS's identity security gap for AI agents: "lacks identity security for AI agents which may limit larger order closings." AI agent proliferation is the defining enterprise security trend of 2025–2026. The Symmetry Systems acquisition ($175M) addresses DSPM/data security, not identity for AI agents — the gap Bloomberg identified remains open.
- ZS has now spent approximately $867M on acquisitions (Red Canary $692M + Symmetry $175M) while simultaneously guiding CapEx to 8–9% of revenue in FY2026 and rising 200bps further in FY2027. This capital allocation pattern — M&A to fill product gaps while organic growth decelerates — is a warning sign, not a growth catalyst.
- SSE market share at ~34% for ZS looks like a ceiling, not a floor, when the next-largest competitor is growing 2x faster. Share erosion at the margin translates directly to net new ARR compression even without absolute customer losses.

### Section 3: Strip Out the Inorganic — Organic New Logo Growth Is the Problem

- Total reported ARR of $3,525M includes ~$130M from Red Canary (per management's own FY2026 guidance). Organic core ARR is therefore approximately $3,390M, implying organic YoY growth of ~21% — not the headline 25%. Investors paying a premium multiple are paying for inorganic revenue dressed as organic momentum.
- Q3 FY2026 total net new ARR was $166M. Red Canary's quarterly net new ARR contribution from renewals and expansions is approximately $34M (estimated $130M annual / 4). Stripping this out, organic net new ARR is approximately $132M — down from Q2 FY2026's reported $155.5M. That is a sequential organic deceleration of 15%+ in a single quarter.
- Red Canary is explicitly experiencing elevated churn post-acquisition. If churn is elevated and the organic Red Canary base is contracting, the inorganic net new contribution going forward is even lower than $34M/quarter, meaning organic net new ARR in Q4 FY2026 and FY2027 faces further headwinds.
- The RPO of $6.5B (+30% YoY) is increasingly contaminated by inorganic contracts from both Red Canary and legacy SIEM/MDR deals that carry lower unit economics than ZIA/ZPA platform deals. RPO growth exceeding ARR growth (30% vs. 25%) reflects longer-duration, lower-quality contracts being booked, not accelerating core platform demand.
- New logo growth — the true leading indicator — is the precise variable management refuses to quantify specifically. The $1M+ ARR customer count of 728 at Q2, Bloomberg's "muted new customer additions" warning, and the organic net new ARR math above converge on a single conclusion: ZS's new logo engine has stalled.

### Section 4: Multiple Compression Math — $127 Is Not Cheap if Growth Settles at 16%

- At $127/share with approximately 150M diluted shares outstanding, market cap is approximately $19B. Assuming net cash of roughly $2.5B, EV is approximately $16.5B. Against FY2027 revenue guidance midpoint of ~$3.90B, EV/NTM revenue is approximately 4.2x — not screamingly cheap, but the real problem is the FCF multiple.
- FY2027 FCF margin: FY2026 guide cut to 22.8–23.3% from 26.5–27%. CapEx increases another 200bps in FY2027. That implies FY2027 FCF margin of approximately 20–21%. At $3.90B revenue and 20.5% FCF margin, FY2027 FCF is approximately $800M — implying 21x EV/FCF at current price. That is expensive for a business guiding 16–17% growth with a broken sales organization.
- For context: CrowdStrike at 20–22% growth trades at approximately 35–40x FCF; Palo Alto Networks at 13–15% ARR growth trades at approximately 25x FCF. ZS at 16% growth with decelerating organic additions and structurally higher CapEx does not deserve a premium to PANW. At PANW's multiple, ZS is worth approximately $19–22B EV — roughly flat to current, with significant downside if growth settles at 14–15%.
- The bear case to $90–100 requires only modest degradation: if FY2027 growth slips to 14–15% (organic net new ARR running at $130–140M/quarter vs. the $155–175M needed), the revenue base is $3.75–3.80B. At 19x FCF on $765–780M FCF (at 20.5% margin), EV is approximately $14.5–14.8B, or a share price of $95–100 accounting for net cash. The stock does not need a disaster — it needs only the FY2027 guide to prove directionally correct.
- The "kitchen sink" bull narrative requires trusting management that guided 26.5–27% FCF margins in February, then cut to 22.8–23.3% in May, citing CapEx pull-forward and sales disruption they did not warn about. That is not a track record that earns the benefit of the doubt on whether the 16–17% growth guide is actually conservative.

**Bear Summary:** The Zscaler bear case at $127 is not primarily about valuation math — it is about three simultaneous impairments arriving at the same time: an opaque senior sales leadership departure with no replacement timeline, accelerating competitive pressure from PANW SASE growing at twice ZS's guided rate, and a structural FCF model deterioration driven by CapEx expansion that management itself acknowledges will worsen in FY2027. Stripping Red Canary's inorganic contribution reveals organic net new ARR running at approximately $132M in Q3 FY2026 — a level that mathematically cannot support re-acceleration to 20%+ growth without a rebuilt sales motion that has not yet started. At 21x EV/FCF for 16% growth with rising capital intensity, no named sales leader replacements, and a product gap in AI agent identity security that Bloomberg explicitly flagged as a deal-size limiter, $127 is a value trap masquerading as a post-crash opportunity. The path to $90–100 requires only that the FY2027 guide proves accurate — not that anything gets worse from here.

### The 3 Critical Divergence Assumptions

**Assumption 1: Sales Leadership Disruption — Temporary Personnel Event or Structural Go-to-Market Break?**

- **Bull version:** The departures were isolated senior personnel events with no product-, pricing-, or competitive-displacement cause. Expansion ARR from 115% NRR and $6.5B RPO mathematically cushions FY2027. New pipeline normalizes within 2–4 quarters as replacement leadership is hired. The guide floor proves conservative by Q2 FY2027.

- **Bear version:** Two unnamed, unreplaced senior leaders carried irreplaceable relationships in $1M+ deal cycles averaging 9–18 months. The opacity signals involuntary departures that depress morale across a 2,000+ person GTM organization. New logo organic net new ARR — already running at an estimated $132M/quarter stripped of Red Canary — decelerates further through FY2027 Q1–Q2, and replacement hiring takes a full year before pipeline normalizes.

- **Why it's the crux:** ZS's installed base NRR engine is durable regardless of sales disruption; the contested variable is whether new logo additions re-accelerate or stall for multiple fiscal years. If stall, the math on a return to 20%+ growth is broken. If recovery, the current multiple is genuinely cheap.

- **Verdict: Lean bear [narrow].** The bear is correct that enterprise sales disruptions at senior levels involving unnamed, unreplaced leaders typically take longer to resolve than two quarters — historical precedent (Salesforce 2019, CrowdStrike 2022) supports 4–6 quarter normalization timelines. The bull correctly notes that 115% NRR on a $3.5B base generates ~$525M expansion ARR independently, preventing catastrophic misses. The bear wins on disruption duration; the bull wins on impact severity being cushioned. Net: the guide is more likely directionally accurate than sandbagged.

---

**Assumption 2: Organic vs. Inorganic Growth — Is the Platform Genuinely Expanding or Being Artificially Inflated?**

- **Bull version:** Red Canary's raised guidance ($95M → $130M) demonstrates real outperformance. Zero Trust Everywhere at 550 customers (4x YoY) and ZFlex TCV at $290M (+65% sequential) are organic platform expansion signals that cannot be attributed to inorganic acquisitions. The core ZIA/ZPA business retains its growth trajectory.

- **Bear version:** Stripping Red Canary's ~$130M ARR contribution reveals organic core ARR growth closer to 21% than 25%. Organic net new ARR per quarter at an estimated $132M is sequentially decelerating. RPO quality is declining as lower-unit-economics MDR contracts contaminate the mix. The headline metrics are structurally misleading.

- **Why it's the crux:** If organic net new ARR is genuinely ~$132M/quarter and decelerating, the path back to 20%+ total growth requires rebuilding both sales leadership AND accelerating ZFlex/Zero Trust Everywhere — two simultaneous recoveries, neither guaranteed. If the bear's methodology overstates contamination, the organic picture is better than it appears.

- **Verdict: Lean bear [moderate].** The bear's math is directionally correct and the methodology is transparent: stripping Red Canary from the headline does reveal slower organic growth. The bull's counter-evidence (Zero Trust Everywhere, ZFlex) describes product expansion signals but does not directly rebut the organic ARR accounting. The critical weakening of the bull position: management has not provided organic/inorganic ARR disclosure, so investors cannot verify whether ZFlex and Zero Trust Everywhere are accelerating organic net new ARR fast enough to offset the deceleration. Opacity on organic metrics in a post-acquisition quarter is a bear signal.

---

**Assumption 3: Valuation Reset — Is $127 Genuinely Cheap or a Value Trap?**

- **Bull version:** At ~10.7x forward revenue, ZS trades at a multiple last seen when it was growing materially slower with a fraction of today's RPO, margin profile, and market share. If growth re-accelerates to even 20% in FY2028 — requiring nothing beyond stable leadership and continued Zero Trust adoption — the entry price proves a generational mispricing of a 34% SSE share leader.

- **Bear version:** EV/FCF of ~21x for 16–17% growth with rising CapEx intensity, no named sales replacement, and a documented product gap in AI agent identity security is not cheap relative to PANW (~25x FCF at 13–15% ARR growth with superior bundled platform and cleaner execution track record). Reaching $90–100 requires only the FY2027 guide to prove accurate — no incremental deterioration needed.

- **Why it's the crux:** Valuation is only attractive if the 16–17% guide is the floor, not the ceiling. If the ceiling, the EV/FCF multiple is expensive relative to peers at that growth rate. Assumptions 1 and 2 directly determine which scenario is right — making this the tightest feedback loop in the thesis.

- **Verdict: Lean bear [narrow].** The bull's revenue multiple argument (10.7x forward) is genuinely low relative to ZS's own history. However, the bear's FCF multiple argument (21x EV/FCF for 16–17% growth) is the more honest denominator for a company at this margin maturity, and it holds: PANW at 25x FCF with arguably cleaner execution represents a legitimate competing allocation. The bull wins that this is not a "disaster" price; the bear wins that it is not a "screaming buy." The stock is fairly priced for a 16% grower and cheap only if re-acceleration materializes — which is precisely the contested variable.

### Closing Assessment

The single most important thing an investor must be right about to own ZS at $127 is whether the FY2027 16–17% growth guide is the floor or the ceiling — and that determination rests almost entirely on whether organic net new ARR re-accelerates from its current estimated $132M/quarter run-rate within the next two to three quarters as sales leadership is rebuilt. The evidence as of May 28, 2026 tilts modestly bearish on this question: unnamed, unreplaced senior departures with no stated timeline, management that missed its own FCF guide by 370–380bps in a single quarter without advance warning, and PANW SASE growing at 2x the guided rate all argue the guide reflects genuine pipeline impairment rather than prudent sandbagging. For portfolio sizing, ZS at $127 warrants a half-position at most relative to a full conviction holding — the installed base moat (115% NRR, $6.5B RPO) prevents a catastrophic collapse to the $60–70 range, but the path to $90–100 is narrow and requires only the FY2027 guide to prove accurate, while the path to $160–170 requires re-acceleration that has not yet started and depends on a sales rebuild the market cannot yet time. The Q4 FY2026 earnings call in August 2026 — when management must name replacement leadership, provide the first FY2027 quarterly guidance, and disclose whether CapEx has peaked — is the binary event that resolves all three divergence assumptions simultaneously.
