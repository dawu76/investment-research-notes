## 1. Executive Summary

### Investment Thesis

Zscaler is the structurally advantaged leader in cloud-native Zero Trust network security, operating a platform that has become mission-critical infrastructure for the world's largest enterprises. As organizations permanently shift to cloud-first, hybrid-work architectures, traditional perimeter-based network security (firewalls, VPNs) becomes technically inadequate and economically wasteful. Zscaler's Zero Trust Exchange — routing 500+ billion daily transactions (nearly 1 trillion AI transactions in calendar 2025) through 160+ global Points of Presence — is the most mature, data-rich, and deeply embedded alternative. With $3.36B in ARR growing 25% YoY (Q2 FY2026), 80% non-GAAP gross margins, 22% non-GAAP operating margins, a 115% Net Revenue Retention rate, and raised FY2026 revenue guidance of $3.309–$3.322B (+24%), Zscaler is transitioning from a high-growth story to a durable compounder. The $692M acquisition of Red Canary and SPLX in Q1 FY2026 extends the platform into MDR/SOC and AI security — expanding TAM beyond the core SSE/SASE market. A $6.1B RPO (+31% YoY) and $2.4B deferred revenue base provide multi-year revenue visibility. The core thesis: Zscaler is replacing entire categories of legacy security products at the world's largest enterprises, and switching costs make departure structurally unlikely once a customer is live at scale.

### Bull Case
Zscaler captures a disproportionate share of the $96B TAM as the secular shift to zero trust accelerates; emerging platforms (Zero Trust Everywhere, AI Protect, ZFlex) drive ARR re-acceleration above 25%; the stock re-rates as investors price in a durable 24–26% compounder with expanding margins.

### Bear Case
Palo Alto Networks and Microsoft commoditize SSE through aggressive bundling, compressing Zscaler's net new ARR growth; organic core (ex-Red Canary) ARR growth stalls around 20%; Red Canary integration churn proves sticky, undermining the expansion narrative; revenue growth decelerates to sub-18%, causing significant multiple compression.

### Target Investor Profile
**Growth investors** with a 3–5 year time horizon; comfortable owning a premium-valuation SaaS compounder in a mission-critical, regulated category. Not suitable for income investors (no dividend) or value investors (trades at a significant revenue multiple). Suitable for technology-focused growth funds, quality-growth crossover investors, and hedge funds with long-biased mandates.

**LTV / CAC Dynamics:** Zscaler does not disclose LTV or CAC explicitly. ### Operating Leverage Dynamics

The infrastructure (PoP network) is semi-fixed; adding users to an existing PoP is near-zero marginal cost. This creates structural gross margin stability at 79–81% as revenue scales. Operating leverage flows through at the SG&A layer: Sales & Marketing and R&D as a percentage of revenue are declining as the existing installed base (generating 115% NRR) requires less incremental S&M spend per dollar of growth.

Non-GAAP operating margin trajectory:
- FY2023: ~15%
- FY2024: ~19%
- FY2025: 22%
- Q2 FY2026: 22.2% (+50bps YoY)
- FY2026 Guidance (Non-GAAP operating income): $742–$748M (+28–29% YoY)

The FY2026 guidance implies ~22.3–22.5% non-GAAP operating margin on $3.31–3.32B revenue — demonstrating continued leverage while absorbing Red Canary/SPLX integration costs.

### Calculated Billings — Metric Status and History

**Zscaler officially reported calculated billings** as a defined non-GAAP metric through FY2025, defining it as "revenue plus the change in total deferred revenue in a period." Starting Q1 FY2026, the company **stopped reporting this metric**, transitioning to ARR as the primary top-line disclosure. The stated reason: billings volatility from contract duration mix shifts distorts quarterly signals (e.g., Q1 FY2025 printed only +13% YoY billings despite healthy underlying ARR growth, causing investor confusion).

Analysts can still infer billings from the deferred revenue changes disclosed in balance sheets:

| Period | Revenue | ΔTotal DR (sequential) | **Implied/Reported Billings** | Y/Y Growth | Status |
|--------|---------|----------------------|------------------------------|-----------|--------|
| FY2024 (Jul '24) | $2,168M | +$455M (annual) | **$2,623M** | ~+30% | Reported ✓ |
| FY2025 (Jul '25) | $2,673M | +$573M (annual) | **$3,246M** | **+23.7%** | Reported ✓ (last year reported) |
| Q2 FY2025 (Jan '25) | $648M | +$95M | **$743M** | **+18%** | Reported ✓ |
| Q1 FY2026 (Oct '25) | $788M | −$117M (seasonal Q4→Q1) | **~$671M** | **~+30% YoY** | Inferred |
| Q2 FY2026 (Jan '26) | $816M | +$4M | **~$820M** | **~+10% YoY** | Inferred — interpret with caution (see below) |

The Q1 FY2026 implied billings acceleration to ~+30% is encouraging. The Q2 FY2026 implied +10% warrants caution as a signal for two reasons: (1) Q2 (Nov–Jan) is Zscaler's seasonally weakest billings quarter — enterprise renewals cluster in Q3–Q4; Q2 FY2025 itself only added $95M sequentially to DR. (2) Contract duration mix shifts distort the quarterly DR figure — the non-current DR declined sequentially Q1→Q2 FY2026 ($405M → $372M), suggesting shorter-duration billing in that quarter rather than demand softness. ARR (+25% YoY) and RPO (+31% YoY) both contradict a demand-weakness reading of the implied +10% billings figure.

### Working Capital & Cash Conversion

Zscaler benefits from a **favorable cash conversion cycle**: enterprise customers pay annual subscriptions upfront, generating large deferred revenue balances. RPO of $6.1B (+31% YoY) — approximately 1.8x forward annual revenue — provides exceptional multi-year revenue visibility. Q2 FY2026 FCF was $169.1M (+18% YoY), with FY2026 FCF margin guidance raised to 26.5–27%.

## 3. Competitive Position & Moat

### Market Position

- **SSE Market Share:** ~34% (industry leading; IDC, Dell'Oro Group data)
- **SASE Market Share:** ~21% overall (strongest in the SSE/cloud access layer)
- **Global 2000 penetration:** ~40% of Global 2000 companies
- **Fortune 500 penetration:** >45% of Fortune 500
- **Gartner Magic Quadrant:** Leader in Security Service Edge (SSE) — 2024 report
- **Government certifications:** FedRAMP High, DoD IL4/IL5, ISO 27001, SOC 2

### Moat Classification

**Primary Moat: Switching Costs (High)**
Zscaler operates at the network layer — all user and workload traffic is routed through the Zero Trust Exchange. Replacing ZS requires: (1) architectural redesign of network egress strategy, (2) reconfiguring security policies across all endpoints and branch offices, (3) retraining security operations teams, (4) re-certifying with regulators if applicable. This is a 12–24 month project with high operational risk, making churn structurally rare.

**Secondary Moat: Scale + Data Network Effect (High)**
500+ billion daily transactions processed; nearly 1 trillion AI-specific transactions in calendar 2025; 3,400+ unique enterprise AI applications detected within the customer base. This proprietary threat intelligence dataset improves detection models for all customers. Competitors with smaller transaction volumes cannot match the breadth of behavioral anomaly detection.

**Tertiary Moat: Regulatory (Moderate)**
FedRAMP High and DoD certifications take 3–5+ years to obtain and are essential for U.S. federal government business. This effectively locks out new entrants from a significant and growing government vertical.

### Moat Durability Evidence

| Indicator | Value | Signal |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| NRR | 115% | Customers expanding, not churning |
| Gross Margin | ~80% | Pricing power; customers not rebidding aggressively |
| $1M+ ARR Customer Growth | 664 → 728 in one quarter (+10%) | Accelerating enterprise platform depth |
| Fortune 500 penetration | 45%+ | Brand and trust validation at the most demanding tier |
| RPO | $6.1B (+31% YoY) | 1.8x forward revenue locked in multi-year commitments |

### Key Competitors & Differentiation

| Competitor | Threat Level | Key Risk |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Palo Alto Networks** (Prisma SASE) | High | Bundling discounts; cross-sell leverage into existing NGFW base; largest head-to-head competitor in enterprise |
| **Microsoft** (Entra Internet Access / Global Secure Access) | Medium-High | "Good enough free" for M365 E5 customers; pricing and simplicity threat at mid-market |
| **Cisco** (SSE/SASE) | Medium | Legacy network trusted vendor; cloud-native architecture inferior |
| **Netskope** | Medium | Strong CASB; private; less scale disadvantage vs. public pure-play ZS |
| **CrowdStrike** | Low-Medium | Endpoint-first expanding toward network security; Red Canary acquisition partially preempts this overlap |

**Key Differentiator:** Zscaler is the only pure-play, born-in-the-cloud network security provider at hyperscale. Palo Alto and Cisco are hybrid (hardware + cloud), which creates architectural complexity ZS avoids. Microsoft's SSE offering lacks enterprise-grade visibility and control. Zscaler's 14+ years of cloud-native operation, 725+ patents, and 500B+ daily transaction dataset create a technical moat that cannot be replicated quickly.

**Q2 FY2026 competitive dynamics note:** Management stated competitive dynamics have seen "not much change" over recent quarters. Americas closed **twice the number of $1M+ deals YoY** in Q2, suggesting the platform is winning where it competes. The ZFlex program ($290M+ TCV in Q2, +65% sequentially) reflects growing customer demand for flexible multi-product commitments at scale.

## 4. Top 3 Growth Drivers & Sensitivities

### Driver 1: Zero Trust Enterprise Adoption (Secular Shift)

**Description:** Enterprises globally are mandated or compelled to replace traditional VPN/firewall architectures with Zero Trust. U.S. federal mandate (Executive Order 14028), increasing ransomware insurance requirements, and cloud migration all drive urgency. This is a decade-long replacement cycle. ZPA (ZTNA) continues to see accelerating adoption as legacy VPN infrastructure ages out.

**TAM Impact:** Zero Trust Security market: $41.7B (2025) → $88.8B (2030) at 16.3% CAGR. SASE market: $15.7B (2025) → $133.8B (2035) at 24% CAGR. Zscaler's own TAM estimate: ~$96B.

**Q2 FY2026 Data Point:** Americas +31% YoY revenue growth — the strongest of the three regions — driven by record $1M+ deal volume (2x YoY). Americas has historically been the most competitive region (vs. PANW and Microsoft), making this growth particularly meaningful.

### Driver 2: Platform Expansion — Zero Trust Everywhere & AI Security (Pricing + Volume)

**Description:** Customers who start with ZIA or ZPA are systematically upsold into additional modules. **Zero Trust Everywhere** (550 enterprise customers, up from 130 a year ago) delivers a 2–3x ARR step-up and represents the platform's highest-expansion tier. ## 5. Risk Framework with Failure Modes

### Risk Matrix

| Risk | Severity (1–5) | Probability (1–5) | Score | Mitigation |
|------|---------------|-------------------|-------|------------|
| Competitive bundling (PANW/MSFT) | 5 | 4 | **20** | Architecture differentiation; government moat; platform breadth |
| Core organic ARR deceleration (ex-Red Canary) | 4 | 3 | 12 | ZFlex, Zero Trust Everywhere accelerating; H2 seasonality |
| Red Canary integration / elevated churn | 3 | 3 | 9 | ARR guidance raised to $130M; renewal push in Feb 2026 |
| Macro/enterprise budget compression | 4 | 3 | 12 | Recurring revenue model; security is last cut in IT budgets |
| AI disruption to security architecture | 4 | 2 | 8 | Zscaler is embedding AI natively; acquired SPLX to address |

### Top 5 Risks in Detail

**Risk 1: Competitive Bundling — Palo Alto Networks**
- **Trigger:** PANW offers zero-cost or heavily discounted Prisma SASE bundles to win or retain ZS accounts; CyberArk integration gives PANW cross-sell leverage into identity + network security
- **Impact:** Slows net new ARR growth; forces price concessions in renewals; NRR declines below 110%
- **Mitigation:** ZS's pure-cloud architecture remains technically superior for cloud-first enterprises; government certifications limit PANW substitution in federal; ZS's data scale is not replicable in the near term; Q2 FY2026 competitive commentary was stable

**Risk 2: Core Organic ARR Growth Stall**
- **Trigger:** Net new ARR ex-Red Canary fails to re-accelerate from Q2 FY2026's 7% YoY; ZFlex and Zero Trust Everywhere adoption slower than expected in H2
- **Impact:** Total ARR growth drifts toward 18–20%; Street estimates require cuts; multiple compresses
- **Mitigation:** H1 FY2026 net new ARR ex-RC already improved to 10% (vs. 1% in prior H1); Americas $1M+ deal volume 2x YoY; management expressed confidence in H2 pipeline

**Risk 3: Red Canary Integration Friction**
- **Trigger:** Post-acquisition MDR churn proves structural rather than transitory; cultural integration issues or talent attrition at Red Canary
- **Impact:** $692M acquisition generates minimal incremental ARR; potential write-down; management distraction
- **Mitigation:** Guidance raised to $130M ARR (from $95M); February 2026 renewal push secured additional contracts; Red Canary has an established, independent revenue base

**Risk 4: Macroeconomic Slowdown / IT Budget Freeze**
- **Trigger:** Recession or significant enterprise budget reduction cycles elongate sales cycles
- **Impact:** Net new ARR additions slow; billings growth decelerates; potential NRR headwind if customers downgrade tiers
- **Mitigation:** Security budgets are among the last to be cut post-pandemic; regulatory mandates continue regardless of macro; ZS's cost-consolidation pitch actually strengthens in a downturn

**Risk 5: Microsoft "Good Enough" Bundling**
- **Trigger:** Microsoft bundles Global Secure Access (SSE) into E5 licensing at no incremental cost, causing mid-market churn
- **Impact:** Pressure on sub-$100K ARR customer cohort; slows total customer count growth
- **Mitigation:** Microsoft's SSE lacks enterprise-grade routing, visibility, and compliance controls; Global 2000 requires ZS's capabilities; Microsoft bundling threat is mainly at lower end of market

### Pre-Mortem

> *"If this investment fails over the next 3 years, the most likely reason is: Palo Alto Networks successfully convinced enterprise customers that a single integrated SASE+NGFW+Identity platform from an incumbent is cheaper and less complex to operate than Zscaler's pure-play approach — combined with organic core ARR growth failing to re-accelerate from the 7% YoY level seen in Q2 FY2026 — resulting in ZS ARR growth decelerating to 12–15% and multiple compression from ~12x revenue to 6–7x, causing significant stock underperformance."*

### Key Assumptions That Must Hold

1. **NRR sustains at 110%+** — if it falls below 105% for two consecutive quarters, the land-and-expand model is breaking down
2. **Net new ARR ex-Red Canary re-accelerates in H2 FY2026** — Q2's 7% was a trough; management guidance implies H2 improvement; any further deceleration challenges the thesis
3. **Non-GAAP operating margins expand or hold at 22%+** — FY2026 guidance implies 22.3–22.5%; margin compression would signal pricing erosion or inefficient investment
4. **ARR growth rate holds at 20%+** — deceleration below 18% would raise questions about TAM saturation or competitive displacement
5. **Red Canary / SPLX integration normalizes** — elevated churn is acknowledged but should decline through FY2026 H2 per management guidance
