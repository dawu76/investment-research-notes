## 1. Macro & Federal Reserve: The "Warsh Regime" & Geopolitical Disinflation

1. **Warsh's FOMC Debut:** Kevin Warsh presided over his first FOMC meeting, holding the federal funds rate steady at **3.50%–3.75%** (a unanimous 12-0 vote and the fourth consecutive hold). Although the rate decision was anticipated, Warsh's debut established a significantly more hawkish tone. 
2. **Elimination of Forward Guidance:** In a major break from the post-2008 Fed playbook, Warsh aggressively moved to retire forward guidance. The FOMC statement was extremely brief—the shortest since Alan Greenspan's tenure—and Warsh refused to submit a personal dot for interest-rate projections, advising markets to rely on incoming data rather than central bank signaling. 
3. **Hawkish Inflexibility & Economic Friction:** The FOMC statement declared that "The Committee will deliver price stability" and reiterated "its policy of maintaining ample reserves in the banking system." Core import prices rose **+0.8%** and housing starts crashed **15.4%**. Wall Street economists pushed their projections for the first rate cut into **2027**, and Goldman Sachs expects the Fed to permanently retire its cut-biased guidance. Headline PCE is expected to remain above **4%** all year.
4. **Strait of Hormuz Peace Accord:** Over the weekend, the US and Iran reached a memorandum of understanding (announced by Donald Trump). This deal reopened the Strait of Hormuz, bleeding the geopolitical risk premium out of the energy market. Brent crude slid to multi-month lows, short-term inflation swap rates moved sharply lower, and consumer sentiment improved on cheaper gasoline. 
5. **SPR Buffer Depletion:** The gasoline "peace dividend" was heavily funded by drawing down the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), which hit a **43-year low**. Analysts warn that oil's downside is driven by draining buffers rather than structural disinflation, leaving the market exposed to inelastic supply.

## 2. AI Technology & Infrastructure: Compute Scaling & Moat Re-allocation

1. **Brockman's Compute Rules All:** OpenAI President Greg Brockman spoke at the Big Technology AI Summit, asserting that "compute rules all." He argued that with frontier labs' model capabilities scaling relatively in sync, the lab that accumulates the most compute will win. OpenAI's capital expansion is funded by its **$122 billion** raise earlier this year. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei described OpenAI's massive spend as "YOLOing."
2. **Model Acceleration & Agentic Workflows:** Jordi Visser reports that since February 28, there have been roughly two dozen major model releases (including Claude Opus 4.7/4.8, Mythos Preview, Fable 5, GPT-5.4/5.5, and DeepSeek V4 Preview). The frontier is transitioning from simple Q&A chat interfaces to active agentic systems executing complex workflows.
3. **Moat Shift from Data to Orchestration:** Jamin Ball (Clouded Judgement) notes that for SaaS, the primary moat was data gravity (the "system of record") because static workflows were hard-coded around it. For AI agents, workflows are dynamic, moving the moat up the stack to the **orchestration layer** (where work is orchestrated) rather than where data resides.
4. **SpaceX IPO & Orbital Data Centers:** SpaceX went public at a record valuation of **~$1.77 trillion**, with shares rising **42%** in its first five days. The prospectus details plans to launch "AI1" satellites by late next year for initial testing of over one million "Orbital Data Centers." These space-based data centers will rely on extensive pre-launch testing since they cannot undergo maintenance or repair in orbit.
5. **Market Gains Concentration:** Torsten Slok (Apollo) and Wintersberger note that the entire S&P 500's year-to-date gains have been driven exclusively by AI and Energy. Stripping out AI-related companies, the S&P 500 index was down **1.12%** week-over-week.

## 3. Power Grid & Hardware: Indium Phosphide Chokehold & CATL's Vertical Strategy

1. **Indium Phosphide (InP) Bottleneck:** Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Coherent CEO Jim Anderson broke ground on a 6-inch Indium Phosphide wafer production line in Texas. Nvidia invested **$2 billion** in Coherent three months ago to support this facility. InP is the core material for optical transmitters and receivers in co-packaged optics (CPO). China controls **60%** of global InP substrate capacity and imposed export controls in Feb 2025. Coherent's CEO recently secured a new batch of licenses from Beijing, but the supply chain remains a primary bottleneck delaying CPO deployment.
2. **CATL's Vertical Energy Play:** Battery giant CATL is assembling a vertical energy infrastructure chain beneath AI compute, committing over **Rmb 15 billion** across three major deals:
   - *Zhongheng Electric:* **Rmb 4.1bn** for a 49% stake in the controlling shareholder. Zhongheng supplies high-voltage DC power systems to Tencent, Alibaba, Baidu, and ByteDance data centers.
   - *VNET Group:* Paying up to **$942 million** for a 38.1% stake in China's first US-listed data center operator, which owns 889 MW of wholesale capacity across 30 cities.
   - *DeepSeek:* Committed **$740 million** (Rmb 5bn) to DeepSeek's **$7.4 billion** first external round (valuing the company at over $50 billion).
   This vertical chain integrates upstream battery storage, midstream power conversion (Zhongheng), downstream data centers (VNET), and anchor end-user demand (DeepSeek, which is expanding Inner Mongolia operations).

## 4. Capacity & Backlog: Debunking Bloomberg's "50% Cancelled" Datacenter Claim

1. **Flawed Sourcing on Cancellations:** SemiAnalysis refutes the widely circulated claim (originating from Bloomberg) that half of 2026 US datacenter capacity will be delayed or cancelled due to China-dependent supply chains. Bloomberg/Sightline Climate estimated that only 5 GW of the expected 12 GW coming online in 2026 is under construction. SemiAnalysis states that the self-builds of the top two hyperscalers alone exceed 5 GW under construction, excluding third-party colocation projects. 
2. **Datacenter Moratoriums:** While **12 states** have introduced moratorium bills and **4 Indiana counties** (Fulton, Marshall, Putnam, White) enacted them, none of these counties had active datacenters in their build pipeline, meaning the immediate capacity impact is zero.
3. **Supply Queues & Backlog Margins:** Hardware capacity relief is slow (Hitachi Energy's South Boston plant, announced in Sept 2025, won't open until 2028). To secure positions in manufacturing queues, buyers are now paying **10%–15%** in upfront prepayments. This has boosted margins for pure-play electrical hardware vendors like Vertiv and Schneider above **20%**.
