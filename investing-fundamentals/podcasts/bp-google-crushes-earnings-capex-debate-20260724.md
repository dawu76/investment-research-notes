# Google CRUSHES Earnings, CapEx Debate & Tesla Q2 | Basis Points

Detailed overview of the weekly tech earnings & macro discussion on *Basis Points*, hosted by **Steven Fiorillo** and **Amit** (Amit Is Investing), published on July 24, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary

In this episode of *Basis Points*, hosts Steven Fiorillo and Amit unpack a volatile week driven by Middle East geopolitical oil supply shocks and a pivotal start to tech earnings season. **Alphabet (Google)** reported stellar Q2 results ($119.8B revenue; GCP $24.8B +82% YoY with margins jumping from 20% to 36%), converting its $257B 24-month backlog at ~$10.3B/month while renting extra compute from SpaceX ($920M/month). Despite negative free cash flow (-$5.85B) due to $195B–$205B FY26 CapEx, the hosts forcefully refute Wall Street sell-side calls (Melius Research) to "Sell Mag 7 for Chip Stocks," demonstrating that hyperscaler CapEx is driving immediate cloud revenue ROI and expanding operating margins.

Conversely, **Tesla** suffered its worst single-day decline (-15%) since Jan 2024 after Q2 operating margins collapsed to 1.4% and operational EPS came in at just **$0.10** (excluding a $0.22 gain from its SpaceX equity stake). Looking ahead to mega-cap earnings, the hosts project **Microsoft's FY2027 CapEx guidance** will surge to **$220B–$240B** following its fiscal year end, driven by double data center capacity expansion and a 20-year Chevron energy agreement, while macro oil risks ($115–$120/bbl threat from Iraqi/Iranian output drops) pose a tactical hazard for Fed policy heading into Jackson Hole.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. Google's Q2 Earnings: Cloud Hyper-Growth and CapEx ROI Validation
Alphabet's Q2 earnings provided definitive evidence that massive hyperscaler CapEx is translating directly into top-line revenue and operating margin expansion:
*   **Cloud Acceleration:** Google Cloud Platform (GCP) revenue hit **$24.8 billion (+82% YoY)**, while operating margins expanded from 20% in the prior year to **36%**. 
*   **Backlog & Token Scale:** Alphabet closed the quarter with **$257 billion in contracted 24-month backlog**, representing a **$10.3 billion monthly conversion rate** (~$30B+ quarterly cloud revenue). Alphabet processes **22 billion API tokens per minute**, with 500 enterprise customers consuming over 1 trillion tokens annually.
*   **SpaceX Compute Rental:** Demonstrating extreme compute scarcity, Google is renting Nvidia GB300 GPU clusters from SpaceX at **~$911M–$920M per month** (paying a 300% premium over baseline rental rates) to convert immediate customer demand before its own data centers come online.
*   **Consumer AI Adoption:** AI Mode surpassed **1 billion monthly active users (MAUs)** in under a year, while the Gemini App hit **950 million MAUs** with daily active users tripling YoY. In the physical economy, Google Cloud's partnership with McDonald's automates **90% of drive-thru orders** without human intervention.

> "You're talking about $257 billion over 24 months... converting at $10.3 billion a month from backlog. GCP revenue grew 82% year-over-year, and margins went from 20% to 36%. And these people want them to stop spending on CapEx? They're all stupid. Look at their cash from operations and EBITDA—the proof is in their financials."
> — *Steven Fiorillo*

#### 2. The Wall Street CapEx Debate: Mag 7 vs. Semiconductor Co-existence
The hosts address the popular sell-side narrative pushing institutional investors to rotate out of Big Tech hyperscalers into semiconductor hardware:
*   **The Sell-Side Thesis:** Analysts at Melius Research issued a prominent note advising clients to "Sell Mag 7 due to negative free cash flow and buy chip companies."
*   **Host Rebuttal:** Discarding premier hyperscalers because short-term CapEx depresses free cash flow ignores their unmatched balance sheet strength, operating cash flow, and capital allocation history. Furthermore, chipmakers (Nvidia trading at ~14x 2028E EPS; Broadcom trading at ~15–16x 2028E EPS) and hyperscalers are not mutually exclusive—both will compound together as compute scarcity persists.

> "Someone from Melius Research today said unequivocally 'sell the Mag 7 because they are going to go negative free cash flow, buy the chip companies.' I am coming to the conclusion that I do not want to buy chip companies at the expense of the Mag 7... It feels better to sleep at night knowing you're in companies that will eventually stop spending CapEx and harvest massive cash."
> — *Amit*

#### 3. Tesla Q2 Operational Breakdown: Margin Collapse and Optimus Timeline Delays
Tesla suffered its sharpest single-day decline (-15%) since January 2024 following a quarter that highlighted weak operational profitability:
*   **Earnings Disconnect:** While top-line revenue hit a record $28.2 billion (+26% YoY), operating margins collapsed to **1.4%** (vs. 5.4% expected). Of the reported $0.33 GAAP EPS, **$0.22 was derived from mark-to-market gains on its SpaceX equity holding**, leaving just **$0.10 in adjusted operational EPS** (-18% YoY).
*   **Optimus & FSD Execution Delays:** Elon Musk acknowledged that building the Optimus supply chain is extraordinarily complex and years away, pausing Model S/X production lines to retool for robotics. FSD/Robotaxi fleet monetization remains limited to 7 cities. Compared to Big Tech peers compounding earnings today, Tesla trades at an extreme valuation premium relative to near-term cash generation.

> "Tesla reported 33 cents of EPS, but 22 cents of that was because of their SpaceX stake. They basically did 10 cents of adjusted operational EPS... Operating margin came in at 1.4% vs 5.4% expected. When you have Meta, Nvidia, and Google doing what they're doing, I don't understand the rational reason to buy Tesla at this level."
> — *Steven Fiorillo*

#### 4. Microsoft Fiscal Year-End & FY2027 CapEx Surge Projection ($220B–$240B)
Looking ahead to the upcoming mega-cap earnings week, Microsoft represents the most critical print:
*   **Fiscal Year Transition:** Because Microsoft's fiscal year ends June 30 (unlike calendar-year peers), management must provide full-year CapEx guidance for **Fiscal Year 2027**.
*   **CapEx Projection:** After CFO Amy Hood guided FY2026 CapEx to $190 billion, the hosts project FY2027 CapEx will escalate to **$220 billion – $240 billion**. Microsoft recently signed a 20-year energy agreement with Chevron to backstop plans to **double its global data center footprint** over the next two years.
*   **Peer Previews:** Meta is expected to increase CapEx while showcasing Instagram/Facebook AI ad conversion gains; Amazon continues to face supply constraints on its proprietary Trainium chips; Apple has an opportunity to introduce a paid "Apple Intelligence Pro" service tier ($3–$5/month).

> "Next week, Microsoft is the most important print because their fiscal year has ended... After $190 billion in FY26, I think the number for Fiscal Year 2027 is going to be between $220 billion and $240 billion. They just signed a deal with Chevron to double their data center footprint over the next two years."
> — *Steven Fiorillo*

#### 5. Macro Oil Supply Deficit & Geopolitical Jackson Hole Risk
Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East present an emerging supply-side threat to energy markets:
*   **Production Drops:** Conflict disruptions have reduced Iraqi crude production from ~4.0M b/d to 2.0M b/d, while Iranian production has dropped from ~3.3M b/d to 2.4M b/d, creating a severe structural deficit.
*   **Oil to $115–$120:** If crude spikes to **$115–$120/bbl** heading into the Fed's August Jackson Hole symposium, energy-driven CPI inflation could force the Federal Reserve to consider interest rate hikes, creating a tactical market drawdown into October midterm seasonal lows.

> "Iraq's production is down from 4 million to 2 million barrels per day. Iran is down to 2.4 million. The strip between supply and demand is getting out of control... Oil can very easily go to $115 or $120 right now. This pushes the Fed into a situation where they very well may hike for the wrong reasons coming out of Jackson Hole."
> — *Steven Fiorillo*
