# Uber as Robotaxi Aggregator (September 2026)

**The claim.** BNP Paribas (Nick Jones, Sept 29, 2026) argues Uber is a major beneficiary of autonomous vehicles: robotaxi costs fall far below human-driven costs, the ride-hail market grows, and Uber captures a large share of autonomous miles because of its demand, scale and 30+ AV partnerships. Price target $106 vs $68.20 on Sept 28 (~56% upside). Read via Benzinga; the underlying note was not seen.

## BNP's numbers

| Item | BNP estimate |
|---|---|
| Uber human-driven cost | ~$1.00/mile |
| Robotaxi operating cost today | ~$1.00/mile (depreciation, energy, maintenance, insurance, remote support); depreciation is the largest item |
| Long-run robotaxi cost | $0.16-0.42/mile |
| Vehicle build cost | Waymo current ~$150k all-in; Waymo Zeekr ~$70k; Tesla Cybercab target ~$30k; realistic near/medium term $60-80k |
| Unknowns | Fleet-ops, licensing, financing and distribution fees, required returns on capital. BNP expects clarity over the next 4-10 quarters |

**Sanity check on the cost range (my arithmetic, assumes ~300k lifetime miles).** Depreciation alone: $150k ≈ $0.50/mile, $70k ≈ $0.23, $30k ≈ $0.10. Add energy, maintenance, insurance and remote support and the $0.16-0.42 range is plausible, but the low end needs Tesla-like vehicle cost *and* high lifetime miles. The bigger point: **today robotaxi cost per mile is about equal to human-driven cost**, so the whole advantage is in the future.

## The case for Uber

1. **Utilization.** In an AV model costs shift from variable (driver) to fixed (vehicle). Khosrowshahi claims Uber can run AVs at ~30% higher utilization than a first-party operator by routing riders to whichever partner has capacity nearby. Higher utilization spreads depreciation, the biggest cost line.
2. **Aggregation of fragmented supply.** 30+ AV partnerships in ~2 years (TechCrunch tracker): Nuro/Lucid (35,000-vehicle order; ~$500M into Lucid and ~$500M into Nuro), Rivian (up to $1.25B, 10,000 R2s, exclusive, 2028 launch), Wayve, Nvidia Hyperion (28 cities by 2028), Zoox (Las Vegas 2026, LA 2027), Baidu, WeRide, Pony.ai, May Mobility, Motional, Avride, VW/MOIA. Developers without their own demand need Uber.
3. **International.** Robotaxi supply outside the US looks more fragmented (Chinese developers in the Middle East and Europe, Wayve in Europe/Japan), which suits an aggregator (Durant, Seeking Alpha).
4. **Valuation already discounts a lot.** 12x FCF for a business compounding bookings above 20%.

## The case against

1. **The best US operator is leaving.** Waymo notified Uber it will launch its own app in Austin and Atlanta in January 2028, ending exclusivity; the Uber contract runs to at least May 2028 (CNBC, July 24). Bloomberg/FT reported Waymo is exploring a full exit. Waymo already runs its own app in most of its 14 cities and did ~500k paid rides/week in March, targeting 1M/week by year-end.
2. **Tesla is vertically integrated.** Paid Cybercab rides started in Austin Sept 4 (45 Cybercabs; ~220 driverless vehicles across six cities). Tesla has no reason to pay a platform fee.
3. **Timing gap.** BofA (Justin Post, Sept 22): Uber's AV partnerships scale from 2028, giving Waymo, Tesla and Zoox an 18-24 month head start; >100k AVs from those three in the US by 2029. (Zoox is also on Uber in Las Vegas and LA, so that overlap cuts both ways.)
4. **Lower cost is not Uber-specific.** Falling cost per mile helps whoever owns demand *and* the lowest-cost fleet. If Waymo or Tesla sell rides directly, Uber's take is a price disadvantage it must offset with utilization. The question is who captures the surplus: AV developer, fleet financier, platform or rider.
5. **Asset-light becomes asset-heavy.** Vehicle purchases (Lucid, Rivian), equity stakes in AV developers, and fleet financing move Uber toward owning depreciating assets. The same vendor-financing loop seen in AI (invest in a supplier who commits capacity back to you) shows up here. See [[burry-ai-capital-cycle-oracle-jupiter-2026-09]] and [[ai-compute-commencement-wall-and-refinancing-trap]] for how that loop behaves in compute.

## The crux: can utilization beat the platform fee?

Illustrative only (my assumptions): at a mature cost of $0.42/mile with depreciation about half of it, a 30% utilization advantage cuts depreciation per mile by ~23% (1/1.3), saving ~5 cents a mile. A 20-30% platform take on a fare is far larger than that. So the utilization argument alone does not win against a direct operator with similar vehicle cost. Uber also needs one or more of: demand that the operator can't generate itself (airport, business travel, international, multi-modal), better pricing through matching and bundling (Uber One, Eats), or suppliers with no alternative channel. That is why the aggregator thesis looks strongest for second-tier AV developers and outside the US, and weakest against Waymo and Tesla in US cities.

## Verdict

The cost claim is reasonable and the direction is right, but it is not a reason to own Uber in particular. BNP's own caveats (licensing, distribution and financing costs still unknown) are exactly the terms that decide Uber's share. Near term the stock is priced for disintermediation risk, while the core business is still growing bookings above 20% and generating ~$11B of FCF (Q2 annualized). The thesis resolves in 2027-28, when Waymo's Austin/Atlanta exclusivity ends and Uber's own partners (Nuro/Lucid, Rivian, Nvidia) start scaling.

- Nuro/Lucid launch in San Francisco and Zoox LA launch (2027).
