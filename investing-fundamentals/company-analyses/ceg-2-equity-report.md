# Constellation Energy ($CEG): Equity Report

**Price:** ~$259.44 (October 20, 2026 close). **Market cap:** ~$91.9B on 354.3M shares outstanding (July 31, 2026). **52-week range:** $228.63-$406.04 (Morningstar). **Next report:** Q3 2026 on November 9, 2026.
Sources: the documents in [[ceg-1-document-sources]]. Figures are from SEC filings unless marked otherwise. "Adjusted" figures are company non-GAAP measures. Ratios marked "this report" are new arithmetic.

## Executive Summary

Constellation is the largest US independent power producer and competitive retail energy supplier. It owns about 55 GW of generation (Q2 2026 release): the largest US nuclear fleet (~22 GW per Morningstar) plus, since the January 2026 Calpine acquisition, the largest US natural-gas and geothermal fleets. It makes money by selling power, capacity and clean-energy attributes from that fleet, and by supplying electricity and gas to about 2.5 million retail and business accounts, including 80% of the Fortune 100.

The economics have improved sharply since the 2022 spin-off from Exelon. Three things drove it: a federal nuclear production tax credit (PTC) that sets a revenue floor through 2032; higher PJM capacity prices; and long-term contracts with hyperscalers (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Google) at premiums to market. Adjusted EPS rose from $8.67 (FY2024) to $9.39 (FY2025), and 2026 guidance is $11.50-12.50 including Calpine. The risks are commodity prices (about 30-40% of earnings are "enhanced" market-driven earnings), execution on the Crane restart and uprates, regulatory change in PJM and Texas, and a balance sheet that took on $12.55B of Calpine debt.

In plain English: Constellation owns America's largest fleet of always-on power plants and sells their output to big companies that will pay up for reliable, carbon-free electricity.

## What They Sell and Who Buys

* **Power generation (wholesale).** ~55 GW across PJM (about 50% of MWh produced), ERCOT (~23%), NYISO (~9%), CAISO (~8%) and other markets (2026 Business Outlook deck). Nuclear produced 88.8 TWh in H1 2026; gas, oil and renewables 62.7 TWh, of which Calpine 51.4 TWh.
* **Long-term clean-energy contracts.** Data-center and corporate buyers want 24/7 carbon-free power with assured delivery:
  * *Microsoft:* 20-year PPA for the ~835 MW Crane Clean Energy Center (ex-Three Mile Island Unit 1) restart, targeted for 2027. A DOE loan guarantee of up to $1B supports it (November 2025).
  * *Meta:* 20-year PPA for all 1,121 MW of Clinton from June 2027, plus a 30 MW uprate (announced June 2025; price undisclosed).
  * *Amazon:* 20-year PPA for 190 MW at Calvert Cliffs (September 30, 2026).
  * *Google:* 20-year agreement for 890 MW of new nuclear capacity from uprates at 11 units, plus a separate 15-year supply agreement for 2,700 MW in PJM (October 6, 2026).
  * *Others:* 920 MW of 15-20 year PPAs signed in Q2 2026 with investment-grade customers (including 176 MW with Walmart), starting 2029-2032. CyrusOne data-center agreements at Calpine's Texas plants: 380 MW at Freestone plus an exclusive 380 MW Phase 2, and 400 MW at Thad Hill.
* **Retail and commercial supply.** About 2.5M customer accounts in 40 states; ~190M MWh of C&I load, "roughly double the next largest supplier"; ~275M MWh of combined electric load served in 2025. Average C&I customer duration is more than five years (Business Outlook deck).
* **Core customer motivation:** price certainty and reliability for large users; for hyperscalers, speed to power and carbon-free attributes. Nuclear and contracted gas plants offer firm 24/7 supply that wind and solar cannot.

## How They Make Money

Revenue is a mix of contracted, regulated-support and merchant streams. Constellation manages the business by region on **RNF (revenue net of purchased power and fuel)**.

**FY2025 segment RNF (pre-Calpine), total $10.85B:**

| Segment | FY2025 revenue | FY2025 RNF | Share of segment RNF | H1 2026 RNF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-Atlantic (eastern PJM) | $6.49B | $3.41B | 32% | $1.80B |
| Midwest (western PJM, MISO) | $5.80B | $3.70B | 35% | $1.83B |
| New York | $2.19B | $1.60B | 15% | $0.80B |
| ERCOT | $1.90B | $1.14B | 11% | $0.49B |
| Other Power Regions | $5.58B | $0.82B | 8% | $0.55B |
| Calpine (from Jan 7, 2026) | n/a | n/a | n/a | $2.15B (28% of H1 segment RNF) |

Shares are this report's arithmetic on segment RNF of $10.67B. Corporate "Other" (unrealized hedges, gas sales, eliminations) is excluded.

**Revenue mechanics:**
* **Energy** sold at market or under hedges and contracts.
* **Capacity** payments: PJM cleared at $329/MW-day for 2026/27, $333 for 2027/28 and $325 for 2028/29, vs. $270 for 2025/26.
* **State programs:** Illinois CMC/ZEC (ZEC program ends May 2027); New York ZEC through 2049 per Morningstar.
* **Federal nuclear PTC:** up to $15/MWh, phasing out between $26 and $44.75/MWh of gross receipts (2026), through 2032.
* **Retail margins:** about $4.25-4.35/MWh on ~245M MWh of power and $0.40-0.45/dth on gas.

**Management's earnings framework (2026 Outlook):**
* **Base earnings (60-70% of total):** long-term contracts, nuclear at the PTC floor, minimum expected fossil earnings and average commercial margins. Base EPS is $6.65-6.75 (2026), $7.60-7.70 (2027) and $11.40-11.90 (2029).
* **Enhanced earnings (30-40%):** forward prices above base assumptions, above-average commercial margins and volatility capture.

## Revenue Quality

* **Predictable portion.**
  * Contracted clean generation rises from 36M MWh at ~$70/MWh (2026) to 53M MWh at $77 (2028) and $88 (2030) (Q2 deck), and that excludes 2026 PPAs.
  * Fixed-price remaining performance obligations total **$11.9B** (June 30, 2026), about $5.2B of it beyond 2030.
  * Through 2032, the PTC floor protects the uncontracted nuclear fleet: only ~5% of the fleet was in the PTC zone at June 30 forward prices. With 2% inflation the floor is $44.75/MWh in 2026, $48.88 in 2028 and $50.88 in 2030 (Q2 deck), so contracted prices of $77-88 sit well above it.
  * Capacity revenue is set three years ahead in PJM auctions.
* **Market-exposed portion.** Enhanced earnings depend on forward power prices (PJM West ATC $72.75/MWh for 2026 and $66.94 for 2027 at June 30), spark spreads for the gas fleet (ERCOT North ~$16-22/MWh) and retail margins. Unrealized hedge marks make GAAP earnings volatile. Q2 2026 GAAP EPS was $1.42 against $2.55 adjusted.
* **Diversification.** Before Calpine, about two-thirds of segment RNF was PJM (Mid-Atlantic plus Midwest). Calpine adds Texas, California and the Southeast; after the deal "half of Constellation's generation comes from its plants in the mid-Atlantic and nearly one-fourth from Texas" (Morningstar).
* **Concentration.** Four hyperscalers anchor most new long-term contracts, and Crane's output is a single-customer contract with Microsoft. This report did not find a customer-concentration disclosure in the filings reviewed.
* **GAAP vs. adjusted gap.** FY2025 GAAP EPS was $7.40 against $9.39 adjusted. GAAP net income was negative in FY2021 (-$205M) and FY2022 (-$160M), mainly from decommissioning-trust and hedge marks rather than operations.

## Cost Structure

**FY2025 income statement:**
* Revenue $25.53B; purchased power and fuel $14.68B (57% of revenue); RNF $10.85B (42.5%).
* Operating income $3.09B (12.1% margin, this report); net income $2.32B.
* FY2024 for comparison: operating income $4.35B on $23.57B of revenue (18.5%), helped by unusually strong portfolio results.

**H1 2026 (with Calpine):**
* Revenue $18.63B; purchased power and fuel $10.38B; O&M $4.03B; D&A $0.89B; operating income $2.91B (15.6%).
* Net interest expense was $536M, against $264M in H1 2025, because of the Calpine debt.

**2026 modeling inputs (Q2 deck):**
* Adjusted O&M about $6.9-7.0B; taxes other than income ~$675M.
* Base depreciation $1.8-2.0B a year; base interest ~$700M.
* Effective tax rate 25-26%.

**Scalability.** Nuclear is mostly fixed cost: fuel is a small share, and O&M, staffing and refueling outages don't vary with prices. Higher power prices fall largely to the bottom line, a positive operating gearing that the PTC limits on the downside. Fleet execution backs this up:
* 2025 nuclear capacity factor 94.7%.
* Q2 2026 refueling outages averaged 23 days vs. an industry average of 38.

## Capital Intensity

* **Capex** was $1.33B (FY2021), $1.69B (FY2022), $2.42B (FY2023), $2.57B (FY2024) and $2.95B (FY2025): 11.5% of FY2025 revenue (this report). H1 2026 was $2.52B as Calpine's fleet and growth projects (Crane restart, uprates) were added. Management identifies **$3.9B of growth capex for 2026-2027**.
* **Reported operating cash flow is distorted.** It was negative in FY2021-FY2024 (-$5.30B in FY2023, -$2.46B in FY2024) because collections on receivables sold under a securitization program ("Collection of DPP") were classified as investing inflows: $7.34B (FY2023) and $10.22B (FY2024). Adding those back gives ~$2.0B (FY2023) and ~$7.8B (FY2024) of operating cash (this report). The program shows no collections in FY2025, when reported operating cash flow was **$4.24B**. FY2025 free cash flow after capex was ~$1.29B (this report).
* **Management's metric** is free cash flow before growth (FCFbG): **$8.4B cumulative for 2026-2027** and **$11.5-13.0B for 2028-2029**. 2026-2027 uses (Outlook deck):

  | Use, 2026-2027 | Amount |
  |---|---|
  | Growth capex | $3.9B |
  | Dividends | $1.3B |
  | Deleveraging | $3.4B |
  | Share buybacks (authorized) | $5.0B |

  Funding comes from $8.4B of FCFbG, $4.5B of after-tax asset-sale proceeds and $0.8B of year-end 2025 cash.
* **Long-dated obligations.** Nuclear decommissioning trust funds of $20.5B against asset retirement obligations of $12.6B (June 30, 2026), plus a $5.9B payable related to regulatory agreement units.
* **Working capital and collateral** swing with commodity prices: net collateral posted of $357M in H1 2026 after $773M posted in FY2025.

**Balance sheet (June 30, 2026):**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total debt | **$24.70B**: short-term borrowings $5.23B (partly bridge financing for divestitures), current long-term $0.36B, long-term $19.11B |
| Cash | $0.70B, plus $0.38B restricted |
| Equity | $32.3B |
| Goodwill | $11.5B (from Calpine) |
| Debt / capital | ~43% (this report) |
| Ratings | BBB+ / Baa1, stable. Moody's lowered its Baa1 downgrade threshold to 25% FFO/debt from 30%. |

The Calpine assets held for sale ($5.74B) and $5.9B of expected gross divestiture proceeds should cut net debt by about $4.5B after tax.

## Growth Drivers

* **Structural: data-center and electrification demand.**
  * Management's base outlook is **20%+ base EPS growth from 2026 to 2029**, with a rolling three-year target of 10%+.
  * The outlook excludes the value of **147M MWh a year of uncontracted nuclear generation in 2029**. Each 1 GW nuclear PPA at a $20-50/MWh premium to the PTC floor adds an estimated $0.40-1.00 of EPS (Q2 deck).
* **Structural: capacity expansion with customer backing.**
  * Crane restart (835 MW, 2027).
  * ~1,100 MW of uprates, including 900 MW that are "possible with customer support". Google's agreement covers 890 MW.
  * License renewals: Clinton to 2047, Dresden to 2049/2051, Ginna and Nine Mile Point 1 filed for 2049.
  * Gas-site co-location deals ("powered land") at Calpine plants: 1 GW at a $10-25/MWh premium adds $0.20-0.50 of EPS.
* **Policy floor and inflation.** The PTC adjusts with inflation. A 3% inflation path instead of 2% is worth about $0.30 of EPS (Q2 deck).
* **Cyclical: power prices, spark spreads and capacity prices.** A $1-3/MWh rise in power prices and spark spreads adds $0.45-1.35 of EPS. PJM Eastern Mid-Atlantic capacity reference prices averaged $279.80/MW-day in H1 2026, about 3.1x the $89.65 of H1 2025 (this report), and auctions have cleared $325-333/MW-day through 2028/29.
* **Capital allocation.**
  * A $5.0B buyback authorization: ~$2.2B repurchased in 2026 through July, ~$2.8B remaining, and ~25.5M shares retired since the separation for ~$4.6B.
  * Dividend growth of 10% a year, to $1.706 annualized in 2026 ($0.4265 quarterly).
  * Diluted shares were 357M for 2026 guidance, down from 361M initially.
* **Acquisition integration.** Calpine contributed $2.15B of H1 2026 segment RNF. The deal was priced at about 7.9x 2026 EV/EBITDA at announcement, and Morningstar calls it immediately accretive.

## Competitive Edge

* **Scale and fleet quality (cost advantage).**
  * Nuclear is the lowest-marginal-cost baseload source, and existing nuclear sites can't be replicated; management estimates the new-build cost of the ~55 GW fleet at more than 3x its enterprise value.
  * Best-in-class operations: a 94.7% capacity factor and short refueling outages.
  * Calpine adds the largest fleet of efficient combined-cycle gas plants.
* **Retail and commercial platform.**
  * Morningstar's **Narrow moat** rests on cost advantage in retail energy supply. Scale, a diversified generation fleet and customer and regional diversity let Constellation be the low-cost supplier in markets with low switching costs.
  * It serves ~190M MWh of C&I load, about twice the next supplier.
* **Being first to the hyperscalers.** Constellation has signed all four of the largest hyperscalers to long-term deals. Its scale lets it offer multi-site, multi-GW deals and uprate capacity that single-plant owners can't.
* **Regulatory support.** PTC through 2032, Illinois CMC, New York ZEC through 2049, DOE loan support for Crane, and FERC approval of Crane's capacity-interconnection-rights transfer (June 2026).
* **Financial evidence of the moat:**
  * Morningstar puts ROE at 27.7% (FY2024) and 15.6% (FY2025), and ROIC at 17.4% and 13.3%.
  * It forecasts ROIC of 11-14% through 2030 against an 8.0% WACC: excess returns, but not wide-moat levels.
  * Capital allocation is rated **Exemplary**.
* **Durability limits.**
  * Returns stay exposed to commodity prices once contracts and the PTC end. Morningstar's bear case is "low gas and electricity prices hurt the profitability of Constellation's nuclear fleet."
  * Retail is "highly competitive with tight margins."
  * Peers are pursuing the same hyperscaler demand. Morningstar rates Vistra and NRG no-moat, with fair values of $106 and $104; their price/fair value ratios are 1.41 and 0.96, against 1.11 for Constellation (October 16 prices).
* **Morningstar's valuation (October 14, 2026).** Fair value **$238**, implying 13x EV/EBITDA, a premium to Vistra and NRG. EPS forecasts are $12.08 (2026), $13.05 (2027), $15.61 (2028), $19.05 (2029) and $19.24 (2030), on a 9.6% cost of equity.
  * **Caveat on the model (this report).** Morningstar's DCF bridge subtracts $8.99B of debt and adds $3.64B of cash. Those are exactly Constellation's December 31, 2025 balances, before Calpine closed. The $12.55B of Calpine debt assumed in January 2026 doesn't appear in the bridge (June 30 total debt was $24.70B).
  * If the omission isn't offset elsewhere in Morningstar's cash-flow forecast, its fair value would be overstated by up to ~$35 a share ($12.55B ÷ 356M shares). Its own 2026 debt/capital forecast of 21.7% is also far below the ~43% on the June 30 balance sheet.
