1. Executive Summary

Constellation owns the scarcest asset in the AI-power trade, but the price needs management's full 2029 plan and a premium multiple. Weighted value ~$244 vs. $259.44; Morningstar's $238 may itself be too high.

Thesis. Constellation owns the scarcest asset in the AI-power trade: ~22 GW of operating nuclear plants that run at ~95% capacity factors. Since January 2026 it also owns the largest US gas and geothermal fleet (Calpine).

  • Management's plan: 2026 adjusted EPS of $11.50-12.50 and 20%+ base EPS growth to 2029.
  • The open questionwhat the market will pay for growth beyond 2029. That depends on whether hyperscalers keep signing premium contracts for the ~147M MWh a year of...

    The open question:

    what the market will pay for growth beyond 2029. That depends on whether hyperscalers keep signing premium contracts for the ~147M MWh a year of nuclear output still uncontracted in 2029.

  • Valuationat ~$259 the stock trades at ~21.6x 2026 guidance and ~18x management's implied 2029 EPS.

    Valuation:

    at ~$259 the stock trades at ~21.6x 2026 guidance and ~18x management's implied 2029 EPS. This memo's probability-weighted value is ~$244 (bear ~$154, base ~$241, bull ~$340), about 6% below the price.

    • Morningstar's $238 is in the same place. As discussed below, Morningstar's figure may be overstated by up to ~$35 because its DCF bridge appears to omit the Calpine debt.

Contracted nuclear earns ~$70-88/MWh against a PTC floor of ~$45-51; retail adds ~$1.4B of margin a year. Costs are mostly fixed, so prices drive earnings.

Revenue mix (FY2025, pre-Calpine): $25.53B of revenue and $10.85B of RNF (revenue net of fuel).

3. Competitive Position & Moat

Largest IPP and C&I retail supplier, with all four largest hyperscalers signed. The moat is real but narrow: outside contracts, nuclear economics follow power prices.

  • Market position.Largest US independent power producer: ~55 GW, the largest nuclear, gas and geothermal fleets, about 10% of US clean energy.

    Market position.

    • Largest US independent power producer: ~55 GW, the largest nuclear, gas and geothermal fleets, about 10% of US clean energy.
    • Largest C&I retail supplier: ~190M MWh of load, about twice the next supplier; 80% of the Fortune 100; 2.5M accounts.
    • It has signed all four of the largest hyperscalers to long-term deals.
  • Moat classificationCost advantage and scale: low-marginal-cost baseload nuclear and efficient combined-cycle gas, plus an integrated retail book that hedges generation.

    Moat classification:

    • Cost advantage and scale: low-marginal-cost baseload nuclear and efficient combined-cycle gas, plus an integrated retail book that hedges generation.
    • Regulatory support: PTC, state ZEC/CMC programs, DOE loan support, NRC license extensions to the 2040s-2050s.
    • Irreplaceable assets: no new large US nuclear plants are being built at scale, and management puts the new-build cost of the fleet at more than 3x its enterprise value.
    • Morningstar assigns a Narrow moat, based on retail cost advantage, and rates capital allocation Exemplary.
  • Durability evidenceCapacity factor of 94.7% in 2025.

    Durability evidence:

    • Capacity factor of 94.7% in 2025.
    • ROIC of 13-17% (FY2024-2025, Morningstar) against an 8.0% WACC.
    • Contracted clean volume rising ~47% from 2026 to 2028 (36M to 53M MWh), with contract terms of 15-20 years.
    • License renewals extend the asset life well beyond 2032.
  • Durability limitsOutside contracts, nuclear economics follow power prices, which follow gas. In 2023 the Illinois ZEC price fell to $0.30/MWh as market prices...

    Durability limits:

    • Outside contracts, nuclear economics follow power prices, which follow gas. In 2023 the Illinois ZEC price fell to $0.30/MWh as market prices rose, which shows how state support and market revenue offset each other.
    • Retail margins compete on price.
  • CompetitorsVistra (VST): nuclear plus a large gas fleet; Meta deal; Morningstar fair value $106, no moat, price/fair value 1.41.

    Competitors:

    • Vistra (VST): nuclear plus a large gas fleet; Meta deal; Morningstar fair value $106, no moat, price/fair value 1.41.
    • NRG: retail-heavy with a gas fleet; fair value $104, no moat, price/fair value 0.96.
    • Talen: single large nuclear site (Susquehanna) with Amazon.
    • Utilities with nuclear (PSEG, Dominion, Southern) are regulated and can't easily sign merchant co-location deals in the same way.
    • Constellation's differentiation: fleet size allows multi-site, multi-GW deals with uprates; the fleet spans every major competitive market; and it operates best-in-class.

Pre-mortem. If this investment fails over three years, the most likely reason is the market no longer paying a scarcity premium for uncontracted nuclear. Hyperscaler capex slows after the 2026 AI-financing scare, forward power prices drift toward the PTC zone, and Constellation signs few new PPAs. 2029 EPS then lands near the low teens instead of ~$17, and the stock re-rates from ~21x earnings to the ~13-14x of other IPPs. Business quality survives; the multiple doesn't.

Key assumptions that must hold:

  • 1. At least 1-2 GW of new premium nuclear contracts by end-2027.
  • 2. Forward power prices stay above the PTC phase-out (~$45-50/MWh) for most of the fleet.
  • 3. The Crane restart delivers in 2027-2028.
  • 4. PJM capacity prices stay near $300+/MW-day with no punitive caps.
  • 5. Net debt falls with the divestitures, and FFO/debt stays above 25%.
  • 6. The market keeps valuing CEG at a premium to IPP peers (~17x+ forward earnings).

Morningstar model caveat (this memo). Morningstar's DCF bridge subtracts $8.99B of debt and adds $3.64B of cash: exactly the December 31, 2025 balances, before Calpine closed.

  • Constellation assumed $12.55B of Calpine debt on January 7, 2026 (10-Q purchase-price allocation), and June 30 total debt was $24.70B.
  • Morningstar's own 2026 debt/capital forecast (21.7%) is about half the ~43% on the June 30 balance sheet.
  • Unless the assumed debt is captured in its 2026 free cash flow forecast (−$9.4B, larger than the $2.5B net cash paid for Calpine), Morningstar's $238 is overstated by up to ~$35 a share. Treat it as an upper bound of a $203-238 range, not a conservative floor.
