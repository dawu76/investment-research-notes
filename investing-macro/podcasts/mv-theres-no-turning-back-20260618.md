# MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back

Detailed overview and analysis of the *MacroVoices* podcast (Episode 537) featuring **Brent Johnson**, founder of *Santiago Capital* and creator of the Dollar Milkshake Theory, hosted by Erik Townsend and Patrick Ceresna. The episode was released on June 18, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Brent Johnson evaluates the geopolitical and macroeconomic aftermath of the recently announced U.S.-Iran Memorandum of Understanding (MOU). He argues that while the kinetic war is paused, the truce is structurally unstable: the agreement grants Iran permanent toll booth control over the Strait of Hormuz after a 60-day window ($1/barrel toll), while Israel has rejected the Lebanon ceasefire conditions. Johnson frames this conflict as part of a larger historical transition: the death of the post-WWII rules-based order in favor of bilateral, nationalist transactions. He asserts that the U.S. is transitioning from a democratic Republic to a centralized global Empire, utilizing its monetary hegemonic assets (the U.S. dollar, treasury market, and stablecoin networks) to maintain dominance. In particular, the rise of USD-backed stablecoins represents a transfer of monetary sovereignty from emerging markets back to the U.S. Treasury. For portfolio positioning, Johnson highlights delayed agricultural supply chain shocks (fertilizer/additives disruption during planting) pointing to food inflation in Q4 2026/Q1 2027, aviation MRO supply chain blockages, and multi-decade sovereign re-armament trends.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Geopolitical Realities of the Iran MOU
Brent Johnson expresses deep skepticism regarding the longevity of the signed memorandum of understanding (MOU) between the U.S. and Iran.
*   **Strait Chokepoint Tolls:** Rather than a free transit lane, the MOU permits only 60 days of toll-free shipping. Thereafter, Iran is authorized to charge a $1/barrel user fee ($2 million per VLCC tanker) for Strait of Hormuz transit.
*   **Israel's Ceasefire Rejection:** The deal mandates a regional ceasefire, including Lebanon. However, Israel has explicitly declared this condition non-binding, warning they will resume military operations if Iran's nuclear materials are not physically removed (only on-site down-blending was agreed upon).
*   **Swift Sanction Waivers:** The deal facilitates a $300 billion financial flow to Iran through third-party banking channels, backed by U.S. Treasury sanction waivers, allowing Iran to utilize the Swift network.
*   **Weaponizing the Oil Market:** Previously, Iran sold its oil to China at a 20-30% discount in Yuan (RMB). By permitting Iran to export oil globally in USD, the U.S. forces China to compete at market rates in USD, directly draining China's dollar reserves.

> "Iran is... charging fees to transit... both versions of the MoU say it's toll-free for 60 days only... and then a dollar a barrel... payable to Iran... [Also] Israel has said... 'We don't agree to that ceasefire'... this isn't over."
> — *Brent Johnson & Erik Townsend*

#### 2. The Law of One Price and the Imperial Transition
Johnson details the structural shift from a unipolar rules-based order to a deglobalized, nationalistic framework.
*   **Death of the Law of One Price:** Historically, commodities traded at a uniform price globally. Today, national security, localized supply chains, tariffs, and export restrictions have caused commodity prices to diverge significantly based on region and domicile.
*   **Republic to Empire:** Parallel to the fall of the Roman Republic (130–60 BC, as documented in *The Storm Before the Storm*), the political swing of the U.S. electorate (Obama, Trump, Biden, Trump) is a symptom of institutional distrust. This cycle historically terminates in a strongman transition from a republic to an empire. While the U.S. faces structural deficits and political dysfunction, the rest of the world is in worse shape, allowing the U.S. to enforce bilateral terms through currency and military power.

> "The law of one price kind of ruled most of my career... [Now] commodity prices... have started to diverge based on where they're traded... [Also] I think it's more likely we see the fall of the republic and the rise of the empire than the fall of the empire."
> — *Brent Johnson*

#### 3. Stablecoins as a Monetary Hegemonic Tool
Johnson identifies stablecoins as a key mechanism for cementing U.S. dollar dominance.
*   **Monetary Sovereignty Export:** Over 99% of global stablecoins are backed by USD due to market utility. As citizens in inflation-ravaged emerging markets store wealth in USD stablecoins rather than their local currencies, monetary sovereignty is transferred away from foreign central banks directly to the U.S. Treasury. This provides a powerful liquidity tool that can be weaponized to create localized currency shortages.
*   **DXY 'The Band' Analogy:** Paraphrasing the oil-band theory from the TV show *Land Man* (where oil must stay between $65-$90 to remain healthy for both producers and consumers), the U.S. dollar index (DXY) must stay within a specific band. If the DXY spikes above this band, it triggers immediate dollar-funding crises in the 16 largest emerging markets (a historical 75% default correlation).

> "99% of all outstanding stablecoins... are US dollar stablecoins... the transfer of monetary sovereignty starts to leave that local jurisdiction... and get transferred to Washington DC... [Also] the dollar is ever going to die, it's going to die of strength."
> — *Brent Johnson*

#### 4. Gold Reserves and the 'Early Withdrawal Penalty'
Emerging market central banks aggressively purchased gold following the 2022 seizure of Russian reserves. However, during global liquidity and energy shocks, these nations are forced to liquidate their gold (gold fell 20% to $4047/oz) to raise physical USD to clear import contracts. Simultaneously, the Fed's rapid interest rate hikes in 2022 caused foreign-owned Treasury portfolios to collapse in value. When foreign central banks sold these devalued Treasuries to raise cash, they realized massive losses—representing a de-facto "early withdrawal penalty" imposed on foreign reserves by the U.S. central bank.

> "Gold fell 20%... those countries who had gold... started selling it... Turkey... the Gulf States... because they needed more dollars... [Fed hikes] were essentially the same thing as an early withdrawal penalty being put on their dollar reserves."
> — *Brent Johnson*

#### 5. Specific Sector Opportunities
*   **Agriculture (Food Shocks):** Key agricultural inputs (fertilizers, chemicals, crop additives) were severely blocked during the spring planting season due to Hormuz shipping delays. This supply deficit points to a delayed food price spike in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027.
*   **Sovereign Re-Armament:** Every nation is forced to rebuild domestic military capabilities due to the breakdown of multilateral treaties, ensuring a multi-decade defense boom.
*   **Aviation MRO (Maintenance, Repair, & Overhaul):** Shipping and transport backlogs in the Gulf have disrupted global aircraft parts overhauls, creating supply chain opportunities in regional aviation logistics.

> "Aviation industry... MRO market... maintenance, repair and overhaul... is another supply chain disruption... [Also] food... many ships did not get out during the planting season... fertilizers, chemicals... sets us up for a potential food cascade."
> — *Brent Johnson*
