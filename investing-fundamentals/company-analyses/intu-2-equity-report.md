## Executive Summary

Intuit is an AI-driven financial platform company serving approximately 100 million customers across small businesses, self-employed individuals, and consumers. The company operates four segments: Global Business Solutions Group (QuickBooks, Mailchimp, QuickBooks Payroll), Consumer (TurboTax), Credit Karma, and ProTax (professional tax software). Intuit generates revenue primarily through subscription and service fees, making it one of the most recurring-revenue software businesses in the United States.

In FY2025 (ended July 31, 2025), Intuit reported total revenue of $18.8 billion, up 16%, with GAAP operating income of $4.9 billion and non-GAAP operating income of $7.6 billion. The company generated $6.2 billion in operating cash flow. Its gross margin stands at approximately 79.6%, and its GAAP operating margin has improved to approximately 26.7%. Intuit has dominant market positions: QuickBooks holds roughly 80%+ share in U.S. small business accounting software, and TurboTax commands approximately 60% of the U.S. consumer tax-preparation market.

In plain terms: **Intuit is the dominant software monopoly for how American small businesses do their books and how millions of Americans file their taxes — with a growing AI-powered platform that makes both stickier every year.**

## What They Sell and Who Buys

### Products and Services

**QuickBooks (Global Business Solutions Group):**  
Cloud-based accounting, payroll, payments, invoicing, and cash-flow management for small and mid-market businesses. Also includes Mailchimp (marketing automation) and QuickBooks Enterprise for larger SMBs. In September 2024, Intuit launched the Intuit Enterprise Suite (IES) targeting businesses with $3M–$100M in revenue, an upmarket push into the mid-market.

**TurboTax (Consumer Group):**  
Self-service and assisted tax preparation for U.S. consumers. TurboTax Live offers human-expert-assisted filing, which grew 47% in FY2025 to $2.0 billion. Includes desktop software and online/mobile platforms. Also operates TurboTax Business for self-employed and small business owners.

**Credit Karma:**  
Personal finance platform offering free credit scores, credit monitoring, personalized financial product recommendations (credit cards, loans, insurance), and tax filing (Credit Karma Tax). Acquired by Intuit in 2020 for $7.1 billion. Revenue is primarily marketplace-driven (financial institution partnerships), making it more economically sensitive.

**ProTax Group:**  
Professional tax preparation software (Lacerte, ProSeries, ProConnect Tax Online) used by accounting professionals and CPA firms.

### Target Customers

| Segment | Customer Type | Geography |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| Global Business Solutions | Small businesses (1–100 employees), self-employed, mid-market (IES) | U.S. (primary), U.K., Canada, Australia, India |
| Consumer (TurboTax) | Individual U.S. taxpayers, self-employed filers | U.S. only |
| Credit Karma | Individual U.S. consumers (financial product shoppers) | U.S. (primary), Canada, U.K. |
| ProTax | CPA firms, tax preparers, accounting professionals | U.S. (primary) |

The unifying customer need is **financial complexity management** — small businesses need help running their books without a full-time CFO; individuals need help navigating an increasingly complex tax code; consumers need guidance navigating credit and lending decisions.

## How They Make Money

### Revenue Model

Intuit operates a **predominantly subscription and recurring service** model, with approximately 84% of FY2025 revenue classified as online services and subscription.

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue | % of Total | Growth (YoY) | Revenue Type |
|---------|--------------|------------|--------------|-------------|
| Global Business Solutions Group | $11.1B | 59% | +16% | Subscription (SaaS) + transaction fees |
| — Online Ecosystem subset | $8.3B | 44% | +20% | Subscription + payments |

**Key pricing dynamics:**
- QuickBooks Online subscription ARPU has grown consistently through tier migration (Simple Start → Plus → Advanced) and platform monetization (payroll, payments add-ons)
- TurboTax Live's rapid growth reflects consumers upgrading from self-serve to assisted service, driving ~2–3x higher revenue per filer
- Credit Karma monetizes through financial product placement; revenue is cyclical with consumer credit appetite

## Revenue Quality

### Recurring vs. Non-Recurring
Intuit's revenue is among the highest-quality in enterprise software:
- **Global Business Solutions:** Subscription-dominant; QuickBooks Online has low churn once workflows are embedded. Online Ecosystem revenue growing at 20%+ reflects both pricing and customer growth.
- **Consumer/TurboTax:** Annual recurrence driven by tax filing obligation — effectively every U.S. taxpayer files each year. The shift to TurboTax Live creates a service relationship that increases retention.
- **Credit Karma:** Variable/marketplace revenue tied to consumer credit cycles. More economically sensitive. Credit Karma revenue contracted ~30% in FY2023 during the rate-hike environment and has since recovered sharply (+32% in FY2025, +23% in Q2 FY2026).
- **ProTax:** Highly sticky, professional-grade software with annual subscription; accountants rarely switch platforms.

### Concentration Risk
- No single customer accounts for a material portion of revenue (highly diversified)
- Geographic concentration: ~87% of revenue is U.S.-sourced
- TurboTax is subject to regulatory risk from the IRS Free File program and potential government-provided free filing alternatives

## Cost Structure

### Gross Margin
Intuit's **gross margin of 79.6% (FY2025)** reflects the high software content of its revenue. The modest compression from 82% (FY2020) to ~80% reflects the growing mix of Credit Karma (lower-margin marketplace) and TurboTax Live (human expert costs embedded in COGS).

### Operating Cost Breakdown (Approximate, FY2025)
| Cost Line | Nature | Scalability |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| COGS (human experts, hosting, support) | ~20% of revenue | Partially variable; AI is reducing marginal cost per assisted filing |
| R&D | ~20% of revenue | High fixed; Intuit invests heavily in AI/ML platform |
| Sales & Marketing | ~25% of revenue | Partially variable; tax season advertising is concentrated in Jan–Apr |
| G&A | ~8% of revenue | Largely fixed |

**GAAP operating margin:** 26.7% (FY2025, up from 24.3% in FY2024)  
**Non-GAAP operating margin:** ~40% (FY2025)  
The difference between GAAP and non-GAAP is primarily stock-based compensation and amortization of acquired intangibles (largely Credit Karma and Mailchimp acquisitions).

### Scalability
Intuit demonstrates strong **operating leverage**: revenue grew 16% in FY2025, while GAAP operating income grew 36%. AI investment is reducing the cost of delivering expert-assisted services (TurboTax Live, QuickBooks Live), improving the unit economics of the fastest-growing revenue lines.

## Capital Intensity

### Asset Requirements
Intuit is a **capital-light software business**:
- **Capex:** Relatively modest; primarily office facilities and data center equipment
- **R&D:** Capitalized and expensed; Intuit employs roughly 18,000 people, heavily weighted toward engineering and data science
- **Working Capital:** Negative in aggregate — customers pay subscriptions upfront (deferred revenue), and Intuit holds significant cash

### Cash Generation
- **Operating Cash Flow:** $6.2B (FY2025) — 33% of revenue, reflecting exceptional cash conversion
- **Free Cash Flow:** Materially below operating cash flow due to capex and deferred tax effects, but still very strong
- **Capital Allocation:** $2.8B in share repurchases in FY2025; dividend yield minimal; Intuit prefers buybacks and strategic M&A

### Balance Sheet
Post-acquisitions (Credit Karma $7.1B, Mailchimp ~$12B in 2021), Intuit carries meaningful goodwill/intangibles. However, its debt levels are manageable given the cash-generative nature of the business.

## Growth Drivers

### 1. AI-Powered Platform Monetization (Structural)
Intuit has deployed a proprietary financial large language model (Intuit GenOS) and launched AI agents across QuickBooks and TurboTax in FY2025. These agents automate bookkeeping tasks, reconciliations, and tax preparation steps. This reduces the cost of delivering assisted services while expanding the addressable use cases. AI is a tailwind for both monetization and margin expansion.

### 2. ARPU Expansion via Tier Migration and Add-Ons (Structural)
QuickBooks customers increasingly migrate from basic tiers to Advanced plans and adopt payroll, payments, and capital products. Average revenue per customer has grown steadily. Online Ecosystem ARPU expansion is a primary lever behind the 20% Online Ecosystem growth rate.

### 3. TurboTax Live Penetration (Structural)
TurboTax Live grew 47% in FY2025 to $2.0B, now representing 41% of Consumer Group revenue. The shift from $50–$100 self-serve to $200–$500+ assisted filing dramatically expands revenue per customer. With ~40M TurboTax users annually, Live penetration from ~20% to 30%+ represents several billion dollars of potential revenue expansion.

### 4. Intuit Enterprise Suite / Mid-Market Expansion (Emerging)
The September 2024 launch of IES targets $3M–$100M revenue businesses — historically QuickBooks' ceiling. This is a new TAM expansion opportunity with significantly higher ACV potential.

### 5. Credit Karma Recovery and Expansion (Cyclical + Structural)
Credit Karma revenue returned to strong growth (+32% FY2025) as consumer credit markets normalized. The long-term opportunity is to build Credit Karma into a "financial assistant" for Intuit's 100M consumer base, cross-selling between TurboTax and Credit Karma.

### 6. International Expansion (Early-Stage)
QuickBooks has meaningful international presence in the U.K., Canada, and Australia. India is an emerging focus. International is a long-tail opportunity with lower near-term materiality.

## Competitive Edge

### Moat Classification
Intuit's competitive advantages are a combination of **switching costs, network effects (data), regulatory complexity as a structural barrier, and brand/trust**.

| Moat Source | Evidence | Durability |
|-------------|----------|------------|
| **Switching Costs (QuickBooks)** | Years of transaction data, custom integrations, accountant ecosystem locked in | Very High — switching requires migrating years of financial history |
| **Switching Costs (TurboTax)** | Prior-year return carryforward, familiar UX, imported data | High — ~66% of TurboTax users return annually |
| **Accountant Ecosystem Network** | ~600,000 accounting professionals recommend or use Intuit products | High — ProTax + QuickBooks Accountant creates a referral flywheel |
| **Regulatory Complexity Moat** | U.S. tax code complexity creates ongoing demand; Intuit lobbies to preserve complexity | High (controversial — FTC and DOJ scrutiny is ongoing) |
| **Brand Trust** | TurboTax and QuickBooks are the default choice for their categories; 60%+ market share in each | High — SMBs default to QuickBooks without evaluation |
| **Data Advantage** | Financial data from 100M customers feeds AI/ML models; proprietary Intuit GenOS trained on financial transactions | Growing — competitors lack the training data density |

### Financial Evidence of Moat
- **Gross margin 79.6%** (FY2025): Consistent with high-switching-cost software
- **ROIC recovered to 14.4%** (FY2025) and improving as Credit Karma and Mailchimp integration matures
- **Online Ecosystem revenue growing 20%+**: Implies customers are both staying and paying more each year
- **TurboTax retention: ~66%** annual return rate despite competitive pressure from H&R Block, TaxAct, and FreeTaxUSA

### Key Competitors and Differentiation

| Segment | Competitor | Intuit's Advantage |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| QuickBooks | Xero, Sage, FreshBooks, Wave | Scale, accountant ecosystem, integrated payroll/payments, AI agents |
| QuickBooks (Mid-Market) | Sage Intacct, NetSuite, Acumatica | Price/familiarity; IES is early but leverages existing customer base |
| TurboTax | H&R Block, TaxAct, FreeTaxUSA | Brand trust, UX, Live offering, 60% market share creates pricing power |
| Credit Karma | NerdWallet, Bankrate, Credit Sesame | Scale (100M user base), integration with TurboTax for tax-refund offers |
| ProTax | Thomson Reuters (UltraTax), Drake Tax, Wolters Kluwer | Strong among small-medium CPA firms; loses at very large firms |
