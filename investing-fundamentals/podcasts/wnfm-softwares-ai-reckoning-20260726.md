# Software's AI Reckoning With Billy Fitzsimmons | What's Next For Markets

Detailed overview of the interview featuring **Billy Fitzsimmons**, Senior Enterprise Software Research Analyst at Piper Sandler, hosted by **Michael Kantrowitz** (Chief Investment Strategist) on *What's Next For Markets*, published on July 26, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary

Enterprise software has suffered a ~25% drawdown since its September 2025 peak, driven by intense investor debate over whether artificial intelligence poses an existential threat to the traditional Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) business model. Billy Fitzsimmons clarifies that fundamental revenue degradation has been limited, with major horizontal SaaS names still delivering double-digit top- and bottom-line growth; the selloff is predominantly severe **multiple compression** triggered by market fears of seat-count cannibalization, "vibe coding" disintermediation, and delayed AI revenue acceleration. 

To navigate this environment, Fitzsimmons outlines three safe-haven strategies: **hyperscalers** capturing compute demand (Microsoft, Oracle), **consumption-based platforms** monetizing data volumes (Datadog, Snowflake, Twilio), and **insulated vertical software** serving physical economy trades (**ServiceTitan / TTAN**, Piper Sandler's top pick). Meanwhile, hyperscaler CapEx is escalating toward $250B annually, forcing balance sheet maneuvers such as Oracle's $20B ATM equity program and Alphabet's $80B equity raise. Looking ahead, upcoming public listings for OpenAI and Anthropic are expected to serve as major sector-clearing events by exposing true AI unit economics and highlighting the free cash flow power of premier SaaS platforms.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Twin Overhangs: Seat Cannibalization and Vibe Coding Disruption
The primary overhang on horizontal application software stems from structural headwinds to the traditional seat-based pricing model (ARPU / ARPC):
*   **Post-COVID Pull-Forward Hangover:** The 2020–2022 remote work boom pulled forward years of cloud migration in communication and HR/payroll software, resulting in saturated seat counts and growth deceleration.
*   **Vibe Coding Threat:** Following the release of GPT-5 in August 2025, frontier AI models demonstrated the ability to generate full-fledged software applications from natural language prompts. The bear case argues that enterprise buyers might build custom internal tools (e.g., custom CRMs via OpenAI/Anthropic models) rather than paying expensive per-seat SaaS licenses. While large enterprises face governance, security, and maintenance constraints that limit custom buildout, the threat of rapid AI-native startup competition has severely compressed multiples.
*   **Unfulfilled Acceleration Promises:** At the start of 2025, consensus modeled revenue acceleration for over 100 major software names in 2026. Because AI-driven revenue monetization lagged expectations, growth continued to slow, amplifying market skepticism.

> "If you went back to the start of 2025 and looked at consensus growth rates for software, consensus across 100 of the large software names was actually modeling that revenue growth would accelerate in 2026... but that really didn't materialize... growth rates continued to decel. That's the concern—is AI disruption showing up in real time?"
> — *Billy Fitzsimmons*

#### 2. Valuation Framework Shifts: Stock-Based Compensation & Capital Allocation
The 25% sector correction represents multiple compression rather than earnings collapse, as top-tier horizontal SaaS companies continue to post double-digit revenue growth:
*   **LTV/CAC Model Obsolescence:** During the zero-interest-rate era, software was valued on high Lifetime Value to Customer Acquisition Cost (LTV/CAC) ratios, forgiving current net losses. Today, institutional investors demand GAAP-adjusted earnings clarity that explicitly accounts for **Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)**.
*   **Capital Return Pivot:** In response to multiple contraction, mature SaaS leaders are shifting capital allocation toward debt issuance and aggressive share repurchases. For example, **Salesforce (CRM)** repurchased **$27 billion in stock** in Q1, demonstrating a transition from dilutive M&A (e.g., all-stock Tableau deal, cash/stock Slack deal) to shareholder yield.

> "The argument can be made that nothing in the numbers has really been disastrous. Most of these companies are still showing double-digit revenue growth and earnings growth... But there's been a shift where investors are evaluating these companies post-stock-based compensation... and companies are now buying back a lot of stock."
> — *Billy Fitzsimmons*

#### 3. Safe-Haven Playbooks: Hyperscalers, Consumption SaaS, and Vertical Trades
Fitzsimmons identifies three distinct buckets to navigate the software downturn:
1.  **Hyperscalers (MSFT, ORCL):** Cloud infrastructure revenue is accelerating at scale because compute demand vastly outstrips GPU supply. However, massive CapEx buildouts are depressing free cash flow, creating secondary balance sheet debates.
2.  **Consumption-Based SaaS (DDOG, SNOW, TWLO):** Platforms that charge per gigabyte, event, or volume unit benefit directly from expanding AI data workloads without exposure to seat-count reduction. These stocks have traded flat-to-up year-to-date, significantly outperforming seat-based peers.
3.  **Insulated Vertical Software (Top Pick: ServiceTitan / TTAN):** Vertical platforms serving physical economy trades (HVAC technicians, plumbers, electricians) are immune to vibe coding and seat contraction. ServiceTitan consolidates 8–10 point solutions (dispatch, scheduling, inventory, procurement, invoicing) into a single end-to-end OS. Furthermore, ServiceTitan is monetizing its proprietary AI suite ("Max"), setting up revenue re-acceleration into 2027.

> "Our favorite name is ServiceTitan (TTAN)... a vertical software company for skilled laborers—HVAC technicians, plumbers, electricians. That's an end market that at least right now is not being disrupted by AI... They combine 8 to 10 different software products into software in a box that just works."
> — *Billy Fitzsimmons*

#### 4. Hyperscaler CapEx War & Balance Sheet Engineering
The AI infrastructure race has pushed annual CapEx expectations across major hyperscalers to **$190B–$250B**:
*   **Balance Sheet Pressure:** Spending has driven free cash flow to zero or negative levels, forcing capital market interventions. Oracle launched a **$20 billion ATM equity program**, while Alphabet announced an **$80 billion equity raise** to fund data center expansion.
*   **Oracle OCI & $300B Backlog:** Oracle's OCI platform gained momentum following a landmark September 2025 deal with OpenAI that expanded backlog to **$300 billion**. Oracle maintains 30% gross margins on mega-tenants (OpenAI) and 40% on standard workloads. To protect margins against component inflation (GPUs, memory), Oracle structures multi-year agreements as **fixed price plus input cost indexing**.

> "Oracle announced they were going to do a $20 billion ATM in equity to fund the difference. Google announced an $80 billion equity raise to help fund the difference... For longer-term contracts, including the OpenAI one, it's fixed price plus input costs, which allows them to protect 30 to 40% gross margins."
> — *Billy Fitzsimmons*

#### 5. Frontier Model IPOs (OpenAI & Anthropic) as Sector Clearing Events
Expected public listings for Anthropic (signaled for 2026/2027) and OpenAI (signaled for 2027) will serve as crucial market catalysts:
*   **Financial Transparency:** S-1 disclosures will replace market speculation regarding revenue run rates, inference costs, and gross margins with audited GAAP financials.
*   **Re-rating Catalyst:** Public scrutiny of AI lab unit economics may highlight the superior cash generation of enterprise software incumbents like **ServiceNow (NOW)**—which grows at 20%+ with 30%+ free cash flow margins—leading to a sector re-rating.
*   **Token Optimization Era:** Enterprise AI spending is transitioning from unconstrained "token maxing" to **token optimization** and model routing, directing routine tasks to cost-effective open-weight models while reserving frontier models for complex tasks.

> "When those companies go public, we could actually see the financials and economics under the hood... It actually might be a clearing event for the whole space and be good for a company like ServiceNow, which is growing 20% plus a year with 30% plus free cash flow margins."
> — *Billy Fitzsimmons*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

*   **Overweight / High-Conviction Longs:**
    *   **ServiceTitan (`TTAN`):** Top sector pick; insulated skilled-trades vertical; workflow consolidation; "Max" AI suite monetization driving 2027 growth acceleration.
    *   **ServiceNow (`NOW`):** High-margin enterprise workflow leader (20%+ revenue growth, 30%+ FCF margin); direct enterprise AI workflow monetization.
    *   **Consumption Platforms (`DDOG`, `SNOW`):** Direct exposure to expanding AI data and log volumes without seat-count risk.
    *   **Microsoft (`MSFT`):** Core hyperscaler infrastructure lead (Azure cloud acceleration); fast-follower bundling (Azure 365 governance layer + Copilot adds >5M/quarter).
*   **Underweight / High-Risk Exposure:**
    *   **Seat-Heavy Horizontal SaaS:** Pure-play per-seat applications with high Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) dilution and lack of consumption/outcome pricing models.
    *   **Commoditized EdTech & Low-Barrier Vertical SaaS:** Sub-sectors vulnerable to direct AI LLM substitution.
*   **Key Monitoring Indicators:**
    *   **Hyperscaler CapEx vs. Cloud Revenue:** Track Microsoft ($190B–$250B guidance) and Oracle OCI CapEx efficiency and credit default swap (CDS) spreads.
    *   **Copilot & Enterprise AI Seat Velocity:** Monitor Microsoft Copilot net seat additions (target >5M–8M/quarter).
    *   **OpenAI / Anthropic S-1 Filings:** Watch for official IPO filings to assess true AI training/inference cost structures and gross margins.
