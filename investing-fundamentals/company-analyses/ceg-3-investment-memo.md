# Constellation Energy ($CEG): Investment Memo

**Price:** ~$259.44 (October 20, 2026 close), down ~12% in a week and ~36% below the $406.04 52-week high. **Market cap** ~$91.9B (354.3M shares, July 31, 2026). **Net debt** ~$24.0B at June 30, 2026 (debt $24.70B less $0.70B cash), or ~$19.5B pro forma for ~$4.5B of after-tax divestiture proceeds. **Next catalyst:** Q3 2026 results on November 9 (Raymond James estimates $3.90 adjusted EPS vs. ~$3.62 consensus).
Business background: [[ceg-2-equity-report]]. Valuation figures marked "this memo" are new arithmetic. Sell-side figures come from secondary aggregators.

## 1. Executive Summary

**Thesis.** Constellation owns the scarcest asset in the AI-power trade: ~22 GW of operating nuclear plants that run at ~95% capacity factors. Since January 2026 it also owns the largest US gas and geothermal fleet (Calpine).
* **What's locked in:** a federal PTC floor through 2032, PJM capacity prices of $325-333/MW-day through 2028/29, and long-term contracts with Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google.
* **Management's plan:** 2026 adjusted EPS of $11.50-12.50 and 20%+ base EPS growth to 2029.
* **The open question:** what the market will pay for growth beyond 2029. That depends on whether hyperscalers keep signing premium contracts for the ~147M MWh a year of nuclear output still uncontracted in 2029.
* **Valuation:** at ~$259 the stock trades at ~21.6x 2026 guidance and ~18x management's implied 2029 EPS. This memo's probability-weighted value is **~$244** (bear ~$154, base ~$241, bull ~$340), about 6% below the price.
  * Morningstar's $238 is in the same place. As discussed below, Morningstar's figure may be overstated by up to ~$35 because its DCF bridge appears to omit the Calpine debt.
* **Conclusion:** a high-quality business at roughly fair value, with no margin of safety. It is worth owning more aggressively only below ~$215.

* **Bull case:** Constellation signs 2-3 GW more nuclear contracts at $20-50/MWh premiums, Crane restarts on time in 2027, and power prices hold. 2029 EPS reaches ~$20.50, and the market keeps paying ~20x for a scarce, contracted clean-power franchise. Value ~$340 (+31%), close to the Street's ~$341-353 average targets.
* **Bear case:** AI-capex financing stress slows hyperscaler contracting, forward power prices drift toward the PTC zone, and earnings land near management's base case, about $13 in 2029. The stock de-rates to an IPP multiple of ~14x. Value ~$154 (-41%).
* **Target investor:** quality-growth investors who accept commodity and policy volatility and want long-duration exposure to US power demand. Not an income stock: the yield is ~0.66% on a $1.706 dividend, though the dividend grows 10% a year. Not a hedge against an AI-capex slowdown; it is the opposite. See [[phm-vs-tol-vs-len-homebuilders-20261020]].

### What the price assumes (this memo)

Discounting today's $259.44 at a 9.6% cost of equity (Morningstar's) to the end of 2028 requires a value of ~$318 at that point.

| 2029 EPS | Multiple needed for ~$318 | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| $11.65 (management base midpoint, base only) | ~27x | Not plausible on base earnings alone |
| ~$17.10 (base midpoint grossed up for 30-35% enhanced) | ~18.6x | Roughly management's full plan |
| $19.05 (Morningstar) | ~16.7x | Morningstar's forecast is above management's implied total |

The price therefore assumes management's full plan **and** a premium multiple held into 2029, or Morningstar's higher earnings at a utility-plus multiple. No growth beyond 2029 is needed at 16-19x, but that multiple itself requires investors to expect it.

## 2. Business Model & Unit Economics

**Revenue mix (FY2025, pre-Calpine):** $25.53B of revenue and $10.85B of RNF (revenue net of fuel).
* Segment RNF: Midwest 35%, Mid-Atlantic 32%, New York 15%, ERCOT 11%, Other 8%.
* H1 2026 with Calpine: segment RNF $7.63B, of which Calpine $2.15B (28%).

**Unit economics (2026-2030 modeling inputs, Q2 2026 deck):**

| Unit | Volume | Price / margin | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contracted clean nuclear | 36M MWh (2026) → 53M (2028-2030) | ~$70/MWh (2026) → $77 (2028), $85 (2029), $88 (2030) | Excludes 2026 PPAs (920 MW, Amazon, Google) |
| Available nuclear at the PTC floor | 101M MWh (2026) → 147M (2030) | $44.75 (2026), $48.88 (2028) → $50.88/MWh (2030) | Upside to market prices; the floor ends 2032 |
| Illinois CMC units | 53M MWh (2026), 23M (2027) | ~$34/MWh | Program ends mid-2027 |
| Gas fleet | ~68-71M MWh (PJM/other), 25M (ERCOT), 26-31M (West) | spark spreads $15-25/MWh | Calpine-dominated |
| Retail power | ~245M MWh | $4.25-4.35/MWh | ~$1.05B of gross margin a year (this memo) |
| Retail gas | 835-850M dth | $0.40-0.45/dth | ~$0.35B a year (this memo) |

* **Spread to the floor:** a contracted nuclear MWh at ~$77-88 earns roughly $28-37/MWh more than the PTC floor of ~$49-51 by 2028-2030 (this memo). That premium is the economic case for signing hyperscaler deals.
* **Operating leverage:** nuclear costs are mostly fixed, at about $7.0B of adjusted O&M for 2026. Management's sensitivity: +$1-3/MWh on power prices and spark spreads adds $150-450M of FCFbG and $0.45-1.35 of EPS. The PTC limits the downside on the 101-147M MWh of available nuclear through 2032, but not after.
* **Cash conversion:**
  * FCFbG of $8.4B (2026-2027) and $11.5-13.0B (2028-2029), against 2026 adjusted net income of about $4.3B (Morningstar).
  * Reported operating cash flow is noisy from collateral and derivatives: FY2025 $4.24B; H1 2026 $1.55B after $357M of collateral posted.
  * The historical negative operating cash flow (FY2021-FY2024) reflects receivables-securitization accounting, not weak cash generation.
  * There is no inventory or receivables cycle in the usual sense. The working-capital swing that matters is collateral posting when prices move.

## 3. Competitive Position & Moat

* **Market position.**
  * Largest US independent power producer: ~55 GW, the largest nuclear, gas and geothermal fleets, about 10% of US clean energy.
  * Largest C&I retail supplier: ~190M MWh of load, about twice the next supplier; 80% of the Fortune 100; 2.5M accounts.
  * It has signed all four of the largest hyperscalers to long-term deals.
* **Moat classification:**
  * **Cost advantage and scale:** low-marginal-cost baseload nuclear and efficient combined-cycle gas, plus an integrated retail book that hedges generation.
  * **Regulatory support:** PTC, state ZEC/CMC programs, DOE loan support, NRC license extensions to the 2040s-2050s.
  * **Irreplaceable assets:** no new large US nuclear plants are being built at scale, and management puts the new-build cost of the fleet at more than 3x its enterprise value.
  * Morningstar assigns a **Narrow moat**, based on retail cost advantage, and rates capital allocation Exemplary.
* **Durability evidence:**
  * Capacity factor of 94.7% in 2025.
  * ROIC of 13-17% (FY2024-2025, Morningstar) against an 8.0% WACC.
  * Contracted clean volume rising ~47% from 2026 to 2028 (36M to 53M MWh), with contract terms of 15-20 years.
  * License renewals extend the asset life well beyond 2032.
* **Durability limits:**
  * Outside contracts, nuclear economics follow power prices, which follow gas. In 2023 the Illinois ZEC price fell to $0.30/MWh as market prices rose, which shows how state support and market revenue offset each other.
  * Retail margins compete on price.
* **Competitors:**
  * *Vistra (VST):* nuclear plus a large gas fleet; Meta deal; Morningstar fair value $106, no moat, price/fair value 1.41.
  * *NRG:* retail-heavy with a gas fleet; fair value $104, no moat, price/fair value 0.96.
  * *Talen:* single large nuclear site (Susquehanna) with Amazon.
  * Utilities with nuclear (PSEG, Dominion, Southern) are regulated and can't easily sign merchant co-location deals in the same way.
  * **Constellation's differentiation:** fleet size allows multi-site, multi-GW deals with uprates; the fleet spans every major competitive market; and it operates best-in-class.

## 4. Top 3 Growth Drivers & Sensitivities

1. **New long-term nuclear contracts (structural).**
   * **Opportunity:** 147M MWh a year of nuclear output uncontracted in 2029, plus ~1,100 MW of uprates (890 MW tied to Google).
   * **Earnings value:** management sizes each 1 GW nuclear PPA at a $20-50/MWh premium to the floor at **+$0.40-1.00 of EPS** and +$125-325M of FCFbG.
   * **Probability-weighted (this memo):** 1-2 GW more signed by end-2027 in the base case; 3 GW in the bull; ~0 in the bear.
2. **Power prices, spark spreads and capacity (cyclical).**
   * **Market levels:** PJM West ATC forwards of $72.75 (2026) and $66.94 (2027) at June 30; PJM capacity at $325-333/MW-day through 2028/29.
   * **Sensitivity:** +$1-3/MWh = +$0.45-1.35 of EPS (management). A $1-3 decline costs about the same until nuclear revenues hit the PTC zone.
   * **Probability-weighted:** flat in the base case, +$2 in the bull, -$2 in the bear.
3. **Capital allocation and the Calpine platform (structural).**
   * **Plan:** $5.0B of buybacks over 2026-2027 (~$2.8B left), $3.9B of growth capex (Crane restart, uprates), 10% dividend growth and $3.4B of deleveraging.
   * **Gas upside:** 1 GW of gas "powered land" deals adds +$0.20-0.50 of EPS; the CyrusOne Texas deals total up to ~1,160 MW.
   * **Management's capital-allocation upside:** +$0.20-0.75+ of EPS.

## 5. Risk Framework with Failure Modes

| Rank | Risk | Severity | Probability | Trigger | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AI-demand and contracting slowdown** | High | Medium | Hyperscaler capex cuts; AI credit stress (e.g. CoreWeave's ~15% bond yields in October 2026); data-center delays | New PPAs stall; the 147M MWh earns floor prices; multiple de-rates (the stock fell ~12% in a week on sector selling) | Signed 15-20 year contracts with investment-grade buyers; PTC floor through 2032; contracted volume already rising to 53M MWh |
| 2 | **Power-price downturn and the 2032 PTC cliff** | High | Medium | Gas price collapse; weak load growth; new supply | Enhanced earnings (30-40% of the total) shrink; after 2032 the uncontracted fleet has no floor | Hedging, retail integration, contracting before 2032; NY ZEC to 2049 |
| 3 | **Regulatory and political intervention** | Medium-High | Medium | PJM capacity-price caps or collars; FERC co-location limits; state affordability backlash (capacity prices ~3x a year earlier); Texas data-center permit freeze | Capacity revenue cut; slower deal structuring; delays like the PJM backstop suspension | Geographic diversity after Calpine; deals that add new capacity (uprates, restarts) are politically easier |
| 4 | **Execution and operations** | Medium | Medium-Low | Crane restart slips (PJM upgrades could stretch full deliverability to December 2030); uprate delays; major nuclear outage; Calpine integration | Delays to 835 MW of Microsoft revenue; outage costs; tail risk of a nuclear incident across the fleet | FERC CIR transfer (June 2026); NRC fuel license approved; a record of fast outages |
| 5 | **Balance sheet and rates** | Medium | Medium-Low | Higher rates; refinancing $5.2B of short-term borrowings; buybacks crowding out deleveraging; FFO/debt below Moody's 25% threshold | Higher interest (H1 2026 net interest $536M vs. $264M); possible downgrade; lower valuation multiple | BBB+/Baa1 stable; $4.5B of after-tax divestiture proceeds; $3.4B of planned deleveraging; DOE loan at Treasuries +37.5bp |

**Pre-mortem.** If this investment fails over three years, the most likely reason is the market no longer paying a scarcity premium for uncontracted nuclear. Hyperscaler capex slows after the 2026 AI-financing scare, forward power prices drift toward the PTC zone, and Constellation signs few new PPAs. 2029 EPS then lands near the low teens instead of ~$17, and the stock re-rates from ~21x earnings to the ~13-14x of other IPPs. Business quality survives; the multiple doesn't.

**Key assumptions that must hold:**
1. At least 1-2 GW of new premium nuclear contracts by end-2027.
2. Forward power prices stay above the PTC phase-out (~$45-50/MWh) for most of the fleet.
3. The Crane restart delivers in 2027-2028.
4. PJM capacity prices stay near $300+/MW-day with no punitive caps.
5. Net debt falls with the divestitures, and FFO/debt stays above 25%.
6. The market keeps valuing CEG at a premium to IPP peers (~17x+ forward earnings).

**Morningstar model caveat (this memo).** Morningstar's DCF bridge subtracts $8.99B of debt and adds $3.64B of cash: exactly the December 31, 2025 balances, before Calpine closed.
* Constellation assumed $12.55B of Calpine debt on January 7, 2026 (10-Q purchase-price allocation), and June 30 total debt was $24.70B.
* Morningstar's own 2026 debt/capital forecast (21.7%) is about half the ~43% on the June 30 balance sheet.
* Unless the assumed debt is captured in its 2026 free cash flow forecast (−$9.4B, larger than the $2.5B net cash paid for Calpine), Morningstar's $238 is overstated by up to ~$35 a share. Treat it as an upper bound of a $203-238 range, not a conservative floor.
