## 1. M&A blacklisting methodology — the month's dominant technical thread

M&A risk in the pairs universe was the single most discussed topic all month, running continuously from June 2 through June 20+.

- **The core problem, established early:** robotkris explained the mechanism directly — when an M&A deal happens mid-month, both tickers in an affected pair tend to move together and in the same direction, producing a stretched z-score in *both* legs simultaneously. This shows up as what looks like a single position at double the intended size. His framing was blunt: *"We found out the hard way pretty early on... I think we got stung like week 3 of trading it or something."*
- **His prescribed fix:** rather than trying to patch this downstream, **exclude M&A targets from the universe upstream, before pair construction** — "For stuff like that, I think there's a strong case for removing it from the upstream universe altogether." He returned to this point later in the month with a broader philosophical framing that deserves fuller treatment given how central it is to his approach: *"there isn't a single correct implementation, only trade offs, is central to running a serious, solo trading operation. And it's hard to get one's head around. So I figure it's worth banging on about."* His point is that most of these portfolio-construction decisions (blacklisting depth, filter ordering, position count) don't have a single right answer — they fall out naturally once you clearly identify your own constraints and work through the logic, rather than copying someone else's setup wholesale.
- **Practical implementation, member-led:** Michael TW 88 built out the most detailed tooling in the channel — an LLM-assisted scanner pulling Alpaca corp actions/announcements/news plus Alpha Vantage news across the full ~330-376 ticker universe, producing structured per-ticker merger/spinoff verdicts (a worked example on HWC concluded "Medium" materiality: ~$2.1B target vs. HWC's $35B+ base is only ~6% of assets, but the ~$377.6M cash deal, ~7.5% EPS accretion, and pending regulatory approval were enough to keep it blocked). By mid-month his blacklist had grown to ~26 tickers, with HWC alone affecting 19 pairs due to the group's bank-heavy universe.
- **Deal-size materiality question:** Rachit pushed back usefully — should blacklisting weight the *relative size* of a deal (HWC/OFB was "a pretty small deal" at ~6% of acquirer assets), rather than blacklisting every M&A headline indiscriminately? This connects to MidKnight's separate worry that he'd become "hyper sensitive to anything that even mentions M&A" and grown his blacklist to the point of being counterproductive.
- **A related, distinct risk — liquidations/delistings:** mm raised REIT ELME as a case study — a company winding down and distributing capital over several months, whose price drop (~$17→~$2.77) was a pre-announced distribution, not organic price action. Michael TW 88's answer: catch these early and blacklist immediately, or run an ad-hoc intraday rebalance rather than waiting for EOD if you're caught holding it.
- **PTPs (publicly traded partnerships) are a separate filter, not to be conflated with M&A:** non-US traders face a 10% withholding tax on PTP tickers (akhan hit this directly via IBKR on ticker WES, which was already on Rachit's PTP blacklist). Multiple members shared practical detection methods — hecta pointed to inferring PTP status from IBKR's Web API `/trsrv/secdef` `type` field (MLP/LTD PART filter), and free third-party sources for M&A monitoring were shared (K Filings — free daily email digest of ~20-30 companies; FMP's mergers endpoint, 250 free requests/day).
- **Universe-survivorship question:** 4D asked whether frequent M&A activity introduces survivorship bias into backtests; mm confirmed the universe construction already accounts for this by including delisted/merged tickers.

**Sentiment:** The most mature, well-worked-through thread in the channel — a real, previously-experienced pain point (robotkris's own "week 3" story) that the community has since built substantial tooling and shared heuristics around. Still an open, evolving area — WES was later removed from the blacklist (6/21) after a deal completed, showing the list is actively maintained rather than static.

Rachit's approach: remove M&A before the depth filter, but *keep* PTPs in for weighting purposes even though they won't be traded, since they still carry useful relative-value information. Michael TW 88's risk filters force him to use depth 3 regardless.
- **robotkris's answer to George deserves the fuller treatment given his standing in the group:** *"I think this is one of those things where there's no right answer, only different trade offs. Relaxing the depth filter brings in marginally lower quality signals but you get more active positions. Is that a good trade off? Depends on a few things... for example if you're trading a big allocation, you probably care about spreading it around more. If you're trading a smaller account, that's less of a constraint. I would expect both approaches to make money."* This is a direct, practical restatement of his broader "trade-offs, not correct answers" philosophy from thread 1, applied concretely here.

## 6. New research: S2 vega-pair relationship persistence study

Michael TW 88 ran the most substantial independent research project posted in the channel this month, extending the group's core "pair relationship persistence" methodology from equities into options/volatility (vega pairs) using data from a platform called S2.

- **Methodology:** built candidate vega pairs from S2's universe (checking ~80% retained VIX-history coverage), estimated correlations across all unordered eligible pairs using one-year overlapping VIX history, Pearson correlation, total-least-squares fitting, and six-month residual z-scores. Correlation-band results showed meaningful ticker reuse across pairs (e.g., 85 pairs at ≥0.90 correlation using 64 unique tickers; well-known relationships like GLD/IAU at 0.978, QQQ/TQQQ at 0.977 topped the list).
- **The core persistence test** (directly extending robotkris's framework from thread 1): does a pair's formation-period correlation predict its forward-period correlation? Results across 623,339 measured pair-vintage rows showed a mean rank IC of 0.45, positive rank IC in 20 of 20 vintages tested, and monotonic quintile lift in 19 of 20 — directionally strong evidence for relationship persistence, though Michael TW 88 was appropriately cautious in his own framing, explicitly labeling it "preliminary research evidence rather than proven tradable alpha" given only 20 overlapping forward windows, survivorship bias in a current universe, and known S2 vendor coverage gaps.
- **robotkris's response is the most substantively useful in the thread and merits fuller treatment:** *"if I understood correctly, this is the relationship scoring part? The next step would be to test whether pairs that scored well in one period tended to score well in the next period. That's the big finding (noisy persistence of profitable pair relationships) that underpins all of the equities stat arb work. I'm not sure what your source data is like, but it's a big data engineering job, but totally worth doing - will tell you very quickly if the idea is worth pursuing or not."* This directly validates that Michael TW 88 is replicating the core empirical finding that underpins the entire equities strategy, now in the options/vol domain.
- **mm offered useful skepticism** — the rank IC of 0.45 "seems a bit sus" (i.e., unusually high), prompting Michael TW 88 to walk through the exact rank-IC definition being used, though he acknowledged mm might not have had full context on what was being measured.
- An open, unresolved technical question Michael TW 88 raised: for vega pairs, should distance/correlation be measured on implied-vol relationships (e.g., constant-maturity variance-swap IV) rather than on the underlying stock prices, since IV relationships behave differently — and he flagged that any tradable version of this would need proper delta-hedging, which "could get messy really fast."

**Sentiment:** The most technically ambitious individual contribution of the month — a genuine extension of the group's core stat-arb thesis into a new asset class, taken seriously and engaged with substantively by robotkris, with appropriately cautious framing from the researcher himself about the gap between "persistence evidence" and "tradable alpha."

## 7. CHF/JPY weekend seasonality — a new cross-strategy find

mm backtested a currency seasonality pattern (short CHF/JPY early week, cover and go flat/USD Thursday-Friday) using currency ETFs (FXF/FXY) as a proxy for traders who can't access FX pairs directly — result: "not great, not terrible, pretty much what you'd expect," modestly improved with 10%/yr vol targeting. This triggered a genuine mechanism debate: MidKnight wondered if it's a carry/weekend-derisking effect (possibly starting Thursday US time due to Asia-Pacific timezones already being "Friday"), which robotkris engaged with at length rather than giving a quick answer — explaining he'd originally hand-waved it as a "weekend risk premium" in a webinar, but on reflection finds it more plausible that it reflects unwinding of carry-funded positions (borrowing in CHF/JPY to fund other-currency exposure) than literal weekend risk-aversion buying. He also flagged an interesting wrinkle in mm's own data — what looks like two distinct regimes in the backtest — and was honest that this could either undermine the theory (inconsistency) or support a rate-differential explanation, concluding the single-plot evidence available so far isn't enough to be confident either way and that "some more careful data analysis could help answer" it.

**Sentiment:** A well-reasoned, still-open research question, notable for robotkris explicitly modeling intellectual honesty about the limits of a single-chart finding rather than offering false certainty.

**Pull quotes:**
