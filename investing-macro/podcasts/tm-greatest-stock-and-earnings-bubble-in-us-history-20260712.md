# We're In The Greatest Stock & Earnings Bubble In US History | Thoughtful Money

Detailed rundown and analysis of the *Thoughtful Money* interview featuring veteran technology analyst and editor of *The High-Tech Strategist*, **Fred Hickey**, hosted by Adam Taggart in mid-July 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Veteran technology strategist Fred Hickey warns that the US equity market is perched on the greatest stock and earnings bubble in history, driven by an accounting mismatch in AI hyperscaler capital expenditures. Hickey notes that component manufacturers are booking immediate profits while tech giants capitalize and defer massive depreciation costs, masking the lack of immediate return on investment (ROI). With corporate and retail speculation at feverish highs, Hickey advocates for extreme capital preservation, holding record levels of cash/Treasuries, and preparing to deploy capital into commodities, gold, and post-crash tech survivors.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Stock and Earnings Bubble Mismatch
Hickey warns that traditional valuation metrics are far more stretched than during the 2000 dot-com peak. The Buffett Indicator (market cap to GDP) stands at 241% vs. 160% in 2000, and the price-to-sales ratio is 3.7x vs. 2.3x. However, the most critical vulnerability is the **earnings bubble**, caused by an accounting mismatch in AI capital expenditures.
*   **Revenues Recognized Immediately:** Component and hardware makers (Nvidia, Micron, Western Digital, Seagate) recognize revenues and earnings immediately when they sell parts.
*   **Expenses Capitalized and Deferred:** The buyers (hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon) do not show these expenses immediately. Instead, they capitalize them and depreciate the hardware over a five-to-six-year period.
*   **Normalized Growth in Single Digits:** Q1 2026 earnings growth was reported at 28%. When adjusting for one-time mark-to-market gains from venture investments (OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX) and stripping out AI component suppliers, the S&P 500's underlying earnings growth is only in the single digits.

> "There's a huge gap between what companies are reporting right now for revenues and earnings and what they really are expending... Nvidia's numbers are soaring and Micron's numbers are soaring... but the hyperscaler's costs don't show up right away... Those expenses are depreciated over five to six years. So, you only see a small part of the expenditures."
> — *Fred Hickey*

#### 2. The AI ROI Deficit & Token-Maxing Backlash
Despite spending $750 billion annually among the top five hyperscalers (plus another $100 billion internationally), corporate clients are seeing very little productivity return on their AI investments.
*   **The token ROI problem:** Hickey cites Chamath Palihapitiya's All-In Podcast discussion, where he revealed that his token spend is doubling every 45 days, yet the return on investment is a maximum of 5%.
*   **Token-Maxing Incentives:** Due to internal "leaderboard" incentives at companies like Amazon and Meta, engineers were encouraged to run redundant loop-requesting scripts to generate high token volumes. This resulted in massive, unexpected bills, such as a reported $500 million single-month token bill for Amazon.
*   **Corporate Spending Caps:** To combat these runaway costs, major companies are putting limits on AI spending. Tesla has implemented a $200 weekly cap per engineer, Coinbase is cutting its AI budget in half, and Microsoft has stopped issuing broad cloud code licenses.

> "Jamath [Chamath] said, 'So my costs are doubling every 45 days, yet my upside is essentially flat. We're going to have to take a look at this and do something about it.' And he's not alone. So even the hyperscalers themselves are starting to question the amount of token spending."
> — *Fred Hickey*

#### 3. Software Efficiencies & The Rise of Chinese Models
A major threat to the hardware-centric AI model is the rapid shift toward software efficiency and low-cost open-source models, primarily originating from China.
*   **Software Over Hardware:** Because U.S. sanctions prevent China from accessing the highest-end Western hardware, Chinese developers were forced to optimize software to extract more performance from lower-end chips.
*   **DeepSeek & Qwen Cost Disruption:** Models like DeepSeek can execute 90% of standard enterprise tasks at 1.5% of the cost of frontier U.S. models. Consequently, Chinese models have surged to 46% of the token market share on OpenRouter, up from an 11% average over the prior year.
*   **Model Shrinking & On-Device AI:** Startups like Vinned Colossus recently demonstrated a breakthrough, shrinking Alibaba's Qwen 3.6 model to run locally on an iPhone 17 Pro. This development threatens the core thesis that AI requires massive, centralized data centers.

> "We have not allowed China to access our best chips... The Chinese have said, 'Well, we got to be smarter. So, we can't win on hardware... let's win on software.' ... With DeepSeek, you can get 90% of your tasks done at 1.5% of the cost, which is insane."
> — *Fred Hickey*

#### 4. Late-Stage Exhaustion & Speculative Spikes
Hickey identifies classic late-stage bubble signals that indicate the market is nearing exhaustion.
*   **Massive Equity Issuance:** Companies are rushing to issue equity while valuations are high (SpaceX's $85B IPO, Hynix's $27B equity offering).
*   **Bond Market Saturation:** Hyperscalers have issued nearly $200 billion in debt. Amazon's recent $25 billion bond offering was so large that it added eight basis points to the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield, forcing Amazon to seek funding in alternative currencies (Euros, Yen, Swiss Francs, British Pounds).
*   **Surging Margin Debt:** Margin debt has spiked to over $1.4 trillion (representing 4.5% of GDP vs. 3% at the peak of the 2000 bubble). Additionally, leverage is compounded by newer tools like zero-day-to-expiration (0DTE) options and highly leveraged ETFs.

> "Amazon's recent $25 billion bond... added eight basis points to the 10-year yield. That's a sign that you might be nearing exhaustion... Margin debt now up to over $1.4 trillion... up 55% year-over-year. As a percentage of GDP, it's 4.5% versus 3% back in 2000."
> — *Fred Hickey*

#### 5. Gold & Sovereign Dollarization
While retail investors have capitulated in precious metals (manifested in persistent ETF outflows and futures open interest hitting a 13-year low), central banks are aggressively buying physical gold as part of a secular de-dollarization trend.
*   **China Treasury Dumping:** The People's Bank of China has cut its U.S. Treasury holdings nearly in half, from $1.3 trillion down to approximately $630 billion, rotating the proceeds directly into gold.
*   **Sovereign Buying Floor:** Central banks now collectively hold more reserves in gold than in U.S. Treasuries. This institutional buying has put a firm floor under gold, preventing it from staying below the $4,000 mark.

> "China is accelerating their buying... they have cut their treasury holdings from $1.3 trillion down to 630 almost in half. And they're continuing to sell treasuries and buy gold... Speculators are completely washed out of gold... There's huge pent-up buying potentially."
> — *Fred Hickey*

#### 6. The Sovereign Debt Gating Problem
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh faces structural limitations that prevent him from aggressively raising interest rates to combat sticky 4% inflation.
*   **Record Interest Expense:** Annualized interest expense on the U.S. national debt has reached a record $1.35 trillion.
*   **Refinancing Risk:** The average interest rate paid on the debt is only 3.3% to 3.4%. With market yields substantially higher (10-year at 4.6%, 30-year at 5%), rolling over existing debt will dramatically balloon the deficit.
*   **The Deficit Feedback Loop:** Raising interest rates further would create a dangerous feedback loop, expanding the deficit, increasing Treasury supply, and forcing yields higher, which limits the Fed's hawkish policy options.

> "Interest expense on debt... was $1.35 trillion. It's a record level... if he was to raise rates, this line goes even higher making the deficit even bigger. And Mr. Trump, I don't think would like it... I don't think he's going to be tough and raise rates."
> — *Fred Hickey*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Aggressive Capital Preservation:** Maximize cash holdings, particularly in short-term U.S. Treasury Bills, to maintain liquidity and build dry powder.
2.  **Underweight AI Hardware & Overextended Tech:** Avoid chasing the bubble in Mag 7 or semiconductor component suppliers (e.g., Nvidia, Micron, Western Digital). Prepare for a potential 60%+ decline in valuations if capital expenditures roll over.
3.  **Accumulate Gold & Gold Miners:** Maintain core exposure to physical gold as a currency hedge against sovereign debt expansion. Accumulate gold miners, which are trading at a historic low valuation of ~10x P/E. Use any market-wide liquidity drawdowns ("whoosh downs") to buy more.
4.  **Overweight Energy & Value Commodities:** Prepare for a capital rotation into undervalued commodity and energy producers, which trade at low P/E ratios with strong cash flows, dividends, and low hedge fund positioning.
5.  **Monitor Key Indicators:**
    *   **Hyperscaler Capex Guidance:** Watch for any scale-backs or spending cuts by Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, or Meta.
    *   **Margin Debt Levels:** Watch for a rollover in margin debt from the $1.4T peak.
    *   **U.S. Interest Expense / Treasury Auctions:** Monitor interest expense growth and yields on 10-year and 30-year notes.
