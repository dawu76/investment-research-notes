# Symbotic Inc. (NASDAQ: SYM) — Equity Analyst Report
**Date:** October 9, 2026 | **Analyst:** Investment Research | **Sector:** Industrials / Warehouse Automation & Robotics
**Reference price:** ~$43 (Oct 5, 2026 close $42.59) | **Fiscal year:** ends late September (FY2026 ended Sep 26, 2026; results due late Nov 2026)

---

## Executive Summary

Symbotic designs, builds, and installs AI-directed robotic systems that automate the storage and retrieval of cases inside large distribution centers. It sells each system as a multi-year installation project (systems revenue, 94% of FY2025 revenue of $2.25B), then earns recurring software-subscription and operations-service fees once the system is running. Revenue has grown from $252M (FY2021) to $2.25B (FY2025), and a $22.5B backlog (Q3 FY26) is about eight years of current revenue.

The economics are improving but still thin: GAAP gross margin rose from 13.7% (FY2024) to 22.3% (Q3 FY26), and the company turned GAAP-profitable only in FY2026. Customer prepayments fund the business. Cash of $1.75B roughly equals $1.74B of deferred revenue, so the balance sheet is less liquid than it looks.

The two dominant risks are concentration and valuation. Walmart was 87.0% of 9M FY26 revenue, and Walmart plus GreenBox (a SoftBank JV that Symbotic co-owns) make up most of the backlog. Symbotic is also an Up-C: only 130M of 605M economic shares are listed Class A. On the full count, the equity is worth ~$26B, not the ~$5.6B many screeners show, and trades at ~9x EV/sales and ~72x EV/adjusted EBITDA (FY26E).

**In plain English: Symbotic builds the robot warehouses that Walmart is putting into every regional distribution center, and the stock is priced as if it will do the same for many other retailers.**

## Share Count and Founder Shares (read first)

Symbotic uses an **Up-C structure** (see [[up-c-share-count]] for the general mechanics). The listed company, Symbotic Inc., owns a minority of the operating partnership, Symbotic Holdings LLC. Pre-merger owners, chiefly founder **Rick Cohen** and his family entities, hold Holdings units directly, paired with non-economic Class V shares. Each unit plus its paired V share can be exchanged 1:1 into Class A.

Source: Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q cover page. At June 27, 2026 the totals were 128.9M / 71.4M / 403.6M = 603.9M.

What this means:

- **Class A is 21.5% of the economic share count.** A Class A-only market cap understates the company's equity value by 4.7x. In Q3 FY26, noncontrolling interests took $43.3M of $55.0M net income (78.7%), matching the 78.5% of shares held outside Class A, which confirms that the V shares are economically live.
- **Voting control.** Cohen, his family, and affiliated trusts hold Class V-3 stock giving them **89.8% of the combined voting power** (FY2026 proxy). Symbotic is a controlled company; outside Class A holders cannot change the board or block transactions.
- **Tax receivable agreement (TRA).** Symbotic pays legacy holders 85% of the cash tax savings created when they exchange units. Expected future TRA payments were **$484.4M** at March 28, 2026 (Q2 FY26 10-Q). This is debt-like and belongs in EV. It grows as more units are exchanged.
- **Supply overhang.** Every exchange converts unlisted V shares into tradeable Class A. Class V-1 fell from 74.7M (Sep 2025) to 71.4M (Jun 2026) through exchanges, and the December 2025 offering included 3.5M shares sold by existing holders alongside 6.5M primary shares at $55.
- **Dilution.** Total economic shares rose from 590.9M (Sep 27, 2025) to 604.9M (Aug 3, 2026), +2.4% in ten months, from the December 2025 primary offering and stock compensation. Stock-based compensation was $183.9M in FY2025 (8.2% of revenue) and $142.9M in 9M FY26.

Two further adjustments push the multiples higher:

1. **Cash is mostly customer money.** $1.74B of deferred revenue (Q3 FY26) must be worked off by building systems. At a ~25% adjusted gross margin, delivering it will consume roughly $1.3B of cash, leaving ~$0.45B of free cash. On that basis EV is ~$26.0B, or 9.3x FY26E revenue.
2. **FY26 earnings include non-cash gains.** 9M FY26 net income of $77.8M includes a $19M unrealized gain on the Nyobolt stake and ~$21M of gains on GreenBox (Exol) warrants. Consolidated P/E on FY26E is roughly 240x; MarketBeat shows ~490x on trailing Class A EPS.

## What They Sell and Who Buys

**Products:**

- **Symbotic System (core).** A dense, multi-level steel storage structure in which autonomous mobile robots ("SymBots") travel at up to ~25 mph, store and retrieve cases, and feed robotic arms that build mixed-SKU pallets sequenced for each store's aisles. AI software orchestrates inventory placement and bot traffic. It replaces conventional racking, forklifts, and manual pallet building in regional distribution centers (RDCs).
- **BreakPack.** Each-level (single-item) handling inside the same system. It is now deploying in about half of Walmart's RDCs (Q3 FY26 call).
- **Storefront / Accelerated Pickup and Delivery (APD) and SymMicro.** Micro-fulfillment systems placed at the back of stores for online orders, built from Walmart's Advanced Systems and Robotics business, which Symbotic acquired in January 2025. The first SymMicro prototype is under installation at a Walmart store.
- **Software and services.** Software subscriptions on operating systems, plus "operation services" (on-site staff, spare parts, maintenance). Recent tuck-ins add dock automation (Box Robotics) and maintenance analytics software (ARMS Innovations).
- **GreenBox (now operating as Exol).** A warehouse-as-a-service JV, 65% SoftBank / 35% Symbotic, that owns Symbotic systems in multi-tenant facilities and rents capacity to shippers. The Atlanta site is live; Lathrop, CA is due within 60–90 days of the August 2026 call.

**Customers:**

- **Walmart:** 85% of FY2025 revenue and 87.0% of 9M FY26 revenue. Under a 2022 master agreement, Symbotic is automating all 42 Walmart U.S. RDCs on a schedule running into the 2030s. The January 2025 APD agreement commits Walmart to buy systems for 400 APDs if performance criteria are met. Walmart is funding a $520M development program, and Symbotic estimated the deal could add more than $5B to backlog.
- **GreenBox:** committed to buy at least $7.5B of systems over six years.
- **Others named in filings:** Target, Albertsons, C&S Wholesale Grocers (privately owned by founder Rick Cohen, a related party), and Southern Glazer's Wine & Spirits, which signed for a second facility in Q3 FY26.
- **Geography:** almost entirely U.S. (and Canada through GreenBox and Southern Glazer's). There is no material international revenue yet.

**Core need:** large grocery and general-merchandise distributors face rising warehouse labor costs, chronic labor shortages, and more SKU proliferation and store-specific order complexity. A Symbotic system stores more product in the same footprint, runs around the clock, and ships pallets that are faster to unload in store. Buyers are paying for lower cost per case and higher service levels over a 15–20+ year asset life.

## How They Make Money

A hybrid model: **project revenue** recognized over time as each system is built, followed by **recurring revenue** once it is operating.

| Segment | FY2024 | FY2025 | % of FY25 | 9M FY26 | YoY trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Systems | $1,705.4M | $2,118.9M | 94.3% | $1,895.7M | +20% YoY in Q3 FY26 |
| Software maintenance & support | $14.2M | $29.6M | 1.3% | $36.6M | +57% YoY in Q3 FY26 |
| Operation services | $68.6M | $98.5M | 4.4% | $95.0M | +49% YoY in Q3 FY26 |
| **Total** | **$1,788.2M** | **$2,246.9M** | 100% | **$2,027.3M** | **+22% YoY in Q3 FY26** |

Sources: Q4 FY25 and Q3 FY26 earnings releases (8-K Ex. 99.1).

- **Systems revenue** is recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis as a system is installed, typically over 18–24+ months. Customers pay milestone-based prepayments, which is why deferred revenue is so large.
- **Software** is a per-site subscription that starts when a system goes live. With 56 systems operational and 77 in deployment (Q3 FY26), software is the cleanest indicator of the future recurring base, but it is still under 2% of revenue.
- **Operation services** covers staffing and parts for customers that outsource operation of their system to Symbotic.

## Revenue Quality

**Predictability: high in the near term, low in the mix.**

- **Backlog of $22.5B** (Q3 FY26), of which management expects ~15% (~$3.4B) to convert in the next 12 months. That covers next year's revenue well before the year starts.
- **Backlog has been flat for three years:** $23.3B (FY2023), $22.4B (FY2024), $22.5B (FY2025), $22.5B (Q3 FY26). Bookings have roughly matched revenue burn. Growth in revenue has come from converting an existing order book faster, not from a growing order book. The next leg of growth depends on new bookings (APD/storefront, GreenBox sites, non-Walmart customers).
- **Recurring revenue is small:** software plus operation services were 5.7% of FY2025 revenue and 6.5% of 9M FY26. The installed base is young, so the recurring stream will compound only as the 77 in-deployment systems go live.

**Diversification: very low.**

- Walmart was 87.0% of 9M FY26 revenue, up from 85.1% a year earlier. Concentration is rising.
- The 10-K says Walmart and GreenBox make up "the vast majority" of backlog. GreenBox is a JV that Symbotic co-owns and has funded ($90.5M in FY2024 and $129.9M in FY2025 of JV contributions, per cash-flow statements). Part of the non-Walmart backlog is therefore demand that Symbotic itself helps finance.
- C&S Wholesale Grocers is owned by Symbotic's founder, which makes it a related-party customer.

**Accounting quality is a live question.** In November 2024 Symbotic restated Q1–Q3 FY2024 after finding it had recognized systems revenue too early. It delayed its FY2024 10-K, disclosed material weaknesses in internal control, received an SEC subpoena, and faces a consolidated securities class action (D. Mass., filed December 2024; still pending). Percentage-of-completion accounting on multi-year projects relies on cost-to-complete estimates. That is where the earlier error occurred, and it remains the main judgment area.

## Cost Structure

| Line | FY2025 | % of revenue | 9M FY26 | % of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost of revenue | $1,824.3M | 81.2% | $1,583.0M | 78.1% |
| GAAP gross profit | $422.6M | 18.8% | $444.3M | 21.9% |
| Adjusted gross profit (ex-SBC etc.) | $472.1M | 21.0% | — | 24.3% |
| R&D | $216.0M | 9.6% | $138.1M | 6.8% |
| SG&A | $298.7M | 13.3% | $258.0M | 12.7% |
| Operating income (loss) | $(115.0)M | (5.1)% | ~$48M | ~2.4% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $147.5M | 6.6% | $239.9M | 11.8% |
| Stock-based compensation | $183.9M | 8.2% | $142.9M | 7.0% |

Sources: Q4 FY25 and Q3 FY26 releases; 9M FY26 operating income is derived from gross profit less R&D and SG&A.

- **COGS dominates:** steel structure, SymBots (motors, batteries, LiDAR), robotic arms, and the field labor that installs them. LiDAR unit cost has fallen below $500 from ~$5,000 four years ago (Q3 FY26 call). Lower component costs and the next-generation storage structure, expected to inflect in H2 FY2027, are the main gross-margin levers.
- **Margin trajectory:** GAAP gross margin went from 13.7% (FY2024) to 18.8% (FY2025) to 22.3% (Q3 FY26), with adjusted gross margin at 25.0% in Q3 FY26. Management guided that margins will "stabilize" rather than keep expanding in Q4.
- **Operating leverage is real but recent.** R&D was flat in dollars while revenue grew, and R&D fell from 9.6% to 6.8% of revenue. Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 6.6% (FY2025) to 13.2% (Q3 FY26). Q4 guidance of $100–105M adjusted EBITDA on $760–780M revenue implies ~13.3%.
- **SBC is large relative to profits.** At ~$190M a year it exceeds GAAP operating income and is more than half of adjusted EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA excludes it.

**Scalability:** better than a typical construction-style integrator, because the robots and software are standardized and each new system reuses the same design. But every system is still a large field installation with steel and labor, so gross margin is structurally capped well below software levels. A mature run-rate of 25–30% gross and mid-teens EBITDA margins is a reasonable ceiling under the current product mix.

## Capital Intensity

- **Low fixed capex:** $79.0M in FY2025 (3.5% of revenue) and $62.8M in 9M FY26 (3.1%), covering property, equipment, and capitalized software. Customers own the systems.
- **Negative working capital, funded by customers.** Deferred revenue rose from $805.5M (FY2024) to $1,367.2M (FY2025) to $1,736.6M (Q3 FY26). This is the main source of cash.
- **FY2025 free cash flow was $787.9M, but $606.4M of it (77%) came from the increase in contract liabilities.** Excluding that, underlying FCF was ~$180M.
- **The reversal is visible:** in Q3 FY26, deferred revenue fell $124M and receivables rose $156M, and FCF was **−$164.6M**. 9M FY26 FCF was $242.8M. Management said the Q3 dip was timing.
- **Other capital uses:** acquisitions ($141.8M net cash paid in FY2025, mainly Walmart's ASR business) and GreenBox contributions ($129.9M in FY2025). These sit below the FCF line but are recurring strategic uses of cash.
- **Equity funding:** the December 2025 offering raised ~$425M net for general corporate purposes, even though the company already held $1.2B of cash. That suggests management does not treat the deferred-revenue-funded cash as freely spendable.
- **No financial debt.** The TRA ($484.4M expected payments) is the only debt-like obligation.

**Cash conversion verdict:** reported cash conversion is flattered by prepayments and will swing negative whenever bookings slow relative to installations. Judge it over multi-year windows, not single years. From FY2024 through 9M FY26, cumulative operating cash flow was $1.11B against cumulative adjusted EBITDA of $0.45B. The gap is almost entirely customer prepayments.

## Growth Drivers

| Driver | Type | Evidence | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walmart RDC rollout (42 sites) and BreakPack retrofits | Structural, contracted | BreakPack in ~50% of RDCs | Largest share of the $22.5B backlog |
| Walmart APD / storefront micro-fulfillment (400 sites) | Structural, conditional | Prototype under install; second ~6 months later | >$5B potential backlog (company estimate), contingent on performance criteria |
| GreenBox warehouse-as-a-service | Structural, JV-financed | Atlanta live; Lathrop next | ≥$7.5B commitment over six years |
| Non-Walmart customers (Target, Albertsons, C&S, Southern Glazer's) | Structural | Southern Glazer's second site | Small today |
| Next-generation storage structure and component cost (LiDAR) | Margin | H2 FY2027 inflection per management | +several points of gross margin |
| Recurring software and services on the installed base | Structural | 56 operational systems; software +57% YoY | <2% of revenue today |
| New categories: perishables, dock automation | Optional | Perishables prototyping within six months | Not sized |

**Structural vs cyclical:** labor scarcity, wage inflation, and e-commerce order complexity are long-run tailwinds for warehouse automation. The overall warehouse automation market is estimated at roughly $27–34B in 2026, growing at ~14–19% a year (Research and Markets, Mordor Intelligence). Symbotic's timing is cyclical in a narrower sense: new orders depend on large retailers' capex budgets and confidence in the technology. A retail capex pause would show up first as flat or falling backlog.

**Industry outlook:**

- Large incumbents (Dematic/KION, Daifuku, Vanderlande/Toyota, SSI Schaefer, Swisslog/KUKA, Knapp, Witron, Honeywell Intelligrated) dominate conventional automated storage and conveyor systems.
- Cube-storage and goods-to-person disruptors (AutoStore, Ocado, Exotec, Geek+, Hai Robotics) win smaller and retrofit projects with faster deployment.
- Symbotic's niche, full-case storage and mixed-pallet building at RDC scale, has few direct substitutes. Witron is the closest technical comparable and is privately held.

## Competitive Edge

**Sources of advantage:**

1. **Integrated system, proven at scale.** Symbotic designs the bots, structure, arms, and software as one system. Fifty-six systems are operating in live RDCs. That operating record and Walmart's commitment to automate its entire RDC network are hard references for a new entrant to match.
2. **Switching costs once installed.** A system is a 15–20+ year fixture built into the building. Software, spare parts, and service tie the customer to Symbotic for the system's life, and the 2037 horizon of the Walmart program locks in the anchor customer.
3. **Data and software.** Every operating site generates telemetry used to tune bot routing and maintenance (the ARMS Innovations acquisition targets this). It is a plausible but unproven advantage.
4. **Strategic alignment with Walmart and SoftBank.** Walmart sold its own robotics unit to Symbotic and funds development; SoftBank co-owns GreenBox. These relationships create privileged demand.

**Evidence on durability, mixed:**

- **For:** gross margin has risen 8+ points in two years as the design matured; the Walmart program keeps expanding (RDCs, then BreakPack, then APDs); bookings have replaced revenue burn for three years.
- **Against:** returns are not yet visible in the numbers. GAAP operating margin was negative through FY2025, and invested capital is negative because customers prepay, so ROIC is not meaningful. Gross margin in the low-to-mid 20s is in line with large integrators, not a premium that would show pricing power. With 87% of revenue from one customer, the moat may protect Walmart's leverage over Symbotic as much as Symbotic's position in the market. The real test is winning large non-Walmart, non-JV customers at similar or better margins, and that has not happened at scale.

**Moat assessment:** narrow and customer-specific today. Symbotic has a defensible technical position in RDC case automation and very high switching costs at Walmart. A broad moat would require proof in the form of a second Walmart-sized customer, rising recurring revenue, and gross margins sustainably above 25%.
