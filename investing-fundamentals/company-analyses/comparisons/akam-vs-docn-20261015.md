# Leased AI Compute: $AKAM vs. $DOCN

**Prices as of October 15, 2026 (intraday):** AKAM $90.65, DOCN $104.29. Closes that day were $92.32 and $109.47. On October 16 both fell further in the neocloud sell-off (DOCN ~$97.38, AKAM ~$88.06 intraday); see [[docn-3-investment-memo]]. Both peaked in May 2026 (AKAM $165.45, DOCN $187.50) and both are down about 45% from there.
**Latest reported quarter:** June 2026. **Next reports:** DOCN November 4, AKAM November 5.
**Built from** [[akam-3-investment-memo]] and [[docn-3-investment-memo]]. Every figure below is from those pages or their source reports. Ratios marked "this page" are new arithmetic on those figures.

## Bottom line

Both companies are building AI compute capacity ahead of revenue. They differ in what has already been secured and in what the price asks for.

* **More de-risked at today's price: Akamai.** Its new demand is under contract: $11.6B from Anthropic over seven years against ~$5.5B of capex, so signed revenue is about 2.1x the money it must spend. The stock trades at ~13x earnings, roughly the price of a no-growth security and CDN business. What remains is counterparty, return and funding risk, and the price already discounts much of it. DigitalOcean's new capacity is mostly *not* under contract: RPO of $894M against ~$3.8B of data-center and equipment leases. It has to win the demand after building the space.
* **Higher expectations in the price: DigitalOcean, by a wide margin.** 76x 2026 EPS guidance and ~34x Morgan Stanley's 2028 EPS, against ~13x for Akamai. DigitalOcean's price needs 2027 revenue growth of about 45% (the DOCN memo's base case, ~$97). Akamai's price assumes roughly no earnings growth through 2027.
* **Safer or more diversified: it depends on which risk you mean.**
  * *Customers:* DigitalOcean is more diversified. Its largest customer is about 8.5% of revenue and the top 25 are about 20%. At Akamai, ~90% of recent compute bookings come from Anthropic. By 2028 Anthropic could be ~30% of Akamai's total revenue (this page, base case).
  * *Business lines and cash engine:* Akamai is more diversified. Security ($604M a quarter, +10%) has a real moat and pays for the bet. Compute is 9% of revenue today. At DigitalOcean, AI is already 21% of ARR and the core cloud grows ~11%.
  * *Price downside:* Akamai is safer. On each memo's scenarios, Akamai's bear case is -34% (deep bear -55%) and DigitalOcean's is -53% (deep bear -76%).
  * *Balance sheet:* mixed. Akamai has more debt in dollars (~$3.0B net) and negative free cash flow through 2027, so it must borrow ~$2-3B more. DigitalOcean has little debt but has committed lease spending of ~3.2x its 2026 revenue, and it funds itself partly by issuing shares (diluted count up ~20% in a year).

**Overall:** Akamai is the better risk-reward at these prices: weighted value ~$107 (+18%) with downside to ~$60. DigitalOcean's weighted value ~$103 is about its price, with downside to ~$49. Akamai's risk is concentrated on one customer, and that should limit the position size. Owning both does not diversify much: they fell together on October 14 (AKAM -8%, DOCN -14.8%) with no company news for either. Both moved with CoreWeave after its bond scare that day.

**What each price assumes:**
* **AKAM:** at ~13x earnings with flat Street EPS through 2027, the market prices Akamai as a legacy business that does not grow and carries a balance-sheet problem. It gives little credit for the $14.4B of signed compute contracts. The Street's EPS barely rises in 2027 because depreciation and colocation costs of the new capacity hit before revenue starts in 2H 2027.
* **DOCN:** the price assumes the AI pivot works. Morgan Stanley's *bear* case still has 2028 revenue of ~$2.2B, about 37% a year compounded from 2026, and today's EV equals that bear-case value. The DOCN memo's bear case lets 2027 growth fall to +30%, and that alone takes the value to ~$49.

## 2. How much of the new demand is secured

| | AKAM | DOCN |
|---|---|---|
| New spending committed | ~$5.5B of capex for the September Anthropic deal ($1.7B Q4 2026, $3.1B 2027, $0.7B 2028) | $2.76B of data-center leases not yet started (11.2-year average) plus a $725M-$1.025B MUFG equipment facility: ~$3.8B |
| Committed spending vs. company size (this page) | ~1.3x FY2025 revenue; ~42% of market cap | ~3.2x 2026 revenue; ~31% of market cap |
| Contracted revenue behind it | $11.6B Anthropic (seven years); $14.4B signed compute commitments in total | RPO $894M ($366M due within 12 months) |
| Contracted revenue ÷ committed spending (this page) | ~2.1x (Anthropic deal alone) | ~0.24x |
| Contract form | Take-or-pay once capacity is delivered | Mostly usage-based; large AI customers on ~3.7-year terms |
| Who carries the demand risk | The customer, if it pays | DigitalOcean: it must fill ~155 MW it has already leased |

The last ratio compares two different things. DigitalOcean's revenue was never built on long contracts, so its RPO understates what its ~$891M core cloud will bring in. The direction still holds. Akamai built after signing; DigitalOcean is signing leases ahead of customers.

**The catch on Akamai's contracts:** contracted revenue does not mean good returns. Morgan Stanley's ~11% deal IRR falls to ~5.5-7.9% with a one-year delay, 15% lower revenue, or 8 points of extra cost, all at or below Morningstar's 7.6% cost of capital. Akamai's main risk is earning too little on the capacity. DigitalOcean's main risk is that the capacity sits partly empty.

## 3. Concentration and diversification

| | AKAM | DOCN |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue mix | Security 55% (+10%), Delivery 36% (-6%), Compute 9% (+39%), Q2 2026 | Core cloud ~$891M ARR (+11%), AI ~$234M ARR (+212%); AI is 21% of ARR |
| Customer base | Large regulated enterprises; 20-30% of internet traffic passes through Akamai (Morningstar) | 680,000+ customers, mostly developers, startups, AI-native firms |
| Largest customer exposure | ~90% of recent compute bookings and ~$13.3B of contracts from Anthropic; ~30% of 2028 base-case revenue (this page) | Top 25 ~20% of revenue (from 9%); one customer ~8.5%+ |
| Moat | Real in security; none proven in compute (Morningstar: no moat overall) | Switching costs in core cloud; AI edge is the software stack, but open-weight models are 88% of tokens, the most price-competitive kind of inference |
| What pays for the buildout | Security and delivery cash flow (OCF 36% of revenue) plus new debt | Core cloud cash flow plus equity raises and equipment leases |

How the 2028 Anthropic share was estimated: the AKAM base case has $2.2B of compute revenue out of $6.42B total. With ~90% of compute from Anthropic, that is ~$2.0B, or ~31%.

## 4. Balance sheet and funding

| | AKAM | DOCN |
|---|---|---|
| Cash (June 30, 2026) | $4.62B | $767M |
| Debt | $7.64B of converts (2027-2033 maturities, 0-1.125% coupons) | $312.3M of 2026 converts due Dec 1, 2026 (cash); $153.2M of 2030 converts (likely to convert into ~3.9M shares) |
| Net debt (ex-leases) | ~$3.0B | net cash ex-leases; pro forma net leverage ~0.7x |
| Lease liabilities | $1.78B operating | $577.7M finance-lease and equipment liabilities, plus $2.76B of leases not yet started |
| Free cash flow | Negative: -$2.70B (2026), -$2.29B (2027) per Morningstar; breakeven 2028 | Positive: adjusted FCF margin guided 11-13%; owner FCF after lease principal ~$116M (2027), ~$265M (2028) |
| Funding route | New debt of ~$2-3B by end-2027, including the $1.15B notes due Sept 1, 2027; buybacks paused; equity ruled out for now | Two equity raises in 2026 ($888M at $77; ~$1.47B at $117.54); equipment leases via MUFG |
| Dilution | Up to 7.7M warrant shares for Anthropic; buyback tailwind gone | Diluted shares up ~20% in a year |
| Net debt / EBITDA | ~5x in 2026-27 (Morningstar) | ~0.7x pro forma |

Akamai's leverage is visible and large in dollars, but it sits on top of a $4B+ revenue business with $4.6B of cash and low-coupon converts. DigitalOcean's leverage hides in leases: rent of ~$246M a year once the sites start, plus ~$209M a year of equipment principal at full MUFG use, against owner FCF of ~$116-265M. On a reported net-debt basis DigitalOcean looks safer. On fixed obligations relative to the size of the business, Akamai looks safer.

Both memos use the same method: 2028 EPS times a scenario multiple. Akamai's multiples (10-17x) are low because it is priced as a mature business. DigitalOcean's (30-45x) assume it stays a growth stock, so a growth miss compounds: lower earnings *and* a lower multiple.

## 6. What to watch

* **November 4 (DOCN Q3):** revenue vs. $304-307M; gross margin vs. MS's 51.4% trough; RPO; first MUFG draw.
* **November 5 (AKAM Q3):** compute growth vs. the ≥50% full-year guide; capex vs. $475-525M; RPO including the $11.6B.
* **December 1, 2026:** DOCN repays $312.3M of converts in cash.
* **February 2027:** both give FY2027 guidance. For DOCN the test is ~50% growth. For AKAM it is EPS vs. consensus $6.71 and the financing plan.
* **2H 2027:** Anthropic revenue starts at Akamai ($150-300M expected in 2027). DigitalOcean's leased sites come online through 2027.
* **September 1, 2027:** AKAM's $1.15B notes mature.

**Shared risk:** both depend on AI inference demand staying ahead of supply into 2028. If GPU supply catches up, Akamai faces re-marketing pressure if Anthropic steps back, and DigitalOcean faces open-model price competition. Morgan Stanley covers both with the same analyst and is Overweight on both. See [[ai-compute-commencement-wall-and-refinancing-trap]] for how capacity built ahead of revenue tends to break.
