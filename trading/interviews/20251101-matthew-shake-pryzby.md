## Themes & Fundamental Analysis

Pryzby explicitly connects his approach to William J. O'Neil's CAN SLIM investment principles, specifically the "N," which stands for New Product, New Management, or New Highs. To Pryzby, this "N" translates directly to theme.
- Theme-Driven Market: He notes that the current market is highly theme driven, suggesting there is often "no reason to trade any other sector besides what's hot".
- Examples of Hot Themes: He cites several examples of themes he has traded, including:
  - AI Healthcare
  - 3D printing stocks (early in his career)
  - Quantum Computing
  - Rare Earth (such as MP)
  - Uranium (trading names like Olo and LEU)

Incorporating themes is less about finding new information and more about gaining confirmation and confidence in existing technical setups.
- Loading Setups: When a hot theme aligns with his technical criteria, it gives him the confidence to "load setups" or put on bigger risk

Case Studies:
- TEM (Tempest): He described this as an "A++ setup" partly because it had the powerful theme of AI healthcare (and other political narratives he noted, such as being a "Nancy Pelosi stock").
- MP (MP Materials): The trade thesis was supported by the hot thematic trade in rare earth combined with positive factors like government involvement ("Trump on the team") and the stock retesting support levels (retest entry).
- RDDT (Reddit): After its initial post-IPO run, the stock became relevant to the AI theme once the market learned it was using language learning models and scraping data, which caused its next explosive move upon earnings.

Pryzby maintains a constant vigilance over the market to identify emerging narratives and thematic strength:
- Theme Tracker (Deep View): He notes that modern tools like the Deep View theme tracker make obtaining this information significantly easier and more effective than in the past.
- Sector Strength and Biggest Gainers: He studies the biggest market gainers and uses screeners (like FinViz) to identify names showing strong technical performance (e.g., up 10% on the week). When he sees multiple names in the same field appearing, he recognizes that a theme and sector strength is emerging.
- Sector Patterns: He dives deeply into sector-specific charts (like the IGV for software or URRA for uranium) because sector patterns provide valuable insights. For instance, he bought Olo and LEU partly because the URRA (uranium ETF) chart was showing signs of "powering up" in consolidation.
- Fundamental Information: While he is a technical trader, he acknowledges that fundamental aspects (estimates and outlook) matter. For example, when assessing shorting opportunities based on earnings gap-downs (like Chewy), the outlook and guidance (future estimates) are the "most important stuff"

He is a "huge" believer in Stan Weinstein's Stage Analysis.
- The thematic narrative often dictates where the biggest moves are likely to occur - i.e. where he looks for Stage 2 breakouts (the phase where prices rise). In essence, themes act as the high-powered fuel for his highly mechanized trading engine.
- The specific technical entry tactics dictate how to engage the move with maximum size and minimum risk.
- Buying Every Flag in Stage 2: If a stock or sector is in an established Stage 2 breakout phase, he seeks to buy every small technical "flag" (consolidation) that sets up within it.
- China Example: When the Chinese internet ETF (KWEB and MCHI) showed signs of transitioning into a Stage 2 breakout phase, he became "hungry for China risk" and sought out individual stocks (like BU and VIPS) that made sense within that newly energized sector narrative.

## The Six Categories of Strategy

Mathew 'Shake' Pryzby defines his overall trading strategy as comprising "sixish broad categories," which he refers to as layers of probability. These layers are combined with his core edge—manipulating small time frames to secure "perfect entries" on setups derived from weekly charts.

Based on his description, these are the categories that define his trading strategy:
1. Top-Down Charting Approach: This approach prioritizes analyzing longer time frames first, meaning the weekly or monthly charts are considered most important, followed by the daily chart, and then the 30-minute chart.
2. Fundamental Factors / Thematic Factors: These are key components of his "layers of probability". Pryzby believes themes are crucial and leverages them to gain confirmation and confidence in his technical setups.
3. Time Frame Continuity: This involves ensuring things "line up" across multiple charts, such as the weekly chart, daily chart, and 30-minute chart.
4. Stage Analysis: He specifically mentions Stan Weinstein's Stage Analysis as a component of his strategy.
5. Supply and Demand / Compression and Expansion: This is a core mechanism he studies, referring to the market action that creates "pressure cooker" situations.
6. Inflection Points: These points are also listed as a component of his overall strategy.

Additionally, his entire system is guided by a focus on Risk/Reward, where he only enters a trade if he anticipates being able to make at least five times his risk (5R)

**[46:35]** Now, the risk with Friday swings is that you're holding over the weekend. So if there's bad news over the weekend, you can't get out. So I keep my position sizes smaller on Friday swings. Maybe instead of my normal $1,000 risk, I'll only risk $500 or $750.

**[46:53]** But the reward can be great. Because if the stock does become the hot chart of the weekend, if everyone is talking about it on Twitter on Sunday, it's going to gap up on Monday. And I'm already in. I can sell into that gap up and lock in a quick gain.

**[49:43]** The key is to not blow up during the drawdown. Don't start taking bigger risks to try to get back to breakeven quickly. Don't abandon your system. Just keep following your rules, keep taking your setups, and wait for the market to come back to you.
