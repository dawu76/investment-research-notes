# Michael Every: Economic Statecraft Changed Everything | The Julia La Roche Show [Michael Every]

Detailed overview of the interview featuring Michael Every, Global Strategist at Rabobank, on *The Julia La Roche Show*, published on July 7, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Michael Every argues that the old globalized economic architecture has broken down, replaced by a world driven entirely by "economic statecraft" and national security. In this new paradigm, traditional central bank models are obsolete, inflation is structurally higher due to geopolitical supply shocks, and investment returns will be dictated by government priorities rather than free-market optimization. Every warns that conflict in the Middle East and Strait of Hormuz will remain a permanent feature, forcing a fragmentation of global trade.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. Neoliberalism to Economic Statecraft
Every asserts that everything from monetary policy to supply chains is now a subset of national security objectives. The pursuit of easy investment returns and unhindered free markets is ending, as states intervene to secure critical resources and manufacturing capabilities.
*   **Geopolitics as Core:** Geopolitical frictions are no longer temporary "externalities" but the primary driver of policy and economic structure.
*   **National Objectives:** Western nations are moving back toward mercantilist strategies to counter China and secure industrial supply chains.

> "Everything is now about geopolitics... using the state alongside everything else around it as an instrument to achieve national security interests."
> — *Michael Every*

#### 2. The Obsolescence of Central Bank Models
Traditional central banking relies on managing demand through a single interest rate, which fails in an environment of permanent, geopolitically-driven supply shocks.
*   **Equilibrium Model Failures:** Raising rates hurts necessary defense and infrastructure investments, while lowering rates fuels unproductive speculation.
*   **Differential Interest Rates:** Every predicts a shift toward "differential interest rates," where strategic national security sectors (defense, semiconductor fabrication) receive cheap capital while speculative sectors are penalized.

> "The models don't work. They never did... we are seeing a series of rolling exogenous supply shocks in commodity after commodity and they're all driven by geopolitics."
> — *Michael Every*

#### 3. Strait of Hormuz and Global Trade Friction
Every expects conflicts, particularly involving Iran, to escalate post-US midterms. He views the disruption of key maritime choke points like Hormuz as a permanent feature that will keep risk premiums high and force the restructuring of global energy supply chains.
*   **Permanent Risk Premium:** The physical risk to maritime routes means inflation will structurally remain higher as companies build redundant, more expensive logistics paths.

> "A choke point can choke... it will always be seen as a liability and so the price will always be higher because people will always be looking for alternatives."
> — *Michael Every*

#### 4. The Public-Private Security Grid
*   **Defense Spending:** Avoid blindly buying defense stocks expecting endless profits. Governments will restrict margins and force reinvestment to ensure affordable munitions.
*   **Technology & AI:** The state will treat AI as a national security asset, potentially capping profits or taking stakes in firms that develop vital tools.

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items
*   **Fixed Income:** Bearish long-duration bonds due to massive government deficit spending on reshoring, defense, and domestic subsidies.
*   **AI Infrastructure:** Focus on AI applied to physical production and automation rather than software apps.
*   **Commodities:** Long energy and materials, as supply chain fragmentation and chokepoints keep risk premiums elevated.
