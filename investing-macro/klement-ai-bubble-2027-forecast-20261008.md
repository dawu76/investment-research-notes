# Klement: AI Bubble Bursts in 2027–28, S&P 500 to 5,000

Joachim Klement, a strategist at Panmure Liberum (a UK broker), told Bloomberg on October 8, 2026 that the AI-led equity trade will break down in 2027 or 2028. His year-end 2027 targets are the most bearish Bloomberg tracks.

> "My core conviction is that the AI bubble will either burst in 2027 or in 2028, so sometime in the next two years."

## The forecast

| Index | Klement end-2027 target | Implied move | Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 | 5,000 | ~−36% from ~7,720–7,810 | Seven other strategists tracked by Bloomberg average ~+14% |
| Stoxx 600 | 430 | more than −30% | — |
| FTSE 100 | 8,260 | — | Reported in one secondary source only |

He does **not** advise selling now. The forecast is a base case to plan around, not a timing call.

## The argument

1. **Hyperscaler free cash flow is "largely depleted."** Data-center capex could exceed $713B in 2026, more than double the prior year. When capex outgrows operating cash flow, each extra dollar of build has to be borrowed.
2. **Debt costs are rising toward "prohibitive."** Stubborn inflation keeps borrowing costs up. That turns a cash-funded build into a credit-funded one at a worse price.
3. **The revenue needed is enormous.** One summary cites a $2–5T increase in annual revenue across the five largest spenders to justify the build. Treat this figure as unconfirmed; it does not appear in the primary quotes.
4. **Breadth is weak.** Fewer than half of S&P 500 members are above their 200-day moving average while the index is near highs.

## The playbook

- **Trigger:** the S&P 500 falling below its 200-day moving average.
- **Response:** "go full defensive" in food, tobacco, and pharmaceuticals (ultra-defensive sectors), not just less tech.
- **Meanwhile:** contingency plans and timing tools instead of pre-emptive selling.

## How it fits the wiki

- **Continuity with Klement's earlier work.** In July, Klement and Francisca Reis estimated that the US cyclically adjusted P/E, corrected for the "earnings bubble," would be **67.6x, 4.6 standard deviations above trend** ^[reading-list/notes/market-newsletter-digest-2026-07-07.md]. The October call puts a date and a target on that valuation view.
- **Timing matches [[ai-compute-commencement-wall-and-refinancing-trap]].** That framework puts the take-or-pay payment shock in 2027–2028 as clusters energize. Its "Path 2" base case embeds a ~35% derate, close to Klement's −36%.
- **Where they differ:** the framework expects losses to land on leveraged neoclouds, GPU-backed debt, and private credit, with hyperscaler risk showing up as multiple compression rather than insolvency. Klement puts financing stress on the hyperscalers themselves. The framework's main caveat also applies here: Nvidia-financed demand could push the reckoning to 2029, which would make a 2027 date early.
- **Financing angle:** overlaps with [[ax-brij-khurana-wellington-ai-capex-crowding-out-bonds-20261001]] on AI capex crowding out the bond market, and with [[rr-this-is-how-the-ai-bubble-actually-pops-20260724]] on the hyperscaler capex prisoner's dilemma.
- **Other bubble calls:** [[tm-greatest-stock-and-earnings-bubble-in-us-history-20260712]] (Fred Hickey) argues the earnings side is inflated too. Valuation background is in [[valuations]].

## Weaknesses in the call

- **Two-year index targets are low-accuracy** for any strategist, and he sits far outside the range of his peers. The value here is the mechanism, not the 5,000 number.
- **"Depleted FCF" is a capex-cycle observation, not a solvency one.** The hyperscalers still have large operating cash flow and access to investment-grade debt. The question is the return on new capital, which shows up first in multiples.
- **The trigger is reactive.** A 200-day moving-average rule cuts the tail but gives up the first leg of any decline and whipsaws in choppy markets.
