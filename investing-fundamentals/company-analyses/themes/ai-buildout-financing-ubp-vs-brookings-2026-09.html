Financing the AI Buildout: what UBP and Brookings found

Two papers with the same title came out eight days apart. One is a bank's bond-market note, the other an academic model. They agree on most of the facts and answer different questions. This page puts both side by side, along with the checks done on their numbers.

UBP Filipe Alves da Silva, Union Bancaire Privée, Sept 16, 2026. 27-page fixed-income note, data mostly from CreditSights, Morgan Stanley, JPM, Bloomberg.
Brookings Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh, Columbia Business School. Brookings Papers on Economic Activity conference draft (dated Sept 4, presented Sept 24-25, 2026). 28 pages, not yet peer reviewed.
Wiki marks checks and estimates made in these notes, not claims made by either paper.

The two papers in one minute

Same subject, different seat at the table. UBP is a bondholder asking who loses money first. Brookings is an economist asking whether the spending can earn a normal return at all.

UBP

"Do I get par back, and who loses first if not?"

  1. The five hyperscalers (MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META, ORCL) now spend more than they generate: ~$820B capex vs ~$750B operating cash flow in 2026; $1.0-1.3T capex vs ~$900B in 2027.
  2. Balance sheets are not the constraint. The four AA-or-better names can add ~$400B of debt in 18 months without a downgrade, ~$600B before falling to single-A. The constraint is the price bond buyers charge.
  3. The risk sits in the order book: $2.3T of cloud backlog, ~40% owed by OpenAI and Anthropic, two private companies.
  4. The bear case has a date on it: signed leases and take-or-pay contracts start billing in 2027-28 whatever lab revenue is by then, while capex growth slows (+111% in 2026, +45% in 2027, +11% in 2028).
Brookings

"Does the spending make economic sense?"

  1. This is a physical capital cycle. A 1GW AI campus costs about $41B. A 183GW US buildout by 2032 costs $10.3T, averaging 3.63% of GDP a year, bigger relative to the economy than canals, railroads, electrification, highways or telecom.
  2. 2026 is the first year hyperscaler capex ($800.5B) exceeds their operating cash flow ($707.1B), pushing funding into leases, joint ventures, project debt, private credit and special-purpose vehicles.
  3. That expands funding capacity but raises asset-level leverage and hides obligations, as Meta's Hyperion/Beignet deal shows.
  4. To earn a 10% return at a 50% margin, the buildout needs $3.725T of annual revenue by 2032, about 80% a year growth for OpenAI + Anthropic from ~$100B today.
  5. No bust call: "premature to conclude that AI infrastructure already poses systemic risk." Main ask: better measurement and disclosure.

Where they agree

Working from different data, the two papers land on the same facts. That makes these the most reliable numbers on the page.

Same case study: Meta's Hyperion / Beignet

Both papers use this Meta and Blue Owl joint venture as the template for how the buildout is being financed off balance sheet.

  • 2.0GW campus in Louisiana, ~$30B total investment. Meta sold an 80% equity stake to Blue Owl for ~$2.5B. Brookings
  • Beignet raised $27B of debt (Oct 2025), the largest single IG corporate bond deal in US history. UBP has it at $27.3B, priced at Treasuries + 225bp. Both
  • Rated A+, one notch below Meta itself. Both
  • ~90% debt-to-assets; debt-service coverage only 1.12x. Brookings
  • Priced at 6.58%, over 100bp above what Meta's own unsecured debt would likely cost, meaning more than $5B of extra interest over the deal's life. Brookings
  • Copy already done: Sopaipilla (Meta/BlackRock, El Paso, 960MW). Brookings: $12.3B A+ bonds, July 2026. UBP: $12.6B at T+288. Both

Same view on where losses land

Neither paper thinks the AA-rated parents are the problem. Both put the fragility in project vehicles, single-tenant structures and chip-collateral debt, where the parents' low reported leverage hides much higher leverage at the asset level.

Both also reach for the railway mania and the telecom/fiber bust as historical parallels (see History).

In plain terms: Microsoft or Alphabet defaulting is not the risk. The risk is the special-purpose company that borrowed 90% of a data center's cost against one tenant's lease, and gets no rent if the building is late.

Takeaways for investors

For shareholders the risk is margins, not default. Lab payment shortfalls will likely show up as "renegotiation dressed as partnership": deferrals called rephasing, and sub-leased capacity hitting a market sold as scarce.
Equity and credit are one trade. ~35% of 2026 funding was equity, and the group holds $600B+ of stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX it could sell. If stocks de-rate, bonds must fill a bigger gap just as appetite is weakest. Six of these names are ~17% of MSCI ACWI, so most portfolios already own that equity.
Oracle is the pivot credit. It is the one name where a lab renegotiation reaches the rating. A downgrade to high yield would force selling by holders with index caps.
OpenAI exposure is mostly indirect. It arrives through ORCL, CoreWeave and project paper for sites built for it. Anthropic's deals put an IG balance sheet between lender and lab (Alphabet guarantees Fluidstack leases, Broadcom backstop). OpenAI's do not.
The cleanest demand signal. Microsoft's backlog grew 84% y/y, but only 25% excluding OpenAI, close to its historical rate. That separates organic demand from lab commitments.
Take-or-pay is really a loan book. Rating agencies have treated take-or-pay as debt in pipelines, shipping and power for 30 years, not yet for compute. If they start, the labs' ~$1.25T of commitments against ~$105B of combined ARR (about 12x) looks like leverage. The notes rate this the report's strongest idea.

Findings only in Brookings

Data center REITs stopped diversifying. Their beta to the S&P 500 rose from ~0.5 to ~1 since 2018 (36-month rolling). Their exposure to non-AI stocks is now comparable to their exposure to AI stocks. They no longer work as a defensive "picks and shovels" holding.
NVIDIA is backstopping its own customers. A $500B credit facility (Aug 2026) from Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR funds NVIDIA chip purchases. NVIDIA guarantees 25% of residual value, up to $125B. The notes compare it to Cisco/Lucent/Nortel vendor financing in 2000-01. Google/Broadcom have a similar TPU arrangement.
$10.3T understates lifetime cost. It excludes future GPU refreshes. 2025-26 chips likely need replacing around 2030 while the buildings and power stay usable.
Funding mix. Morgan Stanley estimate cited by the paper: $2.9T of incremental compute needs 2025-2028, ~60/40 equity/debt. Within debt: ~$800B private credit, ~$200B corporate bonds, ~$150B structured finance.
Lease figures differ by source. Moody's: ~$970B of hyperscaler lease commitments, ~$660B not on balance sheet. That doesn't match UBP's $1.16T because there's no settled definition yet, which is the paper's point about the measurement gap.
Estimates keep rising. McKinsey's April 2025 "$7 trillion race" estimate is about 30% below Brookings' $10.3T made about 18 months later.

Takeaways for investors

  1. One bar you can track. Lab revenue needs to compound at ~80% a year to 2032. Check it every quarter against reported ARR.
  2. The Beignet spread is a price you can watch. The 100bp+ premium over Meta's own debt is what it costs to move an asset off balance sheet. Watch whether it widens or narrows as more copies price.
  3. Reported leverage is the wrong measure. Track the ratio of off- to on-balance-sheet obligations (3-4x now) quarter over quarter as a gauge of how fast risk is leaving GAAP balance sheets.
  4. GPU useful life drives the answer. Going from 6 to 3 years takes required revenue from 9.2% to 14.8% of GDP. Changes in depreciation schedules or GPU resale values tell you more than headline capex growth.
  5. The paper's restraint is itself information. The most rigorous source available, with no commercial stake, declines to call systemic risk. Weigh that against louder bearish newsletter calls without comparable numbers.

The three warning signs

Brookfield studied every sector default cycle above 10% since 1997 (telecom 1998-2004, homebuilders 2006-11, energy 2014-17). Each time: a capex ramp, growth in B- and lower issuance, and negative free cash flow, with defaults spiking about three years later.

  • Capex ramp: here.
  • Negative FCF: arrived in 2026 at Meta, Oracle, Amazon (partly) and Alphabet (one quarter), and deeply at labs and neoclouds.
  • Low-rated issuance: growing (AI ~4.5% of HY, CoreWeave rated B), but much of it sits in private credit outside the index.

Three years from 2026 is 2029. That lines up with UBP's 2027-28 contract start dates and Burry's 2028-29 write-off window. Wiki

How this cycle differs

  • The central borrowers are AA/AAA with large non-AI cash flows. WorldCom and Global Crossing were not.
  • Capacity is in use today (old GPU rental rates are rising). Fiber was mostly dark.
  • The risk has moved into structures (SPVs, guarantees, take-or-pay) that indices and rating agencies don't yet fully count.

For scale: AI capex is ~2.6% of US GDP on one year of hyperscaler spend (wiki estimate), well below the UK railway peak of ~7%.

Fact checks

The notes recomputed each paper's totals and checked claims against other sources in the wiki. Brookings reconciled cleanly. UBP mostly did, and its problems all point the same way: slightly too relaxed about Oracle and guarantees.

UBP Arithmetic

  • ChecksOff-balance-sheet total: 1,164 + 1,569 + 41 + 75 = $2,849B, vs $853B on balance sheet = 3.3x, matching "more than three times."
  • ChecksBacklog: 496 + 684 + 520 + 638 = $2,338B.
  • ChecksLab share: OpenAI $688B + Anthropic $330B = ~$1.02T, ~44% of backlog. Some announced commitments (e.g. MSFT's $250B) may not be booked yet, so the true share could be lower.
  • ChecksInterest isn't the constraint: $600B at ~5% ≈ $30B a year vs ~$790B EBITDA, under 4%.
  • Doesn'tCumulative capex. Page 4 says $6.5T through 2030; page 24 says $5.6T for 2026-30. UBP's own growth path gives ~$1.19T in 2027 and ~$1.32T in 2028, so $5.6T requires capex to fall to ~$1.1T a year in 2029-30, which the report never says. $6.5T implies a re-acceleration to ~$1.6T. Probably a digit transposition, but the path is unexplained either way.
  • OverstatedOracle's index-cap buffer. UBP says Oracle is ~$60B of issuance from the 1.5% cap for low-IG names. Oracle's ~$130B of debt at a 1.17% weight implies a ~$11T index, so the 0.33pp gap is ~$35-40B. Forced selling may be closer than UBP implies. Wiki calc

UBP Against other data

  • AgreesOff-balance-sheet figures match Burry's per company (MSFT >$300B vs UBP $329B; Meta ~$700B vs $737B; Alphabet ~$900B vs $977B; Amazon ~$267B vs $285B). Same filings, so this confirms the math, not the interpretation.
  • UndercountGuarantees: UBP counts $41B + $75B + $29B (Broadcom) because others don't disclose. The FT puts Big Tech residual value guarantees at up to $300B in under a year. The "most dangerous" category is the least measured.
  • Borne outOracle stress arrived after publication: on Sept 24 Oracle sent a force majeure notice on the 2.45GW Project Jupiter site; ~$18B of Jupiter loans traded at 89-91 cents and Oracle CDS hit a record.
  • Higher?OpenAI burn: UBP uses >$200B through 2029. Leaked plans show a revision from $180B to $278B through 2030. Different horizons, but revisions are going up.
  • SlippingAnthropic IPO: UBP watches for an October listing. A second delay has been reported and the IPO window is narrowing.
  • AdjustedAnthropic profit is operating income excluding stock comp in Q2, tracking to GAAP profit in Q3. The $65B ARR is a July exit rate; Q2 revenue annualizes to ~$46B.
  • SupportedPower as the binding constraint: ~$130B of US data center projects blocked or delayed by local opposition this year; $374B+ of construction in progress. Delays help lab cash flow but hurt single-site vehicles that get no rent until completion.
  • OpenChip residual values: A100 rates rose, H100s re-contracted at ~90%, Oracle re-lets 4-year-old GPUs at a 20% premium. But SemiAnalysis says no one will price residual value on 4-6 year old GB300s today, and UBP admits its bullish data "was gathered amid a shortage."

UBP Method

  • Backlog-to-lease coverage (1.5x-4.5x) looks safer than it is. It covers building leases only. Chips and servers are ~60% of site cost and aren't counted, nor are power and operating costs. Oracle's 2.0x coverage sits next to -$24B FCF.
  • "20bp on a 5.5% coupon changes very little" holds for the issuer, not the holder. On a 2063 bond with ~15 years of duration, 20bp of widening is a ~3% price loss, with the 10-year yield at its highest close since 2007.
  • Purchase commitments ($1.57T) overlap with capex forecasts. Adding both double counts, which is why UBP ranks them least alarming.

Brookings Method

  • Return and margin are held constant to 2032. Falling GPU prices, a volume response, or margin squeeze from competition could move the $5.5-7.9/GPU-hour range either way.
  • $10.3T and $3.7T are the central case, not a stress case. How much of the pipeline gets built is a judgment call.
  • The model is unlevered. Real projects run ~90% debt (Beignet), so equity outcomes swing far more with revenue shortfalls than a 10% unlevered hurdle suggests.
  • Hardware refresh is excluded, so lifetime investment is higher than $10.3T.
  • Status: a conference draft, not peer reviewed, and sensitive to its own build-out and GPU-life assumptions, which it states openly.
Two inconsistencies in the source notes themselves

Found while building this page. Wiki calc

  • "~80% a year for ~8 years." The UBP note's comparison section says growth must be sustained ~8 years. Going from ~$100B to $3.725T is ~37x. That works out to ~83% a year over 6 years (2026-2032) but only ~57% a year over 8 years; 80% for 8 years would reach ~$11T. The Brookings note's wording, "about 80% a year through 2032," is the consistent one, and this page uses it.
  • Pipeline realization rate. The Brookings note quotes a "44.5%" realization rate. 182.7GW of a 509GW pipeline is ~36%, and the three scenario buckets (182.7 + 117.2 + 226.9 = 526.8GW) don't sum to 509GW either. This page shows the three GW figures and leaves out the percentage. Worth checking against the PDF.

Glossary

Hyperscaler
The largest cloud operators. Here: Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle.
Neocloud
A newer GPU-rental company (e.g. CoreWeave), usually far more leveraged and dependent on a few customers.
Backlog / RPO
Remaining performance obligations: contracted revenue not yet earned.
Take-or-pay
A contract where the customer pays for reserved capacity whether or not it uses it.
Off-balance-sheet
Obligations (signed leases not yet started, purchase commitments, guarantees) that don't yet appear as debt in reported accounts.
Residual value guarantee
A promise to cover the shortfall if an asset (building, chips) is worth less than an agreed amount at the end of a contract.
SPV / joint venture
A separate company set up to own one project and borrow against it, keeping the debt off the sponsor's books.
IG / HY
Investment grade (BBB- and above) vs high yield or junk (below BBB-). Many funds must sell bonds cut to HY.
New-issue concession
The extra yield a new bond offers over the issuer's existing bonds to attract buyers. Rising concessions mean buyers are getting fuller.
Lease-adjusted leverage
Debt plus lease obligations divided by earnings (EBITDA).
Debt-service coverage
Cash available divided by debt payments due. 1.12x means only 12% of cushion.
Unlevered return
The return on the whole project before any borrowing. Borrowing magnifies gains and losses to equity.
Beta
How much an asset moves with the overall market. 1 means it moves in step.
T+225, SOFR+450
Yield expressed as basis points (hundredths of a percent) over Treasuries or the SOFR floating rate.
