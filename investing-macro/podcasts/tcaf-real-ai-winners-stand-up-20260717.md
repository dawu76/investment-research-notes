# You're About to See the Real AI Winners Stand Up | The Compound and Friends [TCAF 251]

Detailed overview of the interview featuring Jonathan Thomas, CEO of American Century Investments, on *The Compound and Friends* (Episode 251), published on July 17, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Jonathan Thomas discusses the structural shift in the asset management industry, detailing the explosive growth of active ETFs, which have expanded from $50B in 2018 to nearly **$2 trillion** and **11% market share** today. He details the success of American Century's systematic brand, Avantis Investors, which surpassed **$150 billion in AUM** in just 6.5 years using transparent, factor-based portfolios. On markets, Thomas highlights the transition of the AI cycle from the *creators* and *enablers* to the *adopters* who will generate real economic value, noting that legacy growth giants are facing multiple contraction despite rising earnings.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Active ETF Revolution & SEC Rule 6c-11
Thomas outlines a massive structural shift from mutual funds (now in net outflow territory) to active ETFs, which represent a major industry transition.
*   **Asset Flows:** Active ETFs have grown from a tiny $50B niche in 2018 (representing 1–2% of the ETF market) to nearly **$2 trillion in AUM** and **11% market share**. Year-to-date, **40% of all ETF flows** have went into active ETFs.
*   **Rule 6c-11 (The ETF Rule):** Implemented in 2019, this SEC rule eliminated the cumbersome case-by-case exemptive relief process for active ETFs, standardizing creation/redemption rules and drastically reducing time-to-market.
*   **Failure of Semi-Transparency:** Early attempts at "semi-transparent" or "non-transparent" active ETFs failed because managers feared front-running. The market demanded, and rewarded, full transparency.

> "When we entered the space in 2018 with American Century ETFs, there was $50 billion in it. Today there's it's approaching $2 trillion and 11% market share... 40% of the year-to-date ETF flows have gone into active ETFs."
> — *Jonathan Thomas*

#### 2. The AI Transition: Creators/Enablers to Adopters
Thomas argues that the initial phase of the AI bull market—dominated by AI creators (semiconductors, cloud infrastructure, hyperscalers)—is maturing.
*   **The Handoff:** Concentration in the Magnificent 7 is giving way to a broadening market where the real winners and losers will be determined by the *adopters* deploying AI tools to drive efficiency and displace high vendor costs.
*   **Nvidia Multiple Compression:** Nvidia's stock price has stopped reacting positively to upgrades and guidance beats, leading to multiple contraction. The market has fully digested the initial capex hardware thesis.

> "The creators and the enablers of AI were just soaring and now what's starting to happen is the adopters are starting to receive the benefit from it... the winners and losers will be determined by who adopt it and who's deploying it correctly."
> — *Jonathan Thomas*

#### 3. Capex Bubbles: Pets.com vs. Chewy Metaphor
Drawing parallels to the 2000 Dot-com bubble, Thomas notes that capex bubbles often occur because the foundational concept is right but the timing is premature.
*   **The Internet Playbook:** Pets.com was widely mocked as the poster child of the internet bubble, yet today Chewy utilizes the exact same business model successfully. The internet took 20 years to mature, peaking in 2020.
*   **AI Capex:** While AI has structural legs, the current massive capex spend faces a digestion phase before the true enterprise winners emerge.

> "Chewy is doing very well... and it's the same exact concept of Pets.com. Its timing was just better. And the full realization of the internet came. So when I look at this chart, I think the concept of an earnings bubble... is driven by the adopters."
> — *Jonathan Thomas*

#### 4. The Stowers Institute Philanthropic Engine
A key differentiator for American Century Investments is its unique ownership structure, which acts as a powerful client retention tool.
*   **Funding Medical Research:** Jim Stowers Jr. founded the firm in 1958. Today, the majority owner is a trust that directs much of the firm's profits to the **Stowers Institute for Medical Research** in Kansas City.
*   **Foundational Science:** The funds support pure-play, non-pharma research in molecular biology, protein dynamics, and evolutionary genetics.

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items
*   **Allocate to AI Adopters:** Rotate equity exposure away from over-concentrated AI enablers (pure hardware) and allocate to business service adopters utilizing AI to expand margins.
*   **Overweight Transparent Active ETFs:** Utilize transparent, factor-based ETFs (Avantis) over traditional active mutual funds to capture fee and tax-loss harvesting efficiencies.
*   **Identify Denominator Value in Compressed Growth:** Look for high-margin giants experiencing multiple compression despite growing earnings, rather than chasing stocks with high multiple expansion.
*   **Monitor Active ETF Flows:** Track the active-to-passive flow ratio. The continued shift of active share to ETFs will pressure traditional mutual fund issuers.
