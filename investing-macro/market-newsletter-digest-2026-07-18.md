# Market and Investment Newsletter Digest — July 18, 2026

## 1. AI Economics: Moonshot's Kimi K3, Model Price Wars, and the RPO-Private Credit Loop

### Themes & Key Observations
- **Moonshot's Kimi K3 Release**: Moonshot AI launched **Kimi K3**, a massive **2.8-trillion-parameter MoE** model (activating 16 of 896 experts per token) with a 1M token context window. On published benchmarks, it outperforms Anthropic's Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Sol, though it is generally behind GPT-5.6 Sol and Fable 5. The model weights are scheduled to be published on **July 27, 2026**.
- **Model Pricing Wars**: Blended pricing for Kimi K3 is **$5.40 per million tokens** ($3 input / $15 output), comparable to Anthropic's Claude Sonnet. Combined with Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 and SpaceX's Grok 4.5 launches within the past 8 days, frontier model prices are falling at the worst moment for frontier labs. Gavin Baker highlights that declining model-layer margins transfer economics to every other layer (semiconductors, software, power, and neoclouds). Furthermore, because of its 2.8T parameters, Kimi K3 requires high-memory server architectures (GB300 NVL72 and B300) that will drive *more* hardware capex.
- **The RPO and Private Credit Loop**: TSCS Research highlights extreme customer concentration in hyperscalers' backlogs. Microsoft CFO Amy Hood disclosed that OpenAI represents **45%** ($281 billion) of Microsoft's **$625 billion** commercial remaining performance obligations (RPOs). Similarly, Anthropic represents **40%** ($200 billion) of Google Cloud's backlog and over $100 billion of Amazon's. This massive loop is funded by private credit (e.g., Blackstone and Apollo financing Anthropic's $35B compute facility).
- **Jim Chanos's Dot-Com Warning**: Short seller Jim Chanos warns that the AI bubble is "much worse" than the dot-com bubble. Hyperscaler incremental return on invested capital (ROIC) collapsed from **40% to 20%** in 18 months and is heading to **10%** in a year (Oracle is the worst). He flags accounting tricks: "unplugged GPUs" are left in construction-in-progress (CIP), escaping depreciation charges for up to 18 months. Downstream "compute renters" are retreating: neocloud Nebius shifted to an asset-light model, while CoreWeave is reportedly shopping hedges on its GPU asset values.

### Key Data & Quotes
- *"A world where there are only 2-3 dominant frontier labs with 90% inference margins is net negative... Anything that lowers margins and increases competition at the model layer is good for every other AI layer."* — Gavin Baker, Atreides Management
- *"Amy Hood told analysts that OpenAI accounts for approximately 45% of Microsoft’s $625 billion of commercial remaining performance obligations."* — TSCS Research
- *"No one that is dependent upon NVIDIA to exist should trade at a higher valuation than NVIDIA itself. And yet, many, many companies do."* — Jim Chanos, Risk Reversal

## 2. Macro Policy & Geopolitics: Dalio's Bubble Gauge, Warsh's Trilemma, and the Hormuz Oil Shock

### Themes & Key Observations
- **Market Correction & Volatility Unwind**: Nasdaq gapped down below its 50 SMA (falling 2.22% on the week), and the semiconductor index (SOX) entered correction territory. However, this is a violent momentum rotation rather than a broad liquidation (369 S&P 500 stocks rose on Thursday while only 132 fell). George Noble notes that crowded momentum trades, zero-DTE options, and trend-following CTAs are magnifying the unwind.
- **Ray Dalio's Bubble Gauge**: Ray Dalio puts his proprietary bubble gauge at **75%** of the extremes hit in 2000 and 1929. He notes that the bubble pops when paper wealth needs to be converted into cash, typically triggered by Fed tightening or a wealth tax.
- **Kevin Warsh's Congressional Testimony**: Warsh's testimony highlighted Luke Gromen's "trilemma" (hike and trigger Treasury liquidations, cut and reaccelerate inflation, hold and interest compounding eats the budget). Warsh's July 15 testimony defended the June hold (3.50%–3.75%).
- **Strait of Hormuz Escalation**: Brent crude traded above $85/bbl as US strikes targeted Iranian military sites at Chabahar, and Iran struck back at Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, and Qatar. Arjun Murti notes that this crisis "obliterates peak oil demand" arguments, as EV/LNG trucking growth in developing markets (like China) helps sustain economic GDP and structural oil demand.

### Key Data & Quotes
- *"The pricking the bubble typically is the creating the need for the cash... typical thing is tightening monetary policy."* — Ray Dalio, Bridgewater
- *"If the administration won’t let prices rise [above triple digits], then we’re left with the physical market to force it higher."* — Open Insights
- *"The SoH Crisis... is accelerating the structural demand case for alternative energy without denting the ultimate driver: GDP growth."* — Arjun Murti, Super-Spiked

## 3. Operations & Corporates: TSMC Capex, SpaceX Sinking, and Netflix Earnings Realignment

### Themes & Key Observations
- **TSMC Capex Hike & Advanced Packaging**: TSMC raised its 2026 capital expenditure by 15% to **US$60B–$64B** (its second upward revision this year) to support front-end wafer demand (detailed in [[semianalysis-research-2026-07]]). CEO C.C. Wei adopted a partner-friendly pricing stance ("We’d be very happy with 68% gross margin") and welcomed Intel's packaging capacity to ease CoWoS constraints. TSMC is expanding with 12 fabs on 507 hectares in Taiwan.
- **SpaceX Sinking & IPO Volatility**: SpaceX shares fell below their $135 IPO price, down about a third from their peak, as market volatility hit the defense/AI sector. This highlights the exit liquidity mechanics of small float listings (detailed in [[funding-short-squeeze]]).
- **Netflix Q2 Earnings Review**: Netflix fell 9.7% pre-market on a light Q3 revenue guide ($12.86B, +11.7% YoY) despite solid Q2 revenue ($12.56B, +13.4% YoY) and EPS ($0.80). Accrued Interest argues that this is a multiple correction rather than an estimates problem. Netflix trades at **18.7x 2026 EPS** (below the S&P 500), boasting a 43% ROE, lowest-in-industry churn (~2%), and a record $4.7B in buybacks.
