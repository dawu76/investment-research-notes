# Zscaler, Inc. (NASDAQ: ZS) — Equity Analyst Report

**Prepared for:** Long-Term Investors
**Data as of:** Q3 FY2026 (April 30, 2026); FY2025 10-K filed September 11, 2025
**Fiscal Year:** August 1 – July 31
**Revised:** May 28, 2026 — Q3 FY2026 earnings update

---

## Executive Summary

Zscaler is the dominant pure-play provider of cloud-native Zero Trust network security, operating a globally distributed platform — the **Zero Trust Exchange** — that secures internet and application access for enterprise users, devices, and workloads without traditional network perimeters. Revenue is almost entirely subscription-based SaaS, with ~80% gross margins and rapidly improving profitability: the company posted a GAAP net loss of only $41.5M in FY2025 on $2.67B in revenue, down sharply from $202M in losses in FY2023.

Revenue has compounded at roughly 37% annually over the past five years (FY2020–FY2025), decelerating to 23% in FY2025 and re-accelerating to 26% in Q1–Q2 FY2026. Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) reached $3.525B as of Q3 FY2026 (April 30, 2026; +25% YoY), with a net revenue retention rate of 115%. The company processes nearly 1 trillion AI transactions annually and over 500 billion total daily transactions through 160+ globally distributed data centers, generating a proprietary threat intelligence advantage that compounds with scale.

**Q3 FY2026 Update (May 26–27, 2026):** Q3 revenue $850.5M (+25% YoY, beat); non-GAAP EPS $1.08 (beat); RPO $6.5B (+30%); non-GAAP operating margin 23% (all-time high). However, two critical negative surprises: (1) FCF margin guidance cut to 22.8–23.3% (from 26.5–27%) due to CapEx running "in the high single digits as % of revenue" for AI infrastructure pull-forward; (2) FY2027 initial ARR/revenue growth guide of only 16–17%, far below Street's 20%+ expectation, attributable to two undisclosed senior sales leadership departures. Stock fell 31.3% on May 27 to ~$127 — its worst single-day decline ever. The core platform franchise remains healthy (NRR 115%, RPO +30%); the risk is the new-logo sales pipeline under disrupted leadership.

Key risks include intensifying competition from Palo Alto Networks and Microsoft, permanent CapEx model shift (from <3% to ~8–9% of revenue), sales leadership continuity, and the possibility that FY2027 16–17% growth reflects structural deceleration rather than conservative guidance. **In plain English: Zscaler sells the cybersecurity equivalent of a toll highway that replaces corporate firewalls — the platform remains essential infrastructure for cloud-first enterprises, but a sudden management disruption has raised legitimate questions about how fast the new-logo pipeline can refill.**

## What They Sell and Who Buys

### Products

Zscaler's platform is organized around the **Zero Trust Exchange**, a cloud-delivered security broker that sits between users (or workloads) and the internet/applications. Core offerings:

| Product | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **ZIA (Zscaler Internet Access)** | Secure web gateway, cloud firewall, CASB, DLP, ATP — secures outbound internet traffic |
| **ZPA (Zscaler Private Access)** | Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA) — replaces VPNs for private app access |
| **ZDX (Zscaler Digital Experience)** | End-to-end digital experience monitoring |
| **Zero Trust for Workloads** | Cloud-to-internet and app-to-app traffic security for cloud workloads |
| **AI-Powered Threat Intelligence** | Inline behavioral analytics, sandbox, deception, automated threat response |

Since FY2026, Zscaler also acquired **Red Canary** (MDR/SOC-as-a-service) and **SPLX** (AI security posture) for $692M combined, expanding into threat detection/response and AI security.

### Customers

- **Who:** Large enterprises and government agencies; as of FY2025, Zscaler secures nearly **40% of the Global 2000** and **>45% of the Fortune 500**
- **Size cohorts:** 9,400+ total customers; 3,494 customers with $100K+ ARR (FY2025); **728 customers with $1M+ ARR** (Q2 FY2026, up from 664 at FY2025 end — +10% in one quarter)
- **Geography:** Americas 57% of revenue, EMEA 28%, APJ 15% (Q2 FY2026); U.S. ~51%, international ~49% (FY2025 annual)
- **Verticals:** Financial services, healthcare, government (FedRAMP authorized, DoD IL4/IL5), manufacturing, technology

### Core Motivation

Enterprise IT security architecture is undergoing a structural shift away from hub-and-spoke networks (where traffic hairpins through a central data center firewall) toward direct-to-cloud architectures. Remote work, SaaS adoption, and cloud migration have made the perimeter-based model obsolete — and Zscaler's platform is purpose-built to replace it. Buying motivation is a combination of **security mandate**, **cost reduction** (consolidating point products), and **operational simplification**.

## How They Make Money

### Revenue Model

Zscaler operates an **annual/multi-year subscription SaaS model**. Customers purchase a platform subscription priced by the number of users or workloads, with tiered bundles (Business, Transformation, and Enterprise editions) that unlock additional modules. Professional services (implementation, training) are a small ancillary revenue stream.

| Revenue Type | Estimated Mix | Characteristics |
|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Subscription (ZIA, ZPA, ZDX, Workloads) | ~99% | Ratable recognition over contract term; typical 1–3 year contracts |
| Professional Services | ~1% | One-time; revenue recognized as delivered |

### Revenue Segments

Zscaler does not break out revenue by product line publicly. However, directional indicators suggest:
- **ZIA** remains the largest product by installed base and revenue share
- **ZPA** has been the fastest-growing segment in recent years as VPN replacement accelerates
- **Workload/Branch protection** is an emerging growth layer
- Revenue is diversifying within the platform through upsells (higher tiers, additional modules)

## Revenue Quality

### Predictability

Revenue quality is **very high**:
- ~99% subscription-based with ratable recognition over multi-year contracts
- **Deferred revenue** balance provides excellent forward visibility ($2,355.4M deferred revenue at Q2 FY2026, +25% YoY vs Q2 FY2025; RPO reached $6.1B, +31% YoY)
- **Net Revenue Retention (NRR) of 115%** indicates the installed base grows organically without new logos: existing customers expand spending by an average of 15% annually

### Calculated Billings & Deferred Revenue — Methodology and History

**Does Zscaler report calculated billings officially?**
Yes — historically, as an explicitly defined non-GAAP metric through FY2025 (fiscal year ending July 31, 2025). Zscaler's definition:
> *"Calculated billings = Revenue plus the change in deferred revenue in a period."*

Starting **Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended October 31, 2025), Zscaler stopped reporting calculated billings.** The company is transitioning to ARR as its primary top-line performance metric, citing billings volatility from contract duration mix shifts. This mirrors a broader trend (Rubrik never reported billings; CrowdStrike uses ARR) and reflects that quarterly billings can oscillate significantly — Q1 FY2025 printed only +13% YoY at a time when underlying ARR was healthy, creating unnecessary investor anxiety.

| Q2 FY2026 | $816M | +$4M | **~$820M** | **~+10% YoY** | Inferred (not reported) — see caveats below |

The +13% / +32% quarterly range within a single fiscal year illustrates precisely why management moved to ARR reporting — the quarterly signal has too much noise from enterprise renewal seasonality and multi-year contract timing.

**Why the Q2 FY2026 implied +10% deserves skepticism as a standalone signal:**

1. **Q2 is seasonally weak for billings.** Zscaler's enterprise renewals cluster heavily in Q3 (Feb–Apr) and Q4 (May–Jul). Q2 (Nov–Jan) consistently shows the smallest sequential deferred revenue build — Q2 FY2025 added only +$95M sequentially vs. larger Q4 builds. Q2 FY2026's +$4M sequential DR change is partially a seasonal artifact, not a sign of demand collapse.

2. **Contract duration mix shifts distort the quarterly DR signal.** Shorter 1-year contracts generate less deferred revenue than multi-year upfront deals, even at the same total contract value. The non-current DR declined sequentially from Q1 to Q2 FY2026 ($405M → $372M), consistent with shorter-duration billing in that quarter or existing multi-year contracts rolling into the current period. ARR (+25% YoY) and RPO (+31% YoY) both contradict any interpretation of demand weakness in Q2 FY2026.

### Concentration Risk

- No single customer accounts for more than 10% of revenue (disclosed in 10-K)
- Geographic concentration is moderate: ~51% U.S., partially mitigated by ~49% international
- Vertical mix across regulated industries (government, finance, healthcare) adds resilience but also creates procurement cycle complexity

### Land-and-Expand Dynamics

Zscaler's standard go-to-market motion involves landing with ZIA or ZPA and expanding into adjacent modules. Cohort analysis implies customers double or triple their spend over 3–5 years as they consolidate security vendors. The 664 customers at $1M+ ARR (vs. 3,494 at $100K+) demonstrates significant expansion potential within the installed base.

## Capital Intensity

### Asset Model

Zscaler is **capital-light by design**. The Zero Trust Exchange runs on colocated data centers (operating leases), not owned real estate or proprietary hardware. Cloud-native architecture avoids large recurring capex. The company holds no physical product inventory.

- **Capex:** Primarily servers and network equipment for PoP expansion; estimated ~3–4% of revenue
- **Working Capital:** Positive working capital dynamic — subscription customers pay upfront or quarterly, generating substantial **deferred revenue** (a liability that converts to recognized revenue over time)
- **Cash Position:** As of FY2025, Zscaler reported significant cash/investments sufficient to fund operations for 12+ months; the company has been FCF-positive on an annual basis for several years

### Cash Conversion

FCF margin of ~27% in FY2025 ($727M FCF on $2.67B revenue) reflects a strong conversion rate from revenue to cash. FCF has grown faster than revenue in recent years, confirming the operating leverage thesis. Q2 FY2026 FCF was $169.1M (+18% YoY) at a 21% margin — lower than Q1 due to billing timing — with full-year FY2026 FCF margin guidance of 26.5–27%.

## Growth Drivers

### 1. Zero Trust Secular Tailwind (Structural)

The shift from perimeter-based to identity/context-based network security is a multi-decade architectural transition. Regulatory mandates (e.g., U.S. Executive Order 14028 requiring federal agencies to adopt zero trust by 2024), increasing ransomware threats, and hybrid work are structural accelerants. The Zero Trust Security market was $41.7B in 2025 and is projected to reach $88.8B by 2030 (16.3% CAGR per Grand View Research).

### 2. Platform Consolidation (Volume + Pricing)

Enterprises are actively rationalizing their security vendor portfolios from dozens of point products to consolidated platforms. Zscaler's pitch — replace legacy firewalls, VPNs, web proxies, CASB, and DLP with one cloud-native platform — resonates with CISOs seeking simplification. This drives both new logo wins and rapid upsell within the installed base (evidenced by 115% NRR).

### 3. AI Security and Emerging Products (New Products)

The $692M acquisition of Red Canary (SOC/MDR) and SPLX (AI security posture) in Q1 FY2026 extends Zscaler's TAM into threat detection & response and AI workload security. Zscaler processed nearly **1 trillion AI transactions** in calendar 2025 and has detected 3,400+ unique enterprise AI applications. AI Protect generated eight-figure new logo wins (Fortune 500 semiconductor manufacturer) and seven-figure upsells in Q2 FY2026. Red Canary ARR guidance was raised to $130M (from $95M), though integration churn has been elevated — a normal dynamic for acquired MDR businesses.

Notable Q2 FY2026 emerging platform metrics: **Zero Trust Everywhere** reached 550 enterprise customers (up from 130 a year ago), delivering 2–3x ARR expansion per adoption; **ZDX Advanced Plus** surpassed $100M LTM bookings (+80% YoY); **ZFlex** (flexible consumption) generated $290M+ TCV in Q2, up 65% sequentially with typical eight-figure, four-year commitments.

### 4. Government and International Expansion (Geography)

Zscaler holds FedRAMP High, DoD IL4/IL5, and other government certifications that are difficult and time-consuming to obtain, creating a regulatory moat for U.S. federal business. International markets (49% of revenue) continue to expand as multinational enterprises standardize on a single global security platform.

## Competitive Edge

### Moat Analysis

| Moat Type | Strength | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Switching Costs** | High | Zscaler is integrated at the network layer; replacing it requires re-architecting security policy, reconfiguring all endpoints, and retraining security staff — a 12–24 month project |
| **Scale Advantage** | High | 300+ PoPs globally, 500B daily transactions, 14+ years of threat intelligence data — creates proprietary AI training sets competitors cannot replicate quickly |
| **Network Effects** | Moderate | More traffic = better threat intelligence = better security outcomes for all customers; Zscaler benchmarks show its models outperform on novel threats due to dataset size |
| **Regulatory Moat** | Moderate | FedRAMP High, DoD IL certifications take years to obtain; competitors must go through the same gauntlet to address U.S. federal market |
| **Brand / Trust** | Moderate | Dominant Gartner Magic Quadrant positioning (Leader in SSE 2024); trusted by 40% of Global 2000 creates social proof for enterprise procurement teams |

### Key Competitors

| Competitor | Platform | Differentiation vs. ZS |
|-----------|---------|------------------------|
| **Palo Alto Networks** (Prisma Access / SASE) | Integrated SASE + NGFW | Broader network security portfolio; bundling advantage; PANW offers discounts that pressure ZS deal sizes |
| **Cisco** (SSE / SASE) | Cisco+ Secure Connect | Installed base leverage; trusted legacy vendor; weaker cloud-native architecture |
| **Microsoft** (Global Secure Access / Entra) | SSE bundled with M365 E5 | "Good enough" free bundling for M365 customers; a pricing and consolidation threat at the low end |
| **Netskope** | Pure-play SSE | Cloud-native like ZS; private company with strong CASB focus |
| **CrowdStrike** (Falcon Data Protection) | SASE emerging | Strong endpoint brand; expanding into network security from endpoint |

### Durability Assessment

The moat is **durable but not impenetrable**. High switching costs and scale advantages protect the installed base, and 115% NRR confirms customers are not churning. The primary risk is competitive bundling (Palo Alto, Microsoft) pressuring the net new customer acquisition rate and deal values. Zscaler's pure-cloud architecture remains superior for cloud-first enterprises, but hybrid or on-premise-heavy organizations may prefer PANW's integrated hardware+cloud approach.

Financial evidence of moat durability: consistent 79–81% gross margins maintained across rapid scaling; NRR holding at 115%+; $1M+ ARR cohort growing faster than total customer count (implying increasing share within existing accounts).
