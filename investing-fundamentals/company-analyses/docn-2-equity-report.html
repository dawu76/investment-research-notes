Executive Summary

DigitalOcean rents cloud computing to developers, startups and smaller software companies: virtual servers ("Droplets"), managed Kubernetes, databases, storage and app hosting, and now GPU capacity and AI inference services. Revenue is usage-based and billed monthly, from over 680,000 customers. Until 2025 it was a steady business: FY2025 revenue of $901.4M grew 15%, with a 60% gross margin, 42% adjusted EBITDA margin and 19% adjusted free cash flow margin.

In 2026 it became an AI-capacity growth story. Revenue growth roughly doubled to 29% in Q2 (guided 30-31% for the year and 50%+ for 2027), AI customer ARR tripled to $234M, and remaining performance obligations rose 12x to $894M on its first nine-figure annual commitments. To serve that demand it has committed to ~155 MW of data-center capacity, $2.76B of not-yet-commenced data-center leases, and a $725M-$1.025B equipment-leasing facility, and it raised ~$888M of equity in March. The economics are shifting: gross margin fell from 59.9% to 55.0% year over year, free cash flow guidance was cut from 15-17% to 11-13% of revenue, and the top 25 customers went from 9% to 20% of revenue.

In plain English: a profitable developer cloud that is turning itself into a leveraged AI-inference landlord, and the stock is priced as if that works.

What They Sell and Who Buys

  • Core cloud (~79% of ARR): Droplets (virtual machines), App Platform, managed Kubernetes and databases, object storage (Spaces), networking, and Cloudways managed hosting. Buyers are developers, startups and "digital native enterprises" (DNE customers, spending over $500 a month): about 22,000 DNE customers producing 67% of Q2 2026 revenue, up from 59%. The need is simple, predictable cloud infrastructure without hyperscaler complexity or pricing.
  • AI (~21% of ARR, $234M AI customer ARR, +212%): GPU Droplets and bare-metal GPUs (from the 2023 Paperspace acquisition), and increasingly managed AI services: the Inference Engine (6,000+ customers since launch), serverless inference, and Managed Agents (public preview, October 2026). Management says 85% of AI customer ARR comes from non-bare-metal services and over 70% from inference, and about 70% of AI customers with $100K+ ARR also buy core cloud. Buyers are "AI-native" companies building products on open models that want cheaper, simpler inference than the hyperscalers or GPU-only neoclouds.
    • AI ARR mix shift (MS, company data): the non-bare-metal share of AI customer ARR went 43% (Q2 2025) → 68% → 70% → 81% → 85% (Q2 2026). Year over year in Q2 2026, inference services ARR grew 762%, AI customers' core-cloud ARR 158%, and bare-metal GPU ARR fell 20%. Incremental AI ARR was a record $64M in the quarter (+190%).
    • Open Intelligence Summit (October 13, 2026, via MS): token volume on the platform grew 50x from April to September, and open-weight models reached 88% of token usage, up from 27% in May. New products: Managed Agents and Agent Droplets (late September/early October; DigitalOcean runs dedicated microVMs, customers bring their own agent harness; >85 models supported), Agent Data Services (private preview), Agent Learning Services, and Signals and Simulations (public preview). MS: "monetization remains earlier innings."
  • Geography and footprint: global customer base; capacity being added in new U.S. data centers (Richmond and Kansas City launched in 2026; Memphis due 2H 2026), totaling about 155 MW committed, most online by end-2027.

How They Make Money

  • Model: usage-based subscription (pay-as-you-go, billed monthly), plus a growing layer of multi-year committed contracts with large AI customers. ARR was $1,125M at Q2 2026 (+29%).
  • Mix: core cloud ARR ~$891M (this report's arithmetic: total ARR less AI ARR), up ~11% from ~$800M a year earlier; AI customer ARR $234M (+212%). Revenue by customer size: $100K+ customers are 35% of Q2 revenue (+98% growth), $500K+ 26% (+160%) and $1M+ 23% (+214%).
  • 2026 guidance: revenue $1.170-1.180B (+30-31%), with Q4 growth of 35% or more; adjusted EBITDA margin 38.5-39.5%; adjusted FCF margin 11-13%; non-GAAP EPS $1.35-1.40 on ~122-123M diluted shares. Management is "even more confident" in 50%+ growth in 2027 but has not formally guided.

Revenue Quality

  • The legacy base is recurring but not contracted: most customers pay monthly with no commitment. Net dollar retention was 102% in Q2 2026 (a three-year high, up from 99% in Q4 2024), and management says it will stop highlighting NDR because growth now depends on its largest AI customers.
  • Contracted revenue is new and concentrated: RPO was $894M at June 30, 2026, versus $71M a year earlier and $134M at year-end 2025. $366M is expected within 12 months, and the weighted-average life is 3.7 years. The jump came from "multiple nine-figure annual customer commitments" with unnamed AI-native customers. One nine-figure annual commitment is at least ~8.5% of 2026 revenue (this report's arithmetic: $100M ÷ $1.175B).
  • Concentration is rising fast: the top 25 customers were about 20% of Q2 2026 revenue versus 9% a year earlier, and management expects that to "rise modestly" as large deals ramp. The 10-Q still says there is "no material customer concentration," and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue or receivables.
  • AI demand quality: management stresses that most AI revenue is managed inference rather than raw GPU rental. Inference revenue still depends on a small number of AI-native customers whose own funding and model choices can shift quickly. GPU list prices on several fleets rose ~30% in 2026, but management called the Q2 pricing effect "modest."

It is ahead of schedule on three 2026 sites (Richmond Q1, Kansas City Q2, Memphis 2H).
  • How MS models the cash (and what it leaves out): MS forecasts free cash flow of $141.3M (12.0%) in 2026, $325.0M (18.3%) in 2027 and $474.0M (19.6%) in 2028, on cash capex of only $182.2M, $273.2M and $371.4M. Growth Drivers

  1. AI inference and managed AI services (structural, concentration-heavy): AI ARR +212%, inference services up ~800% year over year, Inference Engine adoption by 6,000+ customers, Managed Agents launched. Larger multi-year commitments (nine-figure annual) are the main contributor to RPO.
  2. Capacity coming online (timing-driven): ~155 MW committed, most online by end-2027; Memphis in 2H 2026; 20 MW more for late 2027-2028. Management says a data center takes about a year from lease signing to revenue, so 2027 growth depends on sites already signed opening on time.
  3. Core cloud (structural, steady): core ARR growing ~11%, NDR at a three-year high (102%), and AI customers attaching core cloud services. This is the part of the business that funded the old model.
  4. Pricing: GPU list prices rose ~30% on several fleets in 2026, a cyclical tailwind while compute is scarce.

Competitive Edge

  • What protects the core: simplicity and price for developers and small businesses, a large self-serve customer base (680,000+), and switching costs that rise as customers use more managed services. Evidence: stable 60% gross margin and ~42% adjusted EBITDA margin through 2025, NDR recovering to 102%.
  • What protects AI: less clear. Management argues DigitalOcean sells a full stack (inference engine, agents, orchestration, core cloud) rather than rented GPUs, unlike neoclouds that "rely on GPU rentals" and inference providers "stacking margins on rented GPUs." That is plausible for small and mid-size AI companies. For large AI-native customers signing nine-figure commitments, the purchase decision is closer to price per unit of capacity, where CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda, the hyperscalers and Akamai (see akam-2-equity-report) compete with far larger balance sheets.
  • The sell-side view (MS): DigitalOcean's AI strategy "from day one" targeted agentic and inference workloads, and "platform breadth and depth provide support in a highly fragmented landscape." MS's own listed downside risks are competition, the AI supply/demand balance, and "higher capital intensity weighs on cash flow and margins." The open-weight shift (88% of tokens) cuts both ways: it is DigitalOcean's chosen market, and also the most price-competitive segment of inference, because any provider can serve the same open models.
  • Financial evidence of moat durability: mixed. Margins are falling as AI scales (gross 59.9% → 55.0%; adjusted operating 28% → 24%), returns on capital are modest (~8% ROIC), and the share count has risen ~20% in a year. Growth is strong and contracted RPO is real, but the returns on the new capacity are not yet visible.
