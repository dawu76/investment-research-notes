# There's Going To Be One Hell Of A Hangover When The Market Party Ends | Louis Gave

Detailed rundown and analysis of the *Thoughtful Money* interview featuring **Louis-Vincent Gave**, founding partner and CEO of **Gavekal Research**, hosted by Adam Taggart. The interview was recorded and released on June 28, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Louis Gave warns that global equity markets are "drunk on speculation," running a high risk of an AI capex hangover. Gave argues that the massive U.S. fiscal deficits (7% of GDP) and private AI capex make a deflationary bust highly unlikely, rendering government bonds useless. Instead, Gavenowcasts an "Inflationary Boom" characterized by value stocks, financials, and metals. He highlights the "Godzilla Trend"—the risk of Japan repatriating its $3.5 trillion in U.S. assets (10% of U.S. GDP) post-midterms—and suggests hedging this tail risk using cheap out-of-the-money Yen call options. Gave is structurally bullish on Latin America, Japanese financials, and select Chinese world-class industrial champions.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The McKinsey $6.7T Capex vs. $2T Revenue Mismatch
Gave argues that the AI capex cycle has reached a stage of speculative overbuilding. To justify the projected $6.7 trillion in global AI capex from now until 2030, the AI industry must generate roughly $2 trillion in annual revenue. Given that the entire global advertising industry (including all digital, print, and physical ads) totals only $1.0 trillion, the required revenue run-rate for AI is structurally unrealistic. Gave compares the current environment to the late-1990s telecom boom, where companies like Lucent, Nortel, and Global Crossing went bust supplying hardware for a broadband boom that was real, but took a decade to match its capex assumptions.

> "To justify this kind of capex... you need the AI industry to generate roughly two trillion a year of revenue... The global advertising industry is roughly one trillion a year... The AI capex basically accounted for more than three quarters of the increase in GDP growth... We are very clearly in a market that is massively driven by FOMO."
> — *Louis Gave*

#### 2. The Four-Quadrant Macro Framework: The Death of Bonds
Gave structures his portfolio asset allocation around four distinct macroeconomic quadrants, mapping growth and inflation dynamics:
1.  **Deflationary Boom:** Entrepreneurs produce more with less. Asset class: *Growth Stocks*.
2.  **Inflationary Boom:** Democracies promise more without paying. Asset class: *Value Stocks, Financials, and Metals*.
3.  **Inflationary Bust:** Energy supply shocks spike prices. Asset class: *Energy*.
4.  **Deflationary Bust:** Excessive capex overbuilds and banks go bust. Asset class: *Government Bonds*.

Gave argues that because the U.S. government is running a 7% budget deficit during an economic boom, a deflationary bust is mathematically impossible. Therefore, government bonds serve no hedging purpose and should be completely eliminated from portfolios.

> "The least likely remains the deflationary bust because of today's fiscal policies, because of today's monetary policies... That's why I've been a bear on government bonds... Bonds for an asset class have been dead for five years... I think bonds stay dead."
> — *Louis Gave*

#### 3. The New Portfolio Blueprint: The 60/20/20 Rule
With the traditional 60/40 portfolio dead due to the obsolescence of bonds, Gave proposes a new structural baseline for multi-asset investors: **60% equities, 20% energy, and 20% precious metals**. Energy and metals act as direct hedges against inflationary booms and busts, while equities capture the real growth in productivity. He notes that regulatory constraints keep pension and insurance funds trapped in government bonds, but independent capital should fully migrate to this hard-asset structure.

> "60% equity, 20% energy, 20% metals... Bonds for an asset class have been dead... What you now need is this... And then once you decide that, it's like, okay, which metal do I want right now? Which equities do I want right now? And how do I express the energy?"
> — *Louis Gave*

#### 4. The "Godzilla Trend" of Japanese Repatriation
Gave outlines the massive systemic risk of Japanese capital repatriation, which he terms the "Godzilla Trend." Japan possesses an 80% positive net international investment position, holding over $3.5 trillion in U.S. assets (equivalent to 10% of U.S. GDP). As Japanese inflation holds at 3.5% and JGB yields rise, JGB pension managers (such as the GPIF) will face pressure to bring capital home. Because other pension funds systematically copy the GPIF to avoid career risk, the repatriation flow will be sudden and massive, placing severe downward pressure on U.S. Treasuries, French OATs, and German Bunds. Gave expects Japan to trigger this post-midterms, when President Trump's lame-duck status minimizes political blowback.

> "Japan, unlike the United States, has a net foreign position that is absolutely enormous... 3.5 trillion... that's 10% of US GDP... When do the Japanese start repatriating capital? ... That'll be the next big leg down on bond markets... JGBs start to get interesting. Currency's been crushed, bonds have been crushed."
> — *Louis Gave*

#### 5. The DeepSeek Disruption: BYD vs. Ferrari Models of AI
Gave highlights the emergence of Chinese open-source AI models (such as DeepSeek) as a structural threat to the hardware-centric U.S. capex boom. The U.S. model operates like building a "Ferrari" ( frontier AGI models) and selling it for a cheap price to hook users. China's model, led by DeepSeek, focuses on software efficiency rather than compute size, building a "Toyota/BYD" model that delivers 95% of the performance of U.S. models at only 5% of the cost. Because most corporate enterprises only need basic, cost-efficient productivity tools, they will choose the 95% cheaper option, potentially causing hyperscalers to cut their U.S. hardware capex by 20–30% and bursting the U.S. semi bubble.

> "China's model... is to get to work all you need is a Toyota. Sure, Ferrari is great, but... most people don't need all those bells and whistles. So we're going to produce models that can do 95% of what the US models can do... for 5% of the cost... You're going to gravitate for the one that's 95% cheaper."
> — *Louis Gave*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Eliminate Long-Duration Government Bonds:** Sell long-duration Treasuries and Bunds. Bipartisan fiscal deficit policies remove the risk of deflationary busts, making bonds dead weight in a portfolio.
2.  **Migrate to a 60/20/20 Asset Structure:** Re-allocate portfolios to hold 60% equities (value/financial bias), 20% energy, and 20% precious metals to survive the inflationary boom.
3.  **Buy Cheap Out-of-the-Money Yen Calls (`JPY`):** Utilize historically low FX volatility to purchase JPY call options as cheap portfolio "fire insurance" to hedge the Godzilla repatriation and AI bust tail risks.
4.  **Allocate to Latin American Equities:** Overweight Latin American value assets (e.g., Colombia, Argentina, Brazil) to capitalize on rightward political shifts and under-allocated local pension funds.
5.  **Build Exposure in Japanese Financials:** Accumulate Japanese banks and financials, which will continue to outperform as JGB yield curves steepen and JPY capital repatriates.
