## Executive Summary

Michael Howell discusses the 65-month global liquidity cycle, explaining that liquidity growth peaked at the end of 2025 and is in a structural downswing projecting a trough in mid-to-late 2027. Howell argues that the real economy is crowding out financial asset liquidity, nominal GDP of 6–8% is pushing bond yields toward 6%, and the 2-year Treasury note yield breaking above SOFR signals imminent Fed rate hikes (mirroring the late-2021/2022 tightening cycle where the S&P fell 25% and Bitcoin dropped 75%). Howell details why China's domestic debt debasement anchors gold at 27,000 CNY / $4,000+ USD, why the historical 20x gold-to-oil extraction ratio implies $135–$200/bbl crude oil, and why investors must rotate from tech/growth into late-cycle commodities, energy, and cash. Patrick Ceresna and Massil Beguenane outline a tactical 3:1 reward-to-risk bull call spread on GLD, highlight uranium's 25% breakout in URA, and analyze the Black Sea wheat short squeeze setup.

### Warsh vs. The Markets & Fed Tightening

**Erik Townsend:** The title of your slide deck is *Warsh versus the Markets*. A president who is passionate about getting a Fed chair installed who's going to do nothing but cut, versus a guy who might be inclined to do something different. How should we think about Kevin Warsh's relationship with Donald Trump?

**Michael Howell:** It's going to be a pretty unhappy relationship if that's what Trump is expecting. Central banks don't control interest rates. The market controls interest rates, and it's the long end which drives the short end, not vice versa. Bond yields are rising globally (with the exception of China) because of strong nominal GDP growth. That is putting pressure on the front end of the term structure. To keep repo markets liquid after pushing so much funding into the front end means rapid monetary growth or expanding the Fed balance sheet—both of which are ruled out. Barring clever tricks from Scott Bessent funding via more bills, higher policy rates must come.

**Erik Townsend:** What should we expect here?

**Michael Howell:** Rates are going to have to go up. The 2-year Treasury note yield has broken above SOFR rates. I ran this data through AI, asking: "How accurate is the 2-year note as a predictor of policy rates?" The answer was that it is correct **85% of the time** on both the upside and downside.

We are treading a path that looks remarkably like late 2021 / early 2022. That was a period of considerable unhappiness for investors where the **S&P dropped 25% and Bitcoin dropped 75%**. Risk asset markets do not like central banks that are tightening.

### Collateral Mechanics & Asset Bubbles

**Erik Townsend:** When's the bubble going to pop?

**Michael Howell:** We're already seeing the asset bubble. Central bank easing triggered it, but it runs out of control because the financial system creates liquidity endogenously. After an initial liquidity shock, asset prices rise and collateral levels increase. We live in a financing system where collateral is the mainstay for all credit and loans: **something like 80% of all lending in the world economy is collateral-based**. When collateral values rise, borrowing ability expands, creating recurring liquidity booms and busts. Every previous bubble had a big expansion in liquidity as its root cause.

### China's Great Debasement & Gold

**Erik Townsend:** Has China's Great Debasement ended?

**Michael Howell:** China needs to expand liquidity to address its massive debt problem. If China keeps liquidity expansion internal, it devalues domestic debt—raising domestic prices and wages relative to nominal debt. But this comes at the cost of a currency debasement expressed in a much higher gold price. If they maintain the USD/CNY cross, gold in US dollar terms skyrockets.

China's historic debt is driving gold today, while the West's future debts (aging demographics, defense spending, debt-to-GDP expansion) will drive future Western debt monetization. Gold is underpinned at **$4,000/oz**. In Chinese yuan, the trend line at **27,000 CNY** held exactly where the gold price bounced off.

Furthermore, silver is a high-beta confirmation play. Over the last few trading days, silver has started clawing its way back and outperforming gold, signaling that sentiment is returning to precious metals.

### The Gold-to-Oil Ratio & $135–$200 Oil

**Erik Townsend:** Let's move to energy prices. Some argue that resolving the Strait of Hormuz crisis and UAE expanding output will crash oil. What's your view?

**Michael Howell:** Commodity markets have two moving parts: the currency of denomination (USD) and the real exchange ratio reflecting relative extraction costs.

The gold-oil ratio is a five- to six-year cycle that overlays the global liquidity cycle. In the upswing, rising liquidity boosts gold (monetary hedge); in the downswing, liquidity shifts into the real economy, boosting commodity demand and crude oil. The gold-oil ratio is strongly mean-reverting with long-term historical stability around **20 times**.

If gold is underpinned at **$4,000/oz**:
- Dividing $4,000 by the 20x historical ratio yields **$200/barrel oil**.
- Even at a conservative 30x ratio, oil equates to **$135/barrel**.

Rising oil prices combined with rising bond yields typically mark the ending feature of bull markets. Investors should scale back equity beta.

### Drivers of Bond Yields: Nominal GDP & 6.0% 10-Year Yields

**Michael Howell:** The 10-year US Treasury yield (risk-adjusted / minus term premium) closely tracks the 4-year moving average of Nominal GDP. US nominal GDP is accelerating in the **6% to 8% range**, driven by fiscal spending, AI CapEx, and de-globalization supply chain rebuilding. This pulls 10-year Treasury yields toward **6.0%**.

US Treasury bills currently stand at **22% of total federal debt** (above the 15–20% target). If Treasury increases T-bill issuance toward 30% (early 2000s levels), it constitutes direct monetization, debasing the dollar and driving gold higher.

---

### Japan & The Yen Warning

**Michael Howell:** In Japan, nominal GDP is over 4%, suggesting 10-year JGB yields should be closer to 4% than 3%. The Bank of Japan's yield suppression and reluctance to raise short-term rates drove USD/JPY from 164 down to 155, with 50% retracement. Monetizing government debt through commercial banks creates severe currency turmoil—a warning of what happens if the US resists rate hikes.

---

### Fed Policy Horizon

**Michael Howell:** My view is 100% that the next move is a rate hike. The SOFR minus 2-year yield spread mirrors the 2021–2022 tightening onset. Looking at Fed liquidity cycles from 1985–2025, we have at least **another six months of intensified Fed tightening** before any policy inflection.
