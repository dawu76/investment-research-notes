# Interactive Brokers Group, Inc. (IBKR) — Investment Memo

**Prepared:** March 30, 2026 | **Ticker:** NASDAQ: IBKR | **Price Reference:** ~$185–200 (post-4:1 split, approx. March 2026)
**Market Cap:** ~$83–90B | **FY2025 Revenue:** $6.21B | **FY2025 Net Income (common):** $984M
**Sector Comparison:** Capital Markets / Institutional-Grade Electronic Brokerage

---

## 1. Executive Summary

### Investment Thesis
Interactive Brokers is the most efficiently operated brokerage on earth, generating a 77% pre-tax margin on $6.2B in FY2025 revenues with only 3,182 employees. Its core edge — fully proprietary technology built over 48 years, access to 170+ exchanges globally, and a structural NII advantage from $160B in customer credit balances — has produced five consecutive years of 28–32% client account growth and record revenues. Added to the S&P 500 in August 2025, the company is transitioning from a niche professional broker to a mainstream institutional holding. At ~28–30x forward P/E, the market is paying a modest premium for what is arguably the best-run brokerage franchise globally. The primary risk — interest rate sensitivity on the NII segment — is real but offset by rapid balance sheet growth. The secondary risk — succession uncertainty around founder Thomas Peterffy (~80 years old, 74% voting control) — is existential but unknowable in timing.

### Bull Case
Client accounts reach 6–7M by FY2027. Client equity exceeds $1T. Asia-Pacific expansion continues at 30%+ annual account growth. Operating leverage means every additional $1B in net revenue flows ~$770M to pre-tax income. Stock deserves 30–35x premium P/E for execution consistency, regulatory positioning (no PFOF), and operating leverage.

### Bear Case
Global central banks cut rates aggressively to 1–2%, reducing annual NII by $1.5–2B. Simultaneously, a bear market reduces trading volumes to 2019-equivalent levels (1.2M DARTs/day). Pre-tax income falls from $4.75B to $2.5B; stock re-rates to 20–22x. Market cap declines 35–45% despite no fundamental impairment to the franchise.

### Target Investor Profile
- **Quality-oriented growth investors** who favor consistent compounders over speculative high-beta names.
- Investors seeking exposure to global self-directed investing growth without PFOF/crypto regulatory risk.
- Long-horizon investors (5–10 years) who value founder-aligned capital allocation and conservative risk management.
- **Not ideal for:** income-focused investors (low dividend, complex pass-through structure); investors requiring immediate catalyst.

**NII Sensitivity (Key Risk Metric):**
- A 1% decline in global short-term rates reduces annual NII by approximately **~$335M** (~5.4% of FY2025 revenue).

### Operating Leverage Dynamics
- Pre-tax margin expanded from 57% (FY2022) to 77% (FY2025) as revenue grew 102% vs. minimal cost base growth.
- Total employees grew from ~2,900 to 3,182 over 5 years while revenues more than doubled.
- Each $100M incremental net revenue yields ~$77M incremental pre-tax income at the current margin structure.
- This is among the highest operating leverage ratios of any financial company globally.

### Working Capital & Cash Conversion
- Operating cash flow FY2025: $4.48B (+144% YoY) — extraordinary for a company with $6.2B in revenue.
- No traditional corporate debt; self-funding growth entirely from operations.
- Excess regulatory capital of $14.1B provides both a safety buffer and dry powder for business expansion.

## 3. Competitive Position & Moat

### Market Position
- Largest U.S. electronic brokerage by DARTs as of FY2025.
- 4.4M accounts (small relative to Schwab's ~34M, but disproportionately active and high-value).
- ~$780B in client equity growing at 37% YoY — reaching meaningful scale.
- Dominant in institutional and professional trader segment (hedge funds, family offices, prop desks, introducing brokers).
- Access to 170+ exchanges, 40+ countries, 28 currencies — no retail broker offers broader global access.

### Moat Classification: Technology Cost Advantage + Global Scale + No-PFOF Regulatory Positioning

| Moat Type | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Technology / Cost Advantage | **Strongest moat** — 48 years of proprietary in-house development produces structurally unmatched operating efficiency |
| Global Market Access | **Strong** — no retail competitor offers 170+ exchange access in 28 currencies; genuine differentiator for professionals |
| No-PFOF Model | **Regulatory future-proof** — EU PFOF ban (2026) and potential U.S. restrictions favor IBKR's execution model |
| Scale / NII Float | **Growing moat** — $160B credit balances earning spread; scales with client equity regardless of trading activity |
| Switching Costs | **Moderate** — professional tools (TWS, API), global positions, and margin structures are non-trivial to transfer |
| Brand (Institutional) | **Strong in segment** — IBKR brand = low-cost, sophisticated execution; loyal professional clientele |

### Moat Durability Assessment
Strong and improving evidence across 5-year horizon:
- Pre-tax margins expanded 20 percentage points over FY2022–2025: structural, not cyclical.
- Account growth has not decelerated despite market cycles — adds ~1M net new accounts/year now.
- Revenue per employee at $1.95M is 3.5× Schwab's and essentially untouchable by competitors using traditional staffing models.
- EU PFOF ban directly harms PFOF-dependent competitors while having no impact on IBKR.

**Key Vulnerability:** Thomas Peterffy's voting control (~74%), age (~80), and no publicly disclosed succession plan. If Peterffy departs, the company could undergo a strategic or governance change that disrupts the culture of extreme cost discipline.

## 4. Top 3 Growth Drivers & Sensitivities

### Driver 1: Client Account & Equity Compounding

**Description:** IBKR has grown client accounts at 28–32% annually for 5 consecutive years. Each new account brings: commission revenue, cash balances (NII), and potential margin borrowing. The flywheel effect — more clients → more balances → more NII → more capital → better platform → more clients — has been operating with remarkable consistency.

**TAM Impact:** Global self-directed investing accounts total several hundred million globally; IBKR has 4.4M (under 1% penetration). Asia-Pacific alone represents hundreds of millions of potential self-directed investors. Even 0.1% additional TAM penetration = 200,000+ new accounts.

**Scenario Analysis (FY2027 Client Equity):**

| Scenario | Client Accounts | Client Equity | Annual NII Impact vs. FY2025 |
|----------|----------------|---------------|------------------------------|
| Bull | 7.5M | $1.3T | +$2.0B |
| Base | 5.5M | $950B | +$750M |
| Bear | 4.5M (slowdown) | $650B | -$500M |

**Timeline/Milestones:** Monthly brokerage metrics release (monthly). Watch for sustained >25% account growth rate.

### Driver 2: Commission Revenue (Activity Leverage)

**Description:** DARTs grew from 2.6M/day (FY2024) to 4.04M/day (Q4 2025), driven by elevated market volatility, crypto trading activity, and expanded extended-hours trading. Unlike NII, commission revenue benefits from market turbulence and volatility — it is partially counter-cyclical to credit risk concerns.

**TAM Impact:** IBKR currently processes ~4M DARTs/day vs. U.S. equity market volume of ~12B+ shares/day. Expanding market share in institutional options, futures, and international markets represents a large incremental opportunity.

**Scenario Analysis (FY2027 Commissions):**

**Timeline/Milestones:** Monthly DARTs data (released monthly in brokerage metrics). Red flag: sustained DARTs below 2.5M/day for two consecutive quarters.

### Driver 3: Margin Loan Book Growth

**Description:** Customer margin loans grew 40% YoY to $90.2B in FY2025. In rising equity markets, customers increasingly use portfolio margin and IBKR's competitive margin rates (~SOFR + 50–150 bps for larger accounts, vs. 4–8%+ at retail competitors). Every $10B in incremental margin loans adds ~$150–200M in NII at current spreads.

**TAM Impact:** U.S. margin debt industry-wide is ~$700–800B. IBKR's ~$90B represents ~11–12% share and is growing fast.

**Scenario Analysis (FY2027 Margin Loans):**

| Base | $110B | +$300M |

**Timeline/Milestones:** Monthly brokerage metrics (customer margin loans disclosed monthly). Watch for any credit quality deterioration signals.

## 5. Risk Framework with Failure Modes

### Top 5 Risks

| # | Risk | Severity | Probability | Severity × P |
|---|------|----------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | Interest Rate / NII Compression | High | Medium-High (50%) | **Very High** |
| 2 | Founder Succession / Governance | Very High | Low-Medium (20%) | **High** |
| 3 | Margin Loan Credit Event | High | Low (15%) | **Medium-High** |
| 4 | Commission Cyclicality (Low Volatility) | Medium | Medium (40%) | **Medium** |
| 5 | Competitive Erosion (Zero-Commission Spread) | Low-Medium | Low (15%) | **Low** |

---

**Risk 1: Interest Rate Compression**
- **Trigger:** Federal Reserve cuts rates to 2–3% range; other global central banks follow; NII yield on balances compresses.
- **Impact:** ~$335M NII reduction per 1% rate cut. If rates fall 200bps from current levels, NII could decline ~$670M, partially offset by continued balance sheet growth.
- **Mitigation:** IBKR's balance sheet is growing faster than rates are falling. In FY2025, despite multiple rate cuts, NII hit a record because client equity grew 37% and credit balances grew 34%. The natural hedge is that lower rates drive more equity investing (higher balances) even as yields compress.

**Risk 2: Founder Succession / Governance**
- **Trigger:** Thomas Peterffy (age ~80, ~74% voting control) retires, becomes incapacitated, or passes away without a succession plan that preserves operational culture.
- **Impact:** Strategic direction could shift dramatically. A successor may prioritize different capital allocation (higher dividend, acquisitions, PFOF adoption), potentially impair operating culture and margins.
- **Mitigation:** CEO Milan Galik (in role since 2019) and CFO Paul Brody (with IBKR since 1987) provide operational continuity. Peterffy has structured the business to be highly systematized (technology-driven), reducing key-person dependency at the operational level. The voting structure also means no outside party can force a hostile change.

**Risk 3: Margin Loan Credit Event**
- **Trigger:** A sudden, severe market crash (>30% in a single week) creates customer margin call losses that exceed account equity before IBKR can liquidate.
- **Impact:** Historical precedent — $104M loss (2020 oil crash), $120M loss (2015 Swiss franc peg break). At $90.2B in margin loans, even a 0.5% default rate = ~$450M loss.
- **Mitigation:** IBKR uses real-time automated risk management that liquidates positions within seconds of margin breach. $14.1B excess regulatory capital provides substantial buffer. The company's risk model has been battle-tested across multiple market events.

**Risk 4: Commission Cyclicality**
- **Trigger:** Extended period of low market volatility (VIX < 12 sustained); retail and institutional traders become less active. DARTs revert to 2019-level (~1.2M/day).
- **Impact:** Commission revenue falls from $2.1B toward $1.1–1.3B; partial offset from NII growth.
- **Mitigation:** IBKR's account growth (32% YoY) means even lower activity per account still produces more total trades. International expansion diversifies trading activity timing. Options and futures growth provides higher-revenue-per-trade product mix.

**Risk 5: Competitive Erosion**
- **Trigger:** A well-funded competitor (JP Morgan, Goldman, Fidelity) launches a zero-commission, high-margin-rate platform with institutional-grade tools targeting IBKR's customer base.
- **Impact:** Margin rate competition could reduce NII spread; commission competition could compress per-trade revenue.
- **Mitigation:** The technology moat (48 years of in-house development) is nearly impossible to replicate on a 5-year horizon. Large banks have repeatedly tried and failed (Goldman Marcus, JPM You Invest) to compete effectively in self-directed brokerage.

### Pre-Mortem
*"If this investment fails in 3 years, the most likely reason is: the Federal Reserve cut rates to 1.5–2.0% in response to a recession, simultaneously causing a bear market that reduced DARTs to historical lows and impaired client equity growth. With both NII and commissions under pressure, pre-tax income fell from $4.75B to $2.5B. Trading at 25× compressed earnings, the stock declined 40–50% from peak. The franchise remained excellent but was overvalued at entry, and the interest rate environment proved more adverse than anticipated."*

### Key Assumptions That Must Hold
1. Global short-term interest rates remain above 2.5% (otherwise NII sees material pressure).
2. Client account growth rate remains ≥20% annually (currently 32%).
3. No catastrophic margin loan credit event that damages the balance sheet meaningfully.
4. Thomas Peterffy's governance provides continuity of operational philosophy.
5. No competing brokerage replicates IBKR's global multi-asset access and margin rate structure at scale.
