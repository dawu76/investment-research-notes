# Interactive Brokers Group, Inc. (IBKR) — Equity Analyst Report

**Prepared:** March 30, 2026 | **Ticker:** NASDAQ: IBKR | **Sector:** Financials — Capital Markets / Institutional Brokerage

---

## Executive Summary

Interactive Brokers Group is the parent of Interactive Brokers LLC, one of the world's largest electronic brokerages by Daily Average Revenue Trades (DARTs). Founded in 1977 by Thomas Peterffy and headquartered in Greenwich, CT, IBKR has built a proprietary, fully automated trading and clearing platform over nearly five decades. Revenue is earned primarily through two streams: net interest income on customer cash balances and margin loans (57.4% of FY2025 revenues) and commissions on trades across equities, options, futures, forex, bonds, and crypto (33.8%). The business operates with a pre-tax margin of 77% in FY2025 — the highest of any publicly traded brokerage globally — reflecting extreme operating leverage from in-house technology and a lean employee base (3,182 employees generating $6.2B in revenue, or ~$1.95M per employee). FY2025 was a record year on every major metric: $6.2B in net revenues (+20% YoY), $984M in net income attributable to common stockholders (+30%), 4.4M client accounts (+32%), $779.9B in client equity (+37%), and $90.2B in margin loans (+40%). Added to the S&P 500 in August 2025 and completing a 4-for-1 stock split, IBKR has transitioned from a niche professional broker to a mainstream institutional-grade platform. The primary risks are interest rate sensitivity (NII falls ~$335M per 1% cut), key-person concentration in founder Thomas Peterffy (~74% voting control), and cyclicality in trading commissions. **In plain terms: IBKR is a superbly run, capital-efficient brokerage machine with structurally superior economics, whose main vulnerability is the interest rate cycle — not competitive disruption.**

## What They Sell and Who Buys

**Products / Services:**
- **Electronic Brokerage (core):** Multi-asset trading across 170+ exchanges in 40+ countries, 28 currencies. Assets traded: stocks, options, futures, forex, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds, crypto, precious metals.
- **Margin Lending:** IBKR Margin Loans at competitive rates (typically Fed Funds Rate + 0.5–1.5%), totaling $90.2B as of Dec 31, 2025.
- **Securities Lending:** IBKR Stock Yield Enhancement Program lends customer long positions; earns spread between borrow/lend rates.
- **IBKR Pro vs. IBKR Lite:** Pro accounts use transparent cost-plus commission structure (no PFOF); Lite accounts use zero-commission PFOF model for U.S. equities only (~6% of accounts).
- **Interactive Advisors:** Robo-advisory / model portfolio service.
- **GlobalAnalyst / Research Tools:** Premium research distribution and equity scanner tools.
- **White-Label / Introducing Broker:** Platform provided to hedge funds, family offices, and introducing brokers who re-brand IBKR infrastructure.
- **Overnight Extended Hours Trading:** First major broker to offer extended U.S. equity and ETF trading (170% volume growth in overnight sessions).
- **Prediction Markets:** Added 2024.
- **Crypto Trading:** Available across major jurisdictions.

**Target Customers:**
- Primary: Sophisticated active traders — individual professionals, active retail investors with high account balances.
- Institutional: Hedge funds, proprietary trading firms, family offices, registered investment advisors.
- Introducing Brokers: ~30% of DARTs come from institutional/IB channels.
- Geographic: ~50%+ of client base is international (200+ countries); Asia-Pacific drives majority of new account acquisition. UK, European, Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia operations are material.
- Core motivation: Superior execution quality, global market access, competitive margin rates, and professional-grade tools. IBKR is chosen by professionals who optimize for cost and capability over UI simplicity.

## How They Make Money

**Revenue Model: Hybrid (NII-dominant + transaction-based + ancillary)**

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue | % of Total | Nature |
|---------|---------------|-----------|--------|
| Net Interest Income | $3,563M | 57.4% | Semi-recurring — rate × balance-sensitive |
| Commissions | $2,100M | 33.8% | Variable — tied to trading volume/volatility |
| Other Revenue (fees, market data, etc.) | ~$542M | 8.7% | Partially recurring |
| **Total Net Revenues** | **$6,205M** | **100%** | |

**Historical Revenue Trajectory:**

| Year | Net Revenues | YoY Growth | Pre-Tax Margin |
|------|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| FY2021 | ~$2,967M | — | ~62% |
| FY2022 | ~$3,067M | +3% | ~57% |
| FY2023 | $4,340M | +41% | 71% |
| FY2024 | $5,185M | +19% | 71% |
| FY2025 | $6,205M | +20% | **77% (record)** |

**NII Dynamics:** IBKR earns interest on: (a) customer credit balances (~$160B as of Dec 2025, earning spread between Fed Funds and near-zero pass-through rate to clients); (b) margin loans ($90.2B at ~SOFR + 0.5–1.5%); (c) securities lending (borrowers pay IBKR to borrow hard-to-borrow shares). Despite multiple Fed rate cuts in 2024–2025, NII hit a record in FY2025 because balance sheet growth (+37% client equity) more than offset rate compression.

**Commissions:** IBKR charges on a per-trade, per-contract, or tiered-volume basis. No PFOF on the "Pro" tier (94% of accounts). Revenue per trade is low (~$3–5 blended) but volume is massive (4.04M DARTs/day in Q4 2025).

## Revenue Quality

**Predictability:** Above-average for a brokerage. NII (57%) is more stable than commissions but rate-dependent. Commissions (34%) are cyclical; they correlate with VIX, trading volumes, and global market activity. The business saw only +3% revenue growth in FY2022 (a down-market year) before surging +41% in FY2023 as rates spiked.

**Concentration Risks:**
- Rate sensitivity: A 1% decline in global short-term rates reduces annual NII by an estimated ~$335M (~5.4% of FY2025 revenue). In an aggressive rate-cut environment, this risk is real.
- Single-founder voting control: Thomas Peterffy (~age 80) controls ~74.2% of votes. Board lacks independence meaningful enough to override founder will.
- Margin loan tail risk: $90.2B in customer margin loans creates potential for large credit losses in a sudden market crash (historical precedent: $104M loss from 2020 oil crash; $120M from 2015 Swiss franc event). Excess regulatory capital of $14.1B provides substantial buffer.
- Commission cyclicality: Q4 2025 DARTs of 4.04M/day reflected elevated volatility and crypto activity; could revert to 2–2.5M/day in a low-volatility regime.

**Diversification Strength:**
- Revenue is geographically diverse (Americas, Europe, Asia all contributing meaningfully).
- Multi-asset coverage (stocks, bonds, options, futures, forex, crypto, metals) reduces single-product concentration.
- Institutional + retail mix provides base-load trading volume even in quiet markets.

## Cost Structure

**The Operating Leverage Story:**

IBKR's cost structure is the primary driver of its exceptional 77% pre-tax margin. The company has built all technology in-house (no vendor licensing), operates with 3,182 employees globally (vs. Schwab's ~35,000 for ~$20B revenue), and has no distribution or branch network.

**Major Cost Components:**
- **Execution & Clearing:** Cost of processing transactions; scales modestly with volume.
- **Employee Compensation:** ~3,182 employees globally. Peterffy's own compensation is capped at 0.2% of net income (disclosed annually). Total compensation expense is very low relative to revenues.
- **Technology & Communications:** Ongoing investment in proprietary systems; a competitive moat but also a fixed cost.
- **Occupancy:** Minimal; primarily the Greenwich, CT headquarters and global office network.
- **Interest Expense:** Paid on customer credit balances; grows as balances grow (offset by NII earned on the same balances at a spread).

**Pre-Tax Margin Comparison:**

| Firm | Pre-Tax Margin (approx. 2025) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Interactive Brokers | ~77% |
| Robinhood Markets | ~47% (operating) |
| Charles Schwab | ~25-30% |
| TD Ameritrade (pre-acquisition) | ~30-35% |

IBKR generates ~$1.95M in net revenue per employee — approximately 3.5× Schwab's revenue-per-employee.

## Capital Intensity

**Low Capex, High Regulatory Capital:**

IBKR's core business requires minimal capital expenditure (primarily software development, which is expensed) but must maintain substantial regulatory capital as a broker-dealer.

**Balance Sheet (Dec 31, 2025):**

| Metric | Amount |
|--------|--------|
| Total Assets | $203.2B |
| Total Liabilities | ~$182.7B |
| Total Equity (firm) | ~$20.5B |
| Cash & Cash Equivalents | ~$4.96B |
| Long-Term Debt | Minimal (no traditional corporate debt) |
| Excess Regulatory Capital | ~$14.1B |
| Customer Margin Loans | $90.2B |
| Customer Credit Balances | $160.1B |

**Balance Sheet Nature:** IBKR's $203B in total assets is primarily composed of customer assets held in custody plus margin loans extended to clients — not proprietary leverage. The broker-dealer structure inflates the balance sheet relative to a non-financial company, but IBKR's own capital position ($20.5B equity) is very strong. The $14.1B excess regulatory capital is a meaningful cushion.

**Cash Conversion:** Exceptional. Operating cash flow in FY2025 was $4.48B (+144% YoY). The business self-funds growth from operations with no need for external capital.

**ROIC:** IBKR does not disclose explicit ROIC, but given near-zero traditional debt and high pre-tax income, returns on tangible equity are very high — estimated 25–30%+ on a normalized basis.

## Growth Drivers

**1. Account and Client Equity Growth (Primary, Structural)**
- IBKR has grown client accounts at 28–32% annually for 5 consecutive years (2021–2025), adding 1M+ net new accounts in FY2025 alone. Client equity grew from $295B (FY2022) to $779.9B (FY2025). This drives both NII (more cash balances to earn on) and commission revenue (more active accounts).

**2. International Expansion / Asia-Pacific**
- Asia-Pacific drives the majority of new account acquisition. IBKR's multi-currency, multi-exchange global access is a structural advantage unavailable at U.S.-centric brokers. Regulatory expansions in Singapore, Hong Kong, UK, and EU markets continue.

**3. Margin Lending Scale**
- Customer margin loans grew from ~$64.5B (FY2024) to $90.2B (FY2025), +40% YoY. In an elevated-equity-market environment, clients increasingly use portfolio margin. Each incremental $1B in margin loans generates ~$15–20M in NII at current spreads.

**4. Platform Expansion (Options, Extended Hours, Crypto, Prediction Markets)**
- 170% growth in overnight/extended-hours trading volumes positions IBKR as a leader in global 24-hour equity access.
- Daily options on European indices, expanded crypto listings, and prediction markets add trading surface area.

**5. Operating Leverage on Fixed Cost Base**
- Each 10% increase in net revenues adds ~$620M of incremental pre-tax income given ~77% margins (i.e., almost all incremental revenue flows to the bottom line). This is an extraordinary operating leverage ratio.

**6. S&P 500 Inclusion (2025)**
- Added August 28, 2025. Forced passive buying by index funds increased float liquidity and institutional ownership. Raised the company's profile with generalist investors.

**Structural vs. Cyclical:**
- Structural: Account growth, international penetration, margin loan scale, operating leverage.
- Cyclical: Commission revenue, NII yield (rate-dependent), client equity mark-to-market.

## Competitive Edge

**Primary Moat: Technology Cost Advantage + Global Reach**

IBKR's moat is rooted in:

1. **Proprietary Technology (Cost Moat):** All trading, clearing, risk management, and compliance systems were built in-house since 1977. No licensing fees for third-party OMS/EMS systems. This is the primary driver of the 77% pre-tax margin vs. industry averages of 25–35%.

2. **Global Market Access (Differentiation):** Access to 170+ exchanges in 40+ countries, 28 currencies. No retail competitor offers comparable breadth. This is a structural moat for international and institutional clients who need multi-market execution.

3. **Scale-Driven NII (Financial Moat):** $160B in customer credit balances earns significant spread. Larger balances compound over time as new clients deposit more. The NII flywheel — more accounts → more cash → more NII → more capital to invest in platform — reinforces competitive position.

4. **No PFOF on Pro Accounts:** 94% of IBKR customers use the Pro tier, which routes orders based on best execution rather than PFOF. This is a regulatory moat — as PFOF faces bans in the EU (effective 2026) and potential U.S. restrictions, IBKR's model is future-proof vs. PFOF-dependent competitors like Robinhood.

5. **Institutional Relationships:** Introducing broker relationships, prime brokerage services, and hedge fund clearing provide base-load commission volumes that are significantly less volatile than retail-only flows.

**Moat Durability Evidence:**
- Pre-tax margin has expanded from ~57% (FY2022) to ~77% (FY2025) despite rate headwinds — structural, not cyclical improvement.
- Account growth has been consistent at 28–32% annually for 5 years with no sign of deceleration.
- Revenue per employee ~$1.95M dwarfs all brokerage peers; no competitor has replicated this efficiency.
- Founder-controlled governance ensures long-term orientation over short-term earnings management.

**Key Vulnerability:** Thomas Peterffy (~74% voting control, ~age 80) is both the primary competitive advantage (visionary, long-term focused, cost-disciplined) and the primary governance risk. Succession is the single most important long-term unknown for IBKR.
