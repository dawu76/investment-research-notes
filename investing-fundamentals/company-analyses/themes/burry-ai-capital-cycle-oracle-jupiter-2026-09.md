# Burry's AI Capital Cycle Warning and the Oracle Project Jupiter Force Majeure

Two things landed in the same 48 hours (September 24-27, 2026): a long Substack post from Michael Burry putting a date (2028-2029) on hyperscaler AI write-offs, and a Bloomberg report that Oracle sent a force majeure notice on its Project Jupiter data center. Morningstar treats the notice as a possible delay. Burry's framework treats it as an early warning of the kind of stress that shows up before any write-off is booked.

**Source caveat.** The Economic Times article could not be opened (HTTP 403 via fetch, blocked in the browser). Its claims are taken from syndicated copies of the same story (Yahoo/Stocktwits, and a search snippet quoting the same Burry line), so the "Trump cannot afford to let it fail" section is thinner than the others. Burry's original Substack post was not read directly. All figures below are his estimates as relayed by the press, not company disclosures, unless stated.

**Provenance key.** `yahoo-gurufocus` = Yahoo Finance / GuruFocus (Moz Farooque, Sept 25). `thestreet` = TheStreet (Opeyemi Babalola, Sept 27). `morningstar` = Morningstar Equity Research (Luke Yang, Sept 24). `yahoo-stocktwits` = Yahoo Finance / Stocktwits (Yuvraj Malik, Sept 27). `economic-times` = Economic Times (only the quoted Burry line, via the syndicated copy; not read directly). The `sources:` list above has the URLs.

## 1. Burry's capital cycle argument

- **Net investment at a near 40-year high.** S&P 500 net capital investment (capex minus depreciation) was about 2.07% of US GDP as of June 30, 2026. Burry says only the aftermath of the March 2000 Nasdaq peak ran higher in nearly four decades. He expects the next few quarters to set higher marks, possibly beating the post-2000 level. ^[yahoo-gurufocus] ^[thestreet]
- **The historical mirror.** After the dot-com buildout, depreciation and write-downs pushed aggregate S&P 500 net investment below zero for 12 straight quarters (mid-2003 to mid-2006). ^[yahoo-gurufocus] ^[thestreet]
- **Timing.** Write-offs "perhaps in 2028 or 2029", when the commitments may be so large that "a relatively small write-off has a bigger impact than we can now imagine." He floated 2028 as his base case in August. ^[yahoo-gurufocus] ^[thestreet]
- **Only Apple is spared.** Among the most profitable companies, Burry says the rest are "betting everything on this as their free cash flow turns negative" while borrowing accelerates. ^[thestreet]
- **The tally is about commitments, not spending.** Roughly $3T across MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META and ORCL, counting purchase commitments, future leases, guarantees, construction in progress and other exposures. Most of it sits off the reported capex line, in leases, guarantees and special-purpose vehicles. ^[yahoo-gurufocus] ^[thestreet]

## 2. Oracle: the sharpest version

**The accounting point.** Oracle collected $11.4B of customer prepayments last quarter that its 10-Q says carry "a significant financing component." Under ASC 606 that early cash is treated like a customer loan. Oracle books interest on it and later recognizes more revenue than it collected. Burry's claim: the extra $1.9B "becomes extra revenue created by the structure of the contract," lifting future cloud revenue by nearly 20%, while the offsetting cost goes to interest expense and never touches operating margin. ^[thestreet]

Management's counter: CFO Hilary Maxson calls operating margin central to Oracle's value. Co-CEO Clay Magouyrk says GPUs renewed or resold last quarter went for a 20% premium even though most were four years or older, and that growth capital need not all be Oracle's own. ^[thestreet]

**The duration mismatch.** About $248B of data center leases (disclosed December 2025) start in fiscal 2028 and run 15 to 19 years. Customer contracts typically last about five years (S&P Global Market Intelligence via CoStar). TheStreet's framing: a 30-year mortgage against a roof that wears out sooner. ^[thestreet]

**Backlog and stress markers.** $664B contracted future revenue (CNBC via TheStreet). Oracle closed down 3.5% at $139.53 on September 24 as the cost of insuring its debt hit a record. That is below the $144.63 where Burry disclosed an Oracle short in August. ^[thestreet]

## 3. Project Jupiter force majeure (Morningstar, Sept 24)

- Bloomberg reported that Oracle sent Stack Infrastructure (a Blue Owl subsidiary) a force majeure notice to protect itself from payment obligations if the 2.45 GW New Mexico data center is delayed. Oracle fell about 5% early. ^[morningstar]
- Oracle says the project "remains on our planned schedule." Blue Owl shares fell 4%. About $18B of Jupiter loans were quoted at 89-91 cents on the dollar (FT via Reuters). Site start is 2028. ^[thestreet]
- **Morningstar's read:** Oracle can protect its own financial interests against the developer, but missing capacity could delay $25B or more of revenue, a sizable part of Morningstar's OCI forecasts of $86B (FY2028) and $136B (FY2029). A delay could also threaten the investment-grade rating, since Oracle leans on data center cash inflows to keep leverage in check. ^[morningstar]
- **Morningstar's position:** fair value $220, 4 stars, narrow moat, very high uncertainty. Delay treated as a possibility only, base case is on-time delivery, shares "look undervalued." It says the market reaction does not fully price an actual delay. ^[morningstar]
- **Structural point:** pure clouds like Oracle and CoreWeave depend on third-party developers for the power shell. A November 2025 delivery delay sent CoreWeave down 16%. ^[morningstar]

## 4. "Trump cannot afford to let it fail"

From a later Burry post (reported September 27): "Trump and his team know that the AI narrative and the buildout is the only thing keeping this economy going, and the most positive thing happening this year. They cannot afford to let it fall." He is unsure what Washington could actually do, and says the US government is "in the worst position for fighting a financial crisis/debt crisis in modern history," with "very few arrows in the quiver" and most of them dull. ^[yahoo-stocktwits] ^[economic-times]

**Disclosed shorts** (as reported): NVDA, ORCL, PLTR, Nebius, MU, CRWV and the iShares Semiconductor ETF. Oracle, Palantir and Nebius are the largest. ^[yahoo-stocktwits]

The article also notes that OpenAI and Anthropic are moving toward IPOs that could be among the largest ever, and that Trump made over 1,000 trades in July 2026, mostly tech. The trading claim is single-source and tangential.

## Where the sources disagree

| Question | Burry / TheStreet | Morningstar / bulls |
|---|---|---|
| Is Oracle's backlog real demand? | Prepayment accounting flatters growth, leverage is 4.3x | Backlog and GPU resale premium show real demand, capacity shortage persists |
| Jupiter force majeure | Early warning: stress shows up as letters, loans and delays before write-offs | Protective legal step, delay only a possibility, $220 FV unchanged |
| GPU useful life | Ages faster than telecom networks | Four-year-old GPUs re-let at a 20% premium |
| Timing | 2028-2029 | Demand absorbs costs "well before 2028 maturities" (consensus per TheStreet) |

Morningstar and Burry are not measuring the same thing. Morningstar is asking whether Oracle delivers on time. Burry is asking what the commitments do to returns if utilization or pricing slips. Both can be right.

## What this adds to existing wiki views

- **Commencement wall thesis.** Jupiter is the first named case where a commencement date is contested in public. The framework's point that timing risk lands first on developers, lenders and neoclouds fits so far: the Jupiter loans traded at 89-91 cents and Blue Owl fell 4%. Oracle itself fell 3.5% to 5% on the same news, so this is only partial support. Burry's "first warnings are force majeure letters, stressed loans, delayed payments" restates the same ordering. See [[ai-compute-commencement-wall-and-refinancing-trap]]. Burry's 2028-2029 write-off window overlaps the 2027-2028 payment shock window there, but his claim is about accounting write-offs, not cash payments.
- **Depreciation arbitrage.** Burry's earlier July point (Amazon shortening server lives, depreciation vs. obsolescence) now extends to Microsoft's 25-year data center lives. See [[ai-inference-costs-accounting]] and [[semianalysis-research-2026-07]].
- **Base rates.** [[bayes-and-base-rates]] covers base rates for datacenter project success. Jupiter is one data point on the delay side, not a base rate.
