# Big Uptrend. Tech Momentum Cracking | Katie Stockton on What the Charts Say Could Come Next

Detailed overview and analysis of the *Excess Returns* podcast featuring **Katie Stockton**, founder and Managing Partner of Fairlead Strategies, hosted by Matt Ziegler and Justin Carbonneau. The episode was released on July 8, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Katie Stockton provides a technical analysis of the S&P 500 and NASDAQ 100, noting that while the long-term bullish uptrend remains intact, short-term momentum has deteriorated as evidenced by the rollover of the 20-day moving average. She maintains a neutral short-term bias, watching the 50-day moving average as a key line of defense. Stockton clarifies the technical definition of "overbought" and "oversold" (stochastics above 80% or below 20%), highlighting that sell signals require momentum to downturn. She distinguishes between **Market Breadth** (participation, which remains healthy and supportive of the broad market) and **Market Leadership** (which is highly concentrated in semiconductors and mega-cap tech). Stockton outlines the systematic mechanics of the Fairlead Tactical Sector ETF (**TAC**), which rotates equal-weighted sector allocations into treasuries and gold during market stress. Finally, she provides a technical review of international equities, the dollar, gold (short-term corrective swing), and crude oil (approaching intermediate oversold territory).

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. Short-Term Consolidation vs. Long-Term Uptrend
Stockton points to a divergence in technical timeframes. The long-term trend for both the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ 100 is bullish, with no confirmed sell signals on the monthly MACD or stochastics. However, the short-term trend has turned neutral. The 20-day moving average has rolled over, signifying a loss of near-term momentum. The QQQ is currently testing its consolidation range, with the 50-day moving average serving as crucial intermediate support. A decisive break below the 50-day would signal a deeper weekly trend pullback.

> "The shorter term... there's been a loss of momentum. We're maintaining right now neutral bias... you can see it in... the 20-day moving average... when you have such a steep trend and then it loses enough steam... we do pay attention."
> — *Katie Stockton*

#### 2. Defining Overbought, Oversold, and the "Stochastic Pop"
Stockton clarifies that "overbought" is not a synonym for "expensive" or "bad." Strong trends are naturally overbought. The indicators (using a 14-period Stochastic Oscillator) register overbought above 80% and oversold below 20%. 
*   **The Sell Signal:** Occurs only when the stochastic line rolls back below 80%.
*   **Stochastic Pop/Whipsaw:** In a very strong market, the stochastic indicator will dip toward 50% and then hook sharply back above 80% without reaching oversold territory. This "pop" confirms a continuation of the powerful uptrend.

> "Overbought is a function of positive momentum... it's when you see the downtick in momentum [below 80%]... that's where you have a so-called overbought sell signal... [A stochastic pop] is where the stochastics will come down but they hook right back up."
> — *Katie Stockton*

#### 3. Monthly MACD Whipsaw and Double Confirmation
Stockton discusses the rare technical whipsaw that occurred in March/April 2026. The monthly MACD for the S&P 500 registered a bearish crossover (sell signal) at the end of March. However, the market immediately reversed and logged a bullish crossover in April. Such monthly whipsaws have occurred only three times in the past 20 years. To manage this risk, Stockton advocates for a **double confirmation** rule: waiting for two consecutive closes on the monthly chart (or confirmation from weekly indicators) before shifting long-term positioning.

> "S&P 500... logged a sell signal or bearish crossover in March... but it whipsall higher in April, which is really very unusual... happened maybe three times in 20 years... we always wait for two closes on the time horizon in question... that double confirmation... will help prevent whipsaws."
> — *Katie Stockton*

#### 4. Market Breadth vs. Market Leadership
Stockton notes that market breadth and leadership are behaving differently:
*   **Market Leadership:** Remains exceptionally narrow, concentrated in the semiconductor sector and select mega-cap tech names. The ratio of the NASDAQ 100 to the S&P 500 shows signs of a bearish head-and-shoulders relative top.
*   **Market Breadth (Participation):** Remains healthy. The NYSE cumulative advanced-decline line continues to make new highs, showing that the average stock is still rising. This healthy participation has cushioned the market as mega-caps underperform. Insurers, healthcare (`XLV`), biotech, and electric utilities are experiencing absolute breakouts as tech consolidated.

> "Market breadth is participation... how many stocks are up on up days... market leadership is where is that outperformance coming from... We've had especially narrow leadership to the semis... but the breadth has been pretty decent."
> — *Katie Stockton*

#### 5. Systematic Risk Management & the TAC ETF
For tactical risk management, Stockton suggests using stop-losses at key support levels or buying inverse ETFs (e.g., `PSQ` for the Nasdaq-100) to hedge beta during short-term pullbacks, avoiding the tax implications of selling core long-term holdings. She outlines the process for her ETF, **TAC**:
*   **Equities Allocation:** Equal-weights up to 8 of the 11 S&P sectors displaying the best long-term trend characteristics.
*   **Defensive Allocation:** If fewer than 8 sectors exhibit positive trends, the remaining cash is allocated to short-term/long-term treasuries and gold mini shares (`GLDM`). In a prolonged bear market, the ETF can hold 100% defensive assets and 0% equities.

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Adopt a Neutral Short-Term Posture:** Hold core long-term equity positions but do not add capital at current levels, as the 20-day moving average rollover indicates a consolidation phase.
2.  **Monitor the 50-Day Moving Average Support:** Use the 50-day moving average on `SPY` and `QQQ` as the key risk boundary. If these levels break decisively on a daily close, increase cash or hedging.
3.  **Hedge Volatility via Inverse ETFs:** For taxable accounts, buy partial positions in inverse ETFs (e.g., `PSQ`) to hedge technology exposure during pullbacks, rather than selling stock and triggering capital gains.
4.  **Rotate to Non-Tech Breakout Sectors:** Allocate capital to sectors showing defensive breakouts, specifically insurance, healthcare (`XLV`), biotech, and electric utilities, which are benefiting from relative rotation.
5.  **Watch Gold and Oil Exhaustion Signals:** Monitor gold for short-term swings (as monthly momentum is slowing) and look for counter-trend entry points in crude oil as it reaches Demark downside exhaustion.
