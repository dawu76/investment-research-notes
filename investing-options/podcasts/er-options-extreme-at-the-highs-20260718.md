# It Only Happens at Bottoms | Andy Constan on the Options Extreme That Showed Up at the Highs | Excess Returns

Detailed overview of the podcast interview on *Excess Returns*, hosted by **Jack Forehand** and **Justin Carbonneau** featuring guest **Andy Constan** (Founder and CEO of Damped Spring Advisors), published on July 18, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary

Andy Constan analyzes a rare options market anomaly where extreme call option buying and distorted volatility skew—historically reserved for panic washouts at market bottoms—appeared at all-time market highs in mega-cap tech and semiconductor equities. He demonstrates how retail and systematic call buying forces market makers into short gamma positioning, amplifying upside momentum while creating structural vulnerability to sudden downside gaps when gamma flips. 

Constan also presents his **"Size of the Pie" macro framework**, arguing that consensus earnings growth assumptions across tech giants exceed total nominal GDP growth capacity, making current valuation multiples fragile. To manage concentrated tech exposure, he details options hedging techniques (zero-cost collars and call overwriting) to monetise elevated implied volatility while purchasing downside tail protection.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Options Market Anomaly: Call Euphoria at Market Highs
Constan explains why current options positioning signals structural fragility:
*   **Historical Skew Inversion:** Traditionally, extreme put skew occurs at market bottoms when institutional investors panic and pay high implied volatility premiums for downside protection. In this cycle, speculative demand for out-of-the-money (OTM) call options in mega-cap tech and AI semiconductor stocks drove call implied volatility above put implied volatility *at market highs*.
*   **Gamma Squeeze Reflexivity:** As buyers rush into short-dated OTM call options, market makers (dealers) who sell these calls become short gamma. To maintain delta-neutral hedges, dealers must buy the underlying stock as it rises, creating a self-reinforcing upward melt-up that detaches stock prices from fundamental valuation metrics.

> "Usually, an extreme in options skew where everyone is scrambling for tail protection only happens at panic market bottoms. What we saw at the highs was the exact opposite: an extreme call-buying frenzy that forced dealers into short gamma. That creates a reflexive melt-up on the way up, but when momentum stalls, that same gamma flips and accelerates the drop on the way down."
> — *Andy Constan*

#### 2. The "Size of the Pie" Macro Constraint
Constan outlines his macroeconomic framework evaluating corporate earnings expectations against macroeconomic capacity:
*   **GDP Output Limit:** Corporate earnings represent a slice of total nominal GDP. If consensus earnings projections for S&P 500 tech companies imply 15–20% annualized growth while nominal GDP grows at 5–6%, corporate earnings would eventually have to exceed 100% of national income—an economic impossibility.
*   **Margin Compression Reality:** Without infinite expansion of profit margins or consumer debt, corporate earnings growth must eventually converge toward nominal GDP growth. Expecting perpetual double-digit earnings expansion at current record-high profit margins misprices macro realities.

> "You have to look at the size of the overall economic pie. Investors are modeling earnings growth for mega-cap tech that implies their profits will grow faster than total nominal GDP forever. Corporate earnings cannot exceed the total size of the economy. At some point, the pie constraint forces earnings growth back to reality."
> — *Andy Constan*

#### 3. Options Hedging Mechanics for Concentrated Holdings
Constan details actionable options strategies for investors holding large gains in concentrated tech stocks:
*   **Zero-Cost Collars:** Take advantage of elevated call implied volatility by selling an out-of-the-money call option (e.g., 10–15% above current price) and using the premium collected to buy a protective put option (e.g., 10% below current price). This caps upside participation while eliminating downside tail risk at zero net cash cost.
*   **Covered Call Overwriting:** For investors willing to lock in partial profits, selling call options against long stock positions monetises peak implied volatility during gamma squeezes.

> "If you have massive unrealized gains in concentrated tech names, current options pricing gives you a gift. Call implied volatility is so high that you can sell an upside call and use that premium to buy a protective put, locking in a zero-cost collar that completely insulates your portfolio from a severe drawdown."
> — *Andy Constan*

#### 4. The "Fed Put" Threshold & Macro Liquidity
Constan discusses how Federal Reserve policy interacts with market volatility:
*   **Higher Strike for the Fed Put:** Unlike the 2010–2020 low-inflation regime where the Fed intervened at the first sign of equity weakness, ongoing fiscal deficits and sticky inflation raise the threshold for Fed intervention.
*   **Credit Spreads vs. Equity Volatility:** The Fed will not ease monetary policy to rescue equity multiples if credit markets and high-yield spreads remain stable. Monetary easing will only trigger if tightening financial conditions threaten systemic credit functioning.

> "Investors expecting the Fed to step in the moment the S&P drops 5% are living in the last decade. With sticky inflation and massive fiscal deficits, the Fed put strike is much lower. Unless credit markets break, the Fed is content letting equity valuations adjust."
> — *Andy Constan*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

*   **Options Risk Management & Hedging:**
    *   **Implement Zero-Cost Collars:** Protect concentrated mega-cap tech positions (`NVDA`, `MSFT`) by selling high-IV OTM calls to fund downside put protection.
    *   **Monetise Implied Volatility:** Sell covered calls or cash-secured puts on single stocks experiencing gamma-driven implied volatility spikes.
*   **Tactical Asset Allocation:**
    *   **Rebalance into Value & Equal-Weight (`RSP`):** Reduce market-cap-weighted index concentration; reallocate capital into equal-weight equities and defensives.
    *   **Maintain Cash Reserves:** Keep elevated cash/short-term Treasury balances (`SHV`) to deploy into market drawdowns when gamma unwinds occur.
*   **Key Monitoring Indicators:**
    *   **Options Skew & Cboe SKEW Index:** Monitor call vs. put implied volatility differentials for gamma squeeze warnings.
    *   **Dealer Gamma Positioning (Gamma Exposure - GEX):** Track aggregate dealer gamma levels to identify price thresholds where gamma flips from positive to negative.
    *   **Credit Spreads (HYG/JNK):** Watch high-yield credit spreads to gauge systemic liquidity risk vs. equity factor rotation.
