Executive Summary

Akamai sells three things: security for websites, APIs and corporate networks (55% of Q2 2026 revenue); content delivery and related edge applications (36%, shrinking); and cloud compute capacity, called Cloud Infrastructure Services or CIS (9%, the fastest-growing). Most revenue is recurring, from multi-year enterprise contracts billed monthly with committed minimums plus usage. The legacy economics are good: FY2025 non-GAAP operating margin was 30% and adjusted EBITDA margin 43%, on $4.21B of revenue growing 5%.

In 2026 the company changed its capital profile to chase AI compute demand. It signed over $2.8B of multi-year CIS contracts in H1, plus an $11.6B, seven-year commitment from Anthropic in September, and it is building capacity to serve them. Capex goes from 19% of revenue in FY2025 to about 40% in 2026 before the Anthropic deal, plus about $1.7B more for that deal, funded partly by $3.5B of 0% convertible notes issued in May and a buyback pause. The key risk is whether that capital earns its cost: management's own margin ranges for large deals suggest returns near the cost of capital, and one customer now dominates the backlog.

In plain English: a mature, cash-generative internet-security and delivery company that is betting its balance sheet on becoming an AI cloud.

  • Customers: large enterprises, banks, retailers, media and gaming companies, software vendors and governments. Revenue splits evenly between the U.S. ($550M, +4%) and international ($549M, +6%) in Q2 2026, and no non-U.S. country exceeds 10%.
  • Why they buy: security customers need attacks stopped at the edge before they reach their own infrastructure, and Akamai's global network (one of the largest distributed edge platforms) does that at scale. Delivery customers want reliable, low-latency content at a low cost per gigabyte, which is a commodity purchase. CIS customers increasingly are AI companies that need CPU and GPU capacity outside the hyperscalers. The largest is Anthropic, whose deal covers CPU workloads.

How They Make Money

  • Model: recurring subscription with committed contract minimums plus usage-based overage. Security is mostly subscription; Delivery is usage-heavy (price per traffic volume); CIS is a mix of on-demand usage and large take-or-pay multi-year commitments.
  • FY2025 revenue mix ($4.208B): Security $2.243B (53%, +10%), Delivery $1.257B (30%, -5%), Cloud computing $708M (17%, +12%), of which CIS $314M (+36%). In 2026 Akamai re-cut its segments to Security / Delivery and other cloud apps / CIS, so the 2025 and 2026 splits are not directly comparable.
  • 2026 guidance ($4.445-4.530B, +6-8%): Security high-single-digit growth in constant currency, Delivery and other a mid-single-digit decline, CIS at least +50%. Management expects total growth to reach the low teens in 2027 as CIS contracts ramp.

Revenue Quality

  • Recurring and predictable: remaining performance obligations were $7.6B at June 30, 2026, about 40% of it recognizable within 12 months. That figure predates the $11.6B Anthropic commitment signed in September.
  • Diversification is falling: before 2026, Akamai's base was thousands of enterprise customers with no disclosed concentration. The Anthropic commitment alone is about 1.5x the June RPO and averages about $1.66B a year over seven years (this report's arithmetic, $11.6B ÷ 7), roughly 37% of 2026 guided revenue once fully ramped. MS expects ~$1.7B of annual revenue from it once ramped, held flat under the take-or-pay structure.
  • The Q1 customer is also Anthropic. Morningstar identifies the May 2026 $1.8B, seven-year deal as Anthropic's first, so the two Anthropic deals total $13.3B. MS says ~90% of recent CIS bookings are tied to this customer, out of ~$14.4B of total signed CIS commitments. The relationship "began with a much smaller commitment earlier this year" and expanded within months.
  • Timing of the new revenue (MS): the September contract begins contributing in 2H 2027, generating $150-300M in FY2027 and reaching a ~$1.7B annualized run-rate by the end of 2028. Most of the capex comes before that revenue.
  • The legacy mix is shifting the wrong way for quality: Delivery has declined for several years (-15% in FY2024, -5% in FY2025, -6% in Q2 2026), so the business must grow Security and CIS just to offset it. Security growth slowed from +16% (FY2024) to +10% (FY2025 and Q2 2026), and is guided to high-single-digit for 2026. The fastest security lines (Guardicore segmentation and API Security, $293M in FY2025, +43%) are about 13% of security revenue.

  • Main costs: network and colocation (data-center space and power), bandwidth, depreciation of servers, and people (R&D and sales). CIS adds colocation and depreciation that management says can run about a quarter ahead of revenue.
  • The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is large: in FY2025 non-GAAP operating income ($1.254B) was more than twice GAAP ($567M). The difference is mainly stock-based compensation, acquired-intangible amortization and restructuring. Non-GAAP P/E multiples overstate cheapness relative to GAAP (trailing GAAP P/E ~33x vs. ~13.5x on 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance).
  • Scalability: the security business scales well on shared network assets. CIS is far less scalable: management's illustrative large-deal economics are mid-60s to mid-70s percent cash gross margin and low-20s to low-30s percent non-GAAP operating margin once ramped, below the legacy business's 30%. As CIS grows, the blended margin falls: guidance is 25-26% for 2026, down from 30%.

Capacity is spread across many colocation providers rather than one campus, some is already secured, and the hardware could be redeployed or resold if the customer relationship changes.

  • Convertible notes (principal, coupon, conversion price): 2027 $1.15B, 0.375%, $116.18; 2029 $1.265B, 1.125%, $126.31; 2030 $1.75B, 0%, $201.41; 2032 $1.75B, 0%, $190.81; 2033 $1.725B, 0.25%, $93.01. Each has note hedges and warrants, with warrant strikes of $155-283. Cash interest is small (Q2 2026 total interest expense $9.1M), and coverage is ample (~11x GAAP, ~32x non-GAAP operating income to interest in H1 2026). The pressure point is principal: the $1.15B 2027 notes mature September 1, 2027, and the stock ($90.65) is below every conversion price, so they will be settled in cash or refinanced, not converted. Ratings and covenant (added October 16, 2026): Moody's Baa2 and S&P BBB, both with negative outlooks. The $1.0B revolver's gross leverage covenant (debt including the notes ÷ EBITDA) steps down from 4.75x to 3.50x on December 31, 2026, against an estimated ~4.0-4.2x. See the October 16 update in akam-3-investment-memo.
  • Capital returns: Akamai bought back $800M of stock in FY2025 (10.0M shares at ~$79.77) and $616M in H1 2026, at an average of $134.54 in Q2, then paused buybacks in August to fund CIS. $565M of authorization remains through June 2027.
  • Returns on capital: trailing ROIC is about 4% (stockanalysis.com). Morningstar's own ROIC series is 8.6% in 2025, falling to 6.0% in 2026 and 5.8% in 2027 and recovering only to 8.9% by 2030, against its 7.6% WACC. Its view: the September deal's margin structure is "more accretive than we expected, though not enough to materially improve the firm's returns on invested capital." Morningstar projects net debt/EBITDA of 5.1x in 2026 and 5.0x in 2027 on its EBITDA definition, falling to 1.6x by 2030.
  • Funding (MS): "It appears increasingly likely to us that Akamai ultimately raises incremental capital, most likely debt." MS flags a near-term loop: each additional large CIS win can improve the long-term revenue outlook while forcing another round of near-term EPS and FCF estimate cuts.

Growth Drivers

  1. CIS / AI compute (structural, with financing risk). Guided at least +50% in 2026, accelerating in Q4 and 2027. Demand is backed by contracts: over $2.8B signed in H1, the $11.6B Anthropic commitment (expandable by up to $9B, with warrant vesting tied to the expansion), a >$600M four-year deal with a U.S. technology company (robotics) that ramps through 2027, and GPU capacity that management calls "completely sold out."
  2. Security (structural, slowing). Zero Trust segmentation (Guardicore) and API Security grew 43% in FY2025. LayerX extends into browser security. The core web-application firewall and DDoS businesses are mature and face competition from Cloudflare, cloud-native tools and Zscaler-type platforms.
  3. Delivery stabilization (cyclical/structural decline). Delivery is a declining line, but consolidation (Edgio's exit in 2024, CrowdStrike moving web delivery to Akamai) can slow the decline. Pricing pressure is structural.
  4. Capital allocation. Historically buybacks added 3-5% a year to per-share growth (diluted shares fell from 154.3M in FY2024 to 147.0M in FY2025). With buybacks paused and up to 7.7M warrant shares for Anthropic, that tailwind is gone for now.

Competitive Edge

  • Moat sources: a global edge network built over 25 years, with deep enterprise relationships in large financial, retail and media customers; regulated customers' switching costs (security configurations, compliance, integration); and scale in DDoS mitigation.
  • Evidence of durability: a 30% non-GAAP operating margin and 43% adjusted EBITDA margin in FY2025 show pricing power in security. Security grew 10% in a slow year, and large customers keep consolidating onto Akamai (CrowdStrike in 2026).
  • Evidence of erosion: Delivery is commoditized and shrinking. Security growth halved in two years. Cloudflare grew revenue 36% in Q2 2026 ($696M) against Akamai's 5%, and is winning developer and mid-market share with a unified platform (see net-2-equity-report). ROIC near 4% shows the moat is not producing high returns on the capital being deployed now.
  • In CIS the moat is thinner: Akamai competes with hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google), neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius) and CPU-heavy providers on price per unit of capacity. Its advantages are distributed locations, existing enterprise contracts and willingness to commit balance sheet. That last advantage is the one the bear case questions.
  • The two sell-side views split on exactly this point:
    • Morningstar (no moat since May 2026, cut from narrow): the security business has network effects (20-30% of global internet traffic feeds its threat data) and switching costs, but "with such heavy investment in a no-moat, highly commoditized compute business, we believe the security business is not sufficient to offset the risk of value destruction." Compute pricing is elevated only because supply lags demand; as supply catches up, "per-unit compute pricing should compress materially." Customers increasingly multi-home workloads, and Akamai enters "as a pure capacity provider, with only modest opportunities to cross-sell into security." It is "underfunded relative to competitors such as CoreWeave, IREN, hyperscalers, and Blackstone."
    • Morgan Stanley: comparing Akamai to CoreWeave "risks understating the software and network value embedded in Akamai's offering." The contract runs on Akamai's existing software stack with limited incremental R&D or sales cost. Akamai can attach agent sandboxing, AI security and model-as-a-service later. Its distributed infrastructure "already supports all three hyperscalers as CIS customers."
  • Security moat evidence (Morningstar): security is 53% of revenue, up from 12% a decade ago. Guardicore and Zero Trust products require deep integration into customers' security policies, "making it incredibly complex and risky to rip out." Morningstar's bears-say list: media customers keep insourcing delivery, and hyperscalers keep adding security features to their clouds.
