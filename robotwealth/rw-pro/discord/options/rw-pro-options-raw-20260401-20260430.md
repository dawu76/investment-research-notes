4/29/26, 3:14 PM | robotkris [reply]: Not always. Multiple dates mean the earnings calendar for that ticker has been updated over time (estimated date changes, estimates themselves change, I suspect there's even some duplication from the vendor). In many cases the last date is the best current estimate, but it is not guaranteed to always be the true final earnings date. For upcoming earnings trades, using the latest future date is usually the one you want - but when there's a duplicate future date for a ticker, it's worth checking against another source (Trading View is good for this). I'll put together clearer documentation soon on how to interpret and filter these rows properly - the dataset combines a few things (future earnings dates/estimates, actuals, past estimates) and is a bit confusing.
