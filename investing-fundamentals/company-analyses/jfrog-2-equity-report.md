## Section 1: Secular TAM and Structural Market Position

The software supply chain is not simplifying — it is becoming exponentially more complex, and every dimension of that complexity expands JFrog's addressable surface.

- **AI/ML artifacts create a brand-new artifact category.** Software teams previously managed packages, binaries, and containers. AI teams now manage model weights, training datasets, embeddings, and agent tool registries. JFrog launched its AI Catalog in September 2025 and the MCP Registry on March 18, 2026 — making itself the system of record for MCP servers, agent skills, and agentic binaries before any competitor established a foothold. Every AI-native enterprise that runs models in production needs a governed artifact repository for those models. JFrog is the only universal platform that handles both the traditional software stack and the emerging AI artifact stack simultaneously. The TAM for AI artifact management did not exist three years ago; it exists today and JFrog is first in.

- **Software supply chain security mandates are mandatory budget items, not discretionary spend.** CISA's Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) and Executive Order 14028 require federal contractors and their software vendors to provide SBOMs (Software Bills of Materials) and demonstrate artifact provenance. JFrog Xray + Advanced Security is the architecture that satisfies these requirements at scale. Security Core already represents 10% of ARR and, critically, 16% of RPO — a 60% divergence that is the most important leading indicator in JFrog's model. RPO tracks contract commitments, not yet-recognized revenue. The fact that security's share of RPO is 60% higher than its share of ARR means enterprises are front-loading multi-year security commitments. The mandate-driven pipeline is converting at an accelerating rate.

- **Multi-cloud proliferation makes hyperscaler lock-in structurally impossible for large enterprises.** AWS CodeArtifact supports npm, PyPI, Maven, NuGet, Swift, Ruby, and Cargo — but only within AWS. Google Artifact Registry is GCP-only. Large enterprises running multi-cloud environments (the Fortune 100 standard) cannot use a single hyperscaler's artifact registry without creating cloud-specific fragmentation in their build pipelines. JFrog's universal, cloud-agnostic platform is the only architectural answer to multi-cloud artifact management. The more enterprises add cloud environments, the higher the penalty for not using JFrog.

- **The software proliferation megatrend continuously expands the artifact universe.** More software teams, more microservices, more packages, more containers, more Helm charts, more Terraform modules, more ML models — every increase in software complexity is a direct expansion of JFrog's total addressable artifact surface. JFrog's pricing scales with artifact storage and transfer volume, meaning growth in enterprise software output is a direct and mechanical revenue tailwind. JFrog does not need to win new markets; it needs only for software to continue to proliferate — which it does at a compounding rate regardless of the macroeconomic cycle.

- **The Cursor IDE plugin (March 31, 2026) demonstrates JFrog's developer-layer strategy.** By embedding Xray security scanning directly into Cursor IDE — with 1M+ daily active users — JFrog is inserting itself into the developer workflow at the point of code generation, not just at the point of artifact storage. This is an upstream land-and-expand mechanism that captures AI-native developers before they reach pipeline stages. No competitor is executing this strategy at this precision.

## Section 2: Revenue Quality Metrics

JFrog's revenue quality is exceptional by every institutional-grade metric. The combination of NDR, RPO trajectory, FCF margin expansion, and Q1 guidance shape invalidates the bear framing of 17.5% growth deceleration.

- **NDR of 119% means the existing customer base is a self-funding growth engine.** At 119% NDR, JFrog's installed base alone — with zero new customer acquisition — delivers approximately $100M in incremental annual revenue. The 119 companies spending $1M+ ARR (74 customers) and the 1,168 spending $100K+ represent deeply embedded, expanding enterprise relationships. When an enterprise's software output grows, JFrog's revenue grows automatically via volume-based pricing. This is not managed upsell — it is structural dollar expansion driven by the customer's own software factory scaling.

- **RPO of $566M (+40% YoY) is the most important number in the model.** Revenue grew 24% in FY2025. RPO grew 40%. The backlog is growing nearly twice as fast as recognized revenue. This is not a coincidence — it reflects the systematic shift toward multi-year, larger-commitment contracts as enterprises migrate to Enterprise+ platform subscriptions and co-sign with JFrog's AWS SCA framework. When RPO grows faster than revenue, it means the denominator (future recognized revenue) is pre-loaded and underestimated by consensus models that anchor to the headline growth rate.

- **Q1 FY2026 guidance of $146–148M (+28–30% YoY) directly contradicts the "deceleration to 17.5%" bear thesis.** Full-year guidance of $623–628M implies ~17.5% growth. But Q1 guidance implies 28–30% growth — nearly 12 points above the full-year guide. The only logical explanation is that management is guiding conservatively to full-year while RPO-converted contracts front-load strong H1 performance. The $566M RPO backlog at year-end FY2025 does not sit still; it converts into revenue in H1 FY2026. Analysts who anchor to the full-year 17.5% guide without correcting for Q1 acceleration are misreading the cadence of backlog conversion as structural deceleration. It is not.

- **FCF margin expansion from 6% (FY2022) to 27% (FY2025) is one of the most disciplined operating leverage profiles in enterprise software.** This is a $125M increase in FCF over three years. On $531M in revenue, JFrog is generating real economic cash — $142.3M — while still investing aggressively in R&D, the AWS partnership, security product development, and AI catalog buildout. The leverage mathematics are simple: R&D and G&A are largely fixed costs that scale sub-linearly with revenue. As cloud revenue (45% growth, 46% mix) continues to scale, incremental gross profit flows primarily to FCF rather than to incremental cost. The business is in the middle of a multi-year FCF compounding cycle.

- **Non-GAAP gross margin of 83.3% confirms JFrog is fundamentally a software-economics business, not a services or infrastructure business.** GAAP gross margin of 76.8% reflects the cash cost of cloud hosting. The 6.5-point spread between GAAP and Non-GAAP gross margin is exclusively stock-based compensation — a non-cash charge that does not affect unit economics. At 83% Non-GAAP gross margin, every additional dollar of JFrog revenue adds $0.83 to gross profit before any operating leverage. As the cloud mix shifts from 46% to 55%+ over the next 24 months, GAAP gross margin may face modest pressure, but the underlying unit economics remain pristine.

JFrog is not an artifact repository company. It is a software supply chain platform company in the middle of a product expansion that will double its addressable revenue per enterprise customer. The signals are measurable and accelerating.

- **Security Core at 10% ARR / 16% RPO is a textbook leading indicator of product inflection.** The gap between RPO penetration (16%) and ARR penetration (10%) means enterprises are committing to Security Core at a faster rate than they are consuming it. This is exactly the pattern you see in the early innings of a security cross-sell wave. If Security Core reaches its RPO-implied trajectory, it should exit FY2026 at 14–16% of ARR — a 40–60% increase from current levels, adding approximately $30–50M in incremental security revenue to the base. At 83%+ gross margins, this security revenue flows through to FCF at exceptional rates.

- **JFrog MCP Registry (March 18, 2026) is a category-defining first-mover move in agentic infrastructure.** Model Context Protocol (MCP) is the emerging standard for AI agent tool interoperability. JFrog launched the first enterprise-grade MCP registry — a governed, secure repository for MCP servers, agent skills, and agentic binaries — before any competitor established a credible offering. This is precisely how Artifactory captured the Docker/container wave in 2014: by being first to provide enterprise governance for an emerging artifact type. The agentic development stack is nascent; JFrog is already positioning as the trusted registry at the center of it. This will not appear in revenue for 12–18 months, which means today's investors are getting the optionality for free at 9x revenue.

- **The Cursor IDE plugin (March 31, 2026) embeds JFrog's security into 1M+ daily active developers before they choose their artifact stack.** Cursor is the leading AI-native IDE with over 1 million daily active users as of early 2026. By embedding Xray security scanning directly into the Cursor workflow, JFrog is creating a discovery and adoption pipeline that operates at the developer, not the enterprise procurement, level. Developers who encounter JFrog security in their daily coding workflow will advocate for JFrog adoption when their organization evaluates artifact management solutions. This is the product-led growth flywheel that infrastructure companies use to reduce enterprise sales cycle length — JFrog is executing it precisely.

- **The AWS Strategic Collaboration Agreement (SCA, signed Feb 12, 2025) neutralizes the hyperscaler bundling threat and converts AWS into a distribution engine.** The SCA means JFrog and AWS are co-selling — AWS enterprise salespeople are incentivized to recommend JFrog alongside AWS services. This structurally flips the hyperscaler threat: rather than AWS CodeArtifact displacing JFrog, AWS Marketplace consumption of JFrog Cloud displaces the concern. The SCA is a structural distribution advantage that Sonatype, Harness Artifact Registry, and GitHub cannot replicate without their own hyperscaler partnerships.

- **Platform Enterprise+ at 57% of Q4 FY2025 revenue signals the consolidation flywheel is working.** Customers buying Enterprise+ are consolidating their artifact management, security scanning, distribution, and MLOps onto a single JFrog platform rather than running point solutions. This is the highest-ACV, highest-retention customer motion. As Enterprise+ penetration increases from 57% toward 65–70%, JFrog's average revenue per customer rises, gross retention improves (more deeply embedded), and the natural cross-sell surface for Security Core, Runtime, and MLOps expands automatically.

## Section 4: Why NDR Compression Actually Strengthens the Bull Case

NDR declining from ~130% (FY2021) to 119% (FY2025) is the primary bear argument against JFrog. The argument is wrong. NDR compression in this specific context is a sign of platform maturation and contract structure evolution, not structural erosion.

- **NDR compression paired with +40% RPO growth is definitionally contradictory to churn risk.** If JFrog were experiencing structural erosion — customers leaving, spending less, or finding alternatives — you would expect to see: (a) RPO flat or declining, (b) $1M+ ARR customer count stagnating, (c) revenue growth decelerating below NDR. Instead: RPO grew 40% while NDR was 119%. $1M+ ARR customers grew from 37 (FY2023) to 52 (FY2024) to 74 (FY2025). Revenue grew 24%. These metrics are mutually inconsistent with a churn story. Customers are not spending less — they are locking in longer-term commitments on multi-year contracts, which mechanically suppresses the expansion component measured by trailing NDR while increasing forward revenue visibility.

- **The decline from 130% → 119% NDR reflects a fundamental change in contract structure, not customer behavior.** In 2021, JFrog's enterprise customers were predominantly on 1-year contracts with frequent, high-value expansion events measured within each 12-month NDR window. As customers have migrated to 2–3 year Enterprise+ platform contracts — which is the explicit Enterprise+ upsell motion — their annual expansion events shift from "in-period consumption upsell" to "pre-committed multi-year expansion" that shows up in RPO rather than in trailing NDR. The NDR denominator is growing (larger customer base with higher starting ARR), while the incremental expansion events are being captured in RPO. This is a textbook NDR compression pattern that accompanies platform consolidation, not churn.

- **119% NDR is still exceptional by any absolute standard.** The S&P 500 software median NDR is approximately 105–110%. At 119%, JFrog's existing customer base delivers $19 of organic expansion for every $100 of prior-year revenue — with zero new logo acquisition cost. Even if NDR compresses to 115% by FY2027, the installed base generates $15 of annual expansion per $100, supported by a growing number of $1M+ accounts (74 and growing). This is not erosion; this is the normal maturation of a customer base that has been substantially penetrated at the enterprise level.

- **The NDR compression from 130% → 119% is consistent with the law of large numbers, not competitive loss.** When JFrog's customer base was smaller and its average customer ACV was lower, it was easier to double a customer's spend (e.g., from $50K to $100K) — generating 200% NDR on a single account. As customers are now spending $500K–$3M, the percentage expansion per dollar of upsell is mathematically lower. The absolute dollar expansion per customer is likely higher. NDR compression at scale is the normal trajectory of every enterprise infrastructure company that successfully penetrates the enterprise tier — Datadog, CrowdStrike, and Snowflake have all exhibited this pattern following rapid enterprise penetration.

- **RPO growing 40% while NDR compresses 11 points is a signal to buy, not sell.** The divergence between trailing NDR (backward-looking, 12-month window) and RPO growth (forward-looking, contract-committed backlog) precisely captures the shift in JFrog's business model from short-term-expansion to long-term-commitment. Sophisticated investors should weight RPO growth and the $566M backlog conversion timeline more heavily than trailing NDR when assessing forward revenue quality. The market is pricing NDR compression as if it signals structural erosion. It signals the opposite: customers are committing to JFrog for longer, at higher contract values, with less negotiable exit windows.
