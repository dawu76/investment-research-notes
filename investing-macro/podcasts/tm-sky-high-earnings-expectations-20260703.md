# Sky-High Earnings Expectations Courting Disaster? | Lance Roberts

Detailed rundown and analysis of the *Thoughtful Money* Weekly Market Recap featuring **Lance Roberts**, Chief Investment Strategist at **RIA Advisors**, hosted by Adam Taggart. The episode was recorded and released on Saturday, July 4, 2026 (representing the market week ending Friday, July 3, 2026), coinciding with the nation's 250th Independence Day (Semiquincentennial).

---

### I. Executive Summary
Lance Roberts warns that forward earnings expectations for late 2026 and 2027 are priced at unsustainably high levels, typical of early-stage recession recoveries rather than late-cycle economic expansions. Roberts outlines the structural challenges facing the "sandwich generation" (Gen X and Millennials supporting aging parents while launching children) and advises prioritizing retirement savings over college funding to prevent long-term generational wealth drag. On the market side, Roberts shares the mechanics of RIA's factor rotation model, which recently completed a shift from growth to value and back to growth, rebalancing out of overbought defensive retail names and back into oversold mega-cap growth. He addresses headlines regarding Blackstone's Virginia data center pullback, clarifying that it was a local political zoning technicality rather than an AI demand bust, though real-world power and zoning friction will challenge analysts' rosy capex schedules. Roberts highlights a 10%–15% correction risk before the midterms, mimicking the 1998 Russian default/LTCM drawdown, and holds 10%–15% cash as a defensive shield.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Sandwich Generation Financial Guidelines
Roberts addresses the growing financial strain on Gen Xers and Millennials who must support both their children and their aging parents. He notes that the median retirement savings for Boomers is only $60,000, leaving a massive funding deficit. Roberts provides two critical guidelines:
1.  **Prioritize Retirement Over College:** There are no student loans for retirement. Funding a child's college at the expense of retirement savings increases the probability of becoming a financial burden on that child later in life.
2.  **Early Planning for Senior Care:** Waitlists for senior subsidized housing can stretch to three years or more. Families must consult state senior benefit specialists and apply for subsidized programs early, before a health crisis occurs.

> "Gen Xers... are caught in the middle. We've got this extra expenditure burden... to support our parents along with our kids... The median savings in 401k plans is like $60,000... Thou shalt pay for their kids' college is not in [the Ten Commandments]... Do I want to help my kids now and be a burden on them later?"
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 2. The 2026/2027 Earnings Expectation Mismatch
While Roberts expects Q2 earnings to be fine due to lowered short-term expectations for the Mag 7, he warns that forward earnings growth estimates for the rest of 2026 and 2027 are priced for perfection. The projected growth rates are typically only seen when an economy is emerging from a deep recession, whereas the U.S. is currently three-quarters of the way into a mature economic expansion. Any macro deceleration will force analysts to write down these estimates, leading to a repricing of equities.

> "The thing that worries me... about earnings in general is the expectations going out for the rest of this year and into next year are super high. We're looking at earnings growth rates expectations that normally what you see coming out of a recession, not... three quarters of the way into an economic expansion."
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 3. Factor Rotation Model (Growth vs. Value)
Roberts explains that the traditional buy-and-hold approach based purely on fundamentals has been challenged by rapid market rotations. In the first half of 2026, RIA's factor rotation model completed two full cycles: moving from growth to value, and then back to growth. During the latest rebalance, RIA trimmed overbought defensive value holdings (e.g., Walmart, Costco) that had run up, and redeployed the proceeds into mega-cap growth names (e.g., Microsoft, Apple, Palantir) that had become technically oversold near their 50-day moving averages.

> "We've made two full rotations in that portfolio and we're only six months into the year... There used to be a time where you could just buy the fundamentals of something and it worked... That's no longer the case... We just have to pay attention to where the money's flowing to."
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 4. The Blackstone Virginia Data Center Zoning Pullback
Roberts clarifies the headlines surrounding Blackstone pulling out of a massive 22 million sq ft data center project in Prince William County, Virginia, adjacent to the Manassas Civil War battlefield. Zero Hedge and other outlets reported this as the first sign of an AI demand bubble burst. Roberts notes that the site was defeated purely by a local zoning technicality (the county failed to issue a required public notice during the 2023 rezoning process, which was upheld on appeal in March 2026). At the same time, Blackstone sold three other data centers at massive profits and continues to build out other sites. However, Roberts flags that real-world frictions (noise complaints, Texas grid power caps, and local moratoriums like the one in Reno) will cap the speed of the AI buildout compared to analysts' Excel models.

> "This wasn't a demand story... What actually killed that data center was... they had 2100 acres... next to Manassas Civil War battlefield... The county approved the rezoning... it required public notice and that notice was never given... Compass walked back... QTS folded on July 2nd... Blackstone was selling three of their other properties at huge profits... This is a very local Virginia story."
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 5. 1998 LTCM/Russian Default Correction Analogy
Roberts compares the current late-cycle speculative environment to 1998, when a Russian debt default and the subsequent collapse of Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM) triggered a 15% market correction between July and October. Despite the severity of the correction, it was a healthy reset that cleared out speculative excesses before the final run into the 2000 dot-com peak. Roberts warns that a similar 10%–15% correction is likely before the midterm elections, which would simply return the S&P 500 to its April 2025 levels. RIA is hedging against this by holding 10%–15% cash and keeping bond duration short.

> "Darius Dale... is concerned... of a 1998 style market correction in the next quarter or two... In 1998 there was actually like a 15% market correction during between July and October... A 10 to 15% correction sometime... between now and the midterm election cycle is still a high [probability]."
> — *Lance Roberts*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Maintain 10%–15% Cash Cushion:** Carry 10%–15% cash in portfolios to act as a dry powder reserve and defensive buffer against a potential 1998-style correction.
2.  **Trim Overbought Value/Defense:** Follow the factor rotation model to trim overbought consumer staples (Costco, Walmart) and rebalance into mega-cap growth names at target weights.
3.  **Hedge with Short Duration Bonds:** Keep the bond portion of portfolios heavily skewed to cash and the short end of the yield curve to capture yield while avoiding interest rate risk before the midterms.
4.  **Establish Hard Exit stop Levels:** Put mental stop levels on high-flying semiconductor and tech holdings to protect capital, acknowledging that margin liquidations occur twice as fast on the way down.
5.  **Review Generational Financial Plans:** Prioritize personal retirement contributions over children's college tuition. Consult senior benefits specialists early to establish housing waitlist placement for aging parents.
