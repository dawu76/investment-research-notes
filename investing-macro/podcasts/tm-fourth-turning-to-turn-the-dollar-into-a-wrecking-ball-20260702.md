# Fourth Turning To Turn The Dollar Into A Wrecking Ball? | George Gammon

Detailed rundown and analysis of the *Thoughtful Money* interview featuring **George Gammon**, creator of the *Rebel Capitalist* channel, alongside lead partners **Mike Preston** and **John Loer** from New Harbor Financial. The interview was hosted by Adam Taggart and recorded on July 1, 2026, and released on July 2, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
George Gammon details a highly bearish macroeconomic outlook for Q3 2026, arguing that the structural mechanics of the Eurodollar system create a permanent "short squeeze" on the U.S. dollar, transforming it into a global wrecking ball. Net-importing nations are caught in a double burden: servicing dollar-denominated debts and buying energy in dollars while their local currencies depreciate rapidly. Gammon warns that the U.S. credit cycle is in its middle innings, pointing to halted redemptions in private credit (e.g., BlackRock) and falling valuations of speculative proxies like SpaceX, Anthropic, and MicroStrategy's "Stretch" preferred shares. To protect capital, Gammon advocates for market-neutral pairs trades (such as shorting Lennar homebuilders while being long the S&P 500) and accumulating gold and silver during their current cyclical pullbacks.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Eurodollar Debt Squeeze: Dollar as a Liability
Gammon stresses that most retail investors do not understand that the U.S. dollar is fundamentally a debt-based liability. When commercial banks lend credit, they create dollars out of thin air while simultaneously placing a dollar-denominated liability on the borrower's balance sheet. Because global entities (including foreign corporations and sovereigns) have trillions in dollar-denominated debt, they must continuously buy dollars on the open market to service this debt. This creates a structural "short squeeze" that prevents the dollar from experiencing a sustained crash, even amid massive domestic U.S. deficit spending.

> "Dollars aren't just an asset on a balance sheet. Dollars are simultaneously a liability on a balance sheet. And because they're a liability, that means there's future demand for every almost every single dollar that exists right now... And that is a bid. That's constant demand for dollars."
> — *George Gammon*

#### 2. The Double Burden & Cross-Rate Wrecking Ball
While the DXY index remains relatively stable (due to its heavy concentration in the Euro and Yen), the cross-rates for emerging and developed markets (such as the Japanese Yen at 162, the Indian Rupee, and the South Korean Won) show massive depreciation. Gammon explains that importing countries face a catastrophic "double burden": they must purchase inelastic commodities like oil in U.S. dollars while their domestic currencies are collapsing. To defend their currencies and avoid social unrest, central banks are forced to sell their USD foreign exchange reserves (specifically U.S. Treasuries and gold). Once these reserves are exhausted, a currency enters a death spiral.

> "The dollar is destroying our trading partners. The dollar is destroying our customers... It's because they've got to pull spending... to buy the dollars they need to get the future inputs. That's increasing the supply of yen, increasing the demand for dollars... and that works until you run out of FX reserves. Now you got a big problem."
> — *George Gammon*

#### 3. Halted Redemptions in Private Credit and the Credit Cycle
Gammon states that the global economy is in the middle innings of a classic credit cycle downturn. He highlights that major asset managers (specifically BlackRock) have halted redemptions on certain private credit funds. While critics argue that private credit is too small ($3 trillion) to cause a systemic event compared to the $60 trillion mortgage market in 2008, Gammon counters that banking systems run on confidence and perceived counterparty risk. When counterparty risk fears rise, credit velocity freezes, preventing the rollover of private debt and triggering a liquidity crunch.

> "The monetary system... really works on confidence and perceived counterparty risk. So if you have an event where perceived counterparty risk increases tremendously... the circulation of money and credit slows down. That means fewer dollars... and that just exacerbates the problem... That's what happened in 2008."
> — *George Gammon*

#### 4. The Demographic Threat to the Passive Index Bid
Gammon and New Harbor Financial highlight that the S&P 500 has been held up by the "mindless robot" of passive indexing (automatic inflows from 401ks). However, baby boomers hold $90 trillion in paper wealth. As they retire and stop working, their passive inflows stop and turn into outflows as they sell assets to fund retirement. Because this paper wealth is highly concentrated, any accelerated retirement selling will meet a lack of discretionary buyers below, causing paper gains to vaporize.

> "If the demographic shift turns those passive inflows into passive outflows, then that 90 trillion on the balance sheet, that ain't 90 trillion anymore... because those are unrealized gains. If you have a stock trading at 100 and there's no bid till you get to 50... your 90 trillion in purchasing power goes down to 45 trillion."
> — *George Gammon*

#### 5. Speculative De-Leveraging: SpaceX, OpenAI, and MicroStrategy
Gammon points to recent developments in private markets and crypto proxies as evidence that speculative liquidity has top-ticked. 
*   **SpaceX & IPO Delays:** SpaceX's price has pulled back to $150 (near its private reference baseline), while OpenAI and Anthropic have delayed IPO plans to 2027.
*   **MicroStrategy "Stretch" Preferreds:** MicroStrategy issued preferred shares ("Stretch") paying a high dividend. However, because its liabilities are in dollars and Bitcoin has declined, these preferred shares are trading at a 15% discount to par, exposing private credit and pension funds that bought them for yield.

> "Micro Strategy... their liabilities are denominated in dollars. They're basically in the exact same position as Japan... Now that Stretch is trading... at a 15% discount to par, I wouldn't be surprised in the next six months if you see a lot of these private credit funds that really have even more problems."
> — *George Gammon*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Utilize Pair Trades for Market-Neutral Bear Exposure:** Implement pairs trades (e.g., Short Lennar `LEN` / Long S&P 500 `SPY`) to capture the underperformance of interest-rate-sensitive homebuilders while hedging against general index melt-ups.
2.  **Hold U.S. Treasuries as a Stress Hedge:** Maintain a baseline allocation to short- and intermediate-term U.S. Treasuries. They are highly liquid and will benefit from a safe-haven bid when counterparty risk fears spike and commercial banks restrict lending.
3.  **Accumulate Precious Metals on Volatility:** Treat the 30% correction in gold and 50% correction in silver as a long-term accumulation opportunity. Avoid panic-selling and align allocations with a long-term target (e.g., 10% model weight split between 5% bullion and 5% miners).
4.  **Incorporate Non-U.S. Value Equities:** Diversify away from the overvalued, highly concentrated U.S. large-cap tech sector. Allocate a portion of equity exposure to cheaper international markets (e.g., South America/Latin America, India) that stand to benefit from pro-business policy shifts.
5.  **Monitor Private Credit Redemption Gates:** Closely monitor private credit vehicles and Business Development Companies (BDCs) for further redemption halts or preferred share par discounts (such as MicroStrategy preferreds) as early warning signs of systemic credit freezing.
