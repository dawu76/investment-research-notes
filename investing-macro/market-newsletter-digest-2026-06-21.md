Summary of key themes and observations extracted from investment-related newsletters received on June 21, 2026.

## 1. Macro & Federal Reserve: The "Warsh Regime" & The End of Forward Guidance

1. **Warsh's First Press Conference:** Kevin Warsh presided over his first Fed meeting, resulting in a unanimous 12-0 decision to hold rates steady at **3.50%–3.75%** (the fourth consecutive hold). Despite the hold, the hawkish tone spooked the market, sending the S&P 500 down **1.2%**, with rate-sensitive sectors hit hardest (Real Estate fell **2.5%** and transportation declined sharply).
2. **The Deletion of Forward Guidance:** In a historic shift ending two decades of detailed signaling, Warsh deleted forward guidance entirely. The FOMC statement was cut down to just **130 words**, and Warsh himself declined to submit a "dot" on the dot plot. By refusing to guide the long end of the curve, Warsh sent 10-year TIPS yields spiking immediately.
3. **Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) Revisions:**
   - **Inflation Upgrades:** Core PCE year-end forecast was raised from 2.7% (in March) to **3.3%** in June (a **60 bps** upgrade in a single quarter). Headline PCE was upgraded by **90 bps** to **3.6%**. A return to the 2% inflation target is not projected until **2028**.
   - **Growth Downgrades:** 2026 Real GDP growth was cut to **2.2%** (down from 2.4%). The Atlanta Fed GDPNow Q2 forecast has dropped from **4.3%** (on May 21) to **3.0%** (on June 17).
   - **Hiking Posture:** The dot plot inverted, with 9 of 18 voters now expecting at least one rate hike before year-end (up from zero in March), while only one voter expects a cut (down from 12). The forecast year-end fed funds rate moved to **3.8%**, above current spot pricing of **3.625%**.
   - **Yield Curve Flattening:** The 2s10s Treasury curve flattened from **44 bps** to **27 bps** in twelve trading sessions in June.
4. **Market Sentiment Metrics:** Gold continues to trade above **$4,150**, and the lumber-to-gold ratio sits at **0.15**, the lowest reading on modern record. 

> "He does not need to hike, he just needs to let the market figure it out on its own and the long end does the work for him... Bond market vigilantes are back and the Fed Chair is openly inviting them to do his dirty work for him."

## 2. AI Infrastructure & Capex: The Trillion-Dollar Capex Deluge

1. **Hyperscaler Capex Deluge:** The four largest hyperscalers spent **$416 billion** on capex in 2025 and have guided to **$725 billion** in 2026 (a **77%** increase in a single year). This massive spending is causing a significant drain on cash; for instance, Amazon's reported trailing-twelve-month (TTM) free cash flow fell from **$38 billion** to **$1.2 billion** in Q1 2026.
2. **Accounting Adjustments and Tax Tailwinds:** To cushion the impact on net income, every major hyperscaler extended the useful life of its server fleet to six years between 2022 and 2024. This non-cash accounting change added **$8-9 billion** annually to each company's net income. The *One Big Beautiful Bill Act* (signed July 4, 2025) also made 100% bonus depreciation permanent, pulling tax shields forward.
3. **The AI Debt Wave:** AI-tied investment grade corporate bond issuance hit **$121 billion** in 2025, a **332%** surge over the 2020-2024 average of **$28 billion/year**. Notable deals include Meta pricing **$30 billion** in bonds (September 2025) and Oracle pricing **$18 billion** (October 2025) alongside **$38 billion** in project finance. AI debt now accounts for **14%** of the JP Morgan US IG index.
4. **CoreWeave Balance Sheet:** CoreWeave carries **$14.8 billion** in debt against a debt-to-equity ratio of **4.5x**. It generated a negative free cash flow of **$7.25 billion** last year and remains highly dependent on Microsoft for **70%** of its revenue.
5. **The Revenue Deficit:** Microsoft's annualized AI revenue is **$37 billion** against LTM capex of **$97 billion** (representing only **38%** coverage). McKinsey reports that only **6%** of companies see more than a **5%** EBIT contribution from AI. Consequently, Pivotal Research projects Alphabet's FCF will decline by **90%** between 2025 and 2028.
6. **Comparison to the Telecom Bubble:** The current buildout mirrors the 1996-2001 telecom bubble, where carriers spent **$1 trillion** to lay 80 million miles of fiber, only for less than **5%** of it to be lit by 2004, leading to massive bankruptcies (WorldCom, Global Crossing, Winstar).
7. **AI Controls:** Google DeepMind published an "AI Control Roadmap," framing advanced agents as potential insider threats, while Anthropic reported Claude completed shared robot tasks **18-37x** faster than earlier human teams.

## 3. Power Grid Infrastructure: Transmission Dominance (US vs. China)

1. **US Grid Connection Bottlenecks:** The US grid is severely constrained: only **13%** of capacity that submitted interconnection requests from 2000 to 2019 reached commercial operations by the end of 2024, while **77%** was withdrawn. Additionally, over **90%** of US transmission funding is spent on short, low-voltage local reliability projects rather than long-distance high-voltage lines.
2. **Data Center Power Projections:** Data center power demand is projected to reach **12%** of total US electricity by 2028. In Texas, ERCOT tracks over **225 GW** of large load interconnection requests in late 2025 (up **300%** from 63 GW in late 2024), with data centers making up **73%** of those requests. Some sites are seeking over **1 GW** of capacity individually.
3. **China's Ultra-High Voltage (UHV) Dominance:** In contrast to the fragmented US system, China's centralized model has built massive ±1,100 kV UHVDC lines (such as the Changji-Guquan link) capable of transmitting **12 GW** of power over 3,293 km (enough to power **8 million** homes).
4. **Grid Spending in China:** The State Grid Corporation of China (SGCC), which generates **$546 billion** in revenue, plans to invest **¥4 trillion (~$574 billion)** in grid expansion and upgrades between 2026 and 2030—a **40%** increase over the previous five-year period.
5. **Cost Deflation and Equipment Monopolies:** Ember estimates utility-scale storage costs fell to **$65/MWh** by late 2025. In China, the UHV equipment supply chain is controlled by a regulated duopoly of SGCC and China Southern Power Grid, which command **94%** of the domestic market.

## 4. Software & Cloud SaaS: Net Dollar Retention (NDR) Compression

1. **NDR Compression and Multiples:** Analysis of 95 public software companies highlights a widespread compression of Net Dollar Retention (NDR) metrics since 2020. This compression is a critical valuation driver: every **10 percentage points** of NDR corresponds to roughly **1 turn** of forward revenue multiple.
2. **Valuation Impact:** For a SaaS company with **$250M** in NTM revenue trading at a standard **8x** forward multiple ($2B EV), a 10-point drop in NDR compresses the valuation to **7x** ($1.75B EV), wiping out **$250M** in enterprise value.
3. **Efficiency Benchmarks:** The industry standard for public software companies at scale requires revenue per employee to exceed **$450k**. Opacity on organic vs. inorganic ARR (e.g. failing to break out acquisitions) is increasingly penalized by the market as a valuation decision.

**Sources:**
- *Net Dollar Retention Benchmarks: Where'd All the 130s Go?* (CJ Gustafson / Mostly Metrics)

## 5. Market Sentiment & Sentiment Indicators

1. **Margin Debt Alert:** The expansion rate in margin debt has accelerated to the **fourth-fastest** rate in recent history. Historically, this rapid rise has been a reliable leading indicator of heightened market downside risk.
2. **Bonds as Diversifiers:** Following a historical drawdown since 2020 due to the inflation shock, bonds are reclaiming their role as diversifiers. Bonds are expected to outperform in the event of deflationary downturns where economic activity falls, inflation shrinks, and the Fed is forced to cut rates.
