6/12/26, 1:05 PM | jgold437: I personally feel that giving hedging "plans" is incredibly difficult to do. A hedge is completely different than an edge. An edge is a strategy that has a positive expectation; with defined reasons for the edge and in turn a trading structure to harness that edge. While a hedge is a trade that has negative expected value and whose sole purpose is to limit your exposure. But where to limit, how to express and by how much it is purely a personal decision. If putting the hedge on is your decision, so is what to do with the hedge?

However, all that aside you do need to take some risk to have some reward.

For example with a VRP strategy, the edge is over valued options. The method to express that edge is by selling options. Now say that you want to hedge a VRP strategy. Well what do you want to hedge? A rise in implied volatility? A price movement? What time frame to hedge; 1 d, 1w, the life of the option, a constant hedge?

In the end, I feel the best method for risk management is sizing of strategies.

All that being said, what do I do? I design each of my strategies with some general "hedging". Most of my risk management philosphoy has come from Dave Dredge, "Avoiding deep drawdowns matters more than maximizing arithmetic return"

Here are a few things I personally do:

I will never be net short options (calls or puts) in a strategy. Now which options I buy to hedge depends on which options I am selling. While I will always buy options with the same underlying, the DTE and Delta will depend on what I am selling. Partically, this means that for VRP trades, I almost always trade calendars.
,
For strategies that are short options, but the tail options are way too overpriced, in UVXY for example, I will always dramatically overhedge in the "correlated" product, SPY. I put correlated in quotes as correlations move and break down just as price does. If I am short some UVXY calls and the math tells me to be long 8x puts as a hedge, I'll buy 16x.
,
