Bottom line

  1. Not yet for the group. Even the analyst with Outperforms on PHM and TOL (Stephen Kim, Evercore ISI) says "I don't think this is the right time to buy this group," and may change his view "in the next six months or so." The October NAHB data deteriorated after his interview.
  2. PHM is the builder to accumulate early, but as a multiple re-rating on flat-to-lower earnings, not an earnings-growth story. Size it for a 2027 EPS decline.
  3. LEN: hold or avoid on a 12-month view. The floor at tangible book (~$78, Evercore) is near the price, so downside is limited, but there is no catalyst and land-option financing adds a margin headwind specific to Lennar.
  4. Balance sheets (§6): PHM (net debt ~3% of capital, nothing due before 2031) and TOL (15.6%, ~$3.3B liquidity) are insulated from debt problems in a longer slump. LEN is solvent but burning cash while paying out $1.36B and has $7.33B of land deposits at risk. OC is the most levered (2.4x net debt/EBITDA, ~$1.0B due within 12 months against $271M cash).
  5. ETFs (§7): ITB is the concentrated bet (~70% new-home construction, half in five builders, D.R. Horton 18%) and the right vehicle for the AI-capex hedge. XHB is only ~37% builders, with ~23% in commercial and data-center building systems and ~14% in home-goods retail, so it hedges less and adds AI exposure.
  6. OC is the cleanest risk/reward in the set: earnings are depressed by weather, not the housing cycle. Evercore's $185 target is ~62% above ~$114 (this page).

The industry backdrop (October 2026)

Builder sentiment is at a 14-year low (HMI 29) and 43% of builders cut prices in October, the most since 2020, as the 30-year mortgage rose to 7.40%. Stocks already trade at pandemic-era relative lows (XHB/SPY 0.1206).

Section 3

Owens Corning: the weather trade

Roofing earnings are depressed by two weak storm years, not the housing cycle. Evercore values the depressed earnings at a normal multiple: $185 vs. ~$114.

  • Evercore's top pick: target $185 vs. ~$114. Sum of parts on EBITDA: 11x roofing, 8x insulation, 6x doors. Two-thirds to three-quarters of profit is roofing.
  • Thesis: roofing demand is driven by storms, not construction. Last year no named hurricane made landfall for the first time in many years, and this is expected to be a weak storm year. Kim: "put a higher multiple on depressed earnings, not a lower multiple on lower numbers." Buying window "in the next three to six months."
  • Risks: weather timing is unknowable; insulation is tied to new construction, which is weakening.
Section 4

Catalysts and what would make the group investable

Kim wants builders to stay gloomy and cut production through winter, enter spring 2027 with lean inventory, and then get rate relief below about 6.5%.

Kim's sequence: builders stay "reserved, quiet, and unhappy" and cut production through winter, enter spring 2027 with lean inventory, then mortgage rates ease. He needs rates below about 6.5% for demand to improve materially, which "seems far away." He does not want demand to pick up early and get builders "overexcited into the late fall," repeating the overproduction of the past two winters.

Watch list:

  • NAHB price-cut share falling back from 43%, and incentive use below 60%.
  • Starts and spec inventory staying low through winter.
  • Freddie Mac 30-year moving toward 6.5%.
  • PHM Q3 (late October) and LEN FQ4 (December): margins stabilizing, no large impairments.
  • An AI-capex scare that pulls the 10-year sharply lower (see §5).
Section 5

Homebuilders as an AI-capex hedge

If AI capex falls, the Fed cuts and yields drop, which would help housing. The hedge fails if the same shock hits white-collar jobs.

22V Research's Dennis DeBusschere: "Real Private Residential Fixed investment has not contributed to GDP in 4 years as private fixed investment (AI capex) has accelerated." AI capex and consumer spending have kept growth above trend and the 10-year high. If AI capex fell, the Fed would cut aggressively and yields would fall, so "Long Homebuilders is an interesting AI capex decline hedge."

  • This is the crowding-out argument in ax-brij-khurana-wellington-ai-capex-crowding-out-bonds-20261001 and Goldman's "Competition for Capital" (market-newsletter-digest-2026-09-27). The closest precedent is 2000-2002, when builders rallied as the Fed cut and tech fell.
  • Where the hedge breaksKim says an "unprecedented number" of top-third earners fear losing their jobs within five years.

    Where the hedge breaks:

    Kim says an "unprecedented number" of top-third earners fear losing their jobs within five years. If an AI-capex decline also hits white-collar employment, rates fall but buyers still stay away.

    The hedge works through the rates channel and fails through the jobs channel.

Section 8

Risks

Rates, write-downs and margins with no visible bottom are the main risks; shrinking household formation caps the recovery.

  • Rates rising further (Fed hike, 10-year near 5.2%, bond-supply pressure in bond-supply-tsunami-2026).
  • Impairments turning book value from a floor into a moving target, most for land-heavy small caps, least for land-light PHM and LEN.
  • No visible margin bottom; investors expect gross margins 74bp lower in a year.
  • Labor delays from immigration enforcement and higher gas prices hitting traffic.
  • Shrinking household formation capping multiples after any recovery.
  • Rental and multifamily stress competing for the marginal buyer (multifamily-credit-stress-and-rem-2026-09).
