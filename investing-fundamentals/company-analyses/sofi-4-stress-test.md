## Bull Case

### 1. Long-run market size and SoFi's position in it

- **The bank charter gives SoFi a funding cost lenders without one can't match.** Deposits grew from $7.3B (end-2022) to $45.5B (Q2 2026), about 6.2x in three and a half years, and about 98% are insured. Deposits cost 156bp less than warehouse lines, which saves about $713M a year. That saving alone equals about 68% of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA ($1.054B).
- **SoFi is the scale player in prime personal lending.** Q2 originations were $14.8B in total, including $10.7B of personal loans. Upstart did $4.2B and Happen did $3.1B in the same quarter. Average origination FICO is 742, so SoFi is not chasing the subprime growth TransUnion flagged.
- **There is room to keep growing.** On-balance-sheet personal-loan principal of $26.1B is about 9% of a roughly $280B market. The charter also exempts SoFi from the state usury caps that constrain fintechs without one, and fintechs together hold 42% of unsecured personal-loan originations.
- **Members are compounding quickly.** SoFi has 15.8M members (+35%) and 24.4M products (+42%). Unaided brand awareness rose 190bp to 10.4%, so there is still plenty of room to grow it.

### 2. Revenue quality, measured as a bank

- **Margins are high.** Net interest margin was 5.98% in Q2 2026, roughly double what typical bank-funded lenders earn, and it rests on deposits rather than wholesale funding.
- **Fee revenue is a large share of the mix.** Fee-based revenue hit a record $443M in Q4 2025 (+53%), about $1.8B annualized, or about 37% of 2026 adjusted net revenue guidance ($4.75-4.85B).
- **Contribution margins in the newer businesses are rising.** Financial Services contribution margin went from 37% to 51% in FY2025 on +88% revenue. Lending held at 55% while revenue grew 63% in Q2 2026.
- **Recent loans are performing better than older ones.** Loans originated from Q4 2022 to Q3 2025 show 4.68% cumulative net losses with 35% of principal still outstanding. The 2017 vintage was at 6.43% at the same point, so recent losses are about 27% lower. All-in personal charge-offs of about 3.7% improved 70bp QoQ and 80bp YoY.
- **Tangible book is growing.** Tangible book per share rose 56% YoY to $7.34. The ~$1.6B equity raise accounts for about $1.24 per share of that. Even after removing it, tangible book still grew about $1.38 per share, roughly 29% (my approximation). There have been eleven straight GAAP-profitable quarters.

### 3. Signs from Q2 2026 that new products are working

- **Existing members are buying more.** 51% of new products went to existing members, up from 35% a year earlier. Products per member reached a record 1.54. Selling to current members lowers acquisition cost against $1.095B of sales and marketing spend (30% of revenue).
- **Home loans are a third lending business.** Originations hit a record $1.39B. Student loans added $2.69B, and student charge-offs were 0.61%.
- **The Loan Platform Business (LPB) earns fees without using capital.** LPB brought in $143.3M of revenue in Q2 (about 4.6% of the $3.1B of volume it handled), roughly 12% of total revenue, without holding those loans on SoFi's balance sheet.
- **Newer products are starting to show up.** SoFi Plus has 206K paid subscribers, and Invest (3.9M) and Crypto (388K products) keep growing. SoFiUSD now settles the card program over Mastercard's network, a payments business no state-chartered fintech can offer.

### 4. Why the scenario bears fear most supports the bull case

- **Fair-value marks pull back to par as loans are repaid.** KBW expects a hit of more than $1B before hedging, consistent with the 10-Q figure of -$1.04B fair value for +100bp. After tax that is about $0.60 per share, a one-time move in tangible book from $7.34 to about $6.74. With 25.8% annual prepayment and short loan terms, most of that mark comes back over roughly two years. The fair-value premium is $1.22B, only 4.7% of principal.
- **The marks already assume more losses than SoFi is taking.** Personal loans are valued with a 4.77% default assumption, while all-in charge-offs run about 3.7%. That gap of about 107bp absorbs part of a credit shock before the 10-Q sensitivity (-$211M per +10% in loss rates) applies.
- **There is a large capital cushion.** CET1 of 18.7% against a 7.0% minimum plus buffer is about $5.7B of excess capital on $48.7B of risk-weighted assets. Even the memo's stagflation case (+150% losses, +300bp rates) leaves CET1 at about 9.0%, above the requirement, with no forced raise.
- **Weaker competitors lose funding first.** Upstart and Happen depend on private-credit forward-flow deals with performance triggers. Those triggers can halt funding while CoreWeave bonds yield about 15% and tech CDS are at record highs. SoFi funds from $45.5B of deposits and has $13.7B of liquidity, so it can take share when competitors pull back.
- **The rate risk is two-sided and the entry price is low.** A 100bp cut adds $1.23B of fair value, more than a 100bp hike takes away. Meanwhile the stock is down 56% even though the 2026 guidance was raised. It trades at about 13-14x management's implied 2028 EPS ($1.03-1.12). Applying the memo's base-case 15x multiple to that range gives $15.5-16.8, before giving any credit to the 20-30% ROTCE target.

### Bull summary

At $14.52, the market is pricing SoFi as a lender heading into a credit cycle that impairs it permanently. The evidence points to a bank-funded franchise with 156bp of funding advantage, recent loans running at 27% lower losses, and fee revenue near 37% of the mix. The expected Q3 fair-value hit is non-cash, already cushioned by conservative default assumptions, and reverses as loans pay down within about two years. Capital stays above the requirement even in stagflation. Funding stress at competitors that rely on private credit would likely hand SoFi market share, so buying at about 2x tangible book before the October 27 print gives an asymmetric payoff skewed toward the memo's $23.8 bull case.

## Bear Case

### 1. Credit losses and rising rates hitting fair-value marks together

- **Personal loans are $27.5B, about 2.9x tangible book of $9.47B.** A 1% markdown on that book costs $275M, about 2.9% of TBV. The fair-value premium over principal is $1.22B ($27.5B vs $26.1B). If that premium went to zero, after-tax TBV would fall about $0.92B, or $0.71 a share (roughly 10%), and no borrower would need to default for that to happen.
- **The Q3 setup is already adverse.** The 10-K/10-Q sensitivity is -$1.04B fair value per +100bp. KBW expects more than $1B of negative Q3 impact before hedging. At a 25% tax rate, $1B is $750M after tax, or about $0.58 a share (8% of TBV). That is 2.3x the $327M of adjusted net income SoFi earned in all of H1 2026.
- **The loss assumptions have little room.** The personal-loan marks assume 4.77% defaults and a 4.97% discount rate. With the 10-year Treasury above 5%, that discount rate sits below the risk-free rate. Recent vintages already show 4.68% cumulative net losses with 35% of principal still outstanding, and management's stated ceiling is 7-8%.
- **Industry delinquencies are rising.** Personal-loan 60+ day delinquency was 3.98% in Q1 2026, up from 3.49% a year earlier (+49bp, +14%). In the memo's stagflation case (+150% losses, +300bp), the hit is -$6.3B pretax. TBV per share falls to $3.66 (-50%) and CET1 to 9.0%.
- **Capital is shrinking before any stress arrives.** CET1 fell 4.1 points in six months (22.8% to 18.7%) as risk-weighted assets grew 31%. If that pace continued without a raise, CET1 would be near 10.5% by mid-2027.

### 2. Competition and funding pressure

- **Rivals are growing faster.** Upstart originated $4.2B in Q2 (+50%) and Happen $3.1B (+29%). SoFi's comparable Q2 total was $10.7B, but $3.1B of it was LPB volume for third parties. Personal-loan borrowers shop on rate, so share gains have to be paid for in coupon or in gain-on-sale margin.
- **Gain-on-sale is sensitive.** SoFi sold $3.24B of personal loans from its own book in Q2. A 100bp lower sale premium on that run rate costs about $130M a year pretax, or roughly $101M after a 22% tax. That is about 12% of the $825M 2026 adjusted net income guide.
- **The private-credit buyers can stop buying.** LPB produced $143.3M of Q2 revenue (about 12% of the total) and $4.1B of loans moved through it in the quarter. Forward-flow deals often carry performance triggers. If funding halts, $4.1B a quarter would stay on balance sheet, which at a 15% CET1 target uses about $615M of capital per quarter.
- **Deposit costs are creeping up.** Time deposits due within a year rose to $4.4B from $1.2B (3.7x), which suggests SoFi is using CDs to pull in some of the $5.3B of Q2 deposit growth. The bank is liability-sensitive: +100bp costs $159M of net interest income a year, about 15% of adjusted net income after tax.

### 3. One-off items that flatter the trend

- **Taxes.** FY2024 net income of $499M included a $265M tax benefit, which leaves $234M without it. FY2025's $481M was taxed at about 8.5%. At the 22% rate guided for 2026, it would have been about $410M (-15%). Normalizing taxes is also why the 2026 adjusted net income guide stayed at $825M even after the revenue guide was raised.
- **Charge-offs.** The reported 2.62% personal-loan charge-off rate is helped by selling late-stage delinquent loans. Without those sales the rate is about 3.7%. That 108bp gap on $26.1B of principal is roughly $282M a year of losses that show up in sale prices rather than in the charge-off line.
- **Tangible book growth.** TBV per share rose 56%, from $4.72 to $7.34. At about 7% ROTCE, retained earnings explain only around 7-10 points of that. The rest came mostly from issuing shares, including the ~$1.6B raise at $27.50.
- **Tech Platform.** Segment revenue fell 23% and contribution margin dropped from 30% to 14% after a large client left. The segment carries $1.4B of goodwill (about $1.08 a share), and the annual impairment test was on Oct 1. SoFi took a $247M goodwill impairment in 2023.
- **Second-half guidance.** H2 adjusted net income has to reach about $249M a quarter, 55% above Q2's $160M, at the same time as the expected Q3 rate hit.

### 4. Multiple compression math

- **What today's price assumes.** At $14.52 the stock trades at 1.98x TBV. Justifying that multiple takes 17-20% ROTCE. Current ROTCE is about 7% (8.6% on 2026 guidance). At 20% ROTCE on the projected end-2027 TBV, EPS would be about $1.71, roughly double the $0.82 consensus.
- **Projected tangible book.** Adding retained earnings ($0.38 in H2 2026 and $0.82 in 2027) gives an end-2027 TBV of about $8.54 a share. At 1.0-1.3x that is $8.54-$11.10, which is 24-41% below today's price.
- **ROTCE stuck near 10%.** Using a 12.5% cost of equity, justified P/TBV is 0.67x (with 5% growth) to 0.8x (with no growth). On $8.54 of TBV, that is $5.70-$6.80 a share (-53% to -61%).
- **Bank-like earnings multiple.** At 10-12x 2027 consensus EPS of $0.82, the stock is worth $8.20-$9.84 (-32% to -44%). The memo's bear case ($0.55 EPS x 11) gives about $6.
- **Dilution.** A $2B raise at $14.50 would issue about 138M shares (+10.7%). The 2029 converts can add another 43M.

### Bear summary

The share price needs ROTCE to more than double while the balance sheet faces higher rates, rising consumer delinquencies and a fast-falling CET1 ratio all at once. Fair-value accounting brings that stress straight into book value: the expected Q3 hit alone is larger than H1 earnings. Strip out the tax benefits, the delinquent-loan sales and the equity raises, and organic returns are near 7-10%, which supports roughly 1x book, or $8-11 a share. The upside depends on 2026 guidance landing, loan buyers staying in and credit holding, and a downturn could break all three together.

### The 3 Critical Divergence Assumptions

**1. Balance-sheet stress: will credit and rate marks be temporary and cushioned, or will they wear down TBV for good?**

- **Bull version:** The Q3 mark is a timing effect that reverses as short-duration loans pay off at par. The marks assume 4.77% defaults against 3.7% all-in charge-offs, which leaves a buffer. Capital holds even in stagflation.
- **Bear version:** The marks have little room. The discount rate is thin, industry delinquency rose 49bp, reported charge-offs are flattered by delinquent-loan sales, and every mark hits TBV right away.
- **Why it's the crux:** A ~2x P/TBV multiple only means something if the denominator holds. If TBV falls toward the recession case (~$6.11) or the stagflation case (~$3.66), every valuation built on it falls too.
- **Verdict weight: Lean bull, moderate.**
  - Under the recession case (+50% losses, +100bp) the hit is -$2.1B, CET1 stays at about 15.5% and TBV at about $6.11. That is painful but leaves twice the required capital.
  - The 10-Q credit sensitivity is small: -$211M per +10% in loss rates.
  - For recent vintages to reach the 7% ceiling, losses would need to run about 6.6% on the remaining 35% of principal, late in the loss curve. That is unlikely outside a severe recession.
  - The bear compared the 4.97% discount rate to the 10-year Treasury, which is an error. Personal loans prepay 25.8% a year, so the right benchmark is the 2-year at about 4.8%. The spread is thin, but it is not negative.
  - The bull also made errors:
    - The 4.77% default assumption is likely a gross default rate, and the 3.7% is net of recoveries. Part of that "107bp cushion" is just recoveries.
    - "The mark comes back" overstates it. The mark is earned back through higher accretion over time, and the bank is liability-sensitive (-$159M of NII per +100bp), so higher funding costs offset part of that.
  - The verdict would move to bear if the Q3 mark lands well above the ~$1B pre-hedge figure, or if all-in personal charge-offs rise back above ~4.5%.

**2. ROTCE path: can returns roughly double from ~7-9% to the high teens without new equity?**

- **Bull version:** Contribution margins are rising, cross-selling is lowering acquisition cost, and fee revenue is ~37% of the mix. At 13-14x 2028 EPS of $1.03-1.12, a 15x multiple gives $15.5-16.8, and the 20-30% ROTCE target offers more upside.
- **Bear version:** 1.98x TBV implies about 17-20% ROTCE. Current ROTCE is ~7% (8.6% on the 2026 guide). Earnings have been flattered by tax and charge-off items. At ~10% ROTCE the fair value is 0.7-1.3x TBV, or $6-11.
- **Why it's the crux:** Price is the only lever if balance-sheet stress stays manageable. The stock already prices a large ROTCE improvement that management targets but has not delivered.
- **Verdict weight: Lean bear, clear.**
  - The 2027 consensus EPS of $0.82 on the bear's ~$8.54 end-2027 TBV is about 10% ROTCE.
  - Management's own 2028 EPS of $1.03-1.12 on roughly $9.5 of TBV is only about 11-12%.
  - The bull's $15.5-16.8 is a 2028 value. Discounted two years at 12.5%, it is roughly $12.2-13.3, below today's price. That matches the memo's weighted ~$14.0 (~$12 discounted).
  - H2 2026 needs ~$249M of adjusted net income per quarter, 55% above Q2's $160M.
  - The 22% tax rate (vs ~8.5% in FY2025) takes away the tax help earnings had in 2024-2025.
  - The bull's "~29% organic TBV growth" is not earnings. At ~7-8% ROTCE, trailing earnings explain about $0.40-0.50 per share. The rest of the residual has to come from capital actions or other equity movements, so the bear's 7-10 point figure is closer.
  - Bear errors with smaller effect:
    - The Tech Platform goodwill impairment threat does not touch TBV or CET1, because goodwill is already excluded from both.
    - The bear's end-2027 TBV of $8.54 ignores the Q3 mark. That error actually flatters the bear's own numbers.

**3. Funding and distribution: does the credit sell-off hand SoFi market share, or does it choke SoFi's loan-sale and LPB channels?**

- **Bull version:** Competitors that rely on forward flow lose funding first. SoFi's $45.5B of insured deposits at a 156bp advantage lets it take share.
- **Bear version:** LPB and whole-loan buyers can stop. Gain-on-sale margins are rate-sensitive, and CD growth shows deposit costs creeping up.
- **Why it's the crux:** About 12% of revenue (LPB) plus gain-on-sale on $3.24B a quarter both depend on outside buyers staying in. That is happening during a credit-spread sell-off.
- **Verdict weight: Lean bear, slight.**
  - In Q2 SoFi moved about $6.3B of personal loans off its balance sheet ($3.1B LPB plus $3.24B of own-book sales). That is roughly 59% of the $10.7B of personal originations, so SoFi is half a marketplace lender and exposed to the same buyers.
  - The bull's competitor argument contains a factual error. Happen (formerly LendingClub) has owned a bank since 2021 and funds with deposits; it does not depend on private-credit forward flow. Only Upstart fits the bull's description.
  - The bear also made errors:
    - LPB volume was $3.1B, not $4.1B.
    - A buyer halt does not force SoFi to hold those loans. It can cut LPB originations instead, which costs revenue rather than capital.
    - "Rivals are growing faster" is unsupported, because SoFi's own year-over-year personal-origination growth is not in the data.
  - The deposit advantage is real. Holding one extra quarter of sold volume ($6.3B of RWA) would take CET1 from 18.7% to about 16.5%, which is absorbable for a couple of quarters.
  - Time deposits due within a year rose 3.7x to $4.4B (~9.7% of deposits), a sign SoFi is paying up for growth.
  - On balance, the downside to fee and gain-on-sale revenue is more concrete than the share-gain upside.

### Closing Assessment

To own SOFI at ~$14.50 you have to be right that ROTCE roughly doubles to the mid-to-high teens without new equity, and that it gets there while a rate and credit-spread shock is hitting fair-value marks. Balance-sheet survival is likely, so that is not the question. At today's price you are paying slightly more than the memo's probability-weighted value (~$14.0, about $12 discounted), so there is no margin of safety. The skew runs from about $6 in the bear case to about $23.8 in the bull case. That calls for low-to-moderate conviction and a small starter position (about 1-2% of the portfolio). Add only after the October 27 print shows a Q3 mark near or below ~$1B, the $825M guide held, and TBV/share at or above about $6.75.
