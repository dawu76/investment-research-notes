## Bull Case

### Section 1: Secular TAM and Structural Market Position

The software supply chain is not simplifying — it is becoming exponentially more complex, and every dimension of that complexity expands JFrog's addressable surface.

- **AI/ML artifacts create a brand-new artifact category.** Software teams previously managed packages, binaries, and containers. AI teams now manage model weights, training datasets, embeddings, and agent tool registries. JFrog launched its AI Catalog in September 2025 and the MCP Registry on March 18, 2026 — making itself the system of record for MCP servers, agent skills, and agentic binaries before any competitor established a foothold. Every AI-native enterprise that runs models in production needs a governed artifact repository for those models. JFrog is the only universal platform that handles both the traditional software stack and the emerging AI artifact stack simultaneously. The TAM for AI artifact management did not exist three years ago; it exists today and JFrog is first in.

- **Software supply chain security mandates are mandatory budget items, not discretionary spend.** CISA's Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) and Executive Order 14028 require federal contractors and their software vendors to provide SBOMs (Software Bills of Materials) and demonstrate artifact provenance. JFrog Xray + Advanced Security is the architecture that satisfies these requirements at scale. Security Core already represents 10% of ARR and, critically, 16% of RPO — a 60% divergence that is the most important leading indicator in JFrog's model. RPO tracks contract commitments, not yet-recognized revenue. The fact that security's share of RPO is 60% higher than its share of ARR means enterprises are front-loading multi-year security commitments. The mandate-driven pipeline is converting at an accelerating rate.

- **Multi-cloud proliferation makes hyperscaler lock-in structurally impossible for large enterprises.** AWS CodeArtifact supports npm, PyPI, Maven, NuGet, Swift, Ruby, and Cargo — but only within AWS. Google Artifact Registry is GCP-only. Large enterprises running multi-cloud environments (the Fortune 100 standard) cannot use a single hyperscaler's artifact registry without creating cloud-specific fragmentation in their build pipelines. JFrog's universal, cloud-agnostic platform is the only architectural answer to multi-cloud artifact management. The more enterprises add cloud environments, the higher the penalty for not using JFrog.

- **The software proliferation megatrend continuously expands the artifact universe.** More software teams, more microservices, more packages, more containers, more Helm charts, more Terraform modules, more ML models — every increase in software complexity is a direct expansion of JFrog's total addressable artifact surface. JFrog's pricing scales with artifact storage and transfer volume, meaning growth in enterprise software output is a direct and mechanical revenue tailwind. JFrog does not need to win new markets; it needs only for software to continue to proliferate — which it does at a compounding rate regardless of the macroeconomic cycle.

- **The Cursor IDE plugin (March 31, 2026) demonstrates JFrog's developer-layer strategy.** By embedding Xray security scanning directly into Cursor IDE — with 1M+ daily active users — JFrog is inserting itself into the developer workflow at the point of code generation, not just at the point of artifact storage. This is an upstream land-and-expand mechanism that captures AI-native developers before they reach pipeline stages. No competitor is executing this strategy at this precision.

### Section 2: Revenue Quality Metrics

JFrog's revenue quality is exceptional by every institutional-grade metric. The combination of NDR, RPO trajectory, FCF margin expansion, and Q1 guidance shape invalidates the bear framing of 17.5% growth deceleration.

- **NDR of 119% means the existing customer base is a self-funding growth engine.** At 119% NDR, JFrog's installed base alone — with zero new customer acquisition — delivers approximately $100M in incremental annual revenue. The 74 customers spending $1M+ ARR and the 1,168 spending $100K+ represent deeply embedded, expanding enterprise relationships. When an enterprise's software output grows, JFrog's revenue grows automatically via volume-based pricing. This is not managed upsell — it is structural dollar expansion driven by the customer's own software factory scaling.

- **RPO of $566M (+40% YoY) is the most important number in the model.** Revenue grew 24% in FY2025. RPO grew 40%. The backlog is growing nearly twice as fast as recognized revenue. This is not a coincidence — it reflects the systematic shift toward multi-year, larger-commitment contracts as enterprises migrate to Enterprise+ platform subscriptions and co-sign with JFrog's AWS SCA framework. When RPO grows faster than revenue, the denominator (future recognized revenue) is pre-loaded and underestimated by consensus models that anchor to the headline growth rate.

- **Q1 FY2026 guidance of $146–148M (+28–30% YoY) directly contradicts the "deceleration to 17.5%" bear thesis.** Full-year guidance of $623–628M implies ~17.5% growth. But Q1 guidance implies 28–30% growth — nearly 12 points above the full-year guide. The only logical explanation is that management is guiding conservatively to full-year while RPO-converted contracts front-load strong H1 performance. The $566M RPO backlog at year-end FY2025 converts into revenue in H1 FY2026. Analysts who anchor to the full-year 17.5% guide without correcting for Q1 acceleration are misreading backlog conversion cadence as structural deceleration.

- **FCF margin expansion from 6% (FY2022) to 27% (FY2025) is one of the most disciplined operating leverage profiles in enterprise software.** This is a $125M increase in FCF over three years. On $531M in revenue, JFrog is generating real economic cash — $142.3M — while still investing aggressively in R&D, the AWS partnership, security product development, and AI catalog buildout. As cloud revenue (45% growth, 46% mix) continues to scale, incremental gross profit flows primarily to FCF rather than to incremental cost.

- **Non-GAAP gross margin of 83.3% confirms JFrog is fundamentally a software-economics business.** At 83% Non-GAAP gross margin, every additional dollar of JFrog revenue adds $0.83 to gross profit before any operating leverage. The 6.5-point spread between GAAP (76.8%) and Non-GAAP is exclusively stock-based compensation — a non-cash charge that does not affect unit economics.

### Section 3: Platform Expansion Signals

JFrog is not an artifact repository company. It is a software supply chain platform company in the middle of a product expansion that will double its addressable revenue per enterprise customer. The signals are measurable and accelerating.

- **Security Core at 10% ARR / 16% RPO is a textbook leading indicator of product inflection.** The gap between RPO penetration (16%) and ARR penetration (10%) means enterprises are committing to Security Core at a faster rate than they are consuming it. If Security Core reaches its RPO-implied trajectory, it should exit FY2026 at 14–16% of ARR — a 40–60% increase from current levels, adding approximately $30–50M in incremental security revenue. At 83%+ gross margins, this security revenue flows through to FCF at exceptional rates.

- **JFrog MCP Registry (March 18, 2026) is a category-defining first-mover move in agentic infrastructure.** Model Context Protocol is the emerging standard for AI agent tool interoperability. JFrog launched the first enterprise-grade MCP registry before any competitor established a credible offering. This is precisely how Artifactory captured the Docker/container wave in 2014: by being first to provide enterprise governance for an emerging artifact type. The agentic development stack is nascent; JFrog is already positioning as the trusted registry at the center of it. This will not appear in revenue for 12–18 months, meaning today's investors are getting the optionality for free at 9x revenue.

- **The Cursor IDE plugin (March 31, 2026) embeds JFrog's security into 1M+ daily active developers before they choose their artifact stack.** Developers who encounter JFrog security in their daily coding workflow will advocate for JFrog adoption when their organization evaluates artifact management solutions. This is the product-led growth flywheel that infrastructure companies use to reduce enterprise sales cycle length.

- **The AWS Strategic Collaboration Agreement (SCA, signed Feb 12, 2025) neutralizes the hyperscaler bundling threat and converts AWS into a distribution engine.** The SCA means JFrog and AWS are co-selling — AWS enterprise salespeople are incentivized to recommend JFrog alongside AWS services. This structurally flips the hyperscaler threat: rather than AWS CodeArtifact displacing JFrog, AWS Marketplace consumption of JFrog Cloud displaces the concern.

- **Platform Enterprise+ at 57% of Q4 FY2025 revenue signals the consolidation flywheel is working.** Customers buying Enterprise+ are consolidating artifact management, security scanning, distribution, and MLOps onto a single JFrog platform. As Enterprise+ penetration increases from 57% toward 65–70%, JFrog's average revenue per customer rises, gross retention improves, and the natural cross-sell surface for Security Core, Runtime, and MLOps expands automatically.

### Section 4: Why NDR Compression Actually Strengthens the Bull Case

NDR declining from ~130% (FY2021) to 119% (FY2025) is the primary bear argument against JFrog. The argument is wrong. NDR compression in this specific context is a sign of platform maturation and contract structure evolution, not structural erosion.

- **NDR compression paired with +40% RPO growth is definitionally contradictory to churn risk.** If JFrog were experiencing structural erosion, you would expect: (a) RPO flat or declining, (b) $1M+ ARR customer count stagnating, (c) revenue growth decelerating below NDR. Instead: RPO grew 40% while NDR was 119%. $1M+ ARR customers grew from 37 (FY2023) to 52 (FY2024) to 74 (FY2025). Revenue grew 24%. These metrics are mutually inconsistent with a churn story. Customers are locking in longer-term commitments on multi-year contracts, which mechanically suppresses the expansion component measured by trailing NDR while increasing forward revenue visibility.

- **The decline from 130% → 119% NDR reflects a fundamental change in contract structure, not customer behavior.** As customers have migrated to 2–3 year Enterprise+ platform contracts, their annual expansion events shift from "in-period consumption upsell" to "pre-committed multi-year expansion" that shows up in RPO rather than in trailing NDR. This is a textbook NDR compression pattern that accompanies platform consolidation, not churn.

- **119% NDR is still exceptional by any absolute standard.** The S&P 500 software median NDR is approximately 105–110%. At 119%, JFrog's existing customer base delivers $19 of organic expansion for every $100 of prior-year revenue — with zero new logo acquisition cost. Even if NDR compresses to 115% by FY2027, the installed base generates $15 of annual expansion per $100.

- **RPO growing 40% while NDR compresses 11 points is a signal to buy, not sell.** The divergence between trailing NDR (backward-looking, 12-month window) and RPO growth (forward-looking, contract-committed backlog) precisely captures the shift from short-term-expansion to long-term-commitment. Sophisticated investors should weight RPO growth and the $566M backlog conversion timeline more heavily than trailing NDR when assessing forward revenue quality.

**Bull summary:** JFrog at $45 represents a toll road on the global software factory, available at a fraction of intrinsic value. The secular tailwinds — AI artifact governance, CISA-mandated supply chain security, multi-cloud proliferation, and agentic development infrastructure — are structural reconfigurations, not cyclical bets. The Q1 FY2026 guide of +28–30%, the 40% RPO growth, and the 74 $1M+ ARR customers — each growing at an accelerating rate — form a mutually reinforcing growth engine that consensus is systematically underweighting by anchoring to the full-year 17.5% guidance. At 9x EV/Revenue with $704M in net cash, a $300M authorized buyback, and zero debt, the risk/reward is asymmetrically favorable.

## Bear Case

### Section 1: NDR Compression and Self-Managed Deceleration — The Structural Signal

- NDR has declined in a straight line: ~130%+ (FY2021) → ~125% (FY2023) → ~120% (FY2024) → 119% (FY2025). This is not a rounding error across four data points. This is a measurable, monotonic deterioration of JFrog's expansion economics — four consecutive years of compression with no reversal.

- The math on new logo growth is damning. At 119% NDR on a ~$428.5M base entering FY2025, existing-customer expansion contributed approximately $81M in incremental revenue. Total revenue added in FY2025 was ~$103M ($531.8M minus $428.5M). That means new logo contribution was approximately $22M — or roughly 21% of total incremental growth. The company is overwhelmingly dependent on the existing base to hit growth numbers. If NDR slips another 3 points to 116%, existing-customer expansion drops to ~$70M and new logo growth would need to double just to hold the growth rate flat.

- Self-managed revenue of ~$288.5M growing at +9% YoY is not a "transition story" — it is a structural anchor. With 54% of total revenue compounding at 9%, blended growth rates are mathematically constrained. Even if cloud continues at 45%, the arithmetic ceiling on consolidated revenue growth is approximately 24–25% — and that ceiling falls every quarter as self-managed's share of the base grows in absolute dollars.

- The "cloud transition" argument obscures a critical distinction: a portion of cloud growth represents existing self-managed customers migrating to JFrog Cloud, not net-new incremental revenue. When an enterprise moves from a $500K self-managed contract to a $600K cloud contract, JFrog books that as cloud growth. But the incremental revenue is $100K — not $600K. The headline 45% cloud growth rate conflates genuine new customer wins with revenue-neutral or near-neutral migrations.

- If NDR reaches 115% by FY2027 — a continuation of the historical 2–3 point annual compression — the existing-customer expansion contribution on a ~$625M base drops to approximately $94M. Against a total growth target of $110–115M needed to hit 17–18% revenue growth, new logo growth must supply $16–21M more than it does today. Given that 83% of the Fortune 100 is already penetrated and integrated platforms are capturing greenfield, that math does not close.

### Section 2: Competitive Bundling and Platform Displacement

- GitHub's 750 million developer base is not a competitive risk — it is a competitive fait accompli for greenfield. Every AI-native startup, every developer choosing a stack in 2025 or 2026, defaults to GitHub Actions plus GitHub Packages as the zero-friction path. GitHub Copilot's February 2026 Plan mode and custom agent capabilities deepen this gravitational pull: the entire agentic developer workflow now lives inside one Microsoft surface. JFrog requires a deliberate architectural decision to adopt. Defaults win.

- GitLab's artifact management roadmap is not a hypothetical threat. Maven support is live. npm and Docker are scheduled for H1 2026. Dependency Firewall is on the H2 2026 roadmap. This is a sequenced, credible buildout from a company with $955M in FY2026 revenue — growing 26% YoY, faster than JFrog's guided 17.5%. Every GitLab-shop enterprise evaluating JFrog now has a credible "wait 12 months and consolidate on GitLab" option. The switching cost argument dissolves when the incumbent platform covers the use case natively.

- Harness Artifact Registry reached GA in March 2026. Harness is not a startup — it is a well-funded DevSecOps platform with deep CI/CD integration. An embedded artifact registry in a CI/CD-native platform is architecturally simpler for mid-market engineering teams than deploying and managing JFrog as a separate infrastructure layer. With JFrog's Fortune 100 penetration at 83%, all organic new logo growth must come from the mid-market and growth segments — exactly where Harness competes.

- The 83% Fortune 100 penetration statistic, cited as a moat, is actually a saturation ceiling. JFrog cannot grow Fortune 100 penetration materially from 83%. The remaining 17% have chosen alternatives for reasons unlikely to change. Enterprise expansion now depends entirely on increasing wallet share within existing accounts — directly at the mercy of the deteriorating NDR trajectory.

- AWS CodeArtifact's bundled inclusion across npm, PyPI, Maven, NuGet, Swift, Ruby, and Cargo within the AWS native toolchain creates zero-cost competitive availability for every AWS-native developer team. The JFrog-AWS SCA signed in February 2025 is structurally defensive: JFrog needed the partnership because AWS's native offering threatened to make JFrog redundant for the cloud-native enterprise segment. Partnerships with hyperscalers do not protect against hyperscaler commoditization — they defer it.

### Section 3: Qwak Inorganic Revenue and Organic Growth Reality

- The Qwak acquisition closed June 2024 for $230M. Qwak had "several million dollars" in revenue in 2023 — call it $5–8M. With 12–18 months of JFrog's enterprise distribution network behind it, a reasonable estimate for Qwak's FY2025 contribution is $15–25M. Using the midpoint of $20M, JFrog's organic FY2025 revenue was approximately $511M — implying organic growth of approximately 19% versus the reported 24% headline. The gap between organic and reported growth will not be disclosed, and management will never frame it this way.

- The $230M acquisition price for a business generating $5–8M in revenue represents a 29–46x revenue multiple at the time of close. Even assuming aggressive Qwak growth to $20M by FY2025, the acquisition multiple on current revenue is approximately 11–15x — for a product competing directly against Amazon SageMaker, Google Vertex AI, and Azure ML, each of which is backed by hundreds of billions in hyperscaler R&D investment and bundled into enterprise cloud contracts at no marginal cost.

- At $20M of current Qwak revenue growing at a generous 40% annually, Qwak reaches approximately $39M by FY2027 and $55M by FY2028. The payback period on $230M at a 20% FCF margin on Qwak revenue is approximately 2030–2031 under optimistic assumptions. Against JFrog's total FCF of $142.3M in FY2025, the opportunity cost of $230M deployed into Qwak versus returned to shareholders via buyback is concrete and measurable — not theoretical.

- JFrog authorized a $300M share buyback in early 2026. This is not a confident capital allocation signal. This is management acknowledging that organic reinvestment opportunities do not exist at a return threshold that justifies the deployment. A company with genuine high-return organic growth opportunities does not buy back stock at 9x revenue. The buyback authorization, combined with the Qwak acquisition at 30–45x revenue, reflects a capital allocation posture that is simultaneously expensive inorganically and lacking confidence organically.

- Non-GAAP profitability of $92.1M masks a GAAP operating loss of -$91.9M — a gap of approximately $184M, almost entirely stock-based compensation. At ~1,800 employees, this implies approximately $102K in SBC per employee annually, on top of cash compensation. Investors pricing JFrog on Non-GAAP metrics are ignoring $184M of annual equity dilution that accrues to employees, not shareholders.

### Section 4: Multiple Compression Math

- Starting from $531.8M in FY2025 revenue, JFrog's own guidance implies $625M in FY2026 (+17.5%). Projecting forward at 15% growth — the midpoint between current guidance and a reasonable bear deceleration scenario — yields: FY2026E $625M → FY2027E $719M → FY2028E $827M. This is not an aggressive downside case; it assumes JFrog executes at or slightly below its own guidance rate consistently over two years.

- At 15% revenue growth in FY2028 and Non-GAAP operating margins of 17–18%, Non-GAAP operating income reaches approximately $140–150M. With a modest tax rate and maintenance capex, FCF reaches approximately $200–220M by FY2028 — a reasonable estimate given the FY2025 FCF margin of 26.8% and margin dilution from Qwak integration costs and continued SBC.

- At a 25x FCF multiple — which is the floor for infrastructure software growing in the mid-teens with 119% NDR — the implied FY2028 EV is $5.0–5.5B. Against today's EV of approximately $4.75–5.1B, this represents cumulative upside of 0–15% over two years, or an annualized return of 0–7%. That is not a risk-adjusted return that justifies holding a $45 stock with measurable competitive and structural headwinds.

- The more disciplined scenario: if NDR falls to 115% by FY2027 — a continuation of the 2–3 point annual compression observed over four years — the market will re-rate JFrog as a maturing infrastructure vendor rather than a compounding platform. At that point, a 20x FCF multiple is the appropriate comp set. At 20x on $200–220M FCF, EV = $4.0–4.4B, implying a stock price of approximately $34–38 — a 15–25% decline from today even as JFrog grows total revenue from $531M to $827M (+55%) over the same period.

- TD Cowen cut its price target from $80 to $70 on April 15, 2026, maintaining a Buy. That price target implies approximately 55% upside from $45. For that target to be reached, JFrog would need to re-rate to approximately 11–12x EV/Revenue on FY2027 estimates. That re-rating requires NDR stabilization, acceleration in new logo growth, and security cross-sell validation — all of which run directly against the structural trends described above.

**Bear summary:** JFrog is a real business with genuine switching costs, but the current valuation of ~9x EV/Revenue prices in an execution scenario that the company's own guidance contradicts: revenue deceleration to 17.5%, NDR at a four-year low of 119%, and 54% of the business growing at 9%. The NDR compression from 130%+ to 119% over four years is not a transitory headwind — it is a measured signal that integrated platforms are capturing new logos before JFrog lands and that the existing base's expansion surface is contracting. At $45/share, the math produces near-zero to negative total returns over a two-year horizon even in the base case, and a 15–25% price decline if NDR deteriorates one more standard deviation from its current trajectory.

### The 3 Critical Divergence Assumptions

#### Assumption 1: NDR Trajectory — Does It Have a Floor, or Is It Still Falling?

**Bull version:** NDR compression from 130% → 119% is a contract-structure artifact, not customer-behavior signal. As Enterprise+ penetration increases (now 57% of Q4 revenue), multi-year commits dominate; the 40% RPO growth confirms customers are locking in, not leaving. NDR stabilizes at 117–119% as contract mix fully normalizes.

**Bear version:** NDR has declined 2–3 points per year for four consecutive years with zero reversal quarters. GitHub, GitLab, and Harness are systematically winning greenfield before JFrog lands, shrinking the new-logo base that would otherwise enter as fresh NDR-accretive cohorts. NDR reaches 115% by FY2027.

**Why it's the crux:** NDR is the operating heartbeat of JFrog's entire model. At 119% NDR on a $531M base, existing customers deliver ~$100M in organic annual expansion. If NDR falls to 115%, that contribution drops to ~$80M — a $20M structural reduction requiring new logo growth to accelerate into an increasingly penetrated market. The FCF multiple the stock deserves shifts from 25–30x to 18–22x on this assumption alone, moving the implied equity value by 15–25%.

**Verdict weight: Lean bear [moderate].** Four consecutive years of monotonic decline with no reversal quarter is strong empirical evidence. The contract-mix explanation (multi-year Enterprise+ replacing 1-year contracts) is theoretically coherent but unfalsifiable without segment-level disclosure that JFrog does not provide. The bull case requires trusting management's narrative over four years of observed data. RPO growth is supportive of the bull but does not directly measure or predict NDR — it measures near-term revenue commitment, not long-term expansion velocity.

---

#### Assumption 2: Cloud Growth Quality — Is 45% Growth Genuinely Incremental?

**Bull version:** Cloud at 45% growth and 46% revenue mix reflects real new demand from enterprises choosing JFrog Cloud-native over on-prem. The AWS SCA co-selling agreement structurally adds a new distribution channel. As Enterprise+ cloud penetration increases from 46% toward 55%+, cloud revenue compounds above 40% for at least 2–3 more years.

**Bear version:** A material portion of JFrog's 45% cloud growth is self-managed-to-cloud migration revenue — enterprises converting existing $500K self-managed contracts to $550–600K cloud contracts and booking the full $600K as cloud "growth." This conflation inflates the headline cloud growth rate while masking near-revenue-neutral migrations. H2 FY2026's implied growth rate of 9–10% (if full-year +17.5% guidance is accurate and Q1 is +28–30%) suggests a second-half cliff that is inconsistent with a genuinely accelerating cloud business.

**Why it's the crux:** Cloud mix shift is the primary mechanism through which JFrog can sustain 20%+ blended growth despite the 9% self-managed anchor. If cloud growth is 45% genuinely incremental, blended growth remains structurally elevated for years. If cloud growth is 25–30% incremental and 15–20% migration-related, the underlying demand signal is materially weaker — and the H2 implied deceleration becomes a structural, not seasonal, problem.

**Verdict weight: Lean bear [narrow].** JFrog does not disclose migration vs. new-logo composition of cloud revenue, which by itself prevents definitive resolution. However, the H2 implied growth rate of 9–10% (derived from full-year guidance minus Q1 guide) is difficult to reconcile with a business growing cloud at 45% and executing an accelerating AWS SCA co-sell ramp. The AWS SCA provides a partial bull counterpoint — co-sell pipelines typically have a 6–12 month ramp before appearing in revenue, and H2 FY2026 could reflect SCA acceleration. But the burden of proof is on the bull to explain the H2 implied deceleration.

---

#### Assumption 3: Competitive Displacement — Is GitHub/GitLab/Harness Capturing Greenfield?

**Bull version:** GitHub and GitLab solve the developer workflow problem; JFrog solves the artifact governance problem. These are architecturally complementary, not substitutable. GitHub Packages handles 7 package types — JFrog handles 30+, including Docker at enterprise scale, Helm charts, Conda, and AI model weights. Fortune 100 enterprises and regulated industries require the governance depth that only JFrog provides.

**Bear version:** GitHub's 750M developer community means every new developer learning to ship software defaults to GitHub Packages + Actions — building JFrog-averse muscle memory that shapes enterprise procurement decisions 2–3 years later. GitLab's sequenced artifact buildout (Maven live, npm/Docker H1 2026) gives mid-market enterprises a credible consolidation path. Harness GA directly targets the mid-market — where all of JFrog's remaining greenfield opportunity exists (83% Fortune 100 penetration leaves only mid-market and growth companies as new logos). Three competitors converging on the exact surface JFrog needs to own for new logo growth is not FUD; it's a measurable pipeline constraint.

**Why it's the crux:** JFrog's existing Fortune 100 base provides switching-cost-protected stability. But NDR sustainability and multiple re-rating both require demonstrable new logo momentum. If JFrog cannot add meaningful new logos at $100K+ ARR entry points because GitHub defaults own greenfield and GitLab owns mid-market platform buyers, NDR compression accelerates as the existing base ages and cohort expansion velocity slows without fresh high-NDR entrants.

**Verdict weight: Lean bear [moderate].** GitLab's artifact roadmap is sequenced and credible, backed by $955M in FY2026 revenue growing 26% YoY (faster than JFrog's guided 17.5%). GitHub defaults win greenfield structurally. Harness GA specifically targets mid-market. The bull's "complementary not substitutable" argument is strongest in the Fortune 100 context but weakest in the mid-market segment where JFrog needs new logos. The Cursor IDE plugin is an intelligent counter-move, but it is too new to have generated measurable new logo impact.

### Closing Assessment

The single most important thing an investor must be right about to own JFrog at $45 is whether NDR has a floor at or above 117% — or whether the four-year monotonic decline from 130% → 119% continues toward 115% and below. The appropriate portfolio sizing implication is a sub-full position — no more than 50–60% of a target weight — held on a watch-and-add posture with a clearly defined trigger: two consecutive quarters of NDR at or above 119%, or Q3 FY2026 cloud growth at or above 35% (refuting the H2 cliff). Until that evidence arrives, the four years of observed NDR data outweigh the narrative of contract-structure normalization.
