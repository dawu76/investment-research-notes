# MacroVoices #538 Lyn Alden: Is The War Really Over and What's Next For Markets?

Detailed overview and analysis of the *MacroVoices* podcast (Episode 538) featuring **Lyn Alden**, founder of *Lyn Alden Investment Strategy* and author of *Broken Money*, hosted by Erik Townsend and Patrick Ceresna. The episode was released on June 25, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Lyn Alden analyzes the macroeconomic crosscurrents following the tentative Strait of Hormuz peace agreement. She notes that while the market is pricing out geopolitical risk—pushing WTI crude below its 200-day moving average to $69.18/bbl—the memorandum of understanding (MOU) leaves critical details unresolved, particularly regarding enriched uranium verification and post-60-day transit tolls. On monetary policy, Alden discusses the Federal Reserve's hawkish signaling under new Chair Kevin Warsh, whose dot plot indicates rate hikes to maintain institutional credibility. She presents a case for persistent U.S. deficits (mid-to-high single digits) acting as a powerful "run it hot" nominal driver for high-quality assets, supported by a unique dollar runway. Lastly, Alden examines the AI capex cycle, highlighting that the primary bottleneck is transitioning from chips to electricity, placing structural demand on natural gas. She remains highly skeptical of SpaceX's orbital data center narrative due to launch and maintenance constraints.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Strait of Hormuz Incomplete Peace
Alden emphasizes that despite public celebrations and falling oil prices, the memorandum of understanding (MOU) to resolve the Strait of Hormuz conflict is fragile.
*   **Divided Terms:** Opposing statements from U.S. and Iranian officials reveal a deadlock over uranium down-blending inspections and payment mechanisms. 
*   **The Rebuilding Cycle:** During the three-month closure, the global economy survived by depleting strategic and commercial oil stockpiles (including Egypt implementing an energy curfew to manage gas shortages). Rather than celebrating low prices, buyers must use this price dip to refill operational reserves before winter.

> "The MoU still leaves a ton of details to work out... what's going to happen to the enriched uranium?... Can Iran toll the straight after a 60-day period?... [During the war] Egypt had to do an energy curfew... this is the time to be refilling [reserves]."
> — *Lyn Alden*

#### 2. Hawkish Fed Transition and the 'Co-op' Option
Alden addresses the Federal Reserve's first policy meeting under new Chair Kevin Warsh:
*   **Establishing Credibility:** To avoid appearing as a political puppet for the administration's rate-cut demands, Warsh steered the dot plot toward a hawkish stance (anticipating a hike). The Fed will look through transient energy fluctuations and focus strictly on core services inflation.
*   **Fading Hyper-QE:** Alden maintains her "gradual print" thesis. The Fed will avoid emergency quantitative easing in favor of gradual balance sheet expansions.
*   **The Co-op Option:** Regulators are considering a "co-op option"—relaxing capital and liquidity constraints to allow commercial banks to hold larger quantities of Treasury bonds, providing a pro-liquidity proxy for QE.

> "[Warsh] has historically been on the hawkish side... in order to maintain credibility... they had to come in and say... the numbers are high... [As for liquidity] another option... is to find ways to let banks hold more [Treasuries]... deregulation on capital requirements."
> — *Lyn Alden*

#### 3. Deficit Dominance and the K-Shaped Economy
Alden targets the persistent misunderstanding of U.S. fiscal policy.
*   **The Long Runway:** Emerging markets (like Egypt) face immediate currency crises when deficits blow out because they lack external demand. The U.S. dollar, however, is supported by a massive global network of dollar-denominated cross-border debt, creating inflexible structural demand. This provides the U.S. with a much longer runway.
*   **The K-Shaped Divide:** Large deficits and AI capex act as a "run it hot" nominal driver for high-quality assets. This benefits asset owners and corporations that locked in long-term debt. Conversely, young families are crushed by housing unaffordability and high mortgage rates, fueling political populism.

> "Bears... keep underestimating what the market can do... what they're missing is the fiscal side... large fiscal deficits [are] generally pretty good for the nominal price of assets... but it shows up in this two-speed economy."
> — *Lyn Alden*

#### 4. Stablecoins as Deficit Extensions
Stablecoins have grown to $300 billion and are heading toward $1 trillion.
*   **Limited Patch:** While a $1 trillion stablecoin market backing 50-75% in short-term Treasuries is positive, it only finances 3-5 months of U.S. deficits. It is a helpful variable, not a permanent fix.
*   **Yieldless working Capital:** Stablecoins function as yieldless working capital for trade (e.g., bypassing currency frictions in Africa's 40 currencies), rather than sovereign reserve savings.
*   **Bottom-Up Demand:** In Nigeria, stablecoins are adopted organically for digital payments, whereas Egypt remains a gray-market physical cash economy.

> "Stable coins... extend what is already a pretty long runway for... the treasury, but it's not like a... permanent band-aid... [A trillion dollar market cap] is at four or five months of deficits."
> — *Lyn Alden*

#### 5. AI Capex and the Energy Bottleneck
*   **Memory Demand:** Micron's blowout earnings show that physical hardware requirements (DRAM/HBM) are structural and sustainable.
*   **Model Moat Fallacy:** Front-end LLM software models possess no economic moats due to low customer switching costs. The real value is captured by physical bottlenecks: chips and energy.
*   **Natural Gas as the Bridge:** AI data center energy demand is growing exponentially. While data center developers are investing in nuclear power, reactors take 10 years to build. Natural gas is the only bridge fuel capable of meeting power requirements in the next 5-10 years.
*   **SpaceX Orbital Data Centers:** SpaceX bulls claim launching data centers into orbit allows solar efficiency and natural cooling. Alden is highly skeptical for any investable time horizon (5-10 years) due to high launch costs, fuel burn, and lack of maintenance access.

> "I don't see a lot of economic moat in the AI models... [The bottleneck is] energy... nuclear... takes 10 years... meanwhile, what are we going to do?... Natural gas can help... [And orbital data centers] are eaten up by launch costs and lack of maintenance."
> — *Lyn Alden*
