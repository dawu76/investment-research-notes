# Brian Belski Returns! | The Compound and Friends [TCAF 249]

Detailed overview of the interview featuring Brian Belski, Founder, CEO, and CIO of Humilis Investment Strategies, on *The Compound and Friends* (Episode 249), published on July 3, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Brian Belski, in his 36th year on Wall Street, remains steadfastly bullish on the U.S. stock market, reiterating his target of S&P 7,000 and his long-term thesis of a 25-year secular bull market with 7 to 10 years left to run. He argues that the market is transitioning from a momentum-driven phase to a fundamentally solid, earnings-driven regime, which will lead to a "second derivative less positive" rate of earnings growth and annual returns of 10% to 12%. Pushing back on the AI bubble label, Belski advocates for a broadening market trade utilizing a barbell allocation: megacap quality tech on one side and small/mid-caps (SMID) and value on the other. He also identifies regional bank M&A and exchange valuations as highly attractive opportunities.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. Transition to an Earnings-Driven Market
Belski highlights that the market is shifting away from pure momentum and multiple expansion toward a fundamentally healthy, earnings-driven regime. While this means annual gains will moderate to a historical normal of 10% to 12% ("second derivative less positive"), it makes the bull market far more sustainable.
*   **Earnings Normalization:** S&P 500 earnings growth may slow from a rapid 25% pace to 20%, which will trigger short-term volatility among momentum-chasing investors.
*   **Secular Bull Intact:** Belski reiterates his view that the 25-year secular bull market has 7 to 10 years left, rejecting calls of an imminent structural bear market.

> "We're still bullish, but I think the headline would be beware of the second derivative less positive... typical returns on an annual basis in an earnings-driven market are something like 10 to 12%."
> — *Brian Belski*

#### 2. The Broadening Trade and Barbell Allocation
Belski pushes back against the narrative that the AI trade is a bubble, pointing to the increasingly uncorrelated earnings reactions within the Magnificent 7 (dispersion). He advocates for a barbell portfolio approach:
*   **Megacap Tech:** Focus on highly cash-generative, high-ROE giants. He specifically points to Microsoft as a key focus, noting its 34% ROE and $78B balance sheet cash.
*   **Small and Mid-Caps (SMID):** Position in small-caps (SML) and mid-caps, where forward earnings growth is projected at 28% (vs. 23-24% for large-caps), trading at significant discounts.

> "As long as we see that broadening though, it's very consistent... next 12-month earnings growth for small-cap SML is projected around 28% versus 23-24% for large-cap."
> — *Brian Belski*

#### 3. Regional Bank M&A and Consolidation
In the financial sector, Belski favors a barbell of the very large money-center banks (Chase) and the very small local banks (e.g., Pinnacle, Cenovas).
*   **Stuck in the Middle:** Mid-sized regional banks lack the capital markets scale of the giants and the relationship power of the local banks, making them prime targets for a wave of consolidation.
*   **Super-Regionals:** He expects regionals like PNC and Citizens to merge, creating "super-regionals" that dominate middle-market and commercial lending.

> "The theme on the regional bank side is consolidation... the really small guys win business on local relationships, while the mid-sized ones don't have the relationship power and are stuck in the middle."
> — *Brian Belski*

#### 4. Valuations in Exchanges and Market Infrastructure
Following recent sell-offs, Belski sees significant value in financial exchanges (CME, SIBO, ICE, NASDAQ).
*   **Overdone Sell-off:** Markets overreacted to potential threats from platforms like Kalshi's perpetual listings.
*   **Brokerage Value:** Schwab looks highly interesting after correcting to attractive valuation multiples.

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items
*   **Barbell Allocation:** Maintain core positions in high-quality megacap software/tech (Microsoft, Meta) while aggressively accumulating small-cap (SML) value and dividend-growth names.
*   **Overweight Financial Exchanges:** Position in CME, ICE, or SIBO for cheap, high-margin cash flows insulated from economic cycles.
*   **Accumulate Small-Cap (SML) Quality:** Filter the Russell 2000 to buy SML stocks with positive price-to-free cash flow and earnings consistency, avoiding unprofitable beta.
*   **Avoid Mid-Sized Regionals:** Underweight mid-sized regionals unless positioning for acquisition arbitrage in the oncoming M&A wave.
