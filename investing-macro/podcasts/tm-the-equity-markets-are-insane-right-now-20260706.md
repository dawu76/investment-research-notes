# The Equity Markets Are Insane Right Now | Thoughtful Money

Detailed rundown and analysis of the *Thoughtful Money* interview featuring **Jan Van Eck**, CEO of VanEck (managing over $230 billion in assets), hosted by Adam Taggart. The interview was recorded and released on July 6, 2026, shortly after the U.S. 250th Independence Day.

---

### I. Executive Summary
VanEck CEO Jan Van Eck presents a constructive Q3 2026 outlook, arguing that while public equity markets appear "insane" on absolute price metrics, the current tech rally is structurally supported by historic corporate profit growth. Van Eck categorizes the AI landscape into vertically integrated winners with strong customer-facing moats (e.g., Alphabet, Amazon) and vulnerable commodity-like hardware suppliers (e.g., Samsung, SK Hynix). While maintaining a long-term overweight on AI, gold, Bitcoin, and India, Van Eck highlights near-term opportunities in Business Development Companies (BDCs) and private credit, advising investors to remain fully invested while selectively trimming overextended hardware assets.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. Real Profits vs. Speculative Bubbles (Direct Contrast to Fred Hickey)
In direct contrast to Fred Hickey's thesis that the market is in an unsustainable "accounting mismatch earnings bubble," Jan Van Eck argues that the current valuations are fundamentally anchored by massive, high-margin corporate profits. 
*   **Valuation vs. Long-Term Averages:** The top 10 companies in the S&P 500 trade at 21.6x forward P/E, which is in line with their multi-decade historical average of 20.8x. This demonstrates that profit growth has expanded fast enough to absorb the price appreciation.
*   **Operating Margins of 50%:** In 2000, tech companies traded at high sales multiples with little to no earnings (e.g., Sun Microsystems at 10x sales with low margins). Today's hyperscalers operate at 50% margins and generate massive cash flow that is returned via buybacks and dividends.
*   **Price-to-Sales Limitations:** Price-to-sales ratios are high simply because high-margin, asset-light tech giants dominate a larger share of the S&P 500's market capitalization, rather than indicating a market-wide speculative bubble.

> "The profit growth has caught up and now the valuations of the top 10... are almost the same as their average over this time period... The average is about 20.8 times forward earnings, and they're about 21.6 times. So, there's not some kind of valuation distortion... and that's why I think we can be... comfortable just riding this wave."
> — *Jan Van Eck*

#### 2. The Moat Framework: Vertically Integrated vs. Commodity Hardware
Van Eck advises investors to look at the tech stack through a 10-year survival lens, categorizing companies based on their customer-facing integration and pricing power.
*   **Vertically Integrated Giants (Strong Moats):** Companies like Alphabet (Google), Amazon, and SpaceX control the entire value chain (data centers, private models, user applications, and customer interfaces). They benefit from economies of scale and network effects (e.g., Google using Gemini summaries to lock users into its search ecosystem).
*   **Vulnerable Hardware Suppliers (Weak Moats):** Memory chip makers (Samsung, Hynix) and commodity processor manufacturers (Intel, Cerebras) lack durable competitive advantages. Their current record-high profitability is driven solely by cyclical price hikes during temporary supply bottlenecks, rather than permanent structural changes. 

> "Alphabet or Google, Amazon and SpaceX kind of fall into that [vertically integrated] category... We're a little concerned about the memory companies... do they have a moat that in 5 years will go away? ... Most of their profits... just come because they jacked up prices. They're not even increased volumes that much. That's why I'm a little worried about them."
> — *Jan Van Eck*

#### 3. Headwinds & The "Naked Emperor" in Single-Model Software
While bullish on the infrastructure layer, Van Eck is notably skeptical of single-LLM model providers, citing Anthropic (Claude) as a primary example of a business facing severe headwind.
*   **Corporate Cost Containment:** Enterprises are actively optimizing their token spend. VanEck's internal data shows that while their token usage doubled over the last quarter, their actual dollar spend leveled off as developers routed queries to lower-cost models.
*   **Open-Source Chinese Disruption:** High-performing, low-cost Chinese open-source models have reached parity with Claude on many business tasks. For non-time-critical enterprise applications, businesses are swapping expensive frontier models for cheaper alternatives.
*   **Trust and IP Security Concerns:** Highlighting CNBC interviews with Palantir CEO Alex Karp, Van Eck notes that corporate clients increasingly worry about model providers "learning" from corporate data to build competing products (e.g., Figma's 73% stock decline after partner copycat disruption). Enterprises are rotating to private, multi-model middleware architectures to protect intellectual property.

> "I think the emperor is naked here a little bit... Anthropic is facing these headwinds... Corporations are cost-conscious... There's a lot of competition... The Chinese models have caught up with Claude... and companies don't trust Anthropic anymore because they're basically learning how those businesses run and in some cases competing with them."
> — *Jan Van Eck*

#### 4. Gold, Bitcoin, & Sovereign Debt Gating
Van Eck outlines a 10-year structural thesis for gold and Bitcoin based on the compounding growth of U.S. government debt and interest expenses, which now exceed the U.S. defense budget.
*   **Bitcoin Entry Point:** VanEck's internal consensus points to the Q2/Q3 correction down to the $60k range as an attractive tactical buying window ahead of October.
*   **Gold as an Asian Wealth Story:** Unlike traditional frameworks that link gold to U.S. consumer price inflation, gold is driven by Asian central bank and private wealth accumulation. The recent correction in gold prices is a temporary liquidity reaction to the Strait of Hormuz closure and the Iran war, which impacted Asian trading capital.

> "Gold is not driven by inflation in the United States... It's more of an Asian wealth story. So it's no surprise when Asia gets a gut punch from the Strait of Hormuz closure and the Iran war that gold sells off. That's just not a surprise to me... [Gold and Bitcoin] are 10-year trades."
> — *Jan Van Eck*

#### 5. Private Credit & BDC Resilience
Private credit and Business Development Companies (BDCs) offer highly attractive yields (9%+) that are fully supported by low corporate default rates.
*   **Overblown Credit Concerns:** Despite market fears regarding private credit fragility, high-yield defaults remain historically low.
*   **Reinvestment Cash Flows:** BDC assets like Ares and Blue Owl have bottomed out and represent strong cash-flow plays. Even if capital appreciation remains flat, the 9% dividend yield provides a strong defensive buffer.

> "I love BDCs. You're still getting this 9 plus percent yield... Ares and Blue Owl... their dividend yield is like 9%. So, even if the stock goes nowhere, I've made 9% over the course of a year. I'm perfectly happy with this because I'm getting paid in the meantime."
> — *Jan Van Eck*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Maintain High Equity Allocation:** Remain invested in core equities, as corporate profit growth is expanding sufficiently to absorb current valuations.
2.  **Overweight Vertically Integrated Tech (Moats):** Prioritize customer-facing tech giants (e.g., Alphabet, Amazon) that control the entire tech stack, generate recurring free cash flow, and execute share buybacks.
3.  **Trim Cyclical Hardware (Trim the Top):** Take profits on cyclical hardware, memory manufacturers (e.g., Samsung, Hynix), and commoditized chip suppliers whose valuations are built on temporary bottlenecks rather than structural moats.
4.  **Allocate to BDCs for Yield:** Accumulate high-quality BDCs (e.g., Blue Owl, Ares) to secure a defensive 9%+ dividend yield, taking advantage of the Q1 sell-off.
5.  **Utilize Gold & BTC Corrections as 10-Year Buys:** Accumulate gold and Bitcoin during current geopolitical corrections. Treat Bitcoin's pullbacks to $60k as structural buying opportunities.
