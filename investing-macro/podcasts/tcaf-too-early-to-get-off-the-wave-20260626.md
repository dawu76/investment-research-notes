# Too Early to Get Off the Wave | TCAF 248

Detailed rundown and analysis of *The Compound and Friends* (TCAF) Episode 248, released on June 26, 2026. This episode features hosts Downtown Josh Brown and Michael Batnick, with guest strategists **Ryan Detrick** (Chief Market Strategist) and **Sonu Varghese** (Chief Macro Strategist) from the **Carson Group**.

---

### I. Executive Summary
This episode features a constructive discussion on the resilience of the current bull market, arguing that the three-year-old rally is structurally supported by real corporate earnings and massive hyperscaler Capex rather than speculative vapor. The panel emphasizes a healthy rotation out of mega-cap tech concentration into small caps, financials, and industrials, advising investors to maintain equity exposure and implement judicious diversification rather than timing a market exit.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. Bull Market Health: "Breadth Leads Price"
The current market cycle is undergoing a healthy transition. While mega-cap tech stocks (the "Magnificent 7") have shown short-term consolidation, the rally is broadening.
*   **The "Cruise Ship" Analogy:** Bull markets are like cruise ships; they possess massive momentum and are very difficult to stop abruptly once they are moving.
*   **Healthy Rotation:** The slowdown in mega-caps is actually a bullish signal for the broader market as capital rotates into laggard sectors like industrials, financials, and small-caps, creating a defensive cushion.
*   **Earnings Support:** Unlike the speculative bubble of 2021, the current rally is anchored by real corporate profit growth.

> "The bull market is not yet over, it is supported by real corporate earnings and economic spending rather than mere speculation. It is too early to get off the wave."
> — *Ryan Detrick*

#### 2. Macro Backdrop & Federal Reserve Policy
The macro environment is marked by two structural shifts: the debut of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh and the passing of former Chair Alan Greenspan.
*   **The Greenspan Legacy:** Greenspan passed away on June 22, 2026, at the age of 100. The panel discussed his 18-year tenure, noting his role in driving asset-price-led economic growth (the "Greenspan Put") but also his obscure communication style ("Fedspeak").
*   **Fed Transparency vs. Flexibility:** While Greenspan was famous for saying, *"If I turn out to be particularly clear, you've probably misunderstood what I've said,"* he frequently communicated his views on economic mechanics. The modern Fed under Warsh may prefer less explicit forward guidance to maintain the flexibility needed to respond to high-frequency data.
*   **Rate Expectations:** Despite "sticky" inflation data (consumer prices accelerating to 4.1%) driving hawkish market expectations for July and October rate hikes, real policy rates remain accommodative. 

#### 3. The AI and Semiconductor Capital Expenditure Wave
Hyperscaler capital expenditures (Capex) are driving structural, high-margin revenue growth across the semiconductor supply chain.
*   **Micron Technology (MU) Case Study:** Micron's blowout fiscal Q3 2026 earnings ($41.46B revenue vs. $23.86B in Q2; $28.24B GAAP net income; Q4 revenue guidance of $50B) is concrete evidence of real AI hardware demand. Micron serves as a critical "choke point" for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM3e) and SSDs.
*   **AI Capex vs. Dot-Com Bubble:** The late 1990s tech bubble was built on speculative capacity additions by telecom companies with no revenue or earnings. Today, AI Capex is funded by cash-rich, highly profitable hyperscalers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon) and flows directly to companies showing real-world product sales and net income.

> "Micron's earnings show that the AI demand chain is not just a hype-driven bubble. Hyperscalers are spending real capital on memory chips and servers. This is backed by capital expenditure and real profit growth."
> — *Ryan Detrick*

#### 4. Credit Markets and Small Caps
A key thesis of Carson Group is that credit indicators and small-cap breakouts are flashing green lights for economic expansion.
*   **Small-Cap Breakout:** The iShares Russell 2000 ETF ([[IWM]]) is breaking out, signaling that liquidity is rotating into broader, economically sensitive segments of the market.
*   **Regional Banks as a Lifeline:** Regional banks are the credit lifelines for small-and-medium businesses. Their stabilization and relative strength indicate that credit is flowing smoothly.
*   **Credit Spreads:** Credit spreads remain tight and stable. If there were a systemic macroeconomic contraction on the horizon, spreads would be widening. 

> "Regional banks represent the credit lifeline of small-to-medium businesses. If regional banks are stabilizing and credit spreads are tight, it's very difficult to support a macroeconomic collapse thesis."
> — *Sonu Varghese*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Maintain Equity Exposure:** Avoid the temptation to time the market top or exit equities. The fundamental earnings wave remains intact.
2.  **Diversify Into Cyclicals:** Implement "judicious diversification" by rotating a portion of mega-cap tech gains into small caps ([[IWM]]), industrials, and financials (specifically regional banks).
3.  **Focus on Credit and Breadth:** Monitor [[credit-spread-investing-guide-20251019-notes]] and market breadth rather than focusing solely on inflation headlines. Tight spreads and widening market participation suggest that pullbacks are buying opportunities.
4.  **Be Selective in AI:** Look for structural "choke points" in the AI supply chain (like DRAM/HBM connectivity, similar to the connectivity thesis for [[alab]]) rather than speculative SaaS plays.
