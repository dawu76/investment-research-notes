# The $400 Billion Gap | Warren Pies on Token Panic, a Trapped Fed and What Murders Bull Markets

Detailed overview and analysis of the *Excess Returns* podcast featuring **Warren Pies**, co-founder of 3Fourteen Research, hosted by Matt Ziegler. The episode was released on July 2, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Warren Pies outlines the structural "crowding out" effect in the U.S. economy, where IT and software fixed investment ($1.5 trillion) has dramatically overtaken residential fixed investment ($1.1 trillion). This **$400 Billion Gap** pulls trade labor and resources away from housing, creating a policy dilemma for the Federal Reserve: raising interest rates will not stop rate-insensitive hyperscaler capex, but it will crush the already-weak housing sector. Resi construction payrolls have fallen 2% over the last two months, a historical yellow flag for recessions. On the AI side, Pies disproves the "token panic" bear case: while open-source models are growing, 3Fourteen's proprietary OpenRouter data shows that total token demand is up 40% month-over-month, and frontier token pricing is actually rising. He details 3Fourteen's custom benchmarking tool, **Caliban**, and explains why the current semiconductor bull run (30–31 months in) has room to run compared to the 63-month expansion of the 1990s. In energy, Pies flags extreme Managed Money short positioning (40%) as a highly reliable buy signal for a tactical oil rally to $85–$90 Brent, though long-term clearing prices will remain anchored at $75–$80. He holds his S&P 500 target steady at **7850**.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The $400 Billion Gap and Resource Crowding Out
Pies highlights the stark divergence between two key areas of fixed investment:
*   **IT & Software Investment:** Moonshotting to **$1.5 trillion** annually, driven by hyperscaler AI capex.
*   **Residential Fixed Investment:** Plateauing and declining to **$1.1 trillion** due to high interest rates.
This **$400 Billion Gap** creates a physical "crowding out" effect. Data center construction absorbs specialized labor (electricians, plumbers, trades) and resources, driving up wages and construction costs. This pulls productive capacity away from the housing sector, leading to a 2% decline in residential construction payrolls over two months. 3Fourteen monitors this payroll metric closely: historically, an 8% drawdown in residential construction payrolls has preceded modern recessions.

> "AI is part of the crowding out of some of these traditional consumer sectors... tech fixed investment [is at] 1.5 trillion versus a 1.1 trillion in favor of residential... It's taking up real resources... you can't find a plumber, can't find an electrician... trades are being pulled away from the housing sector."
> — *Warren Pies*

#### 2. The Fed's Interest Rate Dilemma
This structural crowding out leaves the Federal Reserve in a corner. The housing sector is highly interest-rate sensitive and is already showing signs of distress. Conversely, the technology capex boom (hyperscalers spending close to $1 trillion next year) is rate-insensitive: a 25–50 basis point hike will not deter Microsoft, Google, or Amazon from building AI infrastructure. If the Fed hikes rates to cool tech-driven PC inflation (which is currently adding 7 bps/month to core PCE), they will crush the housing sector and the labor market. Pies expects the Fed to remain on a "true hold" at ~3.75%, supported by the core Powell-Williams-Warsh-Waller voting block.

> "The most responsive area of the economy to monetary policy is still the housing market... they would have to do a lot of leaning on that sector... before they even touch the data center buildout. Hyperscaler capex going up above a trillion dollars... 25, 50, 75 basis points of hikes is not going to change that trajectory."
> — *Warren Pies*

#### 3. Debunking the "Token Panic" and AI Demand Collapse
Pies addresses the bearish argument that open-source models and falling token prices represent a collapse in AI demand. 3Fourteen tracks hour-by-hour Neo Cloud GPU availability and OpenRouter token consumption:
*   **Total Token Volume:** Up **40% month-over-month** across the ecosystem, indicating massive structural growth.
*   **Frontier vs. Open Source Mix:** Open-source token usage is growing rapidly (60%+ MoM), but frontier models still grow double-digits.
*   **Pricing Divergence:** Frontier token prices on OpenRouter are actually **rising**, while open-source token prices are falling. If enterprise demand were collapsing, frontier pricing would be falling.
He notes that while the "token maxing" corporate metrics are inefficient and will rationalize, overall compute demand is highly robust. 3Fourteen's custom benchmarking of their proprietary assistant, **Caliban**, shows that open-source models still suffer a significant performance gap compared to frontier models on complex charting and data analysis.

> "Total inference... tokens processed on open router is up 40% month-over-month... We're seeing pricing for frontier tokens rising on open router and we're seeing open source token pricing falling... The worry that token demand is falling off... doesn't wash... The token scramble continues."
> — *Warren Pies*

#### 4. Semiconductor Cycles: 1990s vs. 2026
While 3Fourteen's blow-off terminal top screen currently flags semiconductors as overextended, Pies advises against calling an immediate cycle top. In the late 1990s, semis first registered on their blow-off screen in 1995, but the bull market lasted for **63 months** (gaining 11x) and went through four separate 20%+ pullbacks. The current cycle is only 30–31 months in and is up 4x. Because tech capex represents a structural 3% of GDP, the current cycle will likely be longer and stronger. Furthermore, semiconductor valuations have broken historical cyclical patterns: historically, price-to-sales multiples compressed when margins peaked. Today, both margins and P/S are expanding simultaneously, indicating investors are pricing them like wide-moat non-cyclicals.

> "In semis... [we] screen positive as a potential blow-off terminal top... back in the '90s... that condition lasted for 63 months... over the course of that time it 11x... and we're at like 4x right now... Everything lines up... Be ready for one 20% drop a year... [but] I don't see the end of the cycle."
> — *Warren Pies*

#### 5. Energy: Geopolitical Normalization and extreme Oil Shorts
With the Strait of Hormuz remaining open, the energy market is transitioning from a geopolitical shortage panic to a supply glut, exacerbated by China cutting 4% of global demand by halting imports. However, Pies flags a major tactical opportunity: Managed Money short positioning is at an extreme **40% short**. Over the last 10 years, oil market positioning has been 3Fourteen's single best contrarian signal. When these shorts cover, Brent will likely spike to $85–$90, providing a tactical trading opportunity, though long-term clearing prices will settle around $75–$80.

> "The positioning data is back to... a 40% managed money short position which... is extreme pessimism... The single best signal [for oil] is positioning... If you get a short covering, there'll be a bid... back to 85–90 Brent... but the long-term clearing price of oil is... 75–80."
> — *Warren Pies*

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Maintain Equity Exposure (Target 7850):** Stay long equities, as structural capex (3% of GDP) and a 5%–6% fiscal deficit make a near-term recession highly unlikely.
2.  **Buy Semiconductor Pullbacks:** Expect one 20%+ drawdown in the semiconductor group per year (analogous to the late 1990s). Treat these pullbacks as buying opportunities rather than cycle tops.
3.  **Position for Multiple Contraction in H2:** Acknowledge that with the Fed on hold, multiple expansion is over. Equity gains must be driven by earnings growth (projected at 15% CAGR ex-2026 front-loading).
4.  **Tactically Buy Oil on Extreme Short Signal:** Buy oil or energy exposure to capture a short-covering rally back to $85–$90 Brent, but treat it as a trade rather than a secular holding.
5.  **Monitor Resi Construction Payrolls:** Use the monthly BLS residential construction payroll print as a leading warning system. If the drawdown reaches 8%, reduce overall equity risk.
