# Growing Risk Of Semiconductors Rolling Over & Dragging The Market Down With Them | Lance Roberts

Detailed rundown and analysis of the *Thoughtful Money* Weekly Market Recap featuring **Lance Roberts**, Chief Investment Strategist at **RIA Advisors**, hosted by Adam Taggart. The episode was recorded and released on Friday, July 10, 2026.

---

### I. Executive Summary
Lance Roberts warns of a growing technical risk that semiconductor stocks (tracking the `SMH` ETF, which now commands an 18% market cap weight in the S&P 500) are forming a defined "head and shoulders" rollover pattern that could drag the broader market down in August or September. Roberts updates his AI capex thesis, noting that while data center spending is highly productive, the near-term economic multiplier has run at $2.00–$2.50 of GDP growth per dollar spent (short of the $3.00–$4.00 long-term estimates) due to lags in productivity realization. He flags a critical regulatory change: FINRA and the SEC's June 2026 policy lowering margin minimums to $2,000 and introducing **intraday margin calculations**, which accelerates the speed at which intraday sell-offs trigger margin liquidations. RIA Advisors remains defensively positioned with a 40% bond allocation (primarily cash and short-duration), looking to tactically add long-end duration (raising weight from 5% to 7–9%) if yields spike on temporary oil shocks before disinflation resumes.

### III. Detailed Key Takeaways

#### 1. The Semiconductor Head-and-Shoulders Pattern (`SMH`)
Roberts highlights the extreme concentration in the semiconductor sector, with chipmakers now representing 18% of the S&P 500's total market cap. Technical charts show that `SMH` is forming a highly defined "head and shoulders" rollover pattern. While the pattern has not broken down, the market is pricing in 2029 earnings for many of these names. Any disappointment in the upcoming Q2 earnings reports or guidance from the Magnificent 7 (reporting in the last two weeks of July) could trigger a technical reversal, dragging the capitalization-weighted indexes down.

> "This has been what's really kind of driving the market as of late has been semiconductor stocks... SMH... is kind of building a pretty defined head and shoulders pattern... If these things do reverse, there is going to be a drag in the market. And this is where all your momentum chase is right now."
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 2. The AI Capex GDP Multiplier Lags
Roberts updates his analysis on the productivity returns of AI infrastructure investments. Historically, productive capital expenditures (such as roads, dams, or power plants) provide a $3.00 to $4.00 GDP multiplier over time. Current data shows that the current AI buildout is delivering a $2.00 to $2.50 multiplier. Roberts explains that this is a natural consequence of the early-stage construction phase; the economy must build the physical data centers before corporate software integrations can manifest broad productivity boosts.

> "A year later, we can kind of look back and see that we're getting about... two to $2.50 worth of capex spending... multiplier in the economy... A lot of this is early stage spending and we haven't seen the productivity benefits just yet... We got to build the data center first."
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 3. Intraday Margin Debt Liquidations (SEC/FINRA Rule Change)
Roberts warns that speculative leverage in the market is much higher than raw margin statistics suggest due to retail trading in 2x/3x leveraged ETFs and call options. He flags a critical rule change passed by FINRA and the SEC in June 2026:
*   **Lower Minimums:** The account equity threshold to trade on margin was lowered from $25,000 to $2,000.
*   **Intraday Calculation:** Broker-dealers now calculate margin maintenance requirements *intraday* in real-time, rather than once at the end of the trading day.
This means that an intraday market sell-off (e.g., 5%) will trigger automatic broker liquidations instantly rather than allowing investors to fund accounts by the daily close, creating a potential cascading selling feedback loop.

> "The SEC and FINRA just passed a rule in June that eliminated the personal day trading rule and it lowered the account minimums from $25,000 to $2,000... More importantly... it's now calculated intraday... We're very likely going to trigger margin calls much sooner than we would have previously."
> — *Lance Roberts*

#### 4. The Power Grid and Micro-Nuclear Criticality
Roberts and Taggart agree that the ultimate bottleneck for the AI buildout is not chip supply or software, but power availability. Data centers consume immense electricity, straining the U.S.'s aging electrical grid. Roberts advocates for public-private utility partnerships to build out nuclear power capacity, specifically using Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) and micro-reactors. Taggart notes that three micro-reactors recently achieved fision criticality, including **Aloe Atomics** on July 4th, which successfully ran a soup-to-nuts test of a full-scale micro-reactor using its own in-house staff and production-grade hardware.

#### 5. Continuing Resolutions vs. Actual Budgeting
Roberts rails against the U.S. government's fiscal management, noting that the federal government has not passed a formal budget since 2008. Instead, it has operated entirely via Continuing Resolutions (CRs), which automatically increase the prior year's spending by roughly 8% annually. Under the mathematical rule of 72, this has guaranteed a rapid doubling of government expenditures independent of tax revenues or economic needs.

### IV. Portfolio Positioning & Action Items

1.  **Prepare for a August/September Correction:** Implement downside protection or raise cash in preparation for a seasonal 5% to 10% market correction in August/September, which could be triggered by semiconductor earnings disappointments or geopolitical base strikes in the Middle East.
2.  **Monitor the `SMH` Right Shoulder:** Watch technical support levels on the semiconductor ETF (`SMH`). If the right shoulder completes and breaks down, reduce index beta exposure immediately.
3.  **Tactically Add Bond Duration on Yield Spikes:** RIA Advisors holds a 40% bond allocation, with 35% in cash and short-duration bills. If geopolitical shocks push oil prices to $90–$100 and temporarily spike yields, use the opportunity to raise long-duration bonds to 7%–9% of the portfolio to lock in yields before the 2027 rate-cut cycle.
4.  **Hedge Against Intraday Margin Cascades:** Avoid trading highly leveraged ETFs or options on margin. Real-time intraday margin calculations mean brokers will liquidate positions mid-day during a panic, locking in losses.
5.  **Focus on Energy Infrastructure/Utilities:** Overweight utility and nuclear energy infrastructure plays that stand to benefit from the long-term power grid bottleneck.
