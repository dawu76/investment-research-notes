Question: thoughts on this article - "outcastbeta.com: Drawdown risk = portfolio volatility normalized by Sharpe ratio"? Idea: _"For practitioners who perceive drawdowns as risk, portfolio risk is not measured by portfolio volatility, but by portfolio volatility normalized by Sharpe ratio. - Comment: I’ve always felt that simply targeting a fixed volatility per strategy (or for the portfolio as a whole) misses part of the picture — for example, a strategy with a Sharpe ratio of 2 and 10% vol is clearly less risky than another with the same vol but a SR of 0.8. So I liked the idea in this article of targeting similar expected drawdowns by sizing according to vol/SR, instead of just vol. Has anyone looked into this approach? From what I understand, it’s essentially equivalent to sizing each strategy at the same fraction of Kelly, or something like that. - Euan: This is going to get covered in my portfolio presentation but it really is a matter of what statistics you think you can believe. At one end is equal-weighting, where you basically give up and say that you have no clue whether any of the strats are better than any of the others. This is very hard to beat for stock portfolios because stocks have very unstable statistics. Next is equal-vol. Here you say "returns are too hard to predict but I think I have an idea about vol". Then is mean variance optimization where you think future returns and vols are knowable. This is optimistic but for active strategies it isn't as bad as it is for stocks. You have control. The returns are closer to stationary. Using Sharpe is even more problematic. The uncertainty in a ratio is worse than the uncertainty in the numerator or the denominator. So each case is different and the further up the ladder you go, the more work you need to do to avoid problems. Just remember the purpose of a portfolio is to diversify not to be optimal anything.
- Comment: Makes sense — since there is an estimation problem, you cant necessarily trust the stats. I was thinking about this mostly in the context of relative sizing active strategies like end of month flows vs short $BOIL vs short VXX and etc. Also, agree that the main purpose is to diversify as much as possible with uncorrelated strategies, but how to size one vs the other is the main difficulty I sometimes have... - Comment: i believe these are all linked. The maximum sharpe ratio portfolio is proportional to the inverse covariance matrix multiplied by the expected return, i.e. $\Sigma^{-1} \mu$. now assume the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix are zero. that is a reasonable approximation when your strategies are uncorrelated, so then you would size strategy $i$ proportional to $\mu_i / \sigma_i^2$. this is exactly equivalent to Kelly / fractional Kelly. this is also equivalent to sizing by SR / vol, as that is $SR \* vol = (\mu_i / \sigma_i) \* (1 / \sigma_i) = \mu_i / \sigma_i^2$. if you give up on predicting returns, then you can plug the vector of ones $1$ in where you would ordinarily have the expected return $\mu$. this yields the minimum variance portfolio $\sigma^{-1} 1$. if you assume the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix are zero, then you end up sizing by inverse variance - that is almost inverse vol (a.k.a. equal vol).
